# FDNY: Traffic, Not Bike Lanes, is to Blame for Increased Response Times

Julianne Cuba, Streetsblog NYC, September 19, 2019. The supplied capture includes a post-publication Friday update.
[Original article](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/2019/09/19/fdny-traffic-not-bike-lanes-is-to-blame-for-increased-response-times)
·
[Local PDF](../../raw-sources/news/2019-streetsblog-fdny-traffic-is-the-problem.pdf).

## Issue and reporting basis

The article addresses a New York City dispute over whether bike lanes and Vision Zero street changes slowed emergency
response. It combines secondary reporting of the [2019 Mayor's Management Report](https://www1.nyc.gov/assets/operations/downloads/pdf/mmr2019/2019_mmr.pdf),
an FDNY spokesman's response, statements by firefighters and a former transportation official, prior news coverage,
and a later radio exchange with Mayor Bill de Blasio. It is news reporting with an explicit argument against blaming
bike lanes, not a street-design evaluation.[^cuba2019]

## Main reporting

The article reports average **fire-company response time to all emergencies of 5 minutes 15 seconds**, counting
**FDNY dispatch and travel time only**, up **25 seconds (8.6%)** from fiscal 2015 (July 2014–June 2015). It also
reports increases across fire-truck and ambulance categories, but the specific 25-second comparison should not be
relabelled as an ambulance-only measure or full 911-call-to-arrival time.[^cuba2019]

The management report's quoted explanation is tentative: travel-time increases “may be partly attributed” to
population growth, more vehicles and construction. Asked directly about bike lanes, FDNY spokesman Myles Miller
referred Streetsblog to that report rather than supplying a separate bike-lane analysis. In the later radio exchange,
de Blasio identified congestion as the central issue and rejected the claim that Vision Zero explained the increase.[^cuba2019]

Conflicting accounts remain part of the reporting: FDNY Lt. Adrienne Walsh said pedestrian plazas hamper emergency
service, and union official Bobby Eustace blamed barriers and other Vision Zero changes for restricting passage.
Former DOT official Jon Orcutt said DOT consults FDNY before redesigns and accounts for emergency access. These are
attributed operational accounts and policy positions, not quantified effects.[^cuba2019]

## Limits

The article supplies no incident sample, route exposure, controlled comparison or uncertainty for the response-time
trend. Its categorical headline is stronger than the quoted report's partial, tentative attribution. Neither the
aggregate increase nor the opposing accounts isolate a bike-lane effect or establish that every redesign is harmless.
The underlying management report, linked videos, earlier reporting and radio recording were not independently
reviewed. All nine PDF pages were read as text; substantive article content is on pp. 1–7.[^cuba2019]
