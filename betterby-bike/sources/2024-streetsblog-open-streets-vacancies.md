# Car-Free Streets are Good For Business, Yet Another Report Shows

Sophia Lebowitz, Streetsblog NYC, November 18, 2024. [Original article](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/2024/11/18/car-free-streets-are-good-for-business-yet-another-report-shows).

## Reporting and evidence

The article covers NYC Department of City Planning's [Storefront Activity in NYC Neighborhoods](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/planning-level/housing-economy/nyc_dcp_storefront_report_1024.pdf)
and interviews public-realm officials and Open Streets advocates. The underlying report was not reviewed.
Its reproduced graphic shows lower vacancy on Vanderbilt, Orchard and Columbus than in their surrounding
neighborhoods. These are storefront occupancy comparisons, not sales, profits or before/after changes.[^lebowitz2024]

Original reporting includes North Brooklyn Open Streets volunteer Brent Bovenzi's walking count: Berry Street
had 7.1% vacancy versus 15.1% across Williamsburg. He said three empty storefronts advertised forthcoming
businesses. Vanderbilt volunteer Alex Morano and Bovenzi describe benefits but warn that volunteer labor
limits the program. Bovenzi's remote-worker explanation and their calls for city support are accounts and
recommendations, not evaluated mechanisms or funding effects.[^lebowitz2024]

The article places these observations within citywide pandemic recovery, reporting over 45,000 business
openings since early 2020 and over 130 Open Streets locations at the start of the 2024 season. It does not
attribute those citywide openings to the program. Its photo caption says a Doyers Street restaurant expanded
to two more locations after roadway dining expansion; this is an anecdote, not an estimated intervention effect.

## Limits and relation to existing evidence

Read the full eight-page capture, including substantive reporting and captions on pp. 1–5, and visually read
the p. 2 DCP graphic. The article does not provide survey dates, vacancy definitions, comparison-area
storefront denominators, selection criteria or adjustment for neighborhood differences. Berry Street's count
has neither a storefront denominator nor a documented survey protocol. Lower vacancy does not establish that
car removal caused business growth, and advocate descriptions of “proof” exceed the evidence presented.

This 2024 DCP coverage is distinct from [NYC DOT's 2022 tax-filing study](/sources/2022-nyc-streets-for-recovery.md).
Do not treat occupancy, restaurant sales and filer retention as interchangeable outcomes or this article as an
independent replication of either municipal dataset.
