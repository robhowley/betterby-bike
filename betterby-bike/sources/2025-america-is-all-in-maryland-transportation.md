# Driving Change Together: Maryland's All-In Approach to Clean Transportation

Miguel Moravec, America Is All In, July 17, 2025.
[Original article](https://www.americaisallin.com/driving-change-together-marylands-all-approach-clean-transportation).

## Reporting and policy status

This coalition account describes advocacy for Maryland's Transportation and Climate Alignment (TCA)
Act, first introduced in January 2024. The proposal sought transit, bicycle and pedestrian investment
and accountability for projects increasing pollution and commuter costs. The article reports Governor
Wes Moore's executive order directing evaluation of transportation-plan greenhouse-gas impacts and
identification of annual greenhouse-gas and vehicle-miles-traveled targets. It does not report enactment
of the full TCA Act: advocates planned to pursue passage in 2026.[^moravec2025]

The National Aquarium, Archdiocese of Baltimore and City of Rockville supplied supportive testimony.
Ceres convened businesses and governor's staff; RMI contributed technical policy work and cost
modeling. The article says University of Maryland's Center for Global Sustainability modeling informed
the state's 2023 Climate Pollution Reduction Plan and was cited in the 2025 bill. These are accounts
of policy participation and influence, not independently evaluated attribution or delivered infrastructure.

## Reported evidence

RMI's Smarter MODES tool reportedly projected **over $3,000 annually per average Maryland household**
in avoided vehicle fuel, maintenance and depreciation costs if the state achieved its climate and
mode-shift goals. This conditional scenario is not observed savings or a cycling-project benefit.
The article links the [calculator explanation](https://rmi.org/states-can-quantify-the-benefits-of-climate-friendly-transportation-options-with-rmis-smarter-modes-calculator/)
and [CGS modeling appendix](https://cgs.umd.edu/research-impact/publications/marylands-climate-pollution-reduction-plan-modeling-appendix);
neither underlying analysis was reviewed.[^moravec2025]

A Maryland Sierra Club poll reportedly found 78% support for investing in transportation choices to
offset large-project pollution. No sample size, field dates or questionnaire are supplied. The Aquarium
says roughly a third of its staff commute without individual motor vehicles; the article supplies no
staff denominator or travel survey. Neither claim measures statewide cycling demand.

## Limits and linked finding

Read the complete 13-page capture, with substantive reporting on pp. 1–10 and site material afterward.
Visually inspected pp. 7–8: the calculator caption accompanies a blank image area, not a legible results
chart. The article supplies no model inputs, savings horizon, uncertainty interval or full net-cost
accounting. Supportive testimony and coalition self-reporting do not establish emissions, affordability,
health or access effects; historical policy status is not verified current law.
