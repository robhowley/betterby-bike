# Hoboken Green Infrastructure Strategic Plan: demographics excerpt

The [supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/hoboken-green-infrastructure-strategic-plan.pdf) contains only
printed page 7, “Demographics,” of the named plan. No publication date, author credits, original publication URL
or data references are visible. The whole supplied page was read visually after text extraction returned no text;
the rest of the plan and underlying datasets were not reviewed.[^plan]

## Content and reporting basis

This is planning context, not an intervention evaluation. The page compares Hoboken with Hudson County and the
North Jersey region and identifies vulnerable housing communities in flood-prone areas. It reports:[^plan]

- Carless households: 15.7% in Hoboken, 13.7% in Hudson County and 12.5% in North Jersey, used to argue that transit
  matters for residents' mobility.
- Poverty rates: 10.1% in Hoboken versus 8.6% in North Jersey; for families with children, 6.3% versus 5%.
- HUD-subsidized housing: 46 units per 1,000 population versus 13 in the North Jersey NJTPA region. The paragraph
  also lists public housing as 27 versus 5 and multi-family housing as 16 versus 6, but does not repeat their units
  or explain the housing categories, so these should not be treated as counts or shares of all housing.

The page places Andrew Jackson Gardens and the Colombian Arms Senior Living Community in sewershed H1 and
Hoboken Housing Authority housing in H4. It describes vulnerable populations as concentrated in the city's most
flood-prone areas and argues that green infrastructure there has strong potential to address flooding and improve
residents' lives. A HOPES quotation endorses the city's focus on the flood-affected “Blue Zone.” Photos and a small
housing-community/sewershed map accompany the text; they are not measured outcome evidence.[^plan]

## Interpretation and access limits

The excerpt gives no observation years, dataset definitions, sampling methods, uncertainty or supporting tables.
Its household percentages describe reported vehicle availability, not transit ridership, cycling, commuting shares
or route quality. No before/after or comparison-group outcomes show that green infrastructure reduced flooding,
improved mobility or delivered equitable benefits. The targeting argument and HOPES endorsement are planning
positions, not demonstrated effects. These limitations concern the supplied page, not a claim that the full plan
contains no further analysis.[^plan]

Bundle searches found no matching plan or demographic comparison. Other Hoboken accounts in the bundle concern
street safety and daylighting, not independent confirmation of these figures.
