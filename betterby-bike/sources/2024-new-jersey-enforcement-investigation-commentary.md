## Reporting and attribution

The writer describes an attorney general investigation into **whether** State Police intentionally reduced traffic
violation enforcement in response to an AG-sponsored report said to have found continuing racial bias in traffic
stops. The crop does not establish intent or give the investigation's outcome; the bias report itself was not
reviewed.[^njexcerpt2]

Attributing the figures to the Times, the commentary says citations, including for drunken driving, fell **81%
during an eight-month period beginning in July 2023**. It quotes an “almost immediate uptick in motor vehicle
crashes” but supplies no crash magnitude, baseline or exposure. This differs from the Times passage recorded in
[excerpt 1's Source](2024-new-jersey-state-police-traffic-enforcement-crashes.md), which attaches 81% to August's
year-on-year decline and separately describes a slowdown lasting more than eight months. The descriptions cannot
be treated as interchangeable without the full reporting.[^njexcerpt2]

The writer interprets events as a power struggle or deep tension between the AG's office and State Police.
The commentary quotes Superintendent Patrick Callahan's stated willingness to accept scrutiny and oversight,
then contrasts a 2023 memo attributed to troopers' union head Wayne Blanchard warning of hostile internal and
external entities and highly scrutinized enforcement actions. These are attributed statements and the writer's
interpretation, not findings that the union ordered a slowdown or that oversight caused crashes.[^njexcerpt2]
