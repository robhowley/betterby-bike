# Dramatic fall in London's levels of deadly pollutants after Ulez expansion

Matthew Taylor, The Guardian, March 7, 2025. Read the complete four-page capture: article and
photo caption on pp. 1–3, related-story links and a final “Most viewed” page.

## Reporting and evidence

The article covers a Greater London Authority report on the first year after the August 2023
London-wide Ulez expansion. It says an independent expert advisory group extensively reviewed the
findings. The primary report is not supplied or directly linked in the capture and was not reviewed.
The reporting describes cleaner air, especially in deprived communities, against a background of
council legal challenges and political opposition. Mayor Sadiq Khan defends the expansion; WHO's
Maria Neira, advisory chair Gary Fuller and campaigner Simon Birkett endorse cleaner-air action.[^taylor2025]

Reported results include vehicle-exhaust PM2.5 emissions in outer London in 2024 estimated 31% below
a scenario without the 2023 expansion. Among the most deprived communities near busy roads, the
estimated number exposed to illegal pollution levels in 2023 was 80% lower across London and 82%
lower in outer London than without Ulez. These are different measures and policy comparisons.[^taylor2025]

The article also reports improvement at 99% of monitoring sites since 2019 and describes roadside
NO2 as down 27% “since” Ulez began in 2019. Later
[Guardian coverage](/sources/2026-guardian-london-air-pollution-deaths.md) describes the prior-year GLA
27% result as relative to a no-Ulez scenario instead. Treat this as overlapping reporting with unresolved
comparison wording, not independent confirmation of a temporal decline. The article reports 97%
vehicle compliance and describes a £12.50 daily charge for noncompliant vehicles at publication.[^taylor2025]

## Limits

The capture supplies no model specification, uncertainty intervals, absolute exposed-population counts,
precise deprivation definition or pollutant threshold for the equity result. Exhaust emissions are not
ambient PM2.5 concentrations or total particulate emissions including wear. Its aviation-trip analogy for
CO2 savings omits a mass and explicit accounting period. Political and expert health-benefit statements
are not measured changes in disease or deaths. Neither these estimates nor improved monitoring sites
establish cycling-project benefits, mode shift or elimination of pollution harms.
