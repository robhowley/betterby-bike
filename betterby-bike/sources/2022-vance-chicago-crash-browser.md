# Chicago Crash Browser updates with stats, filters, and news article links

Steven Vance's [August 9, 2022 announcement](https://www.stevencanplan.com/2022/08/chicago-crash-browser-updates-with-stats-filters-and-news-article-links/)
describes updates to the Chicago Cityscape tool he edits. This is a first-person product account, not a
crash-risk study or independent evaluation. The six-page PDF was read, including substantive text,
screenshots and captions on pp. 1–4; the live browser and underlying city records were not reviewed.

## Reporting and evidence

Vance says a free Cityscape account now permits map access, while downloads require special access or a
Real Estate Pro account. Weekly statistics are visible without browser access and summarize crashes,
deaths and people in the two worst injury tiers. The p. 2 caption warns that numbers can change during
the week as police upload reports. The browser uses the city's Crashes table; the exposed crash record
ID allows users to locate related records in the separate Vehicles and People tables.[^article]

Filters cover designated Pedestrian Streets, locations within one block of CTA or Metra stations,
news articles or notes, and officer-recorded hit-and-run status. Missing hit-and-run entries are a
separate option from “No.” Address searches default to one block (660 feet), with an adjustable radius.
Vance intends to attach articles to every fatal pedestrian or bicycle crash, largely from Streetsblog
Chicago, where he identifies himself as editor at large. This is an intention, not demonstrated complete
coverage.[^article]

Footnote 1 describes fatal-severity upgrades that do not reach the city's published crash record.
Vance says he manually changes the browser's “most severe injury” to fatal and records a note so that
the crash appears in fatal-crash searches.[^article]

## Limits and reconciliation

The article gives no audit, frequency of missed updates, correction completeness or exposure denominator.
Screenshots illustrate tool behavior, not a before/after safety evaluation. Neither these features nor
news links demonstrate lower injury risk, complete death counts or current portal conditions. The
[New Jersey access review](2024-new-jersey-crash-data-access.md) addresses a different problem: public
availability and geocoding, not whether later fatal-severity updates reach published records.
