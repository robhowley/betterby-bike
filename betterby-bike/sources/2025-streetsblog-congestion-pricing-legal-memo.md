# Federal lawyers' congestion-pricing strategy memo

Dave Colon, **Streetsblog NYC, April 24, 2025**.
[Original article, captured PDF](../../raw-sources/news/disorder-in-the-court-federal-lawyers-upload-memo-admitting-their-congestion-pricing-case-.pdf).

## Issue and reporting basis

Colon reports that an April 11 internal legal-strategy email was apparently mistakenly uploaded to the
court record in the MTA's challenge to Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy's attempt to end NYC congestion
pricing. The article quotes Assistant U.S. Attorneys Dominika Tarczynska, David Farber and Christine
Poscablo, an MTA attorney's court letter, Riders Alliance's response and a subsequent USDOT statement.
An editor's correction clarifies that the memo's authors worked for the Department of Justice, advising
USDOT, rather than being USDOT staff.[^colon2025memo]

## Reported legal arguments and competing accounts

The quoted attorneys considered two reasons for withdrawing approval unlikely to persuade the court:
that cordon tolling required a toll-free entry route, and that raising revenue rather than only reducing
congestion made the program ineligible under the Value Pricing Pilot Program. They cited legislative
history, FHWA practice and the government's earlier arguments that the program served both congestion
reduction and capital-project funding. Their preliminary administrative-record review reportedly found
no supporting explanation for the policy reversal beyond the secretary's decision itself.[^colon2025memo]

The attorneys suggested a different argument based on terminating a cooperative agreement when agency
priorities change, while noting the agreement lacked explicit termination provisions. The article reports
that Duffy had invoked termination in a subsequent letter and that the MTA sought protection against
threatened funding penalties. The memo also advised evaluating whether termination required a new
National Environmental Policy Act analysis. These are reported legal assessments and procedural steps,
not established legal requirements or a ruling on either side's case.[^colon2025memo]

Riders Alliance welcomed the disclosure and predicted continued faster commutes and transit improvements.
USDOT disputed the memo, attacked the attorneys' conduct and reiterated possible project and funding
restrictions. The article's description of the termination order as illegal and its claims of pollution,
crash and congestion benefits are editorial framing, not findings established by this reporting.[^colon2025memo]
