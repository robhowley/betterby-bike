# D.C. Advocates Sue To Save Key Bike Lane From Trump

Kea Wilson, **Streetsblog, March 25, 2026**. [Original article, captured PDF](../../raw-sources/news/d-c-advocates-sue-to-save-key-bike-lane-from-trump-streetsblog-new-york-city.pdf).

## Issue and reporting basis

Wilson reports that the Washington Area Bicyclist Association sued federal agencies after signs announced
removal of portions of the 15th Street NW bike lane between Constitution Avenue and the Jefferson Memorial.
The article identifies National Park Service oversight, unlike other DC lanes, and reports a Department of
the Interior agreement to delay work until March 30. This is a dated dispute, not evidence that removal
subsequently occurred or that a court ruled on its legality.[^wilson2026dc]

The reporting centers on WABA deputy director Colin Browne's interview and advocacy accounts. Browne contrasts
years of public input for installation with the abrupt removal announcement. The article says notice,
explanation, environmental review and public comment were absent and legally required; the complaint and
applicable law were not independently reviewed. It also reports a leaked January federal memo considering
other corridor removals and congressional efforts to prohibit removals through appropriations language.
Neither effort is presented as an enacted protection.[^wilson2026dc]

## Network importance and competing claims

Wilson describes the corridor as serving roughly **4,000 riders a day** and, after an extension, providing
the only low-stress route across the Potomac to Virginia. The capture does not supply counting dates,
methods, segment boundaries for that total or a network-comfort assessment. These are reported indicators
of the route's importance, not measured consequences of losing it.[^wilson2026dc]

An FHWA spokesperson's statement, quoted through the Washington Post, says the lane dramatically reduced
roadway capacity. Wilson counters with a local analysis reporting **17% higher vehicle speeds**, **46%
fewer crashes across all modes** and **91% fewer bicycle crashes** after installation. No analysis title,
observation periods, raw counts, exposure measures, comparison group or precise evaluated segment is supplied
in the extracted article. Higher speed is not itself a measure of roadway capacity. These percentages cannot
establish causality, risk per trip or the effect of removing the threatened segment. FHWA and Interior did
not respond to Streetsblog's requests for comment.[^wilson2026dc]

## Coverage and contribution

All substantive article text on PDF pp. 1–4 was reviewed; subsequent pages contain newsletter, related-story
and site material. The capture supplies no study methods, tables or outcome evaluation. Linked reporting,
the local analysis, leaked memo and legal documents were not reviewed.

No separate Finding is added: the distinct contribution is the reported removal dispute, federal control
