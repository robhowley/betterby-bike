# MUTCD green-pavement interim approval (2011)

Federal Highway Administration, memorandum from Jeffrey A. Lindley, Associate Administrator for Operations,
15 April 2011. [Original memorandum (local PDF)](../../raw-sources/research/mutcd-green-paint.pdf).
All five scanned pages were read.

## Purpose and basis

IA-14 allowed optional green colored pavement in marked bicycle lanes and lane extensions through intersections and
other traffic-conflict areas, pending MUTCD rulemaking. It was an administrative approval under the 2009 MUTCD, not a
new mandate or a primary evaluation. FHWA described experiments authorized over the preceding decade across U.S.
state and local agencies, including New York City, Portland, Chicago and others. The memorandum says its Office of
Transportation Operations reviewed available data, but supplies no study-level methods, sample sizes or quantitative
outcome tables.[^memo]

## Assessment and conditions

- FHWA reported more accurate cyclist positioning through intersections/conflict areas, favorable cyclist and motorist
  impressions, and no notable negative operational effects in the experiments. Cyclists reported feeling safer and
  motorists reported greater awareness of cyclists; these are not measured injury reductions.
- Use required a written request and FHWA approval, compliance with technical conditions, a location inventory and
  commitments concerning later rulemaking and safety concerns. A state could request approval for its jurisdictions.
- Green pavement supplemented, rather than replaced, required lane lines, symbols and other markings. Permitted
  applications included full or partial lane lengths and specified dotted-line extensions across intersections,
  driveways, ramps and the beginning of turn bays.
- The memo specified daytime/nighttime color coordinates and daytime luminance limits. Retroreflectivity was optional,
  with neither a requirement nor a recommendation for it. Material selection should minimize cyclists' loss of
  traction.[^memo]

## Interpretation limits

FHWA's judgment that the tested applications were successful and posed low safety/operational risk is an agency
assessment, not a quantified crash-risk estimate. The memo does not identify which studies support each conclusion,
provide uncertainty estimates, or establish that adverse effects are impossible. New York City's inclusion among
experimental jurisdictions does not establish whether the separately ingested
[NYCDOT evaluation](/sources/2011-nyc-green-paint.md) supplied particular results; do not count the memo as independent
confirmation of that study. These are the April 2011 approval's terms, not verified current MUTCD requirements.[^memo]
