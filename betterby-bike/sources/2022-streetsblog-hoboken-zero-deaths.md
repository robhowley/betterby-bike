# Hoboken's four years without traffic deaths and next injury-prevention steps

Kea Wilson, Streetsblog USA, July 14, 2022. [Original article, supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/news/a-new-jersey-city-eliminated-traffic-deaths-for-4-years-and-now-it-s-trying-to-end-injurie.pdf).
Read the complete substantive article on PDF pp. 1–6 and visually inspected the Hoboken Vision Zero graphic
on p. 3. Remaining pages contain newsletter, related-story and site material. Linked municipal records and
research were not independently reviewed.

## Reporting and policy context

The article reports four consecutive years without traffic deaths in Hoboken, New Jersey, a dense city of about
60,000 residents. It quotes Mayor Ravi Bhalla and parking and transportation director Ryan Sharp, rather than
presenting a controlled evaluation. Officials had begun lowering the speed limit from **25 to 20 mph**, with a
goal of ending traffic injuries by 2030. Council approval is reported as unanimous; the article does not provide
post-implementation speed or injury results.[^article]

Sharp describes incremental daylighting and curb extensions, with raised intersections and road diets along the
High Injury Network bundled into scheduled repaving largely funded by the state DOT. He says infrastructure
would reinforce the new limit and police would initially emphasize education rather than ticketing. These are
implementation accounts and intentions, not cost-effectiveness estimates or demonstrated enforcement-equity gains.
Sharp also cautions that safety could regress and that Hoboken's pre-war urban fabric may make its task easier
than other cities'. Budget, staffing and political resistance remain constraints.[^article]

## Limits and existing coverage

No annual fatality table, travel exposure, comparison design or uncertainty estimate supports causal attribution
to individual treatments. The article's comparison with nearby Union City and unsuccessful search for another
similar zero-death community do not establish a national ranking or a controlled counterfactual. Its cited
pedestrian impact-speed mortality estimates are not Hoboken outcomes and were not independently checked.

The embedded graphic concerns **2014–2018**, not the four-year zero-death interval. It states 4,451 crashes and
13 resulting in serious injury or death, but also displays mode-specific severe/fatal percentages that cannot
readily be reconciled with that total. Those percentages are not extracted as reliable risk estimates; the
underlying municipal data would be needed to resolve the discrepancy.[^article]

[Open Plans' later curb-management report](2023-curb-the-chaos-solutions-for-cities-at-the-curb.md) already
recounts Hoboken's 2018–2022 zero-death record. This article adds contemporaneous interviews and implementation
context, not independent confirmation of daylighting's effectiveness. [Bike Hoboken's later injury announcement](2024-bike-hoboken-traffic-injury-report-news.md)
concerns nonfatal injury counts in 2023–2024, a different outcome and period.
