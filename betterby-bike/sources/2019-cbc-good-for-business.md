# Love them or hate them, research offers financial case for big city bike lanes

James Dunne, CBC News, June 8, 2019. [Original article](https://www.cbc.ca/news/business/biking-lanes-business-health-1.5165954).

## Reporting basis and scope

CBC reviews academic and business research around five economic arguments: retail sales, customer spending, home
values, construction employment and attracting workers/companies. It explicitly focuses on cities with existing
networks, excluding suburban settings and the impacts of reduced driving lanes. This is selected secondary reporting,
not a systematic review or new evaluation. All eight supplied PDF pages were read as text; substantive reporting is
on pp. 1–7. Embedded research links were recovered, but their underlying publications were not separately read for
this ingestion.[^cbc2019]

## Retail and spending: mostly existing evidence

- NYC's 2012 **up to 49% versus 3%** sales claim repeats the existing
  [Ninth Avenue Finding](/findings/business/nyc-ninth-avenue-retail-sales.md). CBC's separate 2014 “up to 24 per cent”
  comparison links [NYCDOT's report](/sources/2014-nyc-protected-bicycle-lanes.md); use the existing
  [corridor comparisons](/findings/business/nyc-pbl-retail-comparisons.md), not a new independent effect estimate.
- The Salt Lake City link points to [Streetsblog's Broadway coverage](/sources/2015-streetsblog-slc.md), already
  represented by the [sales Finding](/findings/business/salt-lake-broadway-retail-sales.md).
- San Francisco's two-thirds favorable and 4% adverse merchant responses repeat the
  [Valencia perception result](/findings/business/valencia-merchant-business-perceptions.md), not audited sales.
- Toronto's [2017 Bloor Street assessment](/findings/business/toronto-bloor-2017-business-reports.md) adds later
  post-installation reporting, distinct from the earlier customer survey and parking-conversion prediction.
- CBC retains an adverse Vancouver result and short observation window, linking the same Stantec study already
  represented by [Vancouver sales reports](/findings/business/vancouver-separated-lane-sales-reports.md).
- [Portland spending](/findings/business/portland-bike-customer-spending.md) repeats the 24% higher monthly bicycle
  spending at bars, restaurants and convenience stores, and the supermarket exception. The proposed disposable-income
  explanation is the study author's interpretation, not a tested mechanism.
- CBC also reports higher weekly bicycle-customer spending in six French cities and similar Copenhagen results,
  without figures or methods. It links a [French report](https://www.fub.fr/sites/fub/files/fub/Enquete/velocommerce03rapfin.pdf)
  and [Copenhagen commentary](http://www.cycling-embassy.dk/2013/08/26/are-cyclists-good-customers/); these are not
  interchangeable with the existing Copenhagen spending-share measure.[^cbc2019]

## Other economic arguments

The [Delaware Finding](/findings/business/delaware-bike-path-property-values.md) preserves a reported proximity
premium alongside the review's positive-or-null pattern. CBC also repeats Lindsey et al.'s 2004 Monon Trail result:
about **11%** higher sale prices near the trail. Its **one-kilometre** boundary differs from the **half-mile** boundary
in the business report; this remains unresolved, not a second study. See the
[real-estate Topic](/topics/active-transportation-real-estate.md).

A [2011 U.S. project comparison](/findings/business/us-cycling-project-jobs-per-dollar.md) reports **11.4 local jobs
per million dollars** for cycling projects versus **7.8** for road-only projects. CBC also cites a 2014 Canadian
report's installation-cost range of **$20,000/km** for painted lanes to **$1.2 million/km** where road widening is
needed. These historical cost examples are not current budgets or a benefit-cost analysis.

Richard Florida's creative-worker argument, Rahm Emanuel's pro-bike/startup statement and bicycle-lane mentions in
Amazon's second-headquarters request show stated preferences and policy motivations, not measured relocation or
wage effects. CBC also acknowledges gentrification/affordability concerns and continuing opposition, including
Lawrence Solomon's changed position and New Zealand academics' work on “bikelash.” These accounts do not quantify
displacement, congestion relief or the economic costs of opposition.[^cbc2019]

## Limits and synthesis

Favorable sales, spending, property and jobs figures concern different populations, measures and infrastructure.
Several repeat evidence already in the bundle; adverse Vancouver and supermarket results qualify broad benefit
claims. The article supplies little information about controls, uncertainty or study selection. It does not establish
that protected lanes alone cause business growth, housing premiums or net additional employment.

- [Evaluating street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md): distinguish repeated reporting,
  post-installation accounts and employment per dollar.
- [Active transportation and real estate](/topics/active-transportation-real-estate.md): proximity associations,
  conflicting distance descriptions and unmeasured affordability effects.
