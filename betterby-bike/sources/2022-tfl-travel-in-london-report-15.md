# Travel in London, Report 15

Transport for London (TfL), ©2022. [Original supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/2022-tfl-travel-in-london-report-15.pdf).
This annual monitoring report describes travel through 2021 and into 2022 and interprets progress toward the
Mayor's Transport Strategy during pandemic recovery. It combines agency counts, travel estimates, surveys and
police-reported casualties, rather than evaluating one intervention.[^report]

## Reviewed results

- Spring 2022 area counts imply 18% more weekday cycle-kilometres than in 2019; estimated seven-day trips rose
  14% and stages 13%. Revised absolute distance estimates cannot be spliced onto earlier publications, and
  2020–2021 area counts are not necessarily representative of their whole years. The separate consolidated
  annual trip series reports a 5.4% decline in 2021 from 2020, but 23.3% growth from 2019.[^report]
- Cycling growth did not remove demographic gaps. Women were 34% of past-week cyclists in 2021/22, and
  lower-income people remained underrepresented. A separate 2021 survey reported no significant ethnicity
  differences in past-year cycling; that is a different measure and sample, not a refutation of weekly-profile gaps.
  Pandemic LTDS interviewing changed to telephone and is not wholly comparable with earlier years.[^report]
- Police-reported cyclist deaths or serious injuries reached 999 in 2021, up 15% from 2020 and 33% from the
  2017–2019 average, while all-user deaths/serious injuries were 9% below that pre-pandemic average. Counts are
  not risk per ride; pandemic travel changes complicate interpretation.[^report]
- Network proximity rose from 11.5% of Londoners within 400 metres in 2019 to 21.9% in autumn 2022, below
  the 33% aim for 2025. This is coverage, not a measured change in route usability or equitable benefit.
  TfL's expectation of continued cycling growth and call to diversify cycling are interpretations, not forecasts
  validated by later observations or isolated infrastructure effects.[^report]

## Methods, limitations and publication overlap

Area counts cover central, inner and outer London and measure weekday distance. Trip/stage estimates adjust
for weekends using LTDS; a small continuous-counter sample mainly in central/inner London supplies different,
short-term indicators. The 2021 area counts coincided with unusually low demand and conflict with other datasets.
The report explains the revision at a high level but does not supply reproducible calculation details here.
Survey profile comparisons lack subgroup sample sizes and uncertainty estimates in the reviewed pages.
STATS19 casualty counts cover police-reported road casualties, not complete hospital surveillance.[^report]

[Report 11](/sources/2018-tfl-travel-in-london-report-11.md) already represents earlier TfL counts and surveys.
Report 15 adds later observations and revised estimates from related monitoring systems, not an independent
replication. No existing summary of this publication or its extracted 2021–2022 results was found. The separate
*Cycling potential in London's diverse communities* survey is reported secondhand here; its original publication
and microdata were not reviewed. General LTN effectiveness wording on p. 104 is not extracted as a new evaluated
result, and does not independently replicate the earlier Mini-Holland panel.

## Review scope

Partial, cycling-focused review of the accessible 242-page PDF, not a whole-report ingestion. Read contents,
opening overview (p. 11), sections 2.3–2.4 through Tables 2.1–2.2 (pp. 57–61), LTDS methods and opening
trip-rate discussion (p. 77), and chapter 4 (pp. 97–114), including walking context and road-danger limitations.
Visually checked Figure 4.6 (p. 103) and Table 4.1 (p. 112); other figures/tables were read through text extraction,
without inferring exact values from unlabeled bars or lines. Remaining chapters, including cargo-bike potential,
air quality, public transport and development, and underlying datasets/cited studies were not substantively reviewed.
