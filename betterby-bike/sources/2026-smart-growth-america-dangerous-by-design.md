# Dangerous by Design 2026 (Smart Growth America overview)

Smart Growth America, [Dangerous by Design landing page](https://smartgrowthamerica.org/dangerous-by-design/).
Despite the raw filename's “legacy” label, the supplied ten-page capture presents the 2026 edition.
Read the full captured text and the substantive ranking graphics. This is the producer's overview,
not the linked full report; the underlying methods and appendices were not reviewed.

## Reporting and evidence

The overview reports **7,080 US pedestrian deaths in 2024**, among **39,254 total road deaths**,
attributing the latter announcement to NHTSA in April 2026. Pedestrian deaths fell **3.9% from 2023**
and declined for two successive years, yet remained **72% above 2009**. It says the longer-term
increase outpaced population and vehicle-mile growth. Its projection of another **96,615 deaths**
before returning to 2009 levels in **2042** assumes the current decline continues, rather than
predicting a measured intervention effect.[^sga2026]

The report ranks **50 states and 101 metros**. The metro chart compares **2015–2019 and 2020–2024**
with deaths per **100,000 people**. The page warns that improved rank need not mean fewer deaths:
Tucson moved from third to fourth and Charleston from ninth to twelfth while their reported death
counts rose by 18 and five respectively. It does not clarify whether these count differences are
annual averages or five-year totals. Its Memphis prose rate of **5.5** also conflicts with the
chart's bar ending near **6**; no corrected value is inferred. Delaware reportedly reduced its
rate more than any other state but remained tenth-deadliest; only eight states improved in the
page's five-year-period comparison.[^sga2026]

For 2020–2024, the overview reports American Indian and Alaska Native pedestrian fatality rates
at **3.7 times** the national average and Black Americans' rates at **1.7 times** the average.
It says these groups together represented **16% of the population and 22% of pedestrian deaths**.
It attributes disparities to transportation investment and design decisions, but supplies neither
an adjusted causal analysis nor a quantified low-income comparison.[^sga2026]

## Limits and overlap

Some graphic areas on pp. 2, 6 and 7 are blank in the capture; the metro map and comparison chart
on pp. 4–5 are visible. The overview lacks population-source details, rate annualization rules,
walking-exposure denominators, uncertainty and full ranking methods. Its recommendations for
standards, funding and accountability are advocacy, not evaluated redesign effects or cyclist outcomes.

The [2024 announcement](/sources/2024-t4america-dangerous-by-design.md) reports an earlier endpoint
and different baseline. This is updated reporting in the same series, not independent confirmation
of that study or evidence that the earlier growth claim was false.
