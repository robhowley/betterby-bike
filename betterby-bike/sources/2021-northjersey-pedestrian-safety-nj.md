# We gathered 10 years of data on pedestrian fatalities in North Jersey. Here’s what we found

Jessie Gomez, NorthJersey.com, August 10, 2021, updated August 12; Abbott Koloff contributed.
[Original article](https://www.northjersey.com/in-depth/news/watchdog/2021/08/10/pedestrian-safety-10-year-analysis-police-reports-nj/5440882001/).

## Reporting and results

The newspaper requested fatal pedestrian motor-vehicle crash reports for **2010–2020** from 171 police departments
in Bergen, Passaic, Essex, Morris and Sussex counties. It reports **296 deaths**, with fatal incidents in 67 towns.
County totals were Bergen 145, Passaic 61, Morris 49, Essex 30 and Sussex 11. These are collected counts, not
complete regional totals: Essex municipalities relying on county records, including Newark, did not fulfill
requests. The prose's claim that 171 towns submitted data must be read alongside that explicit exclusion.[^gomez2021]

Reported deaths declined from 33 in 2019 to 32 in 2020, after a reported peak of 34 in 2018. The article separately
uses **294 crashes** as its crash denominator, reporting about 15% hit-and-runs and driver citations in roughly
25%. These percentages do not establish the proportion of drivers legally at fault. Its national GHSA trend and
2021 statewide motor-vehicle fatality count concern different geographies, periods and road-user populations.[^gomez2021]

Tri-State Transportation Campaign's Janna Chernetz and Sonia Szczesna describe missing pedestrian infrastructure,
fast traffic and municipal planning/funding needs. Essex prosecutor Adam Wells supplies crash-investigation
experience and safety advice. These are attributed explanations and recommendations, not estimated causal effects
of speed, infrastructure or education. Police reports describe inattention, failure to yield, unsafe speeds and other
contributing behavior, but the article provides no factor-specific analysis.[^gomez2021]

## Limits and access

Read the text of all 34 PDF pages. This subscriber-labelled January 24, 2024 print capture contains this article on
pp. 1–11, a separate [Kyle Morel article](2021-northjersey-pedestrian-safety-programs.md) on pp. 12–22, and a repeat
of Gomez's article on pp. 23–33, followed by a copyright page. The repeat has different displayed clock times but
is not a second publication or independent evidence. No existing bundle summary matched either article.

The road-list graphic and its missing-Essex note on p. 3 were visually checked; retained county and annual counts
are stated in the prose. Embedded videos were not viewed, and underlying police records, GHSA data and state
reports were not independently reviewed. No travel-exposure denominators, completeness audit or controlled
intervention comparison is supplied. The headline calls the interval ten years, although 2010–2020 names eleven
calendar years; retain the stated endpoints rather than calculating an annual average.[^gomez2021]
