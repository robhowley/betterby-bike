# Separated Bike Lanes Means Safer Streets, Study Says

Aaron Short, Streetsblog USA, May 29, 2019.
[Original article](https://usa.streetsblog.org/2019/05/29/protect-yourself-separated-bike-lanes-means-safer-streets-study-says)
·
[Local PDF](../../raw-sources/news/2019-streetsblog-protect-bike-safety.pdf).

## Issue and reporting basis

Short covers a University of Colorado Denver/University of New Mexico study of road safety in 12 large cities and
interviews co-author Wesley Marshall. The article links the [underlying study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jth.2019.03.004),
which was not independently reviewed here. It reports analysis of **17,000 fatalities and 77,000 severe injuries
in 2000–2012**, including Denver, Portland, Dallas, Seattle, San Francisco, Kansas City and Chicago. Marshall says
New York was excluded because its cyclist and lane numbers would overwhelm the models.[^short2019]

## Main reporting

The article reports estimates of **44% fewer deaths and 50% fewer serious injuries than an “average city”** with
protected/separated facilities, concerning all road users rather than cyclists alone. It attributes the association
to infrastructure rather than simply more cyclists. It also reports **no road-safety improvement from painted lanes**,
but gives no effect estimate or uncertainty for that comparison.[^short2019]

Separately, Short describes Portland's bicycle-commuter share rising from 1.2% to 7% in 1990–2015 alongside a 75%
fatality-rate decline, and declines in other cities. These descriptive trends have a different stated period from
the 2000–2012 analysis and do not isolate lane effects. An article update flags gentrification-related safety
disparities and the researchers' concern that improvements in largely gentrified areas raise equity questions.[^short2019]

Marshall proposes slower driving as a mechanism and warns that widely spaced plastic bollards can permit parking
that forces cyclists into traffic. The article supplies no measured bollard-versus-planter comparison. Its adverse
sharrow account links the [Chicago study already summarized in this bundle](2019-chicago-shared-lane-markings.md),
not a new independent result. The interview's “worse than nothing” and false-security claims should not replace that
study's distinctions between weighted rates, counts and uncertain dooring changes.[^short2019]

## Limits and access

All seven PDF pages were read as text; substantive reporting is on pp. 1–4. Embedded research links were recovered,
but linked studies were not reviewed for this ingestion. The article does not define the “average city” comparator,
casualty-rate denominators, model specification, uncertainty intervals or adjustment details. Its causal wording
exceeds what this secondary account can establish. The painted-lane null report is not proof of no benefit everywhere;
the equity warning does not quantify who benefited or establish displacement caused by lanes.[^short2019]
