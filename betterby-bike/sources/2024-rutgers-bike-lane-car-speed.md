# The Traffic Calming Effect of Delineated Bicycle Lanes

Hannah Younes, Clinton Andrews, Robert B. Noland, Jiahao Xia, Song Wen, Wenwen Zhang, Dimitri Metaxas,
Leigh Ann Von Hagen and Jie Gong. *Journal of Urban Mobility* 5 (June 2024), 100071.
[Original publication](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.urbmob.2024.100071) ·
[Local PDF](../../raw-sources/research/rutgers-bike-lane-car-speed-academic.pdf).

## Question and methods

The study asks whether a temporary bicycle lane slowed motor vehicles at the Cookman/Asbury/Kingsley intersection
in Asbury Park, New Jersey. The April 2022 pilot removed nine parking spaces, narrowed Cookman's traffic lanes by
at least one foot and added three-foot buffers, striped lanes, cones and plastic delineators. Asbury Avenue had
paint only because of width constraints; the posted limit was 25 mph (40 km/h).[^younes2024]

Ten sampled dates in March–April provided three no-lane days, five delineated-lane days and two paint-only days.
The authors processed 7–9 am and 4–6 pm, excluding a road-closure hour (39 hours analyzed). Computer vision tracked
vehicles, with LiDAR-based calibration converting trajectories to speeds. Separate log-linear OLS models for six
movements compared each lane condition with no lane, adjusting for directional hourly flow, stopped/free-flowing
behavior, weekends and signal functionality. Outcomes were each vehicle's average and within-trajectory 95th-percentile
speed, not journey times or a percentile across drivers.[^younes2024]

## Main results

For 2,655 right-turning vehicles, delineated lanes were associated with about **21% lower average speed and 27.6%
lower top speed**; paint-only estimates were about 11% and 14.3% lower. Cookman straight movements had smaller
reductions with delineators, while perpendicular Asbury through traffic had no significant reductions. Table 6 also
shows a significant paint-only top-speed reduction for Kingsley-to-Cookman through traffic, qualifying the abstract's
claim that paint-only reductions occurred solely for right turns.[^younes2024]

The authors interpret narrower lanes and a sharper turn as likely calming mechanisms and recommend delineated lanes.
They did not measure crash or injury reductions, establish cost-effectiveness or test permanent protected lanes.
Cones do not provide vehicle-impact protection.[^younes2024]

## Limitations and access

This is one short, nonrandomized pilot, not a general causal estimate. Unobserved vehicle type and nearby bicycles,
seasonal conditions, displaced cones and two malfunctioning-signal days complicate comparisons. The combined design
cannot isolate delineators from changed lane width and turning geometry; its effects may differ from permanent lanes.
The introduction's 9,575 vehicles conflicts with 10,604 summed model observations; Table 1 lists 12,340 observations
without a complete reconciliation. Findings retain table-specific denominators rather than choose one total.[^younes2024]

All 27 pages of the supplied ScienceDirect printout were read as extracted text, including references and endnotes;
Figure 2 (PDF p. 4) and Table 6 (pp. 17–18) were also checked visually. The separately linked supplementary Word file,
raw trajectories and cited studies were not reviewed. PDF locators below refer to the printout, not journal pagination.
