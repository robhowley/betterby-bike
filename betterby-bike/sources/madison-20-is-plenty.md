# Madison's 20 is Plenty program

City of Madison, Wisconsin, Transportation program page, undated.
[Original city page captured as PDF](../../raw-sources/news/20-is-plenty-transportation-city-of-madison-wi.pdf).
All four PDF pages were read. The PDF was created September 22, 2026; this is a capture date, not a publication date.

## Program and implementation

The city describes reducing speed limits from **25 mph to 20 mph on eligible local residential streets**, not
higher-volume roads or every street previously posted at 25 mph. Its stated purpose is greater safety and
neighborhood livability for people walking, cycling and driving. This is a municipal program explanation and FAQ,
not a research study or implementation evaluation.[^city]

The FAQ says new limits take effect when neighborhood signs change. Sign installation reportedly started in
**November 2024**, with completion planned by **December 2025**. The captured page retains that prospective
wording, so it does not establish that installation actually finished. The city offers free multilingual yard
signs, directs reckless-driving concerns to its Traffic Enforcement Safety Team, and says residential streets
will continue to be evaluated for traffic calming as before. The program therefore does not describe replacing
physical traffic-calming requests with lower posted limits.[^city]

## Evidence and limits

The city presents longer stopping distances, narrower driver vision at higher speeds and improved crash survival
at lower speeds as its rationale. It also cites NHTSA for an **84% increase in US pedestrian fatalities from 2009
to 2022 (4,109 to 7,522)**. That is national background reported by the city, not a Madison trend or an estimate
of this program's effect; the underlying NHTSA material was not reviewed.[^city]

No local before/after speeds, compliance rates, collision counts, injury outcomes or comparison streets are
provided. Statements about a safer, more comfortable neighborhood are the city's expectations, not measured
results. No separate Finding was extracted because this page documents policy scope and rollout intentions,
not a demonstrated safety effect or a new evaluated street-design result. Existing Topic conclusions are unchanged.
