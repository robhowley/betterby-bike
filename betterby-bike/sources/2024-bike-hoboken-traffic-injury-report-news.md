# 2024 Bike Hoboken Traffic Injury Report: announcement

Bike Hoboken's announcement is dated March 17, with no year displayed. Both supplied PDF pages were read;
substantive content is on page 1, followed by navigation and newsletter material. This is the organization's
summary of its own analysis, not the full report. It links to the
[underlying report](https://www.bikehoboken.org/s/2024-Bike-Hoboken-Traffic-Injury-Report.pdf), which was not reviewed.

## Reporting and results

Bike Hoboken says it analyzed all Hoboken Police Department traffic injury records for 2023–2024. It reports
144 crash-related injuries in 2023 and 219 in 2024, a 52% increase. Reported increases span motor vehicle
injuries (43%), pedestrian injuries (50%) and cyclist injuries (86%). These are injury counts, not risk per trip.[^announcement]

The announcement also reports five streets accounting for 58% of crash injuries and 11 intersections for 30%
of injury locations. Pedestrians and cyclists reportedly participated in 29% of injury-related crashes but
sustained 49% of injuries. These percentages use different denominators; the page does not supply mode-specific
counts, street names, intersection names or the precise period for those pooled shares.[^announcement]

The organization contrasts the local injury increase with reported statewide increases of 13% in overall
traffic fatalities and 33% in pedestrian deaths. Injuries and deaths are different outcomes, so this comparison
does not show that Hoboken's injury trend exceeded a comparable statewide injury trend. The announcement
promotes proposed solutions in the linked report but does not describe or evaluate them.[^announcement]

## Limits and extracted finding

The capture provides no injury-severity definitions, record-coding methods, exposure, uncertainty estimates,
comparison city or adjustment for reporting changes. It cannot establish why injuries increased or evaluate
Hoboken's street treatments. Existing bundle accounts of Hoboken's earlier zero-death record concern different
years and outcomes, not evidence against these later injury totals.
