# Americans Are Losing Interest in Buying a Car

Giulia Carbonaro, Newsweek, May 4, 2025, 04:00 EDT.
[Original publication, captured PDF](../../raw-sources/news/americans-are-losing-interest-in-buying-a-car-newsweek.pdf).

## Reporting basis

The U.S. news article combines secondary survey results with interviews or statements from iSeeCars analyst
Karl Brauer, Bankrate analyst Mark Hamrick, Cars.com researcher Patrick Masterson and Turo data officer
Albert Mangahas. It discusses rising vehicle costs and uncertainty over April 2025 auto tariffs, alongside
car sharing, rentals, leasing and transit as alternatives. These are historical reports and forecasts,
not current tariff guidance or a transport intervention evaluation.[^newsweek2025]

## Main evidence

WalletHub reportedly found the share of consumers expecting to buy a car in the next six months down
13.4% in April from a year earlier. The article does not give the two underlying shares or establish
that this means a 13.4-percentage-point decline. Hamrick also describes purchases brought forward into
the first quarter to avoid tariffs, a different mechanism from permanently abandoning ownership.

A Harris survey commissioned by car-sharing marketplace Turo reportedly found 48% of all respondents
open to alternatives to purchasing or leasing. Among surveyed car owners, 60% reported increased costs
over four years and 42% believed giving up ownership would significantly ease their financial burden.
Mangahas reports 57% interested in accessing a car without ownership's long-term commitment. These
attitudes do not measure relinquished cars, actual savings or increased cycling.[^newsweek2025]

The article also cites late-2024 Pew reporting that one in ten Americans seldom or never drive,
including 6% who never drive, and historical declines in teenage licensing attributed to USDOT.
Its later use of “non-drivers” for geographic and demographic subgroups leaves unclear whether those
figures include seldom-drivers. Its description of urban 18% as “double” suburban 6% and rural 8%
is also imprecise. These figures are not extracted as a harmonized non-driving series.

## Limits and coverage reconciliation

Read all substantive text on PDF pp. 1–10; pp. 11–12 contain site furniture. The capture supplies no
survey sample sizes, field dates, weighting, uncertainty estimates or full question wording. Underlying
WalletHub, Harris/Turo, Pew and USDOT materials were not independently reviewed; their exact publication
links were not available in the captured text or relevant PDF links. Turo has a commercial interest in
car-sharing adoption. Forecasts about sales and tariff pass-through remain attributed opinions.

Existing [AAA cost estimates](/sources/2024-aaa-your-driving-costs.md) and
[NerdWallet cost reporting](/sources/2024-nerd-wallet-cost-car-ownership.md) concern ownership expenses,
not these attitudes. No matching survey coverage was found in the bundle. The article contributes
purchase-intention context, not another independent estimate of cycling savings or measured mode shift.
