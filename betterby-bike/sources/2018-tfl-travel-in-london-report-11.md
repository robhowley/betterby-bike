# Travel in London, Report 11

Transport for London (TfL), ©2018. [Original supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/2018-tfl-travel-in-london-report-11.pdf).
The annual report describes travel trends through 2017 and into 2018 and interprets progress toward the Mayor's
transport strategy. It is a multi-source monitoring report, not one intervention experiment.

## Reviewed evidence and results

- **Cycling volumes:** estimated cycle trips fell 0.3% in 2016–2017 while estimated daily cycle-kilometres rose
  4.3%, from 3.768 million to 3.931 million. Area counts rose but strategic cordon counts fell. These measures
  cover different aspects of travel; route growth does not establish net new London-wide cycling.[^volumes]
- **Participation and perceptions:** the London Travel Demand Survey (LTDS) indicates demographic differences
  in cycling, but the separate Attitudes to Cycling survey gives materially different ethnicity shares. Safety
  remains a reported barrier; in 2017, 72% of regular versus 43% of occasional cyclists felt confident cycling
  in London. These are perceptions, not injury rates. TfL's recommendation to make cycling safer and more
  inclusive is a policy interpretation.[^surveys]
- **Mini-Hollands:** a longitudinal resident survey compares Enfield, Kingston and Waltham Forest with the rest
  of outer London. Living in areas with substantial implementation was associated with 41 additional minutes
  of past-week walking/cycling in 2017 and 44 in 2018, relative to baseline and control changes. Walking increased
  more than cycling. Cycling participation reached statistical significance only in the first follow-up;
  general proximity-related driving-time reductions were nonsignificant in the second. Cycling-environment
  perceptions improved, but non-cycling items did not change consistently and views that cycling received
  too much investment increased.[^mini]

## Methods and limitations

TfL combines area counts, cordon counts, travel estimates and surveys. Central London area counts are quarterly;
inner/outer counts annual. Winter central counts were about 20% below summer peaks. Trips use a main-mode rule,
whereas distance, vehicle shares and resident participation use other denominators.[^volumes]

LTDS continuously samples about 8,000 London households annually; its cycling subgroup is small enough for
potentially unrepresentative year-to-year fluctuations. Attitudes to Cycling changed its cyclist definition and
switched from telephone to online surveying during its time series. The reviewed report does not reconcile the
two surveys' demographic estimates or provide enough survey detail to pool them.[^surveys]

The Mini-Holland online panel recruited from households, Oyster users and a cycling-news database. Sample sizes
fell from 3,435 at baseline (2016) to 1,712 (2017) and 1,610 (2018). Younger adults, non-white people and people
not employed were underrepresented. Figure 5.29 adjusts for demographic differences and displays 95% confidence
intervals, but the study is nonrandomized, uses recalled past-week travel and evaluates bundled interventions.
The report summarizes University of Westminster work and links the first academic paper,
[Aldred et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2018.05.018); that paper, the later draft and underlying data
were not independently reviewed. They must not be counted as additional confirmations of the same panel.[^mini]

## Review scope

Partial ingestion, not a full review of the 247-page PDF. Read the opening overview paragraphs, contents,
Table 2.1 and its trip definitions (printed p. 25), LTDS methods on p. 35, sections 5.1–5.3 (pp. 75–94)
and all of section 5.5 (pp. 100–107). Visually checked Table 5.1 (p. 76), Table 5.2 and the survey-method
break in Figure 5.14 (p. 89), and Figure 5.29 with its confidence intervals (p. 105). Other chart text was
reviewed through extraction, without deriving numerical values from plotted lines. Santander Cycles, walking,
road casualties, air quality, public transport and development chapters were not substantively reviewed.
