# Goyco v. Progressive: electric-scooter PIP eligibility

## Publication and reading scope

Justice Solomon wrote this unanimous New Jersey Supreme Court opinion, argued January 29 and decided May 14,
2024. The [preserved primary opinion](../../raw-sources/news/a-12-23-david-goyco-v-progressive-insurance-company.pdf)
was read in full: two syllabus pages and all 23 numbered opinion pages. Despite its news-directory location,
this is a judicial decision, not news coverage. The clerk's syllabus expressly is not part of the opinion.

## Facts, issue and holding

An automobile struck David Goyco on November 22, 2021, in Elizabeth, New Jersey, while he rode a Segway
Ninebot KickScooter Max. He sought PIP medical benefits from his own automobile insurer, Progressive, which
denied coverage. His policy tracked the No-Fault Act, covering qualifying automobile use or injury as a
pedestrian. The Court affirmed the denial, with modified reasoning: the scooter rider was not a pedestrian
under N.J.S.A. 39:6A-2(h).[^goyco2024]

The Court used ordinary meanings for terms undefined in the Act rather than importing the separate traffic-law
vehicle definition. The scooter was a vehicle powered by a rechargeable electric motor, not designed for muscular
propulsion, and designed primarily for public-road use. Its 15.5-mph capability, lights and speedometer supported
the last determination; “highway” was not restricted to high-speed roads. The 2019 statute generally applying
bicycle rules to low-speed scooters did not confer pedestrian PIP status. Ordinary bicyclists' status instead
follows the Act's muscular-power distinction.[^goyco2024]

The Court described medical coverage and insurance-cost containment as competing legislative goals and left
any expansion of PIP eligibility to the Legislature. This is legal reasoning, not measured evidence that
coverage expansion increases premiums.[^goyco2024]

## Scope and reconciliation

The holding concerns the scooter, statute and policy before the Court in 2024. No broader policy coverage was
argued. It does not decide every pedal-assist e-bike configuration, exclude every possible recovery after a
scooter crash, or establish current law after later changes. Subsequent legal developments were not reviewed.
No treatment-delay, household-cost, crash-risk or travel-access effect is measured.

The bundle's [Boone commentary](2026-new-jersey-uber-lyft-liability-accidents.md) concerns a different pedestrian
and rideshare-insurance dispute and remains secondary coverage, not a contradiction or another account of this
case. Reported [e-bike operating requirements](../findings/access/new-jersey-2025-e-bike-requirements.md) likewise
do not determine post-crash insurance eligibility.
