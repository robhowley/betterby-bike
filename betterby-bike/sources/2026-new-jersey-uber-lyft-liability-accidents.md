## What the commentary reports

Epstein describes a late-July Appellate Division decision in *Boone v. Unsatisfied Claim and Judgment Fund*.
An uninsured pedestrian was struck by a Lyft driver actively transporting a passenger. The carrier argued that
its policy under New Jersey's Transportation Network Company Act did not require Personal Injury Protection
(PIP) medical benefits for the pedestrian. According to Epstein, the court disagreed: reading New Jersey's
insurance statutes together required the insurer to provide pedestrian PIP benefits. The article later identifies
the opposing insurer as Liberty Mutual.[^epstein2026]

Epstein endorses the interpretation as good law and public policy. He argues that treatment should proceed while
insurers resolve reimbursement, and that pedestrians cannot choose or know an approaching vehicle's insurance
arrangements. His hypothetical comparison of otherwise identical crashes describes the inequity he believes the
insurer's position would create, not measured differences in treatment. References to delivery apps, autonomous
vehicles and robotaxis broaden his policy argument, not the reported facts or established scope of this ruling.[^epstein2026]

## Legal scope and access limits

This is a lawyer's secondary account and advocacy, not a reviewed judicial holding or verified current law.
The court opinion, statutes, policy terms, subsequent proceedings and actual payment records were not examined.
The supplied PDF appears to contain the complete commentary, but does not specify benefit limits or establish
eligibility across other driver activity states, delivery work, cyclists or every Uber/Lyft crash. Despite the
headline's broad liability framing, the described dispute concerns pedestrian PIP medical benefits, not a general
determination of fault, platform liability or all damages. No crash-risk, treatment-delay or health-outcome effect
is measured.[^epstein2026]
