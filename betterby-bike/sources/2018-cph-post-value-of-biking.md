# More investment needed in Copenhagen cycling infrastructure, review suggests

Stephen Gadd, *The Copenhagen Post*, June 25, 2018. [Original article](https://cphpost.dk/2018-06-25/news/more-investment-needed-in-copenhagen-cycling-infrastructure-review-suggests/).
Read the supplied nine-page PDF as text; article content is on pp. 1–3, followed by unrelated site material.

## Reporting and main claims

The article covers Copenhagen Municipality's annual cycling review and calls for investment as cycling grows.
It reports a **43%** work-cycling share in 2017, described as “up 2 percent since 2016,” without defining the
population or clarifying percentage points versus relative growth. Separately, it attributes economic gains of
**4.80 Danish kroner per bicycle kilometre**, rising to **10.09 kroner per kilometre when replacing an equivalent
car journey**, to finance-ministry figures.[^gadd2018]

Deputy mayor for technical and environmental matters Ninna Hedeager Olsen argues that congestion or feeling unsafe
could discourage cycling and worsen mobility. She advocates more and wider cycle paths, potentially at motorists'
expense, and parliamentary permission for congestion zones or road pricing. These are policy positions and predicted
consequences, not evaluated effects. The article also reports 550,000 vehicles crossing municipal boundaries daily,
roughly ten times as many cars as bikes, without explaining the count basis.[^gadd2018]

Its infrastructure roundup reports 3.9 km of new cycle paths, five new super cycle paths in the Capital Region,
3,500 new parking stands and 12,900 abandoned bicycles collected in 2017. It also prints “600 km of green cycling
routes” in the same sentence; the unit and whether this describes new provision or a total remain unresolved,
so that figure is not extracted as a Finding.[^gadd2018]

## Evidence limits and relationships

This is secondary reporting plus an elected official's comments, not an infrastructure evaluation. The municipal
review and finance-ministry calculation are not precisely identified or separately reviewed. The article gives no
survey methods, valuation components, price year or uncertainty. It does not establish realized budget savings,
project returns or causal effects of the infrastructure listed.

The [ULI Source](2016-active-transportation-real-estate.md) covers earlier Farum route counts and a completed-network
return forecast. Those are different measures, not independent confirmation of these commute shares or per-kilometre
values.
