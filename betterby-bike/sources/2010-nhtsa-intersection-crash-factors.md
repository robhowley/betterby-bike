# Crash Factors in Intersection-Related Crashes: An On-Scene Perspective

Eun-Ha Choi, NHTSA National Center for Statistics and Analysis, September 2010, DOT HS 811 366.
[Publisher report](https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/811366).

## Study and methods

The National Motor Vehicle Crash Causation Survey collected on-scene US crash information in January
2005–December 2007. Eligibility required a crash between 6 a.m. and midnight and at least one of the first
three involved vehicles to be a light passenger vehicle towed because of damage. The analyzed 5,470 cases
represent a weighted 2,188,969 crashes, not that many individually investigated events.[^choi2010]

The report defines intersection-related crashes by the critical-reason vehicle's pre-crash event: turning
left, crossing over or turning right at an intersection. It uses descriptive statistics, ratios of critical-reason
shares, generalized logit models and configural frequency analysis (observed versus expected cell counts).
Generally one vehicle per crash received one critical reason. Further modeling focuses on driver-attributed
reasons; traffic-control coding permits multiple responses.[^choi2010]

## Results and interpretation

The intersection definition captures 2,183 cases, a weighted 787,236 crashes (36.0%). Of these, 96.1% had
a driver-attributed critical reason. Among that driver-attributed subset, inadequate surveillance was the
most common reason (44.1%), versus 7.3% in non-intersection crashes. Turning with an obstructed view
accounted for 7.8%, with a reported intersection/non-intersection share ratio of 335. These are conditional
crash-composition measures, not the likelihood of crashing when visibility is obstructed.[^choi2010]

Reported patterns differ by maneuver, traffic control, age and sex. Left-turn crashes at signals or stop
signs overrepresented obstructed-view turns and gap/speed misjudgment; crossing crashes overrepresented
illegal maneuvers and inattention. These associations do not establish demographic driving risk or rank
intersection designs. Atmospheric condition was not significantly associated with critical reason in the
initial analysis (p=0.2625); the author cautions that combining cloudy, rain, snow and other conditions may
hide differences. Proposed uses in collision-warning systems, road design and training were not evaluated.[^choi2010]

## Limits and access

The report explicitly calls critical reason subjective and says it may not be the crash cause or imply fault.
Its driver percentage cannot partition causal responsibility between people, vehicles and infrastructure.
There are no travel-exposure denominators or separate cyclist results. Nighttime and non-tow-away crashes
outside eligibility are not represented. Small cells constrain the pattern analysis, particularly right turns;
the report requires at least five unweighted cases for a cell's Z-statistic to be considered valid. It uses
weighted frequencies and describes a Bonferroni-adjusted 99% threshold, without confidence intervals for
the headline proportions or relative ratios.[^choi2010]

Read all supplied extracted text, including printed pp. i–vii, 1–27 and appendices A–C. Chart values used here
are available in the appendix tables; figures were not separately visually reviewed. The extraction includes
broken formula placeholders in the main text, but Table A1 supplies the total. No underlying records, coding
manual or external cited studies were reviewed.
