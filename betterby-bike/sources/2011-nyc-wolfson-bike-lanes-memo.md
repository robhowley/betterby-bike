# Bike Lanes: memorandum from Howard Wolfson

Howard Wolfson, City of New York, Office of the Mayor, memorandum to “Interested Parties,” 21 March 2011. [Original memorandum (local PDF)](../../raw-sources/research/2011-nyc-wolfson-bike-lanes-memo.pdf).

## Purpose and reporting basis

The memo responds to a New York magazine article and defends the city's bike-lane program. It is an administration's policy statement, not a research evaluation. It names a Quinnipiac poll but supplies no poll documentation, crash-data references, methods, tables or uncertainty estimates for its numerical claims.[^memo]

## Reported claims

- **Safety:** protected-lane installations reportedly “typically” reduce injury crashes for all road users by 40%, and by more than 50% in some locations. The memo also says cycling more than doubled over the preceding four years while fatal cycling crashes and serious injuries declined, attributing that decline to the safer network without presenting an analysis.
- **Pedestrian deaths:** it reports four pedestrians killed in bike–pedestrian accidents in 2001–05 and three in 2006–10, while saying city cycling doubled.
- **Public support:** it quotes the latest Quinnipiac poll as finding 54% of NYC voters saying more bike lanes are good “because it's greener and healthier for people to ride their bicycles,” versus 39% saying they are bad “because it leaves less room for cars which increases traffic.” These are the memo's quoted responses to a framed question, not an independently reviewed poll.
- **Consultation and revisions:** it reports 94 Community Board presentations at public meetings over four years and more than 40 supporting committee/full-board votes and/or resolutions. It names Prospect Park West, Flushing Avenue, Columbus Avenue and Grand Street as board-approved and requested projects. It also describes added parking on Columbus Avenue and complete lane removals on Bedford Avenue and Father Capodanno Boulevard following community input, with other network enhancements.
- **Network and street space:** it reports 255 bike-lane miles added in four years, compared with 6,000 miles of city streets, and says 66% of installed bike lanes affected neither parking nor the number of moving lanes. It gives no denominator or counting basis for that percentage; the lane-mile and street-mile totals are not necessarily comparable coverage measures.[^memo]

## Interpretation and limitations

The safety figures are historical claims attributed to the mayor's office. Unspecified sites, periods, comparison methods and exposure prevent treating the 40% figure as a reproducible causal effect or a cyclist-specific risk reduction. Small pedestrian fatality counts do not describe nonfatal injuries or establish a change in risk. The memo's citywide trends cannot isolate bike lanes from other changes.[^memo]
