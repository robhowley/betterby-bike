# Bike Lane Opponents Want McGuinness to ‘Keep Moving’ … for Killer Truckers

Kevin Duggan, Streetsblog NYC, June 13, 2023. Full captured text read; substantive reporting spans PDF pp. 1–10.

## Reporting and evidence

The article covers opposition to a proposed McGuinness Boulevard redesign in Greenpoint, Brooklyn: four moving lanes
would become two, alongside parking-protected bicycle lanes, pedestrian islands, daylighted corners and loading zones on
each block. Planning followed teacher Matthew Jensen's May 2021 death; DOT said it intended to proceed in 2023. This is
a contemporaneous proposal account, not evidence of the final design or implementation.[^duggan2023]

Original reporting identifies a Broadway Stages-led business coalition and three listed trucking companies whose drivers
had been involved in fatal crashes elsewhere. It includes company responses or refusals to comment, a threatened Chamber
of Commerce lawsuit, and interviews with a supportive shop owner, parents and an advocate. The company's statement
supported safe streets while emphasizing practical business access. The article also draws on THE CITY's reporting about
political connections. These accounts document a contested project, not representative business or resident opinion,
business losses, or proof of opponents' motives.[^duggan2023]

Streetsblog reports 1,443 crashes and 331 injuries since January 2014 in an embedded June 2023 post, while separately
citing city figures of 230 injuries in 2016–2020 and three deaths over roughly a decade. Those periods differ and should
not be combined. Its approximately 30% reduction in fatalities and injuries on other city road diets is attributed to
DOT without evaluation methods. Neither those comparisons nor the companies' crash histories establish this project's
safety effect.[^duggan2023]

DOT reportedly estimated that removing a travel lane could divert 30%–40% of traffic to adjacent roads; officials
separately estimated 30%–50% used the corridor as an expressway shortcut. The suggestion that shortcut drivers might
bypass the neighborhood is an expectation, not a measured disappearance of traffic. Against opponents' emergency-access
concerns, DOT said ambulances and police could use bicycle lanes, without response-time measurements.[^duggan2023]

## Limits and reconciliation

No project before/after outcomes, traffic-model methods, emergency-trip observations or representative opinion survey
are supplied. The article's framing strongly favors the redesign; interviews and agency statements remain attributed
accounts. Linked
[June 2022 DOT planning material](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/mcguinness-blvd-complete-street-jun2022.pdf)
and [May 2023 design material](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/mcguinness-blvd-meeker-ave-ash-st-may2023.pdf)
were identified but not independently reviewed.

No existing bundle coverage of McGuinness was found. Existing
[Ocean City emergency-access reporting](/findings/operations/ocean-city-road-diet-emergency-access.md) describes a
different design with a center turn lane; it does not verify the proposed protected-lane access mechanism here. General
NYC safety evidence remains separate from this pre-implementation dispute.
