# Best Practices for Improving Traffic Crash Data in New Jersey

Robert Noland and Hannah Younes's September 9, 2024 New Jersey State Policy Lab article reports on their
national review of crash-data access and interviews with DOT staff, university portal researchers and
transportation advocates. The full six-page PDF text and Figure 1 were read; substantive reporting is on
pp. 1–4, followed by a figure note, author biographies and site material. The forthcoming full report and
linked portals were not reviewed.

## Reporting and evidence

The authors reviewed raw-data downloads and dashboards/maps for all states and DC. For July 2024 they
report public dashboards or interactive maps in **41 states plus DC**, and downloadable crash data in
**27 states plus DC**, including New Jersey. Connecticut, Georgia, Massachusetts and Utah are highlighted
for comprehensive mapping, querying, exports and timely, complete information; Michigan is described as
the only state offering free redacted crash reports with narratives and diagrams.[^article]

New Jersey's public downloads reportedly lacked geocoded coordinates and extended only through 2021.
Route mileposts were available but harder to use spatially. Safety Voyager and Numetric were not public,
although NJDOT-funded researchers could obtain access. The authors distinguish this restricted access
from public county-level injury statistics in the Children's Hospital of Philadelphia dashboard launched
in May 2024, and regional DVRPC and Hudson County dashboards.[^article]

The article says fatal crashes represent less than 1% of reported crashes and argues that FARS and
near-real-time state fatality information cannot replace nonfatal-crash data. Interviewees generally
supported nonfatal data for infrastructure advocacy. Better geocoding, timeliness and statewide public
access are recommendations, not evaluated safety improvements.[^article]

## Limits and reconciliation

This is a short first-person project account, not a full methods report. It gives no interview sample
size, selection process, coding protocol, portal audit criteria or measured effect of data access on
safety. Figure 1 maps availability, not quality, completeness or cycling exposure. The snapshot does not
establish current portal conditions or a quantified cycling-injury undercount.

The existing [NJTR-1 coverage Finding](../findings/safety/njtr-1-cycling-crash-coverage.md) concerns which
crashes enter the reporting system. This article adds a different limitation: whether existing records
are public, timely and spatially usable. It does not independently confirm the manual's exclusions or
[Hoboken's later reported injury increase](../findings/safety/hoboken-2023-2024-reported-injury-increase.md).
