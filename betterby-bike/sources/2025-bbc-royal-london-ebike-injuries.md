# Royal London Hospital e-bike injury reporting

## Publication and reporting basis

Karl Mercer and Victoria Cook's September 11, 2025 BBC London article reports from the Royal London
Hospital's orthopaedics trauma unit in Whitechapel. The [captured publication](../../raw-sources/news/e-bike-injuries-are-a-massive-burden-say-royal-london-hospital-surgeons.pdf)
contains substantive reporting and photo captions on PDF pp. 1–8, followed by related links and site
material. The full extracted capture was read. It contains no study methods, statistical tables or appendices.
This is original hospital reporting with clinician, parent and policy interviews, not a clinical cohort study.[^bbc2025]

## Cases and reported burden

The morning meeting includes a 32-year-old rider with a tibial plateau fracture after falling off an e-bike
and six-year-old pedestrian Frida with tibia and fibula fractures after an e-bike collision in a park.
The article reports surgery with pins and an expected healing period of around a year for Frida.
Her father describes the bicycle falling onto her and believes the rider was going too fast; speed and
vehicle specifications were not measured or independently established in the article.[^bbc2025]

BBC describes Frida as one of **150 such cases in the unit over the past six months**. The article does
not define the case-selection rule or give rider/pedestrian counts, exact dates, comparison-period totals
or travel exposure. Surgeons Jaison Patel and Nick Aresti describe an increasing workload and more severe
injuries associated with higher speeds. These are clinical observations, not a quantified trend or an
adjusted comparison with conventional bicycles. Aresti also encourages cycling for health.[^bbc2025]

## Policy debate and limitations

Frida's father and the surgeons call for stronger regulation. Walking and Cycling Commissioner Will Norman
welcomes prospective local powers under the English Devolution Bill, discussing vehicle, parking and
battery safety. The article says the bill remains before Parliament with no passage date; these are
anticipated powers, not an evaluated safety intervention.[^bbc2025]

Cycling UK's Duncan Dollimore argues against regulating e-bike usage and cites £7.4 billion in annual
NHS inactivity costs, £1 billion saved by cycling and a roughly 20-to-one health-benefit/risk ratio.
The capture supplies no underlying methods for those claims, which are attributed advocacy context,
not independently reviewed estimates. The reported legal age, motor-power and assistance-speed limits
are September 2025 context, not evidence that the case vehicles complied with those rules.[^bbc2025]

The distinct contribution is a local hospital burden account including harm to a pedestrian. It cannot
establish London-wide incidence, comparative per-trip risk, a speed/weight causal effect or a safest
regulatory approach. The [case-count Finding](/findings/safety/royal-london-ebike-case-count-limits.md)
feeds the [injury-risk synthesis](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md#local-hospital-reporting-does-not-measure-ebike-risk-per-trip).
