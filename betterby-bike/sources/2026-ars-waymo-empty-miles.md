# Autonomous vehicles were supposed to cut traffic

## Publication and coverage

Jonathan M. Gitlin, Ars Technica, June 3, 2026. All substantive article text was read in the
[local PDF](../../raw-sources/news/autonomous-vehicles-were-supposed-to-cut-traffic-what-if-they-don-t-ars-technica.pdf),
pp. 1–3; p. 4 is site furniture. The exact original article URL was not recovered from the capture.
The article links Awad Abdelhalim's Transport Findings paper,
[Millions of Trips, Waymo Empty Miles: California's First Thousand Days of Commercial Robotaxi Service](https://findingspress.org/article/161870-millions-of-trips-waymo-empty-miles-california-s-first-thousand-days-of-commercial-robotaxi-service).
That paper, CPUC records and other linked analyses were not independently reviewed.

## Reporting and evidence

Gitlin reports an analysis of Waymo's California Public Utilities Commission data for August 2023–December
2025: 13.8 million trips, 19.3 million passengers and 86.3 million vehicle miles. Passenger-occupied mileage
reportedly rose from 36% initially to about 56%, then plateaued, leaving about 44% empty toward the end.
Empty mileage includes both waiting for assignments and driving to pickups. Pickup mileage and empty miles
per trip reportedly declined; the study author suggests freeway service partly explains the latter.[^gitlin]

A separate Matthew Raifman analysis of January 2024–September 2025 CPUC data reportedly also found 44%
empty mileage, with two-thirds of empty miles spent waiting for assignments. These overlapping operator
records are not independent replication. Gitlin's observation of apparently empty cars on San Francisco
visits is anecdotal, not the study's sampling method.[^gitlin]

The article compares this with about 40% empty mileage for Uber/Lyft and recalls prior ride-hailing traffic
research. It argues that automation need not reduce congestion and advocates public transport's spatial
efficiency. It also reports Waymo safety advantages while noting school-bus and flooding incidents, and
suggests occupancy differences complicate injury comparisons. None of these passages supplies a matched
safety analysis or an estimate of net traffic caused by robotaxis.[^gitlin]

## Limits and reconciliation

The capture supplies no uncertainty intervals, detailed mileage classification rules, trip-substitution data
or counterfactual congestion measurement. Its roughly 1,000-day description is not reconciled with the stated
calendar window. The reported 44% endpoint should not be applied to all 86.3 million miles. Empty mileage
is operational overhead, not proof that every such mile is additional traffic relative to displaced travel.
The Uber/Lyft comparison lacks matched geography, periods and definitions.

The $4.6-trillion, 20-year transit proposal repeats Transportation for America's scenario already covered in
[Streetsblog's transit expansion Source](2026-streetsblog-world-class-transit.md), not new evidence of benefits.
Existing delivery-robot reporting concerns sidewalk conflicts, not this passenger-service mileage result.
