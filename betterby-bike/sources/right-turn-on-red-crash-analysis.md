# Right-turn-on-red crash analysis (unidentified results excerpt)

The [supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/right-turn-on-red-crash-analysis.pdf) is one cropped image beginning
“The analysis of FARS data showed that.” The title here is descriptive: publication title, author, date, original
URL and printed page number are absent. No full report or external publication was reviewed.[^rtor]

## Reported analysis and results

The excerpt describes U.S. FARS fatal-crash data for **1982–1992** and, separately, crash files from four unnamed
states. It reports approximately **84 fatal crashes per year** involving a right-turning vehicle at an intersection
where right turn on red (RTOR) was permitted. It also reports **485,104 fatalities** over the period and a share
below **0.2% of all fatalities** involving that maneuver/location. Fatal crashes and fatalities are different units;
the excerpt does not provide the matching fatality numerator needed to check that percentage.[^rtor]

Crucially, it states that FARS did not distinguish whether the signal was red. Its suggested range of zero to
84 actual fatal RTOR crashes annually is therefore a classification bound, not an observed RTOR count or confidence
interval. The suggestion that the count “may be closer to zero than 84” is interpretation without supporting detail
in the supplied crop.[^rtor]

A second bullet labels crashes “fatal RTOR crashes” and reports involvement of pedestrians in **44%**, bicyclists
in **10%**, and one vehicle striking another in **33%**. This label is not reconciled with the preceding missing-signal
warning. These are reported crash-involvement shares, not road-user fatality shares or per-trip risks. The visible
four-state result reports RTOR crashes as **0.05% of all traffic crashes** in those states; state names, years,
counts and RTOR coding are unavailable.[^rtor]

## Access, methods and limitations

Text extraction returned no substantive text; all visible content of the single page was read visually. Access is
**results-excerpt only**. There are no methods, limitations section, tables, figures or references in the supplied
file. Inclusion criteria, coding reliability, state sampling, uncertainty estimates and travel/turn exposure cannot
be checked. Only the explicit FARS measurement warning is available. Neither a small share of all crashes nor the
unconfirmed red-signal classification establishes the safety of permitting RTOR or the effect of prohibiting it.
No current crash estimate is supported.[^rtor]

Bundle searches for right turn on red/RTOR, FARS result wording, 84 fatal crashes, 485,104 and 1982–1992 found no
matching publication or underlying result. Missing publication identity prevents definitive duplicate exclusion.
The existing [light-truck turning-crash finding](/findings/safety/rtz-turning-crash-denominator.md) concerns a different
2014–2018 vehicle-type comparison, not a replication of these results.
