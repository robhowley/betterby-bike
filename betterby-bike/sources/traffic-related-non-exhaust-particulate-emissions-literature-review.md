## Review basis and results

The review describes examining peer-reviewed papers, research-project reports, technical publications and licentiate
and doctoral theses, especially on brake and tyre wear. Its stated scope includes importance, physicochemical
characteristics, emission factors and possible adverse health effects. This is secondary synthesis, not a new
field experiment or primary emissions study. Search dates, selection criteria, study counts, geographic coverage
and quality assessment are not supplied in the visible excerpt.[^excerpt]

It distinguishes combustion-related particles from brake, tyre, clutch and road-surface wear, and deposited material
resuspended by traffic turbulence. It estimates almost equal exhaust and non-exhaust contributions to total
traffic-related PM10 emissions. It reports brake wear as 16%–55% by mass of non-exhaust traffic-related PM10 in urban
environments, versus about 3% on freeways, attributing the difference to less frequent braking. Tyre-wear contributions
are reported as 5%–30% by mass, without a separately specified setting.[^excerpt]

The summary also reports brake-wear PM10 mass-weighted mean diameters of 2–6 µm and often bimodal tyre-wear PM10
size distributions, with fine and coarse peaks (the latter at 5–9 µm). An estimated 40%–50% of generated brake-wear
particle mass and 0.1%–10% of tyre-wear particle mass is emitted as PM10. These fractions concern generated wear mass,
not shares of total traffic pollution. The authors anticipate rising relative non-exhaust importance as exhaust
controls tighten; no time series or quantified health effect is visible.[^excerpt]
