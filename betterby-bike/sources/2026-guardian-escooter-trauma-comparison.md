# Guardian's e-scooter trauma comparison

## Reporting basis

Ian Sample's August 6, 2026 Guardian article covers David Bodansky's analysis of national major trauma
registry records for e-scooter, motorbike and bicycle collisions in 18 English and Welsh cities during
2020–2022. The complete two-page capture, including the photograph caption, was read. It links an
[underlying Scientific Reports study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-59829-5), which was not
independently reviewed. The capture contains no statistical tables or appendices.[^guardian2026]

## Reported results

The article reports e-scooter riders were more than three times as likely to have traumatic brain injury
or blood-vessel damage, and 1.5 times as likely to have internal-organ injuries, as motorbike riders in
the analysis. It reports worse outcomes than cyclists for those injury categories without giving numerical
bicycle comparisons. Fracture risk was lower than for either comparator. Brain injuries accounted for
more than a third of adult e-scooter injuries.[^guardian2026]

It also reports 16% of e-scooter injuries involved under-18s and fewer than 6% of riders reported wearing
a helmet. Its statements that women had about a third more collisions and more than twice the likelihood
of severe injury lack case counts, exposure denominators and adjustment details. They should not be read
as sex-specific collision risk per trip.[^guardian2026]

## Interpretation and policy accounts

Bodansky suggests small wheels catching potholes could explain head-first falls. The article attributes
women's chest-injury concerns to handlebar height. Transport psychologist Petya Ventsislavova calls for
private-scooter legislation and mandatory training, discussing stability and earlier behavioral research.
Helmet requirements and design changes are recommendations, not interventions evaluated here. A Department
for Transport spokesperson describes rental trials as a controlled setting for testing wider use. The
article's account of private-scooter restrictions is dated context, not verified current law.[^guardian2026]

## Limits and connections

The article supplies no sample sizes, confidence intervals, adjustment specification or riding exposure.
Its injury comparisons concern registry cases, not a demonstrated ranking of injury incidence per trip
or kilometre. The headline cannot establish that scooters are universally more dangerous, nor that design,
helmet use or sex caused the observed differences. The
[trauma-comparison Finding](/findings/safety/england-wales-escooter-trauma-comparison.md) preserves the mixed
injury profile and denominator limits for the [injury-risk Topic](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).
This is distinct from the bundle's Washington, DC emergency-department cohorts, not a replication of them.

[^guardian2026]: Guardian, August 6, 2026, PDF p. 1, registry setting, injury comparisons and demographic claims; p. 2, helmet reporting, expert explanations and policy responses.
