# Americans Hit the Brakes on Driving, and It Could Shift the Housing Market in Reverse

Allaire Conte, Realtor.com, January 16, 2026. [Original article](https://www.realtor.com/advice/buy/transit-oriented-housing-driving-less/).
Substantive PDF pp. 1–7 were read in full; subsequent pages contain site material. Linked primary research was not reviewed.

## Reporting and evidence

Conte argues that younger adults driving less and older adults aging out of driving increase the need for housing
near transit. The article combines secondary government/research statistics with Realtor.com price comparisons,
its economist Jake Krimmel's market interpretation, Austin Mayor Kirk Watson's zoning remarks and Canadian
researcher Merrina Zhang's comments on accessible transit. It is not an evaluation of transit-oriented development.[^conte2026]

- Reported per-capita vehicle miles fell 2.3% since 2019 and nearly 5% over two decades, attributed to FHWA.
  [UCLA research](https://doi.org/10.7922/G2W0949G) reportedly found young-adult vehicle travel down 19% in
  2001–2017 and daily trips down nearly 50% in 2017–2022. These are different measures, with age bounds and
  uncertainty omitted. Fewer trips do not demonstrate substitution toward walking, cycling or transit.
- Transit trips reportedly remained 23% below 2019 even though ridership rose more than 17% in 2022–2023.
  The article's claim of renewed desire for transit access is an interpretation, not a survey finding.
- [Urban Institute coverage](https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/spurring-housing-development-near-transit-through-federally-funded-land-acquisition)
  supplies a [housing-growth comparison](/findings/access/us-reported-transit-housing-growth.md): 2 million
  units near stations versus 17.6 million away in 2000–2019, alongside faster growth near newer stations.
- Selected [Phoenix and Austin price comparisons](/findings/business/phoenix-austin-reported-transit-home-prices.md)
  suggest market interest but omit sufficient methodology to infer a transit premium.
- A [Journal of Transport & Health study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214140525000027)
  supports [reported older-adult stop access and nondriver use](/findings/access/us-older-adults-reported-stop-access.md).
  Canadian research commentary and a historical estimate of 1.4 billion caregiver rides in 2009 add context,
  not measured effects of new housing or transit projects.

## Interpretation and coverage reconciliation

[SSTI's September 2025 coverage](2025-ssti-declining-travel.md) reports the same UCLA research and
national per-capita figures, so the two accounts are not independent evidence. SSTI's chart identifies
ages 15–29 and personal miles traveled (PMT), while Realtor.com calls the 19% change vehicle travel.
The [PMT/VMT Finding](/findings/access/us-youth-personal-travel-vmt-reported.md) preserves this distinction
and the unresolved VMT denominator rather than treating personal travel declines as driving reductions.

Conte attributes limited construction to land-use and zoning constraints and presents Austin zoning changes as a
response, but supplies no completed-housing or household-affordability evaluation. Her “over $12,000” car-cost
figure links to AAA's **2023** estimate, already represented in the
[2023 new-car cost Finding](/findings/access/us-new-car-ownership-cost-2023.md); it is not a new 2026 cost measurement.
The older-adult study differs from the 2022 study reported in
[Streetsblog's aging coverage](2026-streetsblog-aging-transportation.md), so their use measures should not be pooled.
The housing evidence complements [Build HUBS financing proposals](2026-build-hubs-housing-near-transit.md),
without showing that those proposals delivered homes. No cycling, displacement or causal affordability effect is measured.

[^conte2026]: Supplied PDF pp. 2–3, travel trends; pp. 3–5, housing and prices; pp. 5–7, zoning, costs and aging.
