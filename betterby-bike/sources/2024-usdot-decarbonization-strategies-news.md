# 3 strategies to decarbonize transportation: US DOT report

Dan Zukowski, Smart Cities Dive, July 15, 2024. [Original article](https://www.smartcitiesdive.com/news/us-dot-3-strategies-decarbonize-transportation/721271/).
The substantive article on PDF pages 1–3 was read; the remaining pages contain site promotions and navigation.

## Reporting and policy context

The article covers USDOT's July 12 release of a 44-page report to Congress,
[Decarbonizing U.S. Transportation](https://www.transportation.gov/sites/dot.gov/files/2024-07/Decarbonizing%20U.S.%20Transportation_July%202024.pdf),
drawing on the administration's January 2023 blueprint. It reports three complementary strategies:
reduce distances traveled by people and freight, expand more energy-efficient travel options, and
transition to zero-emission vehicles and fuels. The report itself was not reviewed.[^zukowski2024]

Reported actions include transit-oriented development and convenient access to destinations; walking,
biking and micromobility support; carpooling and employer transit passes; transit and passenger-rail
investment; fuel-economy standards; lower-emission aviation fuels; and freight shifts to rail and maritime
transport. The article also describes EV charging programs with $5 billion for highway charging and
$2.5 billion for corridor and community charging/fueling facilities. These are program descriptions,
not evidence of completed infrastructure or resulting emissions reductions.[^zukowski2024]

The article attributes 33% of US greenhouse-gas emissions to transportation and 70% of transportation
emissions to light-duty vehicles, trucks and buses, without specifying an inventory year or accounting
boundary. It situates the strategy within the then-reported US commitments to cut emissions at least
50% below 2005 levels by 2030 and achieve net zero by 2050. These are historical policy context, not
verified current commitments. Its separate implementation context is opposition to the 2023 highway
CO2 target-setting rule: it reports a March Texas court decision finding insufficient congressional
authority and Republican opposition, not a finding that every decarbonization strategy was blocked.[^zukowski2024]

## Limits and relationship to existing evidence

This is policy reporting based chiefly on a federal report and linked prior coverage, not original
research or an intervention evaluation. It gives no cycling-specific emissions estimate, quantified
mode-shift forecast, uncertainty intervals or before/after outcomes. Court status, inventory shares and
underlying report methods were not independently checked. No new Finding is extracted: the useful
addition is the attributed federal policy framework, not an independent confirmation of emissions savings.

The [street-redesign synthesis](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#climate-targets-are-not-project-emissions-results)
uses this account to distinguish policy mechanisms from measured benefits. Existing
[New Jersey target comparisons](/findings/new-jersey-climate-target-comparison-limits.md) and
[electric two-/three-wheeler oil estimates](/findings/electric-two-wheelers-reported-oil-displacement.md)
address different questions and are not corroborated by listing these strategies.
