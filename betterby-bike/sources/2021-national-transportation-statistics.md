# National Transportation Statistics 2021

U.S. Department of Transportation, Bureau of Transportation Statistics (BTS), 50th Anniversary Edition, 2021.
[Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.21949/1523389) ·
[Supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/2021-national-transportation-statistics.pdf).

## Publication and reporting basis

This national statistical compendium covers transportation systems, safety, economic performance, energy and
environmental impacts. BTS assembles federal, industry and association data, with table-specific sources and
methods rather than one study design. The introduction describes monthly online updates; this summary concerns
only the supplied 2021 edition, not today's revised series.[^nts2021]

The reviewed highway safety tables primarily compile NHTSA data. Fatality Analysis Reporting System (FARS)
records draw on police reports, death certificates and other state records. Fatal crashes involve a motor vehicle
on a trafficway and a death within 30 days; FARS excludes nonpublic roads. Nonfatal injury figures are estimates
from police-reported crashes, not a census of all injured cyclists. The glossary includes possible injuries,
not just hospitalization or serious injury.[^nts2021]

## Relevant results and limitations

- Table 2-1 reports 623 U.S. pedalcyclist fatalities in 2010 and 846 in 2019, with intervening rises and falls.
  Its 891 figure for 2020 comes from NHTSA's **early estimates**, as identified in Appendix F, not a final count.
  These are motor-vehicle traffic fatalities, not all cycling deaths or risk per mile or trip.
- Table 2-2 reports an estimated 49,000 injured pedalcyclists in 2019; 2020 is unavailable, not zero.
  Its 45,000 estimate for 2015 and 64,000 for 2016 cannot establish a comparable increase: CRSS replaced GES
  in 2016 with a different sample design. Starting with 2018 reporting, injury estimates combine FARS injuries
  in fatal crashes with sampled injuries in nonfatal crashes.
- Police-report coverage omits unreported crashes, while the motor-vehicle requirement excludes bicycle-only
  falls. Different modal definitions also prevent treating the compendium's totals as directly comparable
  measures of travel safety. No cycling exposure denominator or intervention analysis accompanies these results.[^nts2021]

## Review scope

Partial ingestion of a 587-page PDF. Reviewed title/citation pages, introduction (printed p. xix),
Tables 2-1 and 2-2 with their notes (pp. 111–115; PDF pp. 136–140), supporting Table 2-19 (p. 136),
glossary fatal-crash/fatality and highway-injury definitions (pp. 345, 347), Appendix E highway fatality,
injury and crash methods (pp. 424–426), and Appendix F highway sources for Tables 2-1 and 2-2 (pp. 505–506).
Visually checked the fatality and injury table rows and year alignment. Table 2-20 was read for context but
its alcohol results were not extracted. Other chapters and most safety tables were not substantively reviewed;
this does not claim full cycling, commuting, economic or environmental coverage. Underlying NHTSA reports and
microdata were not independently reviewed. Searches by title, DOI, publisher and data-system names found no
existing duplicate publication or matching U.S. national pedalcyclist finding in the bundle.
