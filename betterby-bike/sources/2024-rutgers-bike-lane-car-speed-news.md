# Traffic Speeds Decrease When Bike Lane is Present

Kitta MacPherson, Rutgers University, June 3, 2024.
[Original article](https://www.rutgers.edu/news/traffic-speeds-decrease-when-bike-lane-present) ·
[Local PDF](../../raw-sources/research/rutgers-bike-lane-car-speed.pdf).

## Reporting and results

This university news article covers [Younes et al.'s Asbury Park pilot](/sources/2024-rutgers-bike-lane-car-speed.md),
published in the *Journal of Urban Mobility* ([study DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.urbmob.2024.100071)). It describes
computer-vision comparisons of more than 9,000 vehicles before and after temporary bicycle-lane installation at
Cookman and Asbury Avenues, not a separate evaluation.[^rutgers2024]

The article reports **28% lower average maximum speeds and 21% lower average speeds for right-turning vehicles**
with delineated lanes, an 8% reduction for straight traffic, and no slowing for perpendicular traffic. It reports
smaller paint-only reductions of 11–15%, described as confined to right turns. These are rounded accounts of the
same study's results, not additional confirmations.[^rutgers2024]

## Additional reporting and interpretation

Lead author Hannah Younes describes drivers failing to stop before legal right turns on red at the beach-bound
intersection. She hypothesizes that narrower lanes and more visible cones demand greater driver concentration.
Her statement that bike lanes “save lives” and the article's proposed reduction in crash risk/severity are safety
interpretations, not measured changes in deaths, injuries or stop compliance.[^rutgers2024]

The article also reports that Bloustein students surveyed bicycle and electric-scooter riders using the temporary
lane and that most liked bike lanes. It gives no sample size, percentage, questionnaire or response rate. This
preserves a rider-attitude account but cannot quantify public support or demonstrate increased cycling. Its national
pedestrian-fatality figures are background attributed to the Insurance Institute for Highway Safety, not pilot
outcomes; that underlying source was not reviewed.[^rutgers2024]

## Qualifications and access

The [existing directional Finding](/findings/design/asbury-park-directional-speed-comparisons.md) retains distinctions
the article compresses: the approximately 8% figure concerns one straight movement's top speed, not all straight
traffic or average speed. Table 6 also reports a significant paint-only top-speed reduction for Kingsley-to-Cookman
straight traffic, qualifying the article's right-turn-only statement. “Maximum” means each vehicle's within-trajectory
95th-percentile speed, not its absolute maximum or a percentile across drivers.[^younes2024]

This short, nonrandomized, single-intersection pilot does not isolate delineators from narrower lanes and altered
turning geometry. Neither the news article nor its repetition of the study resolves the study's inconsistent vehicle
totals or establishes crash reductions.[^younes2024]

All five supplied PDF pages were read as extracted text; substantive reporting is on pp. 1–2, followed by related
stories and site navigation. The already-ingested primary paper's speed definitions, Table 6 and discussion were
rechecked for the qualifications above. No new Finding was created: speed results already have homes, and the
brief survey account lacks enough detail for a separate quantitative claim.
