# Michigan's proposed older-driver testing requirements

Ryan Jeltema, **ABC12 (WJRT), March 26, 2026**. [Original article](https://www.abc12.com/news/state/bill-would-increase-testing-rules-for-elderly-michigan-drivers/article_a8c5ef70-f532-42bd-bb41-9b54324afb15.html).

## Issue and reporting basis

Jeltema reports that Michigan State Sen. Rosemary Bayer introduced Senate Bill 847 on March 18.
As described, the bill would require vision, written knowledge and driving-skills tests at a Secretary
of State branch office every four years beginning at age 75, then annually beginning at age 85.
It had been referred to the Senate Committee on Transportation and Infrastructure and had not come
up for a vote by the morning of publication. Passage in both chambers and gubernatorial action
remained necessary; these are proposed requirements, not established law.[^jeltema2026]

The article attributes the proposal's personal context to WWJ Radio: Bayer reportedly heard from a
widower whose wife was killed by a 94-year-old driver in 2024. The lead photograph and caption instead
concern older drivers in Los Angeles in 2012. Neither account supplies an age-specific crash rate or
an evaluation of licensing tests.[^jeltema2026]

## Limits and coverage reconciliation

The full three-page PDF was read; substantive reporting is on pp. 1–2, with related-story links and
site material thereafter. The bill text and WWJ reporting were not independently reviewed. The
article contains no testing-effectiveness comparison, crash-outcome data, license-loss estimates,
older-driver interviews or assessment of alternative transportation. It cannot establish whether
age-based testing improves safety, disproportionately restricts capable drivers or changes access.
Its legislative status is only a March 26, 2026 snapshot.[^jeltema2026]

No earlier coverage of this publication or bill was found. No new Finding is warranted: the contribution
is a dated policy proposal, not a measured safety or access result. The
[older-adult mobility synthesis](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#older-adult-mobility-needs-extend-beyond-commuting)
connects it to existing [transit-use reporting](2026-streetsblog-aging-transportation.md) without treating
that evidence as an evaluation of testing requirements or proof of Michigan transportation needs.
