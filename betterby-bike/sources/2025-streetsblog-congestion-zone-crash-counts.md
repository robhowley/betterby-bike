# Congestion Relief Zone is Also a CRASH Relief Zone: Data

Gersh Kuntzman, Streetsblog NYC, January 23, 2025, 2:23 p.m. EST.
Read the six-page [PDF capture](../../raw-sources/news/congestion-relief-zone-is-also-a-crash-relief-zone-data-streetsblog-new-york-city.pdf):
substantive reporting is on pp. 1–4; remaining material is related stories and site navigation.

## Reporting and evidence

Streetsblog analyzes recently updated city crash statistics, crediting Crashmapper. For Manhattan's
Congestion Relief Zone below 60th Street, it reports 90 crashes and 37 people injured during January 6–17,
2025, versus 199 crashes and 76 injuries in its stated comparable 2024 period. Reported declines are 55%
in crashes and 51% in injuries. Against 173 crashes and 51 injuries in its early-2023 comparison, the
reported declines are 48% and 27%, respectively.[^kuntzman2025]

The article also says crashes were below early-2021 and early-2022 levels, but provides no counts in the
text. The chart title, caption and attribution are captured on p. 2, but the plot itself is absent, confirmed
visually. No additional values were extracted from it.

## Accounts and limitations

NYC DOT says it is pleased but that conclusions are premature, noting ongoing monitoring. The article
explicitly acknowledges that weather is not controlled. MTA official John McCarthy interprets fewer vehicles,
less gridlock and calmer traffic as a mechanism. Riders Alliance's Danny Pearlstein calls crash reductions
inevitable; Transportation Alternatives' Ben Furnas calls the numbers promising and advocates pedestrian
space, safer intersections, bus lanes and protected cycling networks. These are interpretations and
recommendations, not measured mechanisms or infrastructure effects.[^kuntzman2025]

This is a short, uncontrolled count comparison, without exposure denominators, mode-specific injuries,
severity breakdown, uncertainty estimates or a reporting-completeness assessment. The prose describes
January 6–17 (10 business days and one weekend) and the same 12-day period in 2024, but describes 2023
as its first 12 days; the chart caption instead says the first 10 working days of each calendar year.
Exact comparator-date alignment is unresolved. The article's separate estimated 7.5% decline in entering
cars has no matched exposure basis sufficient to calculate crash risk. The crash-count analysis is distinct
from existing congestion-pricing air-quality and ambulance-travel evidence, not confirmation of those outcomes.
