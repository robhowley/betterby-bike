# A Week Without Driving … Or Biking?

Tom Fucoloro, Streetsblog USA, October 2, 2025.
[Published article](https://usa.streetsblog.org/2025/10/02/a-week-without-driving-or-biking), republished with
permission from [Seattle Bike Blog](https://www.seattlebikeblog.com/2025/09/30/a-week-without-driving-or-biking/).
Read the complete substantive PDF text on pages 1–5; later pages contain site material.

## Personal account

Fucoloro, Seattle Bike Blog's founder and a car-free parent, considers adapting Week Without Driving into a
walking-and-transit challenge because not everyone who cannot drive can ride a bicycle. He describes his own
Seattle-area trips, not a survey, controlled comparison or completed week-long experiment.[^fucoloro]

- On a Tacoma speaking trip, he reports missing Sound Transit's 594 bus in each direction and waiting the full 30
  minutes for the next service. He acknowledges better planning could have avoided both misses, contrasting the waits
  with the reliability he values in cycling.
- His second grader's changed school-bus route gets her home nearly an hour after the final bell. The cancellation of
  Metro Route 20 removed their useful public-transit connection. Walking takes half an hour each way, so parental
  drop-off and pickup each require an hour-long round trip. Cargo biking or accompanying her own bicycle remains
  convenient; he continues biking her to school rather than impose his experiment on her.
- Carrying hockey equipment on transit would involve 25 minutes of walking across the connections. Some games end after
  transit service or occur at rinks without nearby service, although that night's trip is possible. His usual weekly
  cargo-bike grocery trip would, he anticipates, become smaller, more frequent shopping trips.[^fucoloro]

He acknowledges that familiarity with transit could improve his journeys, that Seattle offers better opportunities for
this challenge than many communities, and that changing habits may reveal enjoyable alternatives. He plans to end the
challenge Friday for a cycling weekend. These are reflections and intentions, not observed post-challenge
results.[^fucoloro]

## Limits and relationship to existing coverage

The account distinguishes being car-free by bicycle from having usable options when cycling is unavailable. It supplies
no representative disability sample, verified service records, systematic travel-time comparison, safety outcome or
intervention effect. His suggestion that eroded school-travel options explain more chaotic pickup traffic is
speculation. The opening claim that one third of Americans cannot drive cites the wider advocacy context and Anna
Letitia Zivarts's book, not a reviewed population estimate; the book was not read.[^fucoloro]

No existing account of this publication was found. It complements the
[commuting and access synthesis](../topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#cycling-can-conceal-poor-alternatives) and
[older-adult mobility coverage](2026-streetsblog-aging-transportation.md), but is neither independent survey
confirmation nor a measured access gain. No new Finding was warranted: the contribution is a clearly attributed personal
illustration of limitations in alternative modes.
