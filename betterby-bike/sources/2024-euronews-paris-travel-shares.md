# Cycling is now more popular than driving in the centre of Paris, study finds

Rosie Frost, Euronews, April 12, 2024. Read all seven pages of the
[archived article](../../raw-sources/news/cycling-is-now-more-popular-than-driving-in-the-centre-of-paris-study-finds-euronews.pdf);
substantive reporting and photo captions are on pp. 1–3. Later pages contain site furniture.

## Reporting and evidence

Frost reports an Institut Paris Region (IPR) study of 3,337 Île-de-France residents aged 16–80,
whose movements were tracked with GPS units and travel journals. The article reports cycling for
11.2% of trips inside central Paris, compared with cars at 4.3%, walking at 53.3% and public transport
at 30%. It contrasts cycling with a reported 3% in 2010. Deputy mayor David Belliard calls the
result a first victory; this is his interpretation, not a causal evaluation.[^frost2024]

The suburban pattern differs: cars accounted for almost half of trips between the city's two ring
roads and 61% within more distant suburbs. Transit accounted for 66% of journeys between the inner
suburbs and Paris and 77% between the outer suburbs and Paris. IPR characterizes the suburban car
pattern as automotive dependence.[^frost2024]

Policy context includes removed parking spaces, pedestrianisation, a February 2024 vote for higher
SUV parking fees and planned restrictions on more polluting vehicles from 2025. Frost describes
around €250 million for cycling improvements by 2026 and the Grand Paris Express construction
program: 200 km of metro lines, 68 stations, four new lines, extensions to two existing lines and
a 76 km outer-suburban rail ring. These are reported plans and construction context as of 2024,
not evidence that the projects caused the travel shares or were subsequently completed.[^frost2024]

## Limits and extracted Finding

The underlying IPR report was not reviewed and its publication link is not supplied in the
extracted article. Fieldwork dates, weighting, recruitment, uncertainty and detailed geographic
boundaries are absent. The article alternates “Paris” and “city centre”; do not generalize its
central-city shares to the whole region. Comparability with the 2010 figure is not established,
and trip shares do not measure the percentage of people cycling, safety or equitable access.
