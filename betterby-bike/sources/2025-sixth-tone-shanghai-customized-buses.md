# Bus Stops Here: Shanghai Lets Riders Design Their Own Routes

Chen Yiru, Sixth Tone, May 9, 2025. [Original article](https://www.sixthtone.com/news/1017072).
Read the full five-page capture, including the platform screenshot and captions; substantive reporting is on pp. 1–3,
with the header-photo caption on p. 4. This is news reporting drawing on domestic media and official accounts,
not an independent service evaluation.

## Resident requests and launch conditions

Shanghai's DZ (dingzhi, or customized) buses let residents propose routes and others opt in or vote.
The reported launch threshold is typically 15–20 passengers per trip. The article says more than 220 DZ routes
had launched across all 16 districts, while the new online platform opened May 8, 2025. Those routes therefore
should not be treated as routes created by the one-day-old platform. Users specify endpoints, times and frequency;
approved routes can begin in as little as three days, not a measured average or guaranteed turnaround.[^sixthtone]

Requests span school, hospital, metro-connection and grave-visiting trips. A popular-customization page aggregates
interest, and group bookings can accelerate approval. Fares are market-based; the article reports no student,
senior or other group discounts at publication. No fare distribution or affordability assessment is provided.[^sixthtone]

## Pilot use and planning constraints

DZ301 links a major metro station to residential blocks, schools and offices. Jiushi Bus Company deputy manager
Wu Yongming told domestic media that average daily passenger flow was 250–260 people, including 170–180 in the
morning peak and 70–80 in the evening. The capture supplies neither a counting period nor a definition distinguishing
unique people from passenger trips; the peak ranges should not be added into a corrected daily estimate.[^sixthtone]

That route followed a resident request in December 2024, on-site observation, commuter conversations, peak-hour
turnover calculations and driver trial runs. Tongji University professor Chen Xiaohong interprets customization as
a way to match demand and capacity within Shanghai's dense network. Passenger Transport Department deputy director
Wang Yixiang says the platform shortens a formerly slow process, but acknowledges uneven demand, low public awareness
and continued reliance on manual fieldwork. These are attributed interpretations and implementation accounts,
not measured improvements in travel time or resource use.[^sixthtone]

## What this adds and does not establish

The article adds an implemented demand-aggregation mechanism to the
[transit-access synthesis](../topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#transit-coverage-and-usable-service-differ),
complementing the existing Wheeling service-gap and Transbay congestion accounts rather than independently confirming them.
A launch threshold can leave dispersed requests unserved, but this report does not measure rejected requests,
unmet need, participant incomes, digital exclusion, mode shift or bicycle connections. More routes and pilot passenger
flow do not establish equitable access, reduced driving or cycling benefits.

No separate Finding was created: the useful contribution is the program's reported operation and constraints,
not an evaluated access effect. Linked [The Paper coverage](https://www.thepaper.cn/newsDetail_forward_30436791)
and [Xinhua coverage](http://www.sh.xinhuanet.com/20250509/cef13b1418e547ea93ff0ca0767de033/c.html)
were not independently reviewed. Later service operation and fare policies remain unverified.
