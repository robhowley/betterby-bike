# Americans want more trains: Amtrak sees record ridership

## Publication and coverage

Leonardo Pellizzari-Astle, Frontier Group, August 26, 2026.
[Original article](https://frontiergroup.org/articles/americans-want-more-trains-amtrak-sees-record-ridership/).
All substantive text in the four-page PDF was read, including the correction. This is advocacy commentary
synthesizing operator statistics, news coverage and funding proposals, not a new travel study.

## Reporting and argument

The article reports record FY2025 Amtrak ridership, up 5.1% from FY2024, and further growth in FY2026.
Its opening 23.9 million and 6% figures link to the May 2026 performance report, not a completed FY2026
annual total. State-supported services reportedly grew 9% year to date through May. New/restored service
examples include Mardi Gras surpassing its first-year forecast before eight months and Borealis growth
from 2024 to 2025. These are [reported ridership results](../findings/networks/amtrak-reported-ridership-growth-2025-2026.md),
not evidence identifying which modes passengers previously used.[^frontier]

Pellizzari-Astle connects growth to new trains, station and infrastructure work, higher driving/flying costs
and passenger preferences. He describes $22 billion in direct five-year infrastructure-law funding and
warns that a proposed House bill would cut passenger-rail funding 43% relative to that law, without guaranteed
baseline funding. These are the article's funding account and prospective warning, not enacted cuts or
measured investment effects. It acknowledges speed and reliability shortcomings while recommending
regional rail expansion and consistent federal investment.[^frontier]

## Limits and reconciliation

- The linked [FY2025 operator fact sheet](https://media.amtrak.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/FY25-Year-End-Ridership-Fact-Sheet.pdf)
  and [May 2026 performance report](https://www.amtrak.com/content/dam/projects/dotcom/english/public/documents/corporate/monthlyperformancereports/2026/Amtrak-Monthly-Performance-Report-May-2026.pdf)
  were identified from PDF links but not independently reviewed. Counts do not establish unique people or causal demand drivers.
- The article supplies no controlled comparison, traveler-origin survey or quantified bicycle access,
  emissions reduction or local economic effect. Its growth and gas-price comparisons do not identify causation.
- Borealis's 140% growth lacks matched operating-period detail; the article's average air fare versus
  advance-purchase minimum rail fare is not a like-for-like affordability comparison.
- Brightline's 17% year-to-date ridership and 16% revenue increases lack an explicit endpoint in the text.
  Its correction specifies Florida trains are diesel-electric, not fully electric.
- Existing [Amtrak bicycle-access coverage](2026-amtrak-bicycle-friendly-trains.md) concerns service scores
  and booking constraints, not these passenger counts. Neither publication establishes rail-induced cycling.
