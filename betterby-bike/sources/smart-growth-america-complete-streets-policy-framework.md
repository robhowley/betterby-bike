# Complete Streets Policy Framework

Smart Growth America, undated resource page describing the National Complete Streets Coalition's
framework, substantially revised in 2018 and refreshed/repackaged in 2023. The entire three-page
capture was read: introduction on p. 1, ten elements on p. 2, and site footer on p. 3. The
[linked framework report](https://www.smartgrowthamerica.org/media/2024/08/Complete-Streets-Policy-Framework.pdf)
was identified from the PDF hyperlink but not reviewed. This capture is the resource page, not that report.

## Basis and recommendations

The coalition describes the elements as based on collective transportation planning/design expertise
and consultation with its steering committee and national engineers, planners, researchers and advocates.
It presents them as guidance for policy at any level of governance, not results from a comparative
implementation study.[^framework]

The page recommends that a policy:

1. State a vision for a connected network serving all users.
2. Define and prioritize underinvested and underserved communities.
3. Cover new construction, retrofits, reconstruction, maintenance and ongoing operations.
4. Specify exceptions, with high-level approval and public notice before granting them.
5. Require private-developer compliance and coordination across agencies and departments.
6. Use current design guidance and set a timeframe for implementing it.
7. Consider project context and current and expected land-use and transportation needs.
8. Establish vision-aligned, equity-aware performance measures reported regularly to the public.
9. Prioritize Complete Streets through project-selection criteria.
10. Specify implementation steps affecting what gets built and where.[^framework]

## Interpretation and limits

These are attributed policy recommendations. The page supplies no adopted-policy sample, scoring
weights, measured construction delivery, crash outcomes or travel/access evaluation. Its claim that
these elements represent best practices does not establish their comparative effectiveness. The
framework's distinction between formal commitment and implementation helps interpret policy rankings,
but this summary does not reproduce or validate the 2025 ranking's scoring method.

- [2025 policy-ranking resource page](2025-smart-growth-america-complete-streets-policies.md) reports
  selected policy scores, not independent evidence of the framework's effects.
- [Street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#eligible-funding-is-not-cycling-expenditure-or-delivered-benefits)
  separates policy design, implementation and measured benefits. No new empirical Finding is extracted.
