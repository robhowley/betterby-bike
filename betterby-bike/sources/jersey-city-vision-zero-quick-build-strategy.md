## Content and reporting basis

The narrative says the city's engineering team chose paint and temporary materials rather than moving curbs or
breaking ground early in its Vision Zero commitment. It describes implementing projects when opportunities arise,
such as street repaving, and endorses this as a quick-build, low-cost approach. It says quick installations often
lead to quicker results, but supplies no measured construction times, costs or outcome comparison.[^excerpt]

For unspecified “recent years,” the excerpt reports 20 miles of protected bike lanes, 679 speed humps, 183 locations
prohibiting right turns on red and 14 intersections with LPIs. Two further entries show “more than 30” and “more than
100,” but their parklet/pedestrian and curb-treatment labels are partly obscured. Superscripts 8 and 9 follow the
introduction to the list; their reference entries are absent.[^excerpt]

## Interpretation and limits

These are reported completed installations, not merely proposed quantities. However, the heading's endorsement of
strategic innovation and the narrative's speed/cost rationale are recommendations and author interpretations, not
observed comparative benefits. The supplied excerpt contains no methods, baseline, observation dates, comparator,
crash or injury outcomes, ridership, cost accounting or evaluation of permanent versus temporary construction.
It does not establish that each listed installation used temporary materials. These limits concern this crop,
not the unseen full publication.[^excerpt]

Bundle searches found no matching strategy excerpt or installation figures. Existing Jersey City bikeshare
commercial research and the New Jersey Bicycle Friendly State Report Card concern different claims; they do not
independently verify these totals. The unidentified references prevent checking underlying-publication overlap.
