# Bike Bus + Pop Up Lane = A Better Way To Get Back To School (And Advocate)

Kea Wilson, Streetsblog USA, August 25, 2025. Full captured text read; substantive reporting is on PDF pp. 1–8.
The article links a
[Journal of the American Planning Association case study](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01944363.2025.2537662),
naming Kurt Kaminer, Mickey Witte and Gillian Hotz. That study was identified, not independently reviewed.

## Reporting and evidence

University of Miami-based BikeSafe added a pop-up lane to an established elementary-school bike bus in Coconut Grove,
Miami. Across three Bike to School Day events in 2023–2024, the article reports an average nearly 30% reduction in cars
in the school drop-off line and doubled bicycle traffic. Riders included parents, siblings and pupils at nearby private
schools, so bicycle counts are not counts of participating elementary-school students alone. Nearly 77% of surveyed
parents supported making the lane permanent.[^wilson2025]

Original reporting centers on co-organizer Kaminer's account of building support for infrastructure through
child-focused events. He says earlier rides without infrastructure did not sustain participation or advance permanent
facilities. However, the pop-up experiment also had not immediately produced a public campaign for a permanent path. His
explanations, an already relatively safe route and uncertainty around adjacent housing development, are interpretations
rather than tested causes.[^wilson2025]

Implementation required volunteers, cones, temporary paint, PTA/district scheduling and bike storage. Kaminer regretted
the brief duration, saying many neighbors never saw the intervention. A child's account from a separate Hialeah ride
illustrates personal enjoyment and lost cycling opportunities after migration, not a measured Coconut Grove
outcome.[^wilson2025]

## Limits and reconciliation

The article supplies no raw traffic counts, observation durations, defined comparison days, parent survey size, response
rate or uncertainty estimates. It cannot isolate the pop-up lane from the organized ride, establish lasting school-mode
change, demonstrate injury reduction or show that support became permanent infrastructure. Its national school-travel
statistic lacks enough denominator detail here to serve as a comparable historical series; its safety-in-numbers
assertion is not an evaluated outcome of these events.

No existing bundle Source or Finding covered this case study. Existing Green Lane survey evidence concerns other
populations and settings; this is a separate, secondhand case report, not independent confirmation of that evaluation or
of the linked Miami study.
