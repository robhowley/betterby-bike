# New Jersey e-bike laws, June 2025 reporting

## Publication and reporting basis

Amanda Wallace, NorthJersey.com, published June 19, 2025, updated June 29, 2025 at 8:33 p.m. ET.
The [original article](https://www.northjersey.com/story/news/new-jersey/2025/06/19/e-bike-laws-nj-classifications-license-registration/84152344007/)
is preserved in a three-page browser print dated July 17, 2025. All three pages' substantive text was read.

The article responds to summer safety reminders from Montville Township, Glen Ridge and Hanover police. It quotes
their Facebook posts and draws its legal explanation from the New Jersey Bicycle and Pedestrian Resource Center,
which it describes as supported by the state Transportation Department. This is secondary legal reporting, not a
study of crashes, compliance or transport access.[^wallace2025]

## What it reports

The article says a 2019 change to Title 39 created a low-speed electric bicycle category for both pedal-assist
(Class 1) and throttle (Class 2) bicycles. It describes these as having a maximum speed of **20 mph** and power of
**750 watts or less**, with no license, insurance or registration requirement. Traditional bicycle laws still apply,
and helmets are required for riders **under 17**. By contrast, its Class 3 motorized-bicycle category requires a
license, registration, insurance and helmet use; it includes electric pedal bicycles reaching **20–28 mph** and gas
pedal bicycles with piston displacement under 50 cc.[^wallace2025]

The center's historical explanation is that earlier rules treated e-bikes as mopeds requiring MVC registration,
but a system written for gas-powered vehicles could not register them. The article also warns that municipalities
may impose location or age restrictions and recommends checking rules in every town ridden through. Statewide
low-speed exemptions therefore do not establish unrestricted local riding access.[^wallace2025]

Background growth claims are attributed to ABC News (269% U.S. market growth in 2019–2022) and the Department of
Energy (1.1 million U.S. e-bikes sold in 2022). They are not New Jersey ridership measurements. The article also
summarizes a Safe Passing Law using a 2022 signing date, due caution and a four-foot passing distance; its chronology
and completeness were not independently checked. Police safety advice is not evidence of a measured safety benefit.[^wallace2025]

## Legal scope and access limits

This preserves what the article reported in **June 2025**, not current New Jersey legal advice. The PDF appears to
contain the full article text, but the linked [2025 micromobility guide](https://njbikeped.org/new-jersey-micromobility-guide-2025/),
[bicycle-law page](https://njbikeped.org/bicycle-laws/), statutes, MVC rules, municipal ordinances and police posts
were not separately reviewed. The simplified speed bands do not resolve the exact 20 mph boundary, motor-assistance
cutoff versus achievable speed, or eligibility of every vehicle design. No later amendments or present-day rules
were checked. No duplicate publication or existing matching legal Finding was found in the bundle.
