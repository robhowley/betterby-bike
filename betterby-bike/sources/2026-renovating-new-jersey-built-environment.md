# Renovating New Jersey’s Built Environment

Grace Opong, New Jersey Future intern, January 20, 2026. Princeton University GHP350/SPI380
policy memo addressed to Fran O’Connor, chair of the New Jersey Target Zero Commission Working Group.
[Supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/renovating-njs-built-environment-intern-report-1-pdf.pdf) ·
[Publisher PDF](https://www.njfuture.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Renovating-NJs-Built-Environment-Intern-Report-1.pdf).

## Purpose and evidence basis

The memo argues for revising the draft Target Zero action plan to connect road safety with physical
activity, mental health and social connection. It synthesizes selected academic research, news,
advocacy and design guidance, not a systematic review or an original New Jersey outcome study.
Its discussion spans walking, cycling, land use, transit and green space.[^memo]

## Recommendations and qualifications

Three priorities conclude the memo: vertically separated bicycle lanes with regular maintenance;
better access to and promotion of the D & R Canal Towpath; and eliminating parking minimums,
especially near transit. Proposed implementation includes cyclist/NJBWC/NJDOT/NJ Transit design
and maintenance collaboration, trail lighting and promotion, and support for S2974 alongside stronger
parking reform. These are the author's proposals, not adopted requirements or measured benefits.[^memo]

The broader package includes road diets after traffic monitoring, mixed-income development near
transit, parking cash-out, inclusive employer transit benefits, connectivity scoring that also considers
nearby destinations, neighborhood-to-trail feeder paths and audits of neighborhoods remote from trails.
The memo recognizes possible congestion and bus delays, unequal access to employer benefits,
affluent-area concentration of cycling investment and perceptions of displacement. It does not
estimate these trade-offs or demonstrate that the proposed package resolves them.[^memo]

## Evidence reuse and material limits

- The cited **44% fewer deaths** comparison repeats Aaron Short's 2019 Streetsblog account of the
  12-city study [already represented here](2019-streetsblog-protect-bike-safety.md). It is not an
  independent confirmation or a cyclist-specific causal estimate. Use the existing
  [bounded finding](/findings/safety/us-cities-protected-lane-casualty-association.md).
- The claim that 70% of New Jerseyans do not cycle because they distrust motorists is relayed through
  Tia Azzi's 2025 policy-lab article, without a sample or denominator in this memo. It cannot establish
  statewide prevalence. The separately reviewed [Rutgers barrier report](new-jersey-black-hispanic-bicycle-barriers.md)
  has its own sampling and denominator limits; no identity between these estimates is assumed.
- Physical and mental health associations from other settings motivate recommendations but do not
  establish health improvements from a New Jersey lane, trail or parking intervention. No effect sizes,
  comparative appraisal, implementation costs or outcome evaluation establish the preferred package.
- The p. 3 passage juxtaposes a pedestrian-fatality record with **687 roadway deaths in 2024** without
  separating the pedestrian count. Do not reuse 687 as pedestrian deaths. Its painted-lane discussion
  also uses “protected” loosely; painted space and physical separation are not interchangeable.
- Draft-plan descriptions, trail conditions, legislative status and design thresholds are historical
  accounts, not independently checked current rules or conditions. Proposed NACTO adoption is not
  evidence that its guidance is legally mandatory.[^memo]

## Reading scope and related synthesis

Read the extracted text of all 22 PDF pages: two opening pages, numbered pp. 1–14 and references
pp. 15–20. Image-only details of the obesity map and conceptual diagram were not visually reviewed;
no values were extracted from them. Cited studies, draft plan, legislation and guidance were not
independently reviewed for this ingestion.

No new Finding: useful content is the attributed policy package, while the quantified safety comparison
already has a home. [Commuting and access](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#policy-packages-do-not-establish-access-or-health-gains)
connects its proposals to the distinction between nearby infrastructure and usable, equitable access.
