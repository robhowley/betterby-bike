## Reporting basis and claims

The visible passage summarizes unnamed studies covering a mixture of projects, regions, counties and states.
It reports that each **10% increase in metropolitan lane miles** is accompanied by traffic increases of
**3%–6% in the first few years** and **6%–10% in the “following five to ten years.”** The screenshot does not
define traffic, identify the study populations or supply methods, comparisons or uncertainty estimates.[^excerpt]

The article interprets this as a road likely filling up before reconstruction. It says more recent U.S. studies
strengthen the claims and a Hungarian study suggests the phenomenon works in reverse when capacity is reduced.
Those are secondary assertions, not separately assessable results: the linked words are visible but their targets
are not supplied in the screenshot. No numerical capacity-reduction result is visible.[^excerpt]
