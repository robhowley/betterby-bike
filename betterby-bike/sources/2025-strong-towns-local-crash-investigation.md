# A Plane Crash Gets an NTSB Investigation. A Car Crash Gets a Shrug

Charles Marohn, Strong Towns, February 3, 2025.
[Original article](https://archive.strongtowns.org/journal/2025/2/3/a-plane-crash-gets-an-ntsb-investigation-a-car-crash-gets-a-shrug).
Read the complete substantive article on PDF pages 1–5; remaining material is biography, navigation and footer.

## Reporting and argument

Marohn contrasts the response to the January 29, 2025 aircraft collision near Ronald Reagan National Airport
with routine US roadway deaths. He quotes Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and NTSB Chair Jennifer Homendy
about the aviation response, then argues that cities should investigate street-design contributions to fatal
road crashes rather than stop at individual blame. The aviation investigation was still in progress when the
article appeared; its anticipated report and recommendations are not completed findings.[^marohn]

The article promotes Strong Towns' own Crash Analysis Studio model and its *Beyond Blame* report, described
as released the previous October. Staff member Edward Erfurt explains the emotional and investigative work
of reconstructing crashes from participants' perspectives. Marohn identifies high-speed road design,
dangerous intersections, inadequate pedestrian infrastructure and poor visibility as recurring contributing
conditions in that work, but supplies no case inventory, coding method or comparison group.[^marohn]

## Proposed local response

Marohn recommends five municipal responsibilities: assign a safety lead empowered to intervene in road design;
establish a crash-response team to review fatal crashes and publish recommendations; conduct holistic Crash
Analysis Studios; implement temporary barriers, lane adjustments and improved crossings while planning
long-term changes; and hold elected officials and public works departments accountable. His claim that many
responses need only paint and cones is an implementation argument, not a costed project evaluation.[^marohn]

## Evidence limits and existing coverage

This is advocacy by the organization offering the process, not a study of its effectiveness. It provides no
before/after crash or injury results, exposure measures, adoption counts, quantified costs or tables supporting
a safety estimate. The stated totals of more than 100 US road deaths daily and more than 40,000 annually lack
a specified reference year or dataset in the article. Its categorical contrast about the absence of federal
investigation is rhetorical framing, not a reviewed account of NTSB jurisdiction or highway investigations.
The *Beyond Blame* report and individual Studio records were not reviewed.[^marohn]

Bundle searches found no existing account of this publication or *Beyond Blame*. Its local post-crash
learning proposal complements [NACTO's delivery guidance](2025-nacto-eight-keys-mobility-infrastructure.md)
and the [street-redesign synthesis](../topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#leadership-guidance-is-not-a-delivery-evaluation),
but does not independently confirm quick-build safety effects. No new Finding was warranted: the useful
addition is an attributed governance proposal, not a bounded empirical result.
