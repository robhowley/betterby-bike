# Status of the Nation's Highways, Bridges, and Transit, 25th Edition

## Publication identity and access

The supplied PDF is **USDOT/FHWA/FTA's 2024 Conditions and Performance Report to Congress**, not
Brookings' *Highway shakedown*. Its recommended citation supplies the year and DOI above. The repository
retrieval manifest records discovery of `https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policy/25cpr/pdf/CP25_Full_Report.pdf#page=52`
from the Brookings page. Neither that article nor another raw PDF was reviewed for this ingestion.

This is a **partial funding-focused reading** of the 590-page capture: front matter and introduction
through printed p. xiv, the opening analytical-method description on p. xv, and Chapter 2's highway
funding discussion on pp. 2-2–2-8, including Exhibits 2-1–2-5. Only extracted text and tables were read;
charts were not visually inspected. Other substantive chapters, model appendices and underlying
Highway Statistics tables were not independently reviewed.

## Purpose and methods

The report consolidates national highway, bridge and transit conditions, performance and finance data,
primarily for 2018, with many historical comparisons spanning 2008–2018. It also develops national
investment scenarios, which are not evaluated here. Core historical analyses predate COVID-19 and
increased investment under the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law.[^report]

Finance statistics come predominantly from state reports based on state/local accounting systems,
using FHWA's reporting definitions. Some private investment enters the accounts as other receipts.
The data distinguish revenues raised, transfers between governments, reserve movements and direct
spending. These are administrative financial accounts, not a household tax-incidence study.[^report]

## Funding results

For 2018, the report records **$237.8 billion** raised for highways, including **$121.3 billion (51%)**
from motor-fuel taxes, vehicle taxes/fees and tolls used for highways. The other **$116.5 billion**
includes general funds, property taxes, other receipts and borrowing. Local governments raised
**$70.1 billion**, including **$7.1 billion** in user charges and **$26.9 billion** in general-fund
appropriations. Their net intergovernmental receipts were **$20.1 billion**, a separate account.
These figures do not locate where drivers paid state taxes or quantify a local-road-user subsidy
to state highways.[^report]

## Limits and discrepancies

Revenue shares are not spending shares: 2018 expenditures totaled $244.5 billion, with the difference
funded by net reserve drawdown. Borrowing is not necessarily a permanent subsidy from non-drivers,
and general-tax payers may also drive. User charges allocated to transit or other nonhighway purposes
are excluded from the highway-revenue numerator. No cycling-only expenditure or redesign benefit is
identified by these aggregates.

Nearby prose and table notes contain inconsistencies: p. 2-7 gives state revenue as $125.4 billion
while Exhibits 2-3 and 2-5 give $126.7 billion; p. 2-8 gives $34.9 billion for general-fund appropriations
while its table gives $39.4 billion including federal funds. Gross-user-receipt notes also differ in
amount/year between Exhibits 2-2, 2-4 and 2-5. These are unresolved, not silently reconciled; the
Finding uses the repeated highway-dedicated totals and explicitly identified table entries.

## Finding

- [2018 highway revenue shares do not measure who subsidizes whom](/findings/us-2018-highway-revenue-accounting.md).
