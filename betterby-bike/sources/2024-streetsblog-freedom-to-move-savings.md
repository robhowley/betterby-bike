# Giving Americans More Transportation Options Could Save Them $6.2 Trillion

Kea Wilson, Streetsblog USA, December 30, 2024. [Article](https://usa.streetsblog.org/2024/12/30/best-of-2024-giving-americans-more-transportation-options-could-save-them-6-2-trillion).
The editor identifies this as a republication of an earlier 2024 story.

## Reporting and evidence

Wilson covers UCS's [Freedom to Move](2024-freedom-to-move.md), whose primary report is already
represented in this bundle. The article reports $6.2 trillion in prospective savings relative to
vehicle electrification alone, including $201 billion in energy infrastructure, $128 billion in
public-health costs and $5.9 trillion in household vehicle costs. It also attributes 250,000 avoided
crash deaths and 3.7 million avoided injuries to reduced driving. These are conditional modeled
benefits, not observed policy outcomes or an additional independent study.[^news]

Original interview material from lead author Kevin Shen explains that alternatives need not eliminate
all household driving: some households might shed a second car or replace selected trips with an
e-bike where destinations are nearby. He frames transportation choice as an equity goal and describes
industry lobbying and historical street-use norms as obstacles. The article reports 2023 lobbying and
contributions by opponents of an emissions-reporting rule, and repeats national non-driving and
housing/transport cost-burden estimates. These are attributed context, not evidence that the modeled
savings reach particular households or that cycling investments deliver them.[^news]

## Reconciliation and limitations

The substantive article text on PDF pp. 1–6 was read; embedded graphics were not independently
transcribed. Model methods and external links were not newly reviewed. The existing primary-report
summary retains the more precise scenario and accounting definitions: 27% below **2023** light-duty
travel by 2050, rather than this article's wording “from 2035 to 2050”; household savings include
maintenance and depreciation, not simply immediately spendable cash. The news article describes
car payments, fuel, insurance and other costs without reconciling those categories to the model.
Its $128 billion health figure is the upper end of the primary report's high-reduction range.
The headline total is not a complete net-benefit estimate after alternative-transport investments.
Crash projections lack sufficient methods here for a separate risk Finding.
