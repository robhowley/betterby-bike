# Bicyclist Behavior in the Presence of Two-stage Turn Queue Boxes

Dewan Tanvir Ahammed, Lejla Cesic, Chengbo Ai, Eleni Christofa, Francis Tainter and Brigham Stevenson, all listed
at the University of Massachusetts, Amherst. The [supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/bicyclist-behavior-two-stage-turn-queue-boxes.pdf)
is a one-page abstract screenshot displaying identifiers **TRBAM-24-05702** and **B768**. No explicit publication
date, DOI or original webpage URL is visible; the identifier alone is not treated as a verified date.[^ahammed]

## Question and methods reported

The study aimed to inventory two-stage turn queue-box design characteristics and examine cyclists' use and
positioning. These treatments offer left turns from right-side cycle tracks/bicycle lanes, or right turns from
left-side facilities, at multilane intersections. The authors documented **81 boxes**, then collected video at
**three Boston, Massachusetts, intersections containing eight boxes**. Trajectory analysis examined positioning
and usage rates for **left-turning cyclists**. The 81-box inventory is not the behavioral sample.[^ahammed]

## Results and interpretation

The abstract reports “noncompliance bicyclist behavior” in use of the boxes at the selected intersections,
contrasting this with unspecified earlier studies. It supplies no usage rates, cyclist counts, site breakdowns,
uncertainty or operational definition of noncompliance. This is an unquantified reported behavioral result,
not evidence that all or most riders avoided the boxes, or that their behavior violated traffic law.[^ahammed]

The authors frame the treatment as intended to improve safety and comfort and suggest the findings could inform
design standards and education. No crash, injury or comfort results, or evaluated educational intervention, are
reported. The claimed contrast with earlier studies cannot be assessed from this abstract.[^ahammed]

## Access and limitations

Text extraction returned no substantive text; the entire single-page image was read visually. Only the abstract
and author/identifier listing were available in the supplied file. No full paper, tables, figures, reference list
or separate methods/limitations sections were included or reviewed. The abstract omits observation dates and
duration, site selection, eligible rider denominator, comparison conditions, trajectory-coding procedures and
specific design characteristics associated with behavior. It cannot establish a causal design effect or typical
use across Boston or elsewhere.[^ahammed]

Bundle searches by title, identifiers, authors and treatment found no duplicate publication or representation of
this study. The [Green Lane intersection evaluation](/sources/2014-lessons-green-lanes.md) concerns different
sites and treatments; it is related behavioral evidence, not another report of this Boston sample.
