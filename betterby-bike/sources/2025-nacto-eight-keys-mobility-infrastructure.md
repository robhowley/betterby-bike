# 8 Keys to Transforming a City's Mobility Infrastructure

NACTO, published by Next City, February 12, 2025.
[Original article captured as PDF](../../raw-sources/news/8-keys-to-transforming-a-city-s-mobility-infrastructure.pdf).
Read the complete substantive article on PDF pages 1–3; pages 4–5 contain related-content and membership material.

## Publication and purpose

Next City republishes, with Island Press's permission, an excerpt from NACTO's *Urban Bikeway Design Guide,
Third Edition*. It is professional guidance about city leadership and bicycle-network delivery, not original
news reporting or a research evaluation. The full guide was not reviewed. The excerpt argues that a public
vision for safe, accessible transportation needs resources and political backing to survive implementation
conflicts.[^nacto]

## Recommendations

NACTO identifies eight leadership responsibilities:[^nacto]

1. Define a connected, high-quality network comfortable for all ages and abilities, prioritizing neighborhoods
   expected to benefit most from safer streets.
2. Organize agencies for coordination, clear handoffs and reliable project delivery.
3. State concrete goals, priorities and policies, linking cycling to transit, jobs, safety and climate goals.
4. Provide sufficient, predictable funding for planned year-over-year improvements rather than one-off projects.
5. Invest in staff, equitable hiring, transparent processes and a supportive workplace.
6. Fund community engagement and communication suited to neighborhood needs.
7. Publish decision-making processes so residents and staff understand how to participate and make decisions.
8. Respond to concerns and adjust delivery while maintaining commitment through opposition.

Its example of **50% of residents within a three-minute walk of a high-quality bike facility** illustrates
how to express a goal. It is not a measured coverage result, adopted city target or tested accessibility
standard. E-bike subsidies and reformed traffic laws are likewise policy examples, not evaluated interventions.[^nacto]

## Evidence and relationship to existing coverage

The excerpt supplies no study population, comparison group, project data, cost estimates or measured safety,
ridership, equity or delivery outcomes. Statements about delivering on time and on budget, building trust and
creating healthier cities are NACTO's rationale and expectations, not demonstrated effects of these eight
practices. It contains no tables or figures supporting outcome estimates.[^nacto]

This adds an organizational delivery perspective to existing quick-build and youth-engagement coverage in
[street-redesign outcomes](../topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#leadership-guidance-is-not-a-delivery-evaluation).
No separate Finding was extracted: the useful content is the attributed recommendation set, not a new bounded
empirical result or independent confirmation of existing infrastructure findings.
