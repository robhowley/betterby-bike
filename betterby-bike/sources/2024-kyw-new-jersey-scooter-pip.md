# KYW: New Jersey scooter injury coverage

## Reporting and reading scope

Mike Dougherty's May 15, 2024 KYW Newsradio article reports on the previous day's New Jersey Supreme
Court decision denying a Segway rider pedestrian Personal Injury Protection (PIP) benefits under his
Progressive auto policy. The full five-page preserved capture was read as extracted text: substantive
reporting and the photograph caption occupy pages 1–2; later pages contain recommendations and site
furniture. A repeated cookie overlay is present, but the extracted article runs through its concluding
coverage discussion.[^kyw2024]

## What the interview adds

Gary La Spisa, vice president of the Insurance Council of New Jersey, attributes the denial to the
statute's muscular-power distinction and says legislative action would be needed to extend pedestrian
PIP eligibility. He estimates that, if eligibility changed and insurance became available, premiums
would reach hundreds of dollars per month. The article supplies no actuarial analysis, policy terms,
insured population or pricing comparison supporting that estimate. It is an industry representative's
conditional prediction, not an observed price or measured effect of the ruling.[^kyw2024]

La Spisa also says riders might have medical-insurance coverage, if insured, or recovery through an
at-fault driver's auto insurance. These are attributed possible alternatives, not guarantees of payment
or a comprehensive legal account. No injured rider, consumer advocate or competing premium estimate
is quoted.[^kyw2024]

## Reconciliation and limits

The underlying [Goyco opinion](2024-goyco-progressive-scooter-pip.md) and its
[existing finding](../findings/access/new-jersey-goyco-scooter-pip.md) already represent the legal result.
This article is not independent confirmation and yields no new Finding. Its original interview is
retained to distinguish industry expectations about costs from the court's actual holding.

The headline and opening generalize to e-bikes and scooters. The bundle's primary-opinion summary
limits the decision to the scooter, policy and statute before the Court, not every pedal-assist e-bike.
The article does not establish current law, treatment access, household costs, crash risk or changes
in travel behavior. Later legal developments were not reviewed for this ingestion.
