# Freedom to Move

Kevin X. Shen, Dave Cooke, Emmanuell De Barros, Mike Christensen, Kim Mitchell and Dorothy Wiley.
October 2024, Union of Concerned Scientists, Alternatives for Community and Environment, Utah Rail
Passengers Association and Allendale Strong. [Publication](https://www.ucsusa.org/resources/freedom-move),
[DOI](https://doi.org/10.47923/2024.15594).

## Scope and approach

This US policy/advocacy report combines economy-wide energy scenarios, an FY2021 public/private surface-
transportation spending account, historical secondary research and coauthored community accounts from
Boston, rural Utah and Shreveport. It advocates transit, walking, cycling and land-use changes together.
These are not independently evaluated interventions.[^freedom2024]

The modeling builds on UCS's 2023 clean-energy analysis. All three scenarios reach net-zero emissions in
2050. The comparison scenario retains EIA-projected travel demand; low/high reductions assume respectively
20%/40% less light-duty vehicle distance in 2050 than that comparison, corresponding to approximately
3%/27% decreases from 2023. The report associates these assumptions with expanded alternatives but does
not estimate a specific investment package's induced travel changes.[^freedom2024]

## Main results

- Table 1 reports $110/$201 billion in cumulative energy-system capital savings for low/high VMT reductions
  over 2025–2050, and 188/376 GW less generating capacity in 2050. Air-quality health savings are
  $32–64/$63–128 billion. Table dollars are 2024 dollars discounted at 2%.
- A separate application of RMI's Smarter MODES calculator puts high-reduction household fuel, maintenance
  and depreciation savings above $5.9 trillion cumulatively through 2050. Avoided depreciation is not all
  immediate cash savings. These results do not establish net benefits after alternative-transport costs.
- The FY2021 spending account puts total public/private surface-transport spending above $2.2 trillion,
  including $1.66 trillion for private vehicle ownership. These are aggregate expenditures, not government
  subsidies alone, typical household bills or industry profits.
- Community accounts describe struggles over Boston transit affordability/service, rural Utah transit and
  Shreveport highway displacement. Recommendations include transit operating funds, flexible highway
  funding, safe active-travel networks and more representative public decisionmaking, not measured effects
  of an implemented national policy.[^freedom2024]

## Access and limitations

Read substantive extracted text of pp. 1–51 and the end of the references; read Table 1 on p. 12 visually.
Other charts were not digitized. External studies, model code, calculators and supplementary methods were
not reviewed. Table A-1 is mentioned on p. 10 but not present in this supplied report's extracted text.
The report provides limited detail on health-model uncertainty and travel-policy implementation costs.
It does not measure cycling uptake, route safety, distribution of scenario savings or improved access.

Internal discrepancies remain: p. 11 reports $108–197 billion energy capital savings versus Table 1's
$110–201 billion, and $174–348 billion gasoline savings versus the table's $177–355 billion. We retain
table figures where used rather than reconcile them without evidence. The cover's auto/oil share above
75% also differs from p. 24's roughly 50% automotive plus 20% oil; this share is not extracted as a Finding.
Historical policy descriptions are not verified current requirements.[^freedom2024]
