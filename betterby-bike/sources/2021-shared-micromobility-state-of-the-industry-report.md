# 2021 Shared Micromobility State of the Industry Report

North American Bikeshare & Scootershare Association (NABSA), third annual report, published **2022**, describing
2021. Research by Toole Design and UC Berkeley's Transportation Sustainability Research Center.
[Publication DOI](https://doi.org/10.7922/G2HD7T0P) ·
[Supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/2021-shared-micromobility-state-of-the-industry-report.pdf).

## Reporting basis

An industry snapshot for the United States, Canada and Mexico, not an intervention evaluation. Operator and agency
surveys were distributed to all known providers and overseeing agencies. NABSA combines responses with its system
database, online operating data, earlier city user surveys, census comparisons and secondary research. A system
requires at least three stations or 20 dockless devices, automated management and operation outside a closed campus.
The report does not provide overall survey response counts/rates or uncertainty intervals. Different results use
different samples, years and weighting, not one representative 2021 rider survey.[^nabsa2021]

## Main results and qualifications

- Estimated annual trips recovered to 128 million in 2021 from 83 million in 2020, but remained below 157 million
  in 2019. About 232,000 vehicles were deployed on an average day, above both earlier years. The 298-city coverage
  count must not be confused with separate bikeshare and scooter system counts.
- Pooled user surveys indicate car replacement alongside substantial walking and transit replacement. Mode
  substitution is reported behavior, not directly measured traffic reduction. The approximately 54 million pounds
  of CO2 avoided is a modeled estimate excluding operations and lifecycle effects, not a net climate assessment.
- The estimated 15.5 million additional physical-activity hours combines 5.7 million pedal-bike, 2.1 million
  e-bike and 7.7 million scooter hours, using trip replacement and average duration. These are not measured health
  outcomes or equal-intensity exercise hours; the methods distinguish light scooter activity from cycling.
- Average city demographic gaps show overrepresentation of high-income, White and 25–44-year-old users and
  underrepresentation of women and older adults. The report describes better representation of very-low-income
  users and People of Color than in 2020, but changing survey coverage is not controlled.
- Survey-reported equity offerings include discounts (92%), alternative payments (85%) and adaptive vehicles
  (21%). Sampled full versus discounted annual access costs average $142 versus $39. Program availability and
  listed prices do not measure uptake, affordable complete journeys or reduced demographic gaps.
- The transit section reports 63% of riders using micromobility to connect to transit, 19% weekly and 18% of trips
  for transit connection. Rider and trip denominators differ; operator/agency survey compilation does not establish
  incremental transit ridership. Policy and integration examples describe actions, not evaluated effects.
- Employment is extrapolated from a limited sample to at least 9,000 jobs. Restaurant-spending and health-economic
  benefits are secondary accounts of Kim and McCarthy (2022) and Clockston and Rojas-Rueda (2021), not new independent
  studies. Those underlying publications were not reviewed during this report's ingestion.[^nabsa2021]
  A subsequently reviewed [Kim–McCarthy manuscript with a May 2023 appendix](/sources/wheels-to-meals-micromobility-restaurant-demand.md)
  uses Earnest Analytics and acknowledges a different provider in a prior version; it is related study evidence,
  not an independent confirmation of NABSA's earlier-version account.

Read all 23 PDF pages, including printed pp. 19–20 methods, supporting charts and final publication citation.
Visually checked the printed p. 5 mode-replacement chart, whose extracted labels were scrambled. Title, DOI,
NABSA, shared-micromobility and underlying-study-name searches found no duplicate publication or matching finding
in the bundle. External studies and original survey data were not independently reviewed.
