# A.G. investigating alleged State Police traffic enforcement slowdown

## Publication and reporting basis

Nikita Biryukov's **December 11, 2024** New Jersey Monitor article reports Attorney General Matt Platkin's
announcement of an investigation into an alleged State Police traffic-enforcement slowdown. The complete
substantive two-page [article capture](../../raw-sources/news/a-g-investigating-alleged-state-police-traffic-enforcement-slowdown-new-jersey-monitor.pdf)
was read. Its evidence consists of Platkin's quoted statement, secondary reporting of a New York Times investigation,
and background on a July 2023 racial-disparity analysis, not an original crash evaluation.[^monitor2024]

## What the article reports

- Citing the Times, it reports **more than 60% fewer traffic violations issued between August and March compared
  with the same period the previous year**, coinciding with increased motor-vehicle crashes. The surrounding chronology
  places this after July 2023, but the sentence does not explicitly year-label the August–March interval or quantify crashes.
- Platkin said an investigation was already underway. Former U.S. Attorney Preet Bharara would serve as special counsel
  with the authority of Platkin's office; Platkin retained final decision-making authority. This is an announced inquiry,
  not a finding about responsibility or motive.
- The article says the July 2023 report found Black and Latino motorists more likely to be stopped, searched and asked
  to exit vehicles during **2009–2021**, while searches were less likely to yield evidence. It provides no effect sizes
  or adjustment methods. It also recounts a decade-long consent decree beginning in 1999 and the NAACP's 2024 call
  for new agency leadership.[^monitor2024]

## Relationship to existing coverage and limits

The [existing enforcement Finding](/findings/safety/new-jersey-enforcement-crash-trends.md) already captures
Times-derived accounts of an 81% citation decline and a 27% highway-crash increase. This article adds an explicitly
attributed investigation announcement and a different period-specific enforcement summary, not independent replication.
Its more-than-60% August–March figure must not replace the earlier August-only 81% comparison or be treated as
resolving another excerpt's conflicting eight-month description.

Neither the Times article, underlying traffic records nor racial-disparity report was independently reviewed here.
No cyclist-specific outcome, exposure denominator, controlled crash comparison or completed investigation result is
supplied. The article does not establish that lower enforcement caused crashes, nor quantify policing disparities
independently of the cited report.[^monitor2024]
