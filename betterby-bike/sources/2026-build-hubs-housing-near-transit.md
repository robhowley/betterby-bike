# A Few Legal Tweaks Could Unlock A Mother Lode of Housing Near Transit

Kea Wilson, Streetsblog USA, January 28, 2026. [Original article](https://usa.streetsblog.org/2026/01/28/a-few-legal-tweaks-could-unlock-a-mother-lode-of-housing-near-transit).
The complete substantive article on PDF pages 1–5 was read; later pages contain site navigation and promotions.

## Reporting and proposal

The article covers the proposed Build Housing, Unlock Benefits and Services (Build HUBS) Act, which
would change access to low-cost TIFIA and RRIF financing for transit-oriented development. Wilson
reports that, despite eligibility being expanded more than a decade earlier, the programs had produced
only one transit-oriented building project, a 2024 library in Mount Vernon, Washington, rather than
housing. This is the article's historical account, not an independently reviewed program inventory.[^wilson2026hubs]

Supporters attribute limited delivery to environmental reviews before land acquisition, investment-grade
credit requirements and hurdles in regional transportation planning. The article presents bipartisan
sponsors' arguments, including Senators Lisa Blunt Rochester and John Curtis, and comments from
Alecia Hill of LOCUS, a developer/investor coalition. It does not present opposing testimony or an
independent assessment of the proposed changes.[^wilson2026hubs]

Reported reforms include clarifying eligible development within a half-mile of bus rapid transit or
multimodal paths, alongside traditional bus and rail stops; involving HUD in administration; better
rates for projects providing more affordable units; and delegated lending with federal oversight.
Proponents say the changes could unlock up to **100,000 new housing units**, but the article supplies
no estimation method, delivery horizon or uncertainty range. This is a prospective advocacy claim,
not observed construction or a reviewed model result.[^wilson2026hubs]

## Limits and relationship to existing coverage

This is policy reporting, not an intervention evaluation. Bill text, primary financing records and
supporters' numerical estimates were not independently reviewed. The article does not establish
enactment, current eligibility, added ridership, cycling uptake, displacement effects or lower housing
costs. Wilson's expected cycle of stronger transit, local economic activity and walkable development
is an interpretation, not a measured result.

The [access synthesis](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#housing-finance-proposals-are-not-delivered-access)
uses this account to add financing constraints to the distinction between proximity and usable access.

without independently confirming their benefits. No new Finding is extracted: the useful contribution
