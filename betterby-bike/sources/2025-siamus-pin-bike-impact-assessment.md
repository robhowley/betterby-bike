# SIAMUS Deliverable C.2: Impact assessment models

## Publication and access

SIAMUS project, FB Innovation srls; dated Corato, 13 August 2025. The project sheet names Angelo
Domenico Capogna as project manager and four FB Innovation participants, rather than a conventional
author list.
[Original report](https://zenodo.org/records/16912224/files/Deliverable%20C.2%20-%20Impact%20assessment%20models.pdf) ·
[Supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/siamus-how-pin-bike-is-transforming-urban-mobility-with-data-incentives-and-gamification.pdf).

## Survey and setting

Pin Bike combines trip certification, incentives and gamification. This project-authored evaluation
invited approximately 7,000 users in Bari, Bergamo, Bologna, Lecce, Pescara, Sassuolo, Florence, Turin
and Trento through the app and received 517 responses, mainly from Bari, Bologna and Florence.
Seven cities' projects had ended; Florence and Bologna remained active. Respondents averaged 47 years
old; about 52% were male and 35% female, with other/undefined categories in the gender chart.[^siamus2025]

Questions combine recalled pre-program travel, reported use, motivations, infrastructure assessments,
shopping and hypothetical future behavior. Demographics were retrieved through app IDs. The report
calls this mixed stated/revealed preference evidence, but the extracted outcomes are questionnaire
responses, not demonstrated sensor-based before/after trip measurements. It mentions historical
questionnaires without clearly separating their contribution to the reported results.[^siamus2025]

## Results and interpretations

- About 43% reported cycling before the initiative and 25% reported prior car use. Chart 10 shows
  29% using Pin Bike daily and 45% at least four days weekly, summarized in the prose as approximately
  75% frequent cycling. These are respondent reports, not a citywide mode share or measured increase.
- Reported modal-choice motivations shifted toward environmental sustainability (17% to 31%) and
  economic benefit (16% to 27%). Incentives/rewards were cited by 33% as a participation driver.
  Recalled and current commute-time distributions were described as almost identical, not a travel-time gain.
- Ease, convenience and safety together averaged 4.1/5, while users also reported dirty or obstructed
  cycle infrastructure. This combined perception rating is not a collision-risk measure.
- Likelihood of continuing cycling without incentives averaged 4.2/5; project ratings were about 4.7/5,
  and 98% said they would participate again. These are intentions and satisfaction, not observed retention.
- For shopping while cycling, 43% reported purchasing only when necessary and 26% cycling specifically
  to shop. No merchant revenue or incremental local spending was measured.[^siamus2025]

The authors interpret these responses as significant, lasting behavioral change and environmental,
social and economic benefits. The descriptive survey does not identify those causal effects.

## Limitations

517 of roughly 7,000 invited users is about 7.4% (calculated from the report). Exceeding its stated
365-person sample-size target does not establish representativeness in a voluntary app survey.
Nonresponse, selection into Pin Bike, recall and favorable-response biases remain possible. City
weights, fieldwork dates, item-specific denominators, uncertainty intervals and controlled comparisons
are not supplied. Different projects and active/ended statuses are pooled; incentives and gamification
cannot be separated from infrastructure, existing cycling habits or other influences.

Chart 10's rounded daily/4+ categories sum to 74%, near the prose's approximately 75%; its question
asks about Pin Bike service use, which is narrower than all cycling. The text's assertion that all
users started cycling is not established by this distribution, which includes 1% reporting no use.
Neither the prior-car share nor stated future cycling measures displaced car trips, post-incentive
retention, emissions savings or equitable access.
