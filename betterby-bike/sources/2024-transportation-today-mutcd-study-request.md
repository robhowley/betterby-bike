# Congressional request for a MUTCD study (2024)

Liz Carey, *Transportation Today*, December 5, 2024.
[Captured article](../../raw-sources/news/congressional-democrats-urge-gao-to-do-traffic-control-standard-study-transportation-today.pdf).
Read the full four-page capture; substantive reporting occupies pages 1–2, followed by website material.

## Reporting and relevance

The article reports that Representative Rick Larsen and Senator John Fetterman urged the US Government
Accountability Office to study how practitioners use the Federal Highway Administration's Manual on Uniform
Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD) in multimodal roadway design. It describes the manual as setting standards
for installing and maintaining traffic control devices, including stop signs, bicycle lanes and crosswalks.[^article]

Quoting the lawmakers, the article says their offices had heard from engineers that the manual's structure,
language and surrounding professional culture discouraged departures from its guidelines, even where alternatives
might suit local conditions. The quoted letter acknowledges that the MUTCD permits engineering discretion.
The requested inquiry would examine how the manual informs designs, how much practitioners exercise discretion,
and the facilities, projects and contexts in which they most often depart from its guidance.[^article]

The lawmakers cite more than 35,000 annual US roadway deaths and more than 7,000 pedestrian deaths in 2022,
calling the latter the highest number since 1981. These are quoted motivations for the request, not results
of an analysis linking the manual to fatalities.[^article]

## Limits and relationship to existing evidence

This is reporting on an oversight request, not a completed GAO study, a revised standard or an evaluation
of bicycle-lane safety. The article provides no engineer sample, survey methods, frequency of departures,
project comparisons or causal estimate of the manual's effects. Its account of practitioner reluctance comes
through the lawmakers; it includes no separate engineer interviews or FHWA response. The underlying letter
and any subsequent GAO work were not reviewed.[^article]

The existing [2011 green-pavement interim approval](2011-mutcd-green-pavement-interim-approval.md) documents
historical conditions for a specific treatment. This article adds a later concern about implementation and
discretion, not evidence that those approval conditions caused unsafe designs or independent confirmation
of the earlier operational assessment. No new Finding is extracted because the article supplies a policy
request and attributed concerns rather than a distinct measured result.
