# Bus lanes make busy roads work better

## Publication and reporting basis

Eric Murphy's [February 3, 2026 SSTI article](https://ssti.us/2026/02/03/bus-lanes-make-busy-roads-work-better/)
summarizes [research identified by DOI 10.1016/j.tra.2026.104884](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2026.104884).
The entire three-page PDF was read as extracted text; substantive reporting occupies pp. 1–2 and p. 3
contains website information. The linked study was not reviewed. The article supplies quotations and
qualitative results, not methods, tables or a named study population.[^murphy2026]

## Reported results and recommendations

The article says dedicated bus space improves performance under high demand, describing at least 40%
of road space as a busy-route “sweet spot” and quoting the study's recommendation to allocate approximately
half to buses. It describes a feedback mechanism: slow buses deter ridership, while priority and shorter
waits attract passengers. These are reported research interpretations, not observed mode-shift estimates
in this article. For moderate traffic, it reports that more bicycle space can improve road performance;
it also relays a recommendation for limited, managed bus access to general-purpose lanes at high demand.
No travel-time effect sizes or operational conditions for that access are given.[^murphy2026]

The author advocates proactive allocation rather than allocation based only on present use. A separate
claim that bus lanes improve safety, even without buses present, links to earlier SSTI coverage rather
than presenting new safety measurements.[^murphy2026]

## Limits and coverage reconciliation

The article does not identify the research design, sites, demand thresholds, road-space denominator,
mode-choice assumptions or performance metric. Its 40% and approximately-half formulations should not
be treated as a validated universal design rule. No measured bicycle-lane, collision, reliability or
emissions effect is supplied. Linked safety coverage and other background links remain unreviewed.

Bundle searches found no existing coverage of this article or DOI. The existing
[Baku simulation Source](/sources/2026-bus-lane-implementation-expediency-different-lane-numbers.md)
and [NYC bus-priority report](/sources/2025-how-much-faster-are-we-moving.md) are different publications;
this coverage neither replicates their estimates nor resolves their measurement limits.
