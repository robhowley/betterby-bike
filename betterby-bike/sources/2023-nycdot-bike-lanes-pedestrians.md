# NYC DOT Completes East New York’s Longest Protected Bike Lane Project, Delivering Much-Needed Safety Near Schools

New York City Department of Transportation, September 21, 2023, press release.
[Original release (local PDF)](../../raw-sources/news/2023-nycdot-bike-lanes-pedestrians.pdf).

## Announcement and reporting basis

NYC DOT announced substantial completion of **3.18 miles of parking-protected lanes on Cozine and Wortman avenues**
in East New York, Brooklyn. It described connections to the existing Fountain Avenue protected lane and local
parks, and expected benefits for seven nearby schools. Three schools abut the corridors, according to the release's
subtitle. This is an agency project announcement, not an independent evaluation of outcomes.[^nycdot2023]

The release also reports a connecting two-way protected lane on Louisiana Avenue, standard lanes on Stanley Avenue,
and a painted-pedestrian-space redesign at Dumont and New Lots avenues. Additional improvements near P.S. 325 were
being implemented, including a 14-foot painted sidewalk expansion. DOT describes two years of outreach involving
more than 35 schools and NYCHA resident associations and surveys at 14 locations, plus neighborhood treatments
including more than 60 Leading Pedestrian Intervals. These are implementation accounts, not measured safety
results.[^nycdot2023]

## Safety claims and equity rationale

DOT says protected-lane designs have reduced **deaths and serious injuries by 18.1% for all road users** and by
**39% among senior pedestrians** where installed. It proposes shorter pedestrian crossings and lower vehicle speeds
as mechanisms. Neither percentage is presented as a measured result of the newly completed East New York project.
Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez and Transportation Alternatives organizer Kathy Park Price endorse the project's
safety benefits; these statements are attributed expectations, not separate supporting studies.[^nycdot2023]

DOT identifies East New York as a Tier 1 Priority Investment Area under a formula weighing non-white and low-income
population shares, jobs and population density, and prior investment history. The formula describes project
prioritization, not an evaluation of equitable safety outcomes. The release also reports about 650 protected lane
miles citywide, explicitly including off-street paths, as of this announcement.[^nycdot2023]

## Limits and access

Both PDF pages were read as text, including the concluding advocacy quotation. No underlying safety study is named
or linked in the supplied release. It supplies no study sites, observation periods, casualty counts, comparator,
exposure denominator, uncertainty, adjustment methods or senior age definition for the percentages. Their overlap
with earlier NYC analyses is unknown; they should not be counted as independent confirmation. No project-specific
before/after safety, speed or school-travel results are provided. The PDF's links do not identify the release's own
web URL; the local copy is the publication reviewed.[^nycdot2023]
