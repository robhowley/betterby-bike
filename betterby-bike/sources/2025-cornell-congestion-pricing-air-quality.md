# Congestion pricing improved air quality in NYC and suburbs

David Nutt, Cornell Chronicle, December 8, 2025. Read the complete three-page
[local capture](../../raw-sources/news/congestion-pricing-improved-air-quality-in-nyc-and-suburbs-cornell-chronicle.pdf),
including substantive reporting on pp. 1–2; p. 3 contains newsletter and related-story material.

## Reporting and evidence

The university news article reports a study published that day in *npj Clean Air*, co-led by Timothy Fraser
and Yeonkyeong Gina Park, with senior author Oliver Gao. The
[linked study](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44407-025-00037-2) was not independently reviewed.
Researchers used air-quality, traffic, demographic and meteorological data with statistical modeling.
The article describes 42 metropolitan-area monitors, 518 days and 17,758 observations, without explaining
missing observations or giving the full observation dates, model specification or uncertainty intervals.[^nutt2025]

For January–June 2025, it reports average daily maximum PM2.5 in Manhattan's Congestion Relief Zone
(at or below 60th Street) was 3.05 µg/m³ lower than a projected no-pricing average of 13.8 µg/m³,
a 22% reduction. Reported declines were 1.07 µg/m³ across the five boroughs and 0.70 µg/m³ in the
broader geographic area. Vehicle entries into the zone reportedly fell about 11% during the same period,
when peak passenger/small-commercial tolls were $9.[^nutt2025]

## Interpretation and limitations

Fraser interprets wider-area improvement as evidence against simply relocating pollution, and suggests transit
use or rescheduled deliveries as mechanisms. Gao anticipates public-health benefits and compares the reduction
with reported Stockholm and London figures. These are researcher interpretations, not measured behavior,
health outcomes or controlled cross-city comparisons presented in this article. Regional averages cannot rule
out increases at individual locations. The article's annual-average EPA/WHO exposure benchmarks are not directly
comparable with its daily-maximum outcome over six months.[^nutt2025]

This is institutional secondary coverage, not another independent study. Opening claims about crashes, noise,
delays and revenue are background attributed to other reporting, not outcomes demonstrated by the air-quality
analysis. Existing NYC emergency-response and London emissions-model sources concern different outcomes;
this article does not estimate a cycling-infrastructure effect.
