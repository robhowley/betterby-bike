# Who's Hurting Whom?

Undated Streetsblog chart captured from `nyc.streetsblog.org`. [Supplied chart (local PDF)](../../raw-sources/news/whos-hurting-whom-pedestrian-injuries.pdf).
The screenshot credits Streetsblog and NYPD and displays a Datawrapper footer, but no author, publication date,
article URL or retrievable data-link target. The year 2024 describes the data, not a known publication date.[^chart]

## What the chart reports

The chart labels **9,647** “All pedestrian injuries” and **9,610** “Car/truck,” with a two-category legend for
car/truck and e-bike. Subtraction implies **37 e-bike injuries**, but no numerical e-bike label is visible.
The caption says the chart is based on NYPD data for 2024 and argues that cars and trucks are the clear danger to
pedestrians because they are heavier and faster than e-bikes. The counts are secondary reporting; the explanation
about weight and speed is Streetsblog's interpretation, not a tested mechanism in the supplied material.[^chart]

## Access and limitations

The entire one-page image, including caption, legend, labels and footer, was read visually after text extraction
returned no substantive text. No surrounding article or primary NYPD dataset was reviewed. NYC is the apparent
setting from NYPD and the publisher's domain, but geographic coverage is not explicitly defined in the chart.

No extraction method, injury-severity definition, vehicle-coding rules, exposure denominator or treatment of other
vehicle types is supplied. The chart's “all” label cannot establish complete coverage of pedestrian injuries.
These are reported injury counts, not fatalities, per-trip risks, estimates of fault or measured bike-lane effects.
The e-bike slice is small but nonzero; neither its size nor the car/truck total establishes risk per encounter.[^chart]
