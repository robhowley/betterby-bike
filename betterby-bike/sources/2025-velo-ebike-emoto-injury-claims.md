# Velo's e-bike and e-moto injury commentary

## Publication and reporting basis

Alvin Holbrook's Velo article, updated December 4, 2025, combines secondary injury statistics,
PeopleForBikes advocacy, vehicle descriptions and recommendations for US regulation. The complete
13-page extracted PDF was read, including photo captions; substantive coverage occupies pp. 2–11.
There are no research methods, statistical tables or appendices. Underlying studies, laws and agency
records were not independently reviewed.[^velo2025]

## Claims and classification argument

The article reports a **1,800% increase in California e-bike incidents from 2018 to 2023**, a national
49-fold increase in head trauma from 2017 to 2022, and 23 e-bike riders among 30 NYC bicycle deaths
in 2023. It also describes a 2024 Marin County study as finding nearly one death per eight e-bike trauma
patients transported to an emergency room by ambulance and a death rate 37 times that for pedal-bicycle
crashes. The capture does not supply underlying case counts, uncertainty, vehicle specifications or
travel exposure for these comparisons. The selected ambulance/trauma population cannot stand for all
riders or trips.[^velo2025]

Holbrook argues that high-powered electric motorcycles and mopeds marketed as e-bikes account for the
most dangerous incidents. He contrasts pedal-assist and throttle classes with machines whose speed
limiters can be disabled and whose motors exceed ordinary e-bike specifications. PeopleForBikes counsel
Matt Moore argues that a vehicle exceeding limits in any mode is not an electric bicycle. These are
classification and marketing arguments, not a vehicle-level audit of the injury statistics. The article
provides no class-specific case breakdown proving its headline attribution.[^velo2025]

## Policy positions and limits

The author advocates federal enforcement, truthful advertising and consistent state definitions rather
than sweeping e-bike bans. These are proposed responses, not evaluated safety measures. The account's
legal summaries reflect its publication date and are not independently checked legal guidance.

Battery-fire totals are reported alongside crash injuries, but have different mechanisms and denominators;
no battery provenance or vehicle-class breakdown establishes that e-motos caused those fires. The article
also advocates e-bikes for affordable mobility and cites climate benefits. Its million-barrels-per-day oil
claim overlaps the broader electric-two-wheeler estimate already discussed in the
[2023 oil-demand coverage](/sources/2023-electric-two-wheelers-oil-demand.md), not independent evidence
specific to pedal-assist bicycles.[^velo2025]

The distinct contribution is the tension between useful device classification and unsupported allocation
of aggregate injury totals. The [classification Finding](/findings/safety/velo-ebike-emoto-attribution-limits.md)
feeds the [injury-risk synthesis](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md). Existing
[CPSC surveillance](/sources/2023-cpsc-micromobility-deaths-injuries-hazard-patterns.md) and
[NEISS analysis](/sources/2024-united-states-micromobility-injury-burden-2019-2022.md) are not independent
confirmation of the article's e-moto attribution.
