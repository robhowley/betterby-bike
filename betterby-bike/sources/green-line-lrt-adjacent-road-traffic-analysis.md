# Green Line LRT adjacent-road traffic analysis (conclusion excerpt)

The [supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/green-line-lrt-adjacent-road-traffic-analysis.pdf) contains one image
headed **“Chapter 5: Conclusion.”** The title above is descriptive, not a verified publication title. Authors,
publication date, report title, original URL and printed page number are absent. The excerpt concerns Green Line
light rail transit (LRT) and adjacent roads in the Twin Cities area; it also mentions a PNR location-choice
model task but provides no results for that task.[^greenline]

## Question and reported methods

The traffic task used a before/after, treatment/control quasi-experimental design. The conclusion describes two
difference-in-differences models using what it calls a “negative binomial link function.” One examined annual
average daily traffic (AADT) before and after opening; the other examined changes over three time periods.
Both controlled for road classification, land use and transit supply. The underlying model specification,
sample, treatment boundary, control selection and calendar years are not supplied.[^greenline]

## Results and interpretation

The conclusion reports **18% lower AADT on adjacent road segments**, all else equal. The estimated reduction
weakened from **22% in the first two operating years to 16% in the next two years**. These are reported adjusted
traffic-volume effects, not percentages of travel-time savings or observed citywide driving reductions.[^greenline]

The authors suggest induced land-use development and travel switching from other routes or modes as explanations
for the weakening effect. Their expectation of continued attenuation is explicitly a hypothesis to be tested,
not an observed later result. They also warn that failing to control for road classification, land use and
transit supply could substantially bias estimates.[^greenline]
