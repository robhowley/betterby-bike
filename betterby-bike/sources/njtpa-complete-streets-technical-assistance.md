# Complete Streets Technical Assistance

North Jersey Transportation Planning Authority (NJTPA), undated program webpage with an FY 2026
program section. The eight-page PDF capture includes the program description, current project scopes,
completed-project directory, outreach photo and caption, video heading and footer. The linked local
reports and video were not reviewed. This is the agency's account of assistance, not an evaluation.

## Program and project scope

NJTPA funds assistance delivered through Sustainable Jersey and Rutgers University's Alan M. Voorhees
Transportation Center. Selected municipalities receive free direct technical assistance for a specific
Complete Streets task; **no direct funding to municipalities is provided**. The stated aim is to help
communities develop solutions for all transportation users, including people walking, cycling and using
transit, children, older adults and people with disabilities.[^csta]

The FY 2026 section names four municipalities receiving assistance:

- **Fort Lee:** assess a half-mile of Main Street in the central business district, documenting conditions
  and recommending safety, accessibility and stormwater improvements.
- **Leonia:** assess local north–south roads and other areas affected by George Washington Bridge
  cut-through traffic, with stakeholder engagement and traffic-calming recommendations.
- **Pompton Lakes:** assess Wanaque Avenue and the downtown neighborhood for walking, cycling, safety,
  stormwater resilience and downtown vitality.
- **Ship Bottom:** plan bicycle-route alternatives to Long Beach Boulevard, emphasizing wayfinding
  and signage for visitors, seasonal residents and bicycle commuters.[^csta]

The completed-project directory reports five participating municipalities in 2024–2025, seven in
2022–2023, eight in 2020–2021 and nine in 2018–2019. These are cohort participation counts, not a count
of distinct municipalities across all years: places recur. Listed outputs include walkable-community
workshops, policies, conceptual renderings, bicycle corridor/network plans and demonstration work.
The page specifically says the 2024–2025 Belleville and Verona projects included temporary
demonstration projects.[^csta]

## Interpretation and limits

The program describes a route from policy ambitions to local assessments and plans without giving
municipalities direct grants. Its project list does not establish that all recommendations were built,
how participants were selected, or how much assistance cost. Safety, accessibility, resilience and
vitality in current scopes are objectives, not measured improvements. There are no before/after crash,
travel, access or economic outcomes, comparison groups or causal estimates in this capture.

No new empirical Finding is extracted. [Street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#eligible-funding-is-not-cycling-expenditure-or-delivered-benefits)
uses this program to distinguish planning assistance from municipal funding and delivered benefits.
The separate [NJTPA trip-potential analysis](2023-njtpa-trip-potential-analysis.md) addresses spatial
planning scores, not an evaluation of this assistance program.
