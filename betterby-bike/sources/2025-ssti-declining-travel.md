# Driving trips are dropping, especially among young people

Chris McCahill, State Smart Transportation Initiative, September 9, 2025.
[Original article](https://ssti.us/2025/09/09/driving-trips-are-dropping-especially-among-young-people/).

## Reporting basis

The article combines SSTI's FHWA/FRED national vehicle-mile chart with secondary coverage of
[Fung et al.'s UCLA report](https://doi.org/10.7922/G2W0949G), using National Household Travel Survey
(NHTS) data from 2001–2022. Read the full four-page capture, including both charts and captions on
p. 2; pp. 3–4 are credits and site material. The underlying report and datasets were not reviewed.

## Reported results

National total vehicle miles traveled (VMT) in 2024 reportedly exceeded 2019 by 0.5%, while per-capita
VMT was 2.3% lower and nearly 5% below 2004. The chart uses separate axes for total VMT in trillions
and per-capita VMT in thousands, so these are not interchangeable growth measures.[^ssti2025]

For young adults, the article reports daily personal mileage down 19% in 2001–2017 versus 10% for
older adults, then average daily trips down nearly 50% and personal mileage down 41% in 2017–2022.
The accompanying chart identifies youth as ages 15–29 and its comparison group as ages 30–59;
its vertical axis is daily personal miles traveled (PMT) per capita. It does not represent all older ages.
The article also claims fewer but longer car trips kept youth VMT roughly unchanged since 2017,
without specifying that measure's denominator or reconciling it with the PMT decline.[^ssti2025]

## Interpretation and limits

The quoted researchers suggest online shopping and digital recreation may replace outings. McCahill
interprets longer remaining trips through the idea of stable daily travel time and recommends planning
for slow traffic growth rather than a return to pre-2000 trends. These are explanations and planning
recommendations, not evaluated causal effects, measured peak-period relief or demonstrated cycling gains.
Sample sizes, survey comparability, uncertainty definitions and VMT denominators are absent; chart
error bars are visible but their meaning is not explained in the capture.

[Realtor.com's housing coverage](2026-realtor-transit-housing-driving.md) cites the same UCLA report
and repeats the national per-capita figures, not independent confirmation. SSTI adds the PMT chart,
post-2017 mileage comparison and explicit longer-car-trip caveat. Realtor.com's description of the
19% decline as vehicle travel should not erase SSTI's PMT distinction. The
[Streetsblog youth account](2024-streetsblog-gen-z-driving.md) reports Wang's different 2009–2017
analysis and age group; those estimates should not be pooled with Fung et al.'s.
