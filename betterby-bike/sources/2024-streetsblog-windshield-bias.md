# Car Dependency is a Public Health Threat

## Publication and coverage

Kea Wilson, Streetsblog USA, September 9, 2024. Read the full eight-page capture, including
substantive reporting on pp. 1–5 and the survey graphic and caption on p. 3.
The [original article](https://usa.streetsblog.org/2024/09/09/driving-is-a-public-health-threat-but-americans-are-too-car-brained-to-see-it)
reports Tara Goddard's US replication of a 2023 British study associated with Ian Walker and
includes an interview with Goddard. The linked paper,
[Windshield Bias, Car Brain, Motornormativity: Different Names, Same Obscured Public Health Hazard](https://findingspress.org/article/122974-windshield-bias-car-brain-motornormativity-different-names-same-obscured-public-health-hazard),
was identified from the PDF link but not reviewed.

## Reporting and evidence

The article describes “motonormativity” as cultural bias favoring car travel. It compares agreement
with similarly worded statements about driving and non-driving harms. Reportedly, 75% of US
respondents accepted possible serious injury as a natural part of driving, versus 34% for working;
the corresponding British figures were 61% and 31%. For keeping fumes away from populated areas,
94% of US and 75% of British respondents endorsed smokers' responsibility, versus approximately
one-fifth in either country for drivers' exhaust.[^article]

The findings were not uniformly fatalistic about driving: 55% of US respondents disagreed that
society should simply accept driving's consequences because expecting less driving is pointless,
versus 31% in Britain. For the alcohol version, disagreement was 70% and 52%, respectively.
The graphic compares five paired questions, including theft responsibility and delivery-driver
rule-bending; its bars are differences in percent agreement, not shares of people classified as
biased. Its caption says private-property and emissions comparisons use absolute difference
scores to align their direction with the other questions.[^article]

Goddard interprets workplace protections and anti-smoking campaigns as examples of harms becoming
less accepted, and argues that US built environments make driving seem compulsory. She recommends
examining biases in planning, engineering and investment decisions. These are interview explanations
and proposals, not tested mechanisms or evaluated policy interventions. The article's separate WHO
comparison of 2021 road deaths provides context, not evidence that attitudes caused national death-rate
differences.[^article]

## Limits and reconciliation

The article does not supply survey sample sizes, recruitment, fieldwork dates, weighting, assignment
of question versions or uncertainty estimates. National representativeness and exact cross-country
comparability cannot be assessed from this capture. These are reported attitudes, not observed driving,
measured support for a specific redesign, or evidence that changing attitudes improves safety.

No existing bundle account of this survey was found. It complements, rather than replicates,
[Green Lane resident surveys](2014-lessons-green-lanes.md), which concern particular facilities and
local support. The linked British reporting and primary studies were not independently reviewed.
