# New Jersey Target Zero: Draft Safer People Actions

June 12, 2025 memorandum from <targetzero@rutgers.edu> to New Jersey Target Zero Action Plan
Working Group members. [Original memorandum, supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/news/2025-06-12-safer-people-pdf.pdf).
Despite its news-directory location, this is a 57-page planning draft, not a news article.

## Purpose and evidence basis

The draft collects 27 proposed actions for the state's Comprehensive Safety Action Plan, informed by
input-form submissions, working-group breakout discussions and emailed comments. Action tables name
possible agencies, action types and sometimes timeframes or evaluation measures; accompanying notes
summarize selected research and examples. It is explicitly incomplete and unprioritized, with final
inclusion left to Commission agency members after consultation. It supplies no original intervention
outcome evaluation, systematic-review method or evidence that the proposals were adopted.[^draft]

## Cycling and street-design proposals

- **Crossing design:** prioritize high-risk/high-injury locations, convenient crossings, slower traffic,
  refuge islands and leading pedestrian intervals; train engineers and address historic investment
  inequities rather than treating crossing behavior solely as an individual responsibility (pp. 6–8).
- **Physical daylighting:** use curb extensions, planters or posts to prevent corner parking; pilot
  quick-builds at persistent illegal-parking locations and plan subsequent permanent construction.
  Proposed evaluation includes crash rate, severity and driver stopping for pedestrians (pp. 17–18).
- **Cycling laws:** enact a dooring law explicitly covering pedestrians and bicycles on sidewalks,
  shoulders and bicycle lanes (p. 27); consider stop-as-yield and red-as-stop rules (pp. 39–40);
  adopt a vulnerable-road-user law providing enhanced legal recourse (pp. 42–43).
- **Education and investigation:** update commercial and general driver instruction, teach new laws
  at renewal, expand age-appropriate pedestrian/cyclist education, and investigate serious crashes
  for preventable roadway conditions as well as individual conduct (pp. 9–10, 15–16, 23–26, 32–34).
- **Enforcement:** expand dangerous-driving enforcement and research/expand bicycle patrols, while
  retaining concerns about discriminatory enforcement and police treatment of cyclists (pp. 28–29, 44–46).

These are proposals and rationales, not measured New Jersey cycling or safety benefits.[^draft]

## Mixed evidence and limits

The daylighting notes repeat [NYC DOT's already represented study](2025-nyc-daylighting-street-safety.md):
physical treatments had favorable pedestrian results while signs alone lacked clear benefits. They add
no independent confirmation. The draft's treatment rankings and warning about universal daylighting
should not override that study's design and attribution limits, retained in the
[existing injury finding](/findings/safety/nyc-daylighting-before-after-injuries.md).

For Idaho Stops, the draft relays a [Connecticut DOT review](https://portal.ct.gov/dot/-/media/dot/programs/vision-zero/assets/idaho-stop-study.pdf):
a 14.5% first-year injury decline in Idaho, but no statistically significant safety benefit or demonstrated
long-term increase in risk. It also retains judgment demands at complex intersections, public-education
needs and the review's call for more crash data. This is secondary reporting, not a reviewed primary
study or proof of either effectiveness or no harm. Stop-as-yield and red-as-stop are distinct permissions;
the draft's compressed wording must not be read as allowing passage through red lights without stopping.

The wider package includes impaired/distracted-driving measures, seatbelts, designated drivers,
telematics and Safe System training. It explicitly acknowledges weak evidence that designated-driver
incentives reduce crashes, cannabis-testing limitations, and telematics privacy/adoption barriers.
Many cost, priority and evaluation fields remain blank. Legal-status assertions describe the draft's
2025 account and were not independently checked; expected benefits are not observed results.[^draft]

## Reading scope and synthesis

Read the substantive extracted text and action tables across all 57 pages. Linked studies, laws and
programs were not independently reviewed for this ingestion. No new Finding: the useful addition is
an attributed draft policy package; its daylighting results already have a home and its other research
summaries do not supply enough methods for a stronger standalone result.

[Street-redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) distinguishes proposed implementation
and evaluation from demonstrated effects. The [January 2026 policy memo](2026-renovating-new-jersey-built-environment.md)
is separate later commentary about revising the Target Zero plan, not evidence of this draft's adoption.
