# Berkeley OKs plan that penalizes residents for owning multiple cars

Susana Guerrero, SFGATE, June 11, 2026.
[Original article](https://www.sfgate.com/bayarea/article/berkeley-parking-price-increases-22268791.php) ·
[Captured PDF](../../raw-sources/news/berkeley-oks-plan-that-penalizes-residents-for-owning-multiple-cars.pdf).

## Reporting basis

The article covers an 8–1 Berkeley City Council vote on parking charges, citing transportation planner Elliott
Schwimmer, interim Public Works director Wahid Amiri's May 19 letter, councilmembers and Berkeleyside reporting.
It says a second and final ordinance vote was still scheduled later that month. This is a dated policy report,
not an evaluation or confirmation of subsequent implementation.[^sfgate2026]

## Main reporting

- Paid parking would end at 8 p.m. instead of 6 p.m., include Sundays and expand through 30 new pay stations
  in high-demand commercial areas, including Hopkins Street, Fifth Street and parts of San Pablo Avenue.
- Residential permits would change from $85 per vehicle to $100 for the first car, $125 for the second and
  $150 for the third, effective May 1, 2027, for 2028 permits. These are permit fees, not a general tax on owning cars.
- Parking tickets would rise from $43 to $64 and street-sweeping fines from $49 to $73. The combined package
  was projected to increase annual revenue by $5.3 million, partly addressing garage-related debt and other costs.
- Cashless hardware was planned over three to five years. Schwimmer projected $95,000 annual savings from
  removing cash handling and described credit-card payment and eventual reloadable Clipper-card acceptance
  for people without credit cards or smartphones. That alternative was prospective, not demonstrated access.
- Officials argued evening/Sunday charges and additional meters would improve turnover and reduce cruising,
  congestion and emissions. Supporting councilmember Rashi Kesarwani called the change necessary; dissenting
  member Ben Bartlett called enforcement-based revenue regressive and warned of harm to small businesses.
  Neither position is supported here by measured post-change outcomes.[^sfgate2026]

## Limits and reconciliation

Read the full eight-page PDF as extracted text; substantive reporting appears on pp. 2–7. No primary ordinance,
meeting record, financial model or cited Berkeleyside article was reviewed. General changes were expected
sometime after July 1, without a specific launch date, and a warning-citation pilot was planned. The separate
permit and hardware timetables must not be collapsed into that initial rollout. Current rules remain unverified.

Existing [car-cost coverage](2026-moneygeek-car-ownership-cost.md) concerns standardized new-vehicle ownership
expenses; [parking/housing evidence](2025-vtpi-parking-housing-affordability.md) concerns parking supply and
housing-cost scenarios. Neither already represents this Berkeley fee proposal, and the proposal does not
confirm either source's estimated savings. There are no measured changes in car ownership, cycling, parking
availability, business revenue or household burden to extract as a new outcome Finding.
