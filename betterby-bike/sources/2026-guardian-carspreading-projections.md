# Carspreading could lead to extra 400 crash deaths a year by 2040

Ajit Niranjan, The Guardian, June 24, 2026; corrected June 25, 2026.

## Reporting basis

The article covers [Transport & Environment's vehicle-size analysis](https://www.transportenvironment.org/articles/ever-bigger-car-size-at-a-crossroads),
contrasting continued European car growth through 2040 with a scenario returning average sizes to
2015 levels. It quotes T&E analyst Lucien Mathieu and independent researchers Brian Caulfield and
Hannah Budnitz. The report and linked research were not independently reviewed.[^niranjan2026]

## Main reporting

- New cars sold in Europe reportedly grew an average 1.2 cm longer, 0.5 cm taller and 0.5 cm wider
  each year since 2000. The graphic credits Inovev, EEA and Dataforce and excludes vans.
- Continued growth versus right-sizing reportedly produces **400 additional vulnerable-road-user
  deaths annually by 2040, including 79 children**. The correction identifies 2,600 as cumulative
  deaths, not an annual total. These are modeled differences, not observed deaths or cyclist-only estimates.
- The projections use Belgian research reporting 27% higher vulnerable-road-user death risk per
  10 cm higher bonnet and US research reporting an 81% increase for children. The article does not
  supply sufficient methods to assess adjustment, risk denominators or transfer to future European fleets.
- Continued growth reportedly removes 8.5–14% of cities' on-street parking by 2040, with about
  100,000 spaces each in London and Berlin. Budnitz questions the implicit gradual remarking of bays:
  larger cars may instead occupy two spaces or encroach on other users' space.
- Additional energy requirements are reported as 100 million barrels of oil imports and 22.5 TWh
  of electricity, without a clear annual/cumulative accounting period. Air-pollution and climate-related
  deaths are excluded. Budnitz calls the projections probably conservative because weight is omitted;
  this is expert interpretation, not a quantified sensitivity analysis.
- Authors recommend limits on bonnet height and width, size-discouraging taxes and standards accounting
  for drivers' visibility of young children. These are proposals, not evaluated policy effects.[^niranjan2026]

## Coverage and limitations

Read all three PDF pages, including the correction, and visually inspected the p. 2 graphic. Its labels
and axes survive but the plotted lines are absent; no chart endpoints were extracted. The article omits
uncertainty intervals, baseline death totals, fleet-turnover assumptions and detailed geographic coverage
of the mortality model. The supplied PDF is the reviewed publication; its original article URL is not
embedded. Links to the T&E report and the Belgian and US inputs are present, but their contents remain
unreviewed. General vehicle-hazard claims overlap existing research summaries; this scenario is not an
independent crash study or an estimate of cycling-infrastructure benefits.
