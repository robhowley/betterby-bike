# Delivery Workers Are the Safest Cyclists On the Road, Study Finds

Sophia Lebowitz, Streetsblog NYC, January 29, 2026.
[Original article](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/2026/01/29/delivery-workers-are-the-safest-cyclists-on-the-road-study-finds).

## Reporting and evidence

The article covers a Hunter College observational study of more than 1,700 micromobility riders at
155 NYC intersections over three weeks in late 2025, observed by 56 researchers. Vehicles included
bikes, e-bikes, mopeds and scooters; approximately 40% were delivery workers. The capture includes an
observation-site map and Table 2, plus interviews with researcher Mike Owen Benediktsson and Workers'
Justice Project director Ligia Guallpa. The [linked study](https://nyc.streetsblog.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2026/01/MICROMIBILITY-STUDY.pdf)
was not independently reviewed.[^article]

Delivery workers reportedly waited for green more often than recreational/commuter riders. The article
also reports less sidewalk riding and helmet non-use among workers, without numerical group comparisons
for those two outcomes. Its reproduced table associates protected lanes with lower sidewalk use but
shows mixed patterns for other behaviors, not uniformly better compliance or lower speeds.[^article]

Benediktsson interprets professional experience, livelihood concerns and feeling protected as possible
explanations. Guallpa advocates more lanes, better roads, parking and delivery zones. These are attributed
explanations and recommendations, not measured mechanisms or evaluated interventions. The reporting places
them against the Adams administration's 2025 e-bike speed-limit and criminal-summons policies.[^article]

## Limitations and distinct context

The headline's “safest” claim exceeds the reported outcomes: observations do not measure collisions,
injuries or exposure-adjusted risk. Selection methods, observation times, behavior-specific denominators,
classification reliability, adjustment and uncertainty are not supplied. Table 2's infrastructure counts
sum to 1,662, less than the article's overall “more than 1,700”; the reason is not explained.
The article's correction notice specifies late 2025 and recreational/commuter riders.[^article]

A separate MIT planning student's study reportedly found 74% motorized micromobility at five protected-lane
locations. That is a different sample, not the Hunter delivery-worker share or a citywide estimate.
The article also says DOT sensors do not distinguish vehicle types. Neither primary study was read.
All eight capture pages were read; substantive coverage ends on p. 5.[^article]
