# Boston Tries Out ‘Better Barriers’ to Sub For Battered Bollards

## Publication and reporting basis

Christian MilNeil's September 30, 2025 Streetsblog Massachusetts article reports Boston's trial of
alternatives to flexible-post bollards. The [captured article](../../raw-sources/news/boston-tries-out-better-barriers-to-sub-for-battered-bollards-streetsblog-massachusetts.pdf)
was read in full as extracted text, including substantive reporting and captions on PDF pp. 1–5.
Evidence consists of a phone interview with Chief of Streets Jascha Franklin-Hodge, Peter Cheung's
installation account and photograph caption, city plans and manufacturer descriptions, not a comparative
evaluation. The exact original article URL was not recovered from the capture text.[^milneil2025]

## Installed trial and proposed changes

- Workers installed Zicla Zebra barriers on Arlington Street in Back Bay during the preceding week.
  These elongated molded-plastic domes with reflective stripes were bolted into the buffer formerly
  occupied by flexposts. Franklin-Hodge said the constrained site would make concrete a tighter fit;
  expansion elsewhere depended on monitoring performance and feedback.
- Boston planned cast-in-place concrete islands, including along the two-way Boylston Street bikeway
  between Tremont and Charles streets later that fall. Other planned uses included keeping cars away
  from crosswalks and adding median islands; Euclid, Armington and McBride streets appeared in repair plans.
- Franklin-Hodge described dowels, forms and poured concrete without excavation or new curbs, claiming
  installation could take a day rather than a multi-year project. He contrasted this with difficult
  construction of earlier Tremont Street islands. This is an official's construction-method account,
  not measured comparative delivery time or cost.
- The city sought greater protection and durability while retaining some emergency-vehicle flexibility,
  and intended to move away from flexposts in most circumstances. The manufacturer claimed visibility
  and impact resistance for Zebras; neither claim was independently tested in this reporting.[^milneil2025]

## Limits and coverage reconciliation

No before/after crashes, vehicle incursions, maintenance costs, winter performance, emergency-response
times or rider-comfort measures are supplied. Planned concrete installations are not confirmed completed
works, and the captioned Boylston rendering depicts a proposal rather than an observed outcome. The embedded
video and linked municipal documents were not reviewed.[^milneil2025]
