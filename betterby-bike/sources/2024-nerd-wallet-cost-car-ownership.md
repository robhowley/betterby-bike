# What Is the Total Cost of Owning a Car?

NerdWallet, updated June 14, 2024; supplied PDF captured August 1, 2024. Byline names Shannon Bradley;
author biographies also list Whitney Vandiver and Elizabeth Renter. Edited by Julie Myhre-Nunes.
[Original article](https://www.nerdwallet.com/article/loans/auto-loans/total-cost-owning-car).

## Reporting basis

This U.S. consumer-finance explainer combines secondary cost estimates, a monthly expense calculator, NerdWallet's
own inflation index and budgeting advice. Its methodology cites [AAA's Your Driving Costs study](http://newsroom.aaa.com/auto/your-driving-costs/)
for a new vehicle owned for five years and driven 15,000 miles annually, and
[Experian's Q1 2024 State of the Automotive Finance Market report](http://www.experian.com/automotive/auto-credit-webinar-form.html)
for payments. These linked sources were not separately reviewed. The index uses Bureau of Labor Statistics CPI
pricing and Consumer Expenditure Survey data, but the article does not supply detailed weights.[^nerdwallet2024]

## Main evidence

- AAA's reported 2023 new-vehicle ownership estimate is $12,182 annually, or $1,015 monthly, including loan
  interest, depreciation, fuel, insurance, maintenance and fees. This is not an average for all existing cars.
- The prose reports Experian monthly payments of $735 for new cars, $595 for new-car leases and $523 for used
  cars in Q1 2024. Payments alone are not total ownership costs.
- NerdWallet reports 11% annual ownership-cost inflation in May (in this June 2024 update), and a 38% increase
  since April 2019, for an index covering gas, repairs/maintenance, parking, insurance and licensing.
- Advice includes reviewing major costs, refinancing, shopping for insurance and cheaper fuel, and aiming to
  spend less than 15%–20% of take-home pay on vehicle costs if possible. These are recommendations, not measured
  affordability thresholds or evaluated interventions.[^nerdwallet2024]

## Interpretation and limits

The calculator displays $1,256 in monthly expenses using a car payment and running costs, without a separate
depreciation entry. It is not the same measure as AAA's ownership estimate. Defaults conflict with the prose:
$723/$522 new/used payments rather than $735/$523, and a $64 monthly fees field labeled as based on $675 annually,
while the prose gives $762. The source does not reconcile these figures. Depreciation is explicitly described as
value loss rather than a cash payment. Individual costs vary with vehicle, mileage, location, insurance and repairs;
none of these averages measures savings from replacing a trip with cycling or giving up a car.[^nerdwallet2024]
