# Active Transportation and Real Estate: The Next Frontier

Urban Land Institute (ULI), March 2016, Building Healthy Places Initiative. Contributing authors: Rachel MacCleery,
Ed McMahon and Matthew Norris. [Original report (local PDF)](../../raw-sources/research/2016-active-transportation-real-estate.pdf).

## Scope and reporting basis

The report examines how walking and cycling infrastructure relate to development decisions, property values and
commercial activity. It combines a narrative overview of external research with ten development profiles (eight U.S.,
one London and one Singapore) and five infrastructure profiles: Philadelphia's Circuit trails, Copenhagen and London
cycle superhighways, Minneapolis's Midtown Greenway, and bike sharing in Paris, Montreal and Hangzhou. Projects were
identified through ULI contacts, not systematic sampling. Evidence includes developer/property-manager accounts,
agency figures and secondary study summaries; some projects were still planned or under construction.[^report]

## Reported results

- **Property values:** ULI reports a 148% aggregate value increase for nearly 1,800 parcels within 500 feet of the
  Indianapolis Cultural Trail, a $69,139 proximity difference around Pennsylvania's Radnor Trail, and a Montreal
  repeat-sales study associating additional nearby Bixi stations with higher multifamily unit values. These are
  different measures, not interchangeable estimates of a trail premium.
- **Retail:** Salt Lake City's Broadway upgrade coincided with 8.8% sales growth versus 7% citywide despite removal
  of 30% of on-street parking. The package also changed sidewalks and crosswalks. Other examples mix merchant
  perceptions, commercial rents, vacancy changes and spending surveys rather than measuring one common outcome.
- **Development:** developers describe trail access, bike storage and repair facilities as marketing advantages.
  MoZaic reported full occupancy as of 2016 and developer-attributed rent premiums after a publicly and privately
  financed trail connection. Silver Moon Lodge reported over 95% occupancy with 23 car spaces for 154 income-restricted
  apartments. Hassalo on Eighth reported lease-up ahead of schedule; Bici Flats' rent claims were still expectations.
- **Networks and forecasts:** Copenhagen's Farum route recorded 52% more cyclists in 2012–14, alongside reports of new
  bicycle commuting. The completed Copenhagen network's 19% economic return and London's net £76 million health and
  environmental benefit over 30 years were forecasts, not observed returns. Midtown Greenway advocates attributed
  over $750 million of housing development to the corridor, without a counterfactual analysis.[^report]

## Interpretation and limitations

ULI argues that public infrastructure and private development can reinforce each other through better access, amenities
and shared financing. The profiles document concrete arrangements but do not isolate their effects on rent, lease-up,
travel behavior or profitability. Selected success stories and interested participants' accounts cannot establish the
frequency of successful projects, typical returns or the distribution of benefits. Property appreciation is not itself
an affordability gain; the report does not evaluate displacement or whether existing renters benefited.[^report]

Mixed and adverse context remains relevant: Salt Lake City had 18% business-owner opposition; ULI describes Bixi's
2014 bankruptcy despite its popularity and associated property-value gains; and London's earlier, inadequately
separated CS2 route had six cyclist deaths in 2011–15. These do not constitute controlled effect estimates, but should
not disappear behind the report's favorable framing. The new London routes' safety benefits were anticipated.[^report]

Underlying studies were not independently reviewed. Many summaries omit sample sizes, estimation methods, observation
periods or uncertainty. The Indianapolis passage leaves its valuation window unclear. ULI also repeats the Montreal
cycle-track injury comparison already represented by the [primary Source](/sources/2011-montreal-cycle-track-injury-risk.md);
its “2010” label is not the primary paper's 2011 publication year, and the repetition adds no independent evidence.
