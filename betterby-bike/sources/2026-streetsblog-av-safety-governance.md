# Driverless cars and aviation-style safety governance

Marco Conner, Streetsblog USA, June 18, 2026. Conner is policy counsel to Families for Safe Streets
(FSS) and helped develop the framework he advocates. Read the complete captured article, including both
FSS comparison charts (PDF pp. 4–5); substantive text ends on p. 6 and the biography appears on p. 7.

## Argument and proposed safeguards

Conner argues that being safer than average human driving is an inadequate AV approval standard.
FSS's proposed Gain & Guard Rule requires AVs to be at least ten times safer on average across their
approved operational design domains, and never worse than human drivers within any single domain.
These domains specify roads, weather and other permitted operating conditions. The longer-term ambition
is 100 times safer, not a measured outcome or enacted requirement.[^conner]

The proposed aviation-style process moves from simulation to controlled real-world testing and permits
full deployment only after meeting the rule. FSS calls for standardized mandatory reporting, independent
serious-incident investigations, and authority to suspend or revoke deployment. Persistent traffic-law
violations, including blocked crosswalks and passing stopped school buses, would trigger escalating
intervention even without immediate measurable injury.[^conner]

For victims, FSS proposes automatic compensation up to an unspecified threshold without proving fault,
funded by a pooled industry fund with contributions related to company market share. Manufacturers,
parts suppliers, fleet operators, software providers and ride-hailing platforms would participate;
responsible firms would settle cost allocation after initial payment. Claims above the threshold could
still be disputed. These are advocated arrangements, not a description of established AV liability law.[^conner]

## Evidence and limitations

This is an opinion article previewing an FSS position paper and June 24 webinar, not an independent
safety evaluation. It repeats Waymo's claim of roughly eight to twelve times safer performance over
millions of miles, while explicitly noting limited data and the need for independent verification.
It supplies no outcome definition, exact mileage, domain weighting, comparator construction or uncertainty
intervals that would establish the proposed threshold is met, particularly for people walking or cycling.
Neither the Waymo material nor the linked [FSS position paper](https://familiesforsafestreets.org/autonomous-vehicles)
was reviewed.[^conner]

The aviation comparison cites up to 1,000-fold lower passenger-mile fatality rates and contrasts about
40,000 annual roadway deaths with nearly zero commercial passenger-flight deaths. The article does not
provide methods or matching observation periods for those comparisons. Its charts summarize FSS's
regulatory and liability argument, not measured intervention effects or a comprehensive legal review.
The different populations in aggregate roadway and passenger-flight counts cannot establish how much
of the safety difference governance causes. The headline is not a modeled estimate of lives saved.[^conner]
