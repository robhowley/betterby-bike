# NYC's Blue Highways boat-to-bike delivery pilot

Ethan Stark-Miller, amNewYork, December 11, 2025.
[Original article](https://www.amny.com/news/blue-waterways-nyc-ship-goods-brooklyn-manhattan/). Read the complete
captured article, substantive PDF pp. 1–5; later pages contain related articles and site material.

## Reporting and proposal

The article reports NYC DOT's December 11 launch of a freight pilot with the Economic Development Corporation (EDC) and
DutchX. Boats would move cosmetics, fashion, lifestyle and small household items from Brooklyn Marine Terminal in Red
Hook to Pier 79 in Midtown Manhattan. DOT said DutchX would begin with 300–400 parcels per day, followed by pedal-assist
electric-bike deliveries throughout Midtown. This is announced initial throughput, not an observed delivery
series.[^starkmiller2025]

At the launch, DOT Commissioner Ydanis Rodriguez said nearly 90% of goods entering the city travel by truck and argued
that waterborne middle-mile transport plus cargo-bike last-mile delivery would reduce congestion and emissions. EDC
official Anton Fredriksson similarly promoted cleaner, safer and more efficient deliveries. These are attributed
official claims, not results of a pilot evaluation. The article supplies no denominator or method for the truck-share
estimate.[^starkmiller2025]

The report connects the pilot to the October
[Blue Highways Action Plan](https://edc.nyc/sites/default/files/2025-10/NYCEDC-Blue-Highways-Action-Plan_10-31-2025.pdf),
including identification of at least 25 freight opportunity sites and a proposed advisory council in early 2026. It also
describes announced Brooklyn Marine Terminal redevelopment into an all-electric hub and a planned Hunts Point marine
terminal. These are historical plans, not verified completed facilities; the linked plan was not
reviewed.[^starkmiller2025]

## Evidence limits and coverage

This is launch reporting based on agency information and officials' statements, not a study. It reports no achieved
parcel volume, truck trips avoided, travel times, delivery costs, emissions inventory, crash outcomes or comparison
group. The transport chain makes bicycle freight substitution plausible as a proposed mechanism but does not quantify
benefits or establish subsequent operation.

Existing [Curbed cargo-bike coverage](2025-curbed-amazon-cargo-bikes.md) and its
[street-space Finding](../findings/design/nyc-cargo-bike-space-conflicts.md) concern separate Amazon driver interviews
and explicitly limited safety data. This source adds a distinct boat-to-bike pilot, not independent confirmation of
safety or efficiency gains. No new Finding is extracted from an announced program without measured outcomes.
