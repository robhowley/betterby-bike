# Brooklyn cyclist's double summons

## Publication and reporting basis

Kevin Duggan's June 16, 2025 Streetsblog NYC article reports Phaedra Paulson's account of an e-bike
stop in Brooklyn the preceding Wednesday. The [captured publication](../../raw-sources/news/double-whammy-nypd-slaps-brooklyn-mom-with-criminal-summons-190-fine-streetsblog-new-york-.pdf)
contains the article and photo caption on PDF pp. 1–4, followed by related coverage and site material.
All extracted pages were read; p. 5 was also inspected visually because the prose introduces a chart,
but that space is blank in this capture. There are no study methods, tables or appendices.[^duggan2025]

## Reported incident and access costs

Paulson says she stopped at a red light on Dean Street at Fifth Avenue while carrying work materials
from Manhattan, then followed another e-bike through the red when no cars or pedestrians were present.
An officer stopped both riders at Flatbush Avenue. The article reports a criminal summons for the red
light and a separate $190 failure-to-yield ticket. Paulson disputes the pedestrian allegation; an unnamed
police spokesperson said the agency was looking into the case. These are allegations and a rider's
account, not court findings. The photograph shows her with her daughter, but the reported incident
was a work-materials trip and does not establish that the child was present.[^duggan2025]

Paulson describes using a Rad Power e-bike for roughly two years and nearly 2,000 miles, including
school transport for her six-year-old and work trips between boroughs. She owns a car but prefers
cycling. She anticipates losing a workday to summons court, alongside the $190 charge if she does not
contest it. The article documents expected burdens of enforcement on an existing transport choice,
not a paid fine, conviction, measured income loss or an observed switch to driving.[^duggan2025]

## Limits and contribution

Streetsblog calls this its first known case of both summons types during the crackdown it dates to
April 28. That is the outlet's reporting scope, not a verified citywide first or prevalence estimate.
The piece uses strongly critical language about the police campaign; its claims about motivations and
other stops are contextual reporting, not independently reviewed administrative evidence.[^duggan2025]

The text attributes 0.04% of pedestrian injuries in the first three quarters of an unspecified year to
e-bike riders, without counts or definitions. The missing chart and ambiguous period prevent resolving
that figure here. The bundle's [separate 2024 Streetsblog chart](streetsblog-whos-hurting-whom.md)
implies 37 of 9,647 injuries, about 0.38%, with different or unclear coverage. Neither figure should be
silently substituted for the other or treated as independent confirmation, risk per trip or proof of fault.

No new Finding is created from this single disputed incident. It adds original reporting to
[access-barrier synthesis](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#reported-barriers-extend-beyond-having-a-nearby-route),
not evidence of population deterrence, discriminatory enforcement or the campaign's safety effects.
Court records, the underlying injury data and linked coverage were not reviewed.
