# Amtrak is booming: Why more Americans are taking its trains than ever before

## Publication and coverage

Kelsey Vlamis, Business Insider, January 2, 2026, 6:10 AM ET.
[Original article preserved as PDF](../../raw-sources/news/amtrak-is-booming-why-more-americans-are-taking-its-trains-than-ever-business-insider.pdf).
All substantive article text was read (PDF pp. 1–7). This is news reporting with interviews and personal
travel experience, not a study. The original article's web URL was not recoverable from the supplied PDF.

## Reporting

Vlamis reports 34.5 million customer trips and $2.7 billion adjusted ticket revenue for Amtrak FY2025,
which ended in September. The sentence's 10% year-over-year increase follows the revenue figure;
it should not be substituted for a ridership growth rate. These totals extend the existing
[reported-ridership Finding](../findings/networks/amtrak-reported-ridership-growth-2025-2026.md), rather
than independently confirming the later Frontier Group coverage of the same operator's results.[^vlamis]

The article reports $22 billion in direct five-year Amtrak funding under the 2021 infrastructure law and
$5.5 billion in FY2025 capital investment, up 24% year over year. It describes new Acela trains,
long-distance locomotives and Superliner upgrades. NYU's Sarah Kaufman interprets investment as improving
comfort and reliability; University of Delaware rail specialist Allan Zarembski discusses convenience,
cost and the need for public capital support. Amtrak spokesperson Marc Magliari emphasizes driving as
a competitor and the ability to work onboard. These are attributed explanations, not measured drivers
of growth. Political funding uncertainty and persistent long-distance delays on freight-owned tracks
qualify the optimistic account.[^vlamis]

Vlamis's Los Angeles–Chicago Southwest Chief journey cost nearly four times as much as flying and took
about 38 hours longer, but she valued the scenery, communal meals and leisure. This illustrates one
traveler's preference, not typical prices or an affordable commuting alternative. Interviewees distinguish
shorter corridors, where most riders travel, from cross-country experiences. Claims about younger riders,
lower-emission preferences and experiential travel lack quantified demographic or prior-mode data.[^vlamis]

## Limits and reconciliation

No operator dataset, primary evaluation, representative passenger survey or emissions accounting was
reviewed. The article supplies no controlled comparison separating investment, service changes and
traveler preferences. Revenue growth is not passenger growth, and customer trips are not unique people.
Its opening travel anecdote cannot establish conditions on the short routes that dominate patronage.
Repeated FY2025 reporting is one operator evidence stream, not two independent studies.

The [League's bicycle-access benchmark](2026-amtrak-bicycle-friendly-trains.md) addresses carriage and
booking constraints absent here. More rail trips alone establish neither bicycle access gains nor reduced driving.
