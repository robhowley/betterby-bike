# There’s a Big Problem With Your Car’s Tires

## Publication and reporting basis

David Zipper, Slate/Future Tense, November 9, 2023, with a November 13 correction.
The five-page capture contains substantive reporting on pp. 1–4 and a publisher footer on p. 5.
It combines linked research, regulatory documents, an Earthjustice attorney interview and industry
statements with the author's recommendation to drive less. It is not a new toxicology study.

## Reported evidence and regulatory response

The article explains that tires shed particles onto roads and into runoff, including from electric
vehicles. It identifies 6PPD as a tire-life-extending chemical and 6PPD-quinone as its reaction product
associated with acute fish mortality. It links a [2020 Science study](https://www.science.org/doi/10.1126/science.abd6951)
on coho salmon and reports subsequent concern about other fish species. The primary studies were not
reviewed; the article supplies neither exposure concentrations nor species-specific dose-response
comparisons. Its California account notes a historical coincidence between coho disappearance and
6PPD adoption, not a separate causal estimate.[^article]

In August 2023 the Yurok, Port Gamble S’Klallam and Puyallup tribes petitioned the EPA to prohibit
6PPD. Zipper reports that the EPA granted the petition the following November, beginning a process
that could lead to a ban, not announcing an enacted ban. The linked
[EPA decision letter](https://www.epa.gov/system/files/documents/2023-11/pet-001845_tsca-21_petition_6ppd_decision_letter_esigned2023.11.2.pdf)
was not independently reviewed. The article gives no agency decision deadline; Earthjustice attorney
Elizabeth Forsyth expects several years. California had separately required manufacturers to seek
alternatives. The correction identifies the Yurok as based in Northern California, rather than
Washington state.[^article]

The article reports manufacturers searching for replacements while the U.S. Tire Manufacturers
Association warns that a premature prohibition could harm safety and the economy. The association
did not answer Zipper's question about an appropriate elimination timetable. These are attributed
positions, not measured costs or proof that alternatives are safe.[^article]

## Limits and interpretation

The EPA is quoted as having limited data for human-health risk assessment. The article cites possible
human harms and concerns about chemical mixtures but provides no quantified human risk. Its claim
that tire compounds account for more than three-fourths of ocean microplastics cites a Pew article
without reproducing the denominator or methods; that figure is not adopted here as a Finding.
Predictions about tire replacement after a ban, heavier EV tire wear and future regulation are dated
claims, not verified present conditions. The recommendation to substitute transit, trains and bicycles
for driving is the author's policy argument, not a measured intervention benefit.[^article]

The existing [JRC wear review](traffic-related-non-exhaust-particulate-emissions-literature-review.md)
and [London non-exhaust model](2025-eit-non-exhaust-emissions-road-transport.md) address particulate
emissions and their interpretation. This reporting adds aquatic-chemical and regulatory context, not
independent confirmation of their PM estimates or a basis for ranking EVs against combustion vehicles.
No new Finding is extracted from the secondary toxicology account or proposed regulatory outcomes.
See the [street-redesign synthesis](../topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#pollution-source-shares-are-not-measured-redesign-benefits).
