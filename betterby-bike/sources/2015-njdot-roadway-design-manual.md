# NJDOT Roadway Design Manual 2015

New Jersey Department of Transportation. [Supplied manual (local PDF)](../../raw-sources/research/2015-njdot-roadway-design-manual.pdf).
The cover says 2015, but this 447-page file incorporates later revisions: the introduction bears BDC19MR-01,
the contents BDC21MR-01, and PDF metadata records November 2022 creation/modification. Section 15 bears
BDC15MR-01. Do not treat the entire file as an unchanged 2015 edition or verified current guidance.

## Review scope and basis

Reviewed the cover, contents for navigation, introduction §§1.1–1.3 (printed pp. 1-1–1-3), and the extracted
prose of Section 15, Traffic Calming (printed pp. 15-1–15-22; PDF pp. 426–447). Visually checked printed
p. 15-19. Figure-only pages and diagram dimensions were not reviewed; other substantive chapters and the
referenced ITE, AASHTO and other agency publications were not reviewed. This summary is limited to that material.

The manual supplies engineering guidance for the State Highway system. Its introduction distinguishes controlling
design elements requiring approved exceptions from other informational guidance. Section 15 addresses Department
administered or financed projects, gives a proposed-speed-limit criterion of 35 mph or below, discusses Main Streets,
and says FHWA approval is required for traffic-calming devices on National Highway System routes.[^manual]

This is a design manual, not a sampled before/after study. The reviewed chapter gives objectives, design controls,
device descriptions and references, but no study-level samples, comparison groups, crash tables or uncertainty
estimates supporting its effectiveness statements.

## Relevant guidance

The chapter describes physical changes to alignment and roadway geometry as ways to moderate speeds, shorten
pedestrian crossings and make movements more predictable. It covers volume controls, vertical and horizontal speed
controls, and combinations of treatments; bicycle facilities, pedestrian accessibility and emergency-vehicle access
appear as design considerations, not evaluated outcomes.[^manual]

Its advice is treatment-specific: bicycle lanes may calm traffic through lane narrowing or lane removal, but colored
or textured pavement is recommended in combination with other treatments rather than as a measurable speed control.
Mid-block curb extensions are not recommended specifically to slow traffic, while still being considered at crossings.
These are attributed guidance statements, not effect sizes or an independently reviewed null study.[^manual]

Cyclist accommodation is not uniformly continuous separation: curb extensions should avoid bicycle operating space,
but the chapter directs on-street lanes to end before roundabouts and at the beginning of mid-block median-island
tapers, with cyclists merging into traffic. It does not compare the safety or comfort of these arrangements.[^manual]
