# Tired of Cyclists Riding on the Sidewalk? Build More Bike Lanes

Eric Jaffe, CityLab, August 14, 2014.
[Original article][original] · [Local PDF](../../raw-sources/news/2014-citylab-riding-on-sidewalk.pdf).

[original]: https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2014-08-14/tired-of-cyclists-riding-on-the-sidewalk-build-more-bike-lanes

## Reporting and evidence

Responding to NYPD's two-week Operation Safe Cycle enforcement campaign, Jaffe argues for bicycle infrastructure
as a response to sidewalk riding. He recognizes pedestrians' concerns and cites John Kelly's Washington Post
reporting, including a rider's account that fear of motor traffic motivates sidewalk use. That account explains a
possible motivation, not its prevalence among cyclists.[^citylab2014]

The article cites [Washington Post coverage][post] of three D.C. corridors and a PeopleForBikes/America Walks
compilation covering D.C., Denver and New York. Across six selected corridors, it reports sidewalk-riding declines
of 27–81% after bicycle lanes were created, alongside higher bicycle use. Denver's reported bicycle growth was 12%,
Columbus Avenue's 56% and Prospect Park West's 190%. These are secondary before/after comparisons, not six new
studies conducted by CityLab.[^citylab2014]

[post]: http://www.washingtonpost.com/blogs/dr-gridlock/wp/2014/08/07/with-bike-lanes-fewer-riders-on-sidewalk/

## Interpretation and limitations

Jaffe acknowledges the small, advocate-selected sample. He favors infrastructure over enforcement spending and
suggests accepting steeper sidewalk-riding fines where lanes exist. His claim that infrastructure makes the whole
streetscape safer is a policy interpretation: the article supplies no injury outcomes or comparative evaluation
of enforcement and infrastructure.[^citylab2014]

The D.C. prose describes reductions in the **share** of cyclists on sidewalks, but the chart labels reductions in the
**number** of sidewalk bicycles. Those measures are not interchangeable when total cycling rises. Baseline counts,
observation dates, collection methods, controls and uncertainty are absent. The underlying municipal observations,
Washington Post articles and advocacy compilation were not separately reviewed; the supplied PDF does not identify
a primary study report for the six-corridor comparison.

All four PDF pages were read as text and the p. 3 chart was inspected visually. Its unlabeled bar heights were not
used to infer exact D.C. ridership increases. The August 2024 print date and unrelated story inserts are not the
article's publication date or evidence.
