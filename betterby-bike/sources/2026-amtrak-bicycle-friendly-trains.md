# Bicycle-Friendly Trains

## Publication and access

Will Simpson, League of American Bicyclists, on behalf of the Amtrak Bike Task Force, 2026.
[Supplied report](../../raw-sources/research/leagueofamericanbicyclists-amtrakreport-2026-pdf.pdf).
The executive-summary page retains “Amtrak Bike Task Force Report (Draft, v8)”; final publication status
was not independently established. This is a focused text reading of printed pp. 1–20, part of p. 21,
Appendices B–P, part of Q, U and UU, not a full review of the case studies, letters or references.
The original capacity workbook, external publications and live booking system were not reviewed.

## Basis and results

This advocacy benchmark adapts the European Cyclists’ Federation framework to 46 Amtrak lines.
Manual scoring assigns 50 points to bicycle spaces, 40 to ticketing/information and 10 to station staffing,
with bonus/minus adjustments up to 10 points. Capacity comes from an Amtrak workbook supplied September 24,
2025; staffing uses schedules for the week of March 9, 2026. International routes count only U.S. stations.
The author discloses AI assistance interpreting results, not generating scores.[^amtrak2026]

The report identifies 39 lines with carry-on and/or trainside checked bicycle service and seven without
these services; boxed-bike transport does not earn equivalent credit. Cascades (87) and Gold Runner (86)
lead its published scores. Dual-service corridors score higher than single-service lines, but receive a
built-in bonus and benefit from state investment. These are rubric comparisons, not estimated effects
of adding carriage options. Staffing is explicitly an imperfect proxy for usable station-level service:
carry-on can work at unstaffed stops, and some checked-service exceptions exist.[^amtrak2026]

Appendix L reports a July 23, 2026 booking test in which combining a bike-eligible Northeast Regional
outbound with an ineligible Acela return suppressed the bicycle add-on for the whole round trip without
explaining why. This is an observed interface limitation, not a measured rate of failed journeys.
The report recommends clearer station information, booking changes and expanded access. Its Airo example
projects Vermonter capacity increasing from six to ten spaces and a score from 51 to 71, not an observed
ridership or access benefit.[^amtrak2026]

## Qualifications

- Appendix UU flags conflicting Capitol Corridor capacities. The retained Amtrak figure gives 46 points;
  using the corridor operator's unverified figure would give 83. The discrepancy was unresolved July 21, 2026.
- The p. 17 summary says six lines are Good, but p. 16 lists seven scores in the 60–79 band. This mismatch
  remains unresolved. Do not treat narrative distribution statistics as reconciled against the table.
- Page 17 describes the dual-mode gap as both more than 27 points and later 23 points. Group means support
  approximately 27–28 points, not a causal service improvement estimate.
- Line scores mix observation dates and cannot establish current station eligibility, availability for a
  particular bike, equitable access, safety, mode shift or net environmental gains. Equipment projections
  remain distinct from delivered service.
