# Don't Cut Corners

New York City Department of Transportation, August 2016. The cover identifies this 62-page study;
“PowerPoint Presentation” is generic PDF metadata, not the publication title.
[Local PDF](../../raw-sources/research/powerpoint-presentation.pdf) ·
[Original publication](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/left-turn-pedestrian-and-bicycle-crash-study.pdf).

## Scope and methods

The study supports Vision Zero and Local Law 21 of 2014 through three analyses:
citywide NYSDOT records of NYPD-reported crashes in 2010–2014; manual review of 1,105 crash reports
from high-left-turn-injury locations in 2009–2013; and three-year-before versus three-year-after
injury counts for five treatment groups totaling 478 intersections. Treatment data span 1996–2014,
with different years and sample sizes by treatment. Injuries include fatalities; KSI means killed or
severely injured. The selected manual-review sample is not representative of every NYC intersection.[^report]

## Main results

- Left-turning vehicles accounted for 19% of pedestrian/bicyclist KSI, versus 6% for right turns;
  86% of left-turn KSI were pedestrians. These are casualty shares, not risks per turn.
- At 149 protected-lane intersections, combined pedestrian/bicyclist left-turn injuries fell 15%
  and left-turn KSI 53%; total combined KSI fell 20%, while through-movement KSI rose 16%.
- Left-turn restrictions, turn bays, left-turn-only signals and leading pedestrian intervals had
  lower combined left-turn injury counts. Turn bays nevertheless had more cyclist injuries
  (48 to 80 across movements), while combined pedestrian/cyclist total injuries were nearly flat.
- The detailed review found 69% of crashes on receiving streets at least 60 feet wide, and 51%
  when turning from minor approaches. DOT interprets wider radii, visibility and driver workload
  as mechanisms; the report does not isolate their causal contributions.[^report]

## Limits and recommendations

Simple before/after counts lack untreated controls, exposure adjustment and uncertainty intervals.
Selection of high-injury locations, regression to the mean, background trends and combined project
changes limit attribution. Small KSI samples precluded percentage-change analysis for three treatments.
Combined pedestrian/cyclist results must not be presented as cyclist-only benefits. Movement columns
can overlap when a crash involves multiple vehicle movements, so totals need not sum.[^report]

DOT proposed expanded LPIs, protected lanes and a 100-intersection turn-calming pilot. Daylighting,
hardened centerlines, split LPIs and several other toolbox treatments were not evaluated here;
2016 plans and expected benefits are not completed evaluations or current policy. The p. 48 prose
mistakenly labels its 21% total-injury decline as another left-turn result; its table separates the
41% left-turn decline. Page 22's 8,304 intersections, 18% and approximately 39,000 citywide intersections
are internally inconsistent and are not used as an exact concentration estimate.[^report]

All 62 pages' extracted text was read, including treatment tables. Image-only diagram details were
not independently analyzed; underlying datasets and cited studies were not reviewed. The earlier
[2014 Manhattan report](/sources/2014-nyc-protected-bicycle-lanes.md) covers overlapping avenue projects,
with different scope and measures; this is not independent replication of its safety results.
