# 2024 Bicycle Friendly State Report Card: New Jersey

## Publication and access

The League of American Bicyclists, 2024. [Supplied report card](../../raw-sources/research/2024-new-jersey-bicycle-friendly-state-report-card.pdf).
The card identifies the New Jersey Bike + Walk Coalition as the state advocacy group and points to the
[League's state program](https://bikeleague.org/states) and [awards database](https://bikeleague.org/bfa/awards).
Read the entire one-page PDF as extracted text and visually, including all three tables and the methods note.
The separate Report Card Use Guide, database, survey responses and underlying federal data were not reviewed.

## Reporting basis and results

This is an advocacy benchmarking report, not an intervention study. Its methods note says the ranking uses a
survey of more than 100 data points completed by state transportation departments and state bicycling advocates.
It also presents a table labeled “Federal Data on Biking,” without dataset names or observation years.[^njcard2024]

New Jersey ranks **26/50 nationally**, down from the prior card (prior rank unstated), and **9/11 in the Eastern
region**. The actions table credits a Complete Streets law/policy, a safe-passing law of at least three feet and
a statewide bike plan within ten years. It marks spending at least 2% of federal funds on biking/walking and
speed-limit laws for slow roads as **not achieved**. All five action indicators show no change since the prior card.
These are historical program assessments, not verification of current law.[^njcard2024]

| Federal indicator as labeled | New Jersey | Rank of 50 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commuters biking to work | 0.3% | 29 |
| Fatalities per 10,000 bike commuters | 14.1 | 28 |
| Per-capita FHWA spending on biking and walking | $0.94 | 49 |

Category scores are percentages of available points, not outcome percentages: Funding **40% (43/50)**;
Infrastructure **75% (26/50)**; Laws **46% (30/50)**; Policies **71% (14/50)**;
Capacity & Support **74% (18/50)**; Safety **48% (27/50)**; Every Ride Counts **15% (34/50)**.
The Safety category rank is distinct from the federal fatality-indicator rank.[^njcard2024]

## Feedback and qualifications

The League says New Jersey reached the 2% federal-spending threshold only once in the preceding ten years,
and that people walking and biking account for more than 30% of traffic fatalities. It recommends consistent
funding and higher per-capita spending **and/or improved reporting so investments are counted**. The $0.94
figure therefore should not be read as total state/local cycling investment or as a fully audited spending total.
Neither the spending interval nor the fatality-share years are specified.[^njcard2024]

The card recommends clearer authority/guidance for 20 mph urban/residential limits, describing default urban
limits above 20 mph, and a safe-door-opening law, describing New Jersey as one of eight states without one.
It praises Hoboken and Jersey City for years without roadway traffic deaths and describes Assembly-passed
Target Zero Commission legislation as a prospective statewide action plan. These are attributed examples and
recommendations, not measured effects of a particular policy or evidence the statewide plan was implemented.[^njcard2024]

No detailed scoring weights, uncertainty estimates, numerator definitions, data periods or matching exposure
methods appear on the page. The 2024 publication year does not establish 2024 measurement. Rankings do not
isolate policy effects, commute share does not cover all cycling, and fatalities per bike commuter are not a
per-trip or per-mile risk estimate. No matching publication or underlying League assessment was found in the
bundle; existing New Jersey news excerpts and national fatality tables are distinct publications, not independent
confirmations of these figures.
