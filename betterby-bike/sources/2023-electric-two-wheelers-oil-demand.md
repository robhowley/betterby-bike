# 280 million e-bikes are slashing oil demand far more than electric vehicles

Muhammad Rizwan Azhar and Waqas Uzair, Edith Cowan University. The Conversation, republished by
Ars Technica on November 18, 2023. [Original publication](https://theconversation.com/the-worlds-280-million-electric-bikes-and-mopeds-are-cutting-demand-for-oil-far-more-than-electric-cars-213870).
The five-page PDF's substantive article text on pages 1–4 was read; page 5 is site navigation.

## Reporting and argument

This is academic commentary synthesizing secondary estimates and advocating smaller electric vehicles
for short urban trips, not original travel or emissions research. It contrasts electric cars' lower
operating costs with their road-space, electricity and battery-material demands, and argues that bikes
and mopeds can complement cars and public transport. It acknowledges that longer trips, larger groups
and outer-suburban or regional travel may favor a car.[^azhar2023]

The article reports more than 280 million electric mopeds, scooters, motorcycles and three-wheelers
on the world's roads in the previous year, versus over 20 million electric cars and 1.3 million
commercial EVs. It attributes approximately one million barrels/day of avoided oil demand, about 1%
of global demand, to the smaller vehicles and describes displacement as four times that of electric
cars. These are fleet-wide estimates, not an e-bike-only count or a per-vehicle efficiency ratio.
The article links [BloombergNEF's Electric Vehicle Outlook 2022](https://bnef.turtl.co/story/evo-2022/page/2/1);
its methods and underlying data were not reviewed.[^azhar2023]

Other secondary claims include UK e-scooter trips producing up to 45% less CO2 than alternatives
(linked [UCL report](https://www.voi.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/06/UCL-C_Emissions-Savings-Emissions-from-Shared-E-Scooters.pdf)),
and US research estimating about 7% lower transport emissions if e-bikes reached 11% of vehicle trips
(linked [ITDP coverage](https://www.itdp.org/2019/09/24/e-bikes-e-scooters-drivers-of-climate-action/)).
Neither linked source was reviewed; the article does not supply their comparators, system boundaries
or uncertainty. Its IEA-attributed 2028 oil-demand peak is a forecast made in 2023, not an observed outcome.

## Limits and extracted evidence

The broad headline's “e-bikes” label includes several motorized categories in the body. “Last year”
implies 2022, but the linked BNEF outlook itself is dated 2022, leaving the estimates' precise reference
year unresolved. The article provides no modeling details, uncertainty interval or bicycle-only split.
Oil displacement is not equivalent to lifecycle greenhouse-gas savings or a measured effect of bike lanes.

Australian running-cost illustrations are not measured household savings: its roughly $20 annual e-bike
charging example excludes purchase and maintenance, and its car calculation gives $0.14/km and 12,000 km
but $1,820 fuel cost, an arithmetic inconsistency. Short-trip statistics likewise do not establish actual
car substitution. These claims are retained as commentary context, not extracted as verified cost results.
