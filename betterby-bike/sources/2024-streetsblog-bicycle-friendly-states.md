# America Has A New ‘Friendliest’ State for Cycling

## Publication and access

Kea Wilson, Streetsblog USA, December 12, 2024. Read the supplied PDF's full substantive article
(pp. 1–6) as extracted text. The article links the [League of American Bicyclists' state program](https://bikeleague.org/bfa/states/).
Its national report, survey responses, scoring weights and underlying datasets were not independently reviewed;
no values were extracted from the embedded ranking graphic.

## Reporting and results

Wilson reports Washington reclaiming first place from Massachusetts in the League's 2024 Bicycle Friendly
States ranking; Oregon, California and Minnesota complete the top five. Mississippi is last, with Missouri,
Nebraska, South Carolina and Alabama also at the bottom. League policy director Ken McLeod explains that
rankings combine public crash/funding data and a DOT survey to capture both outcomes and effort, and to
provide actionable feedback. A high rank is not a finding that a state provides adequate cycling safety.[^wilson2024]

The article reports only **22 states operating or funding bicycle survey/count programs**, four fewer than
in 2022. It calls this a new survey question, without explaining comparability with the earlier count.
McLeod suspects increased outsourcing to mobility-data companies, but the article does not test that explanation
or compare their accuracy with physical counters.[^wilson2024]

Reported biking/walking share of federal funds fell from **2.3% in 2017 to 2.0% in 2023** (the article's
“last year”), despite increased dollar spending. McLeod says annual biking/walking spending exceeded $1 billion
in four years, including the latest two, while other spending grew faster. He explicitly excludes discretionary
grants such as Safe Streets and Roads for All from the League's data. These figures are not total federal,
state and local active-travel investment; detailed accounting definitions are absent.[^wilson2024]

McLeod recommends advocates pursue the League's five actions: a spending threshold, Complete Streets policies,
safe-passing laws, statewide bicycle plans and laws supporting slower streets. His concerns about the incoming
Trump administration's grant choices are prospective opinion, not observed funding changes or policy effects.

## Coverage and limitations

The existing [New Jersey report card](2024-new-jersey-bicycle-friendly-state-report-card.md) is a directly
reviewed state output of the same 2024 League program, not independent corroboration. This article adds national
context and an interview, but cannot supply the card's missing indicator years or establish per-mile injury risk.
It reports rising cycling deaths without supplying the exposure series needed to interpret risk trends. Rankings,
funding and reported program presence do not identify effects of bicycle infrastructure or demonstrate equitable access.
