# Giant Cars Are Going Global

David Zipper, Slate, December 10, 2024. Research assistance credited to Sanjana Singh Raichur.
The [supplied publication](../../raw-sources/news/big-cars-are-going-global-i-hope-other-countries-don-t-make-these-mistakes.pdf)
has a browser title beginning “Big cars are going global”; its displayed headline is used here.

## Reporting basis

This reported commentary combines international sales and energy statistics, prior safety research,
policy examples and interviews with BloombergNEF transport analyst Colin McKerracher and European
Transport Safety Council spokesperson Dudley Curtis. It argues for early regulation of growing vehicle
size, rather than presenting an original crash or policy evaluation. “Car bloat” includes both a shift
toward SUVs/pickups and enlargement within models.[^zipper2024]

## Main reporting and competing pressures

- SUVs reportedly accounted for nearly half of worldwide car sales in 2023, versus 20% about 15 years
  earlier. The separate US four-in-five figure includes pickups, so these are not identical categories.
- Citing the [IEA's SUV sales/emissions commentary](https://www.iea.org/commentaries/suvs-are-setting-new-sales-records-each-year-and-so-are-their-emissions),
  Slate reports that 2023 growth in large cars added 500,000 barrels/day to global oil consumption and
  accounted for 20% of the global increase in human-caused CO2 emissions. The IEA material was not separately read.
- Zipper describes blind spots, taller pedestrian impact points, vehicle incompatibility and greater
  energy requirements. McKerracher attributes expansion partly to higher manufacturer margins and an
  arms race in which buyers fear being in smaller vehicles; these are interview interpretations, not
  an estimated causal decomposition of sales growth.
- Curtis criticizes EU weight-scaled emissions targets. The article also describes Australian pickup
  tax exemptions and US regulatory incentives as contributing to growth. It reports weight/emissions
  purchase charges in Norway and France, and a collapse in Hongqi sales after Norway's 2023 weight fee
  for large EVs, but gives no sales counts or controlled estimate of that fee's effect.
- Reported responses include Edinburgh's SUV advertising restriction on city property, Paris's vote
  to triple SUV parking fees, and proposals for vehicle-design taxes and SUV-free zones. France's
  advertising restriction is described as planned for 2028. These historical reports and proposals
  are not evidence of current implementation or measured injury/emissions reductions.[^zipper2024]

## Coverage and limits

Read the complete five-page PDF text, including substantive reporting on all five pages. No original
methods, statistical tables or outcome charts are supplied. Linked studies, policy documents and IEA
calculations were not independently reviewed; the capture does not supply a verified original-page URL.
Sales shares do not describe the entire on-road fleet, and the article provides no cyclist exposure,
crash-rate estimates or evaluated benefits from restrictions. The safety discussion overlaps
[The Economist's occupant-weight analysis](2024-economist-big-cars-killing.md) and
[Road to Zero's vehicle-hazard synthesis](2024-road-to-zero-massive-hazards-light-trucks.md),
not independent confirmation of those results. Its international interviews and policy coverage are distinct.
