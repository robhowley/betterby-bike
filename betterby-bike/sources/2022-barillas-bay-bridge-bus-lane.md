# Adding a bus lane to the Bay Bridge? Why it's needed

Oscar Barillas, Oscar's Newsletter, June 18, 2022. [Original article](https://oscarsnewsletter.com/adding-a-bus-lane-to-the-bay-bridge-why-its-needed/).
Read substantive PDF text on pp. 1–18. Page 19 is site material; the apparent illustration pages 10 and 12 render blank,
and an embedded Guatemala video is unavailable.

## Personal commute account and proposal

Barillas describes using AC Transit's F and NL Transbay routes from Emeryville and Oakland's Adams Point around
2018–2020. He valued direct bus access to his San Francisco workplace but says Bay Bridge traffic made departures after
about 6:30 a.m. unattractive, leading him to use BART instead. At Adams Point he describes a 30-minute walk to BART. His
point is that bus coverage can reach homes poorly served by rail, while congestion can undermine that practical
advantage.[^barillas]

He estimates riding the buses about 60% of the time over two years and says a dedicated lane probably would have made
him a 100% daily bus rider. This is a personal counterfactual, not an observed ridership response. He proposes a lane
for AC Transit, Lynx, intercity coaches and private charter buses, and urges support for the then-current California
AB-455. He anticipates large commuter benefits while acknowledging a lane could lengthen his own driving commute;
neither effect is evaluated.[^barillas]

## International examples and evidence limits

The article illustrates its argument with Mexico City, Curitiba and Guatemala City BRT. Citing a
[UN sustainable-development case page](https://sustainabledevelopment.un.org/index.php?page=view&type=99&nr=49&menu=1449),
it reports Mexico City route commute time falling from 1.5 hours to 1 hour and annual CO2 reductions of 35,000 tons.
These are secondary figures without evaluation methods in the article, not projected Bay Bridge effects. Historical
system descriptions draw on Wikipedia and linked journalism. His Guatemala pollution memories and preference for cleaner
centralized buses are personal reflections, not exposure or health measurements.[^barillas]

There is no sampled commute diary, traffic model, before/after intervention, cost-benefit calculation or population
access analysis. Section headings and prose differ on move dates, so the account should not establish a precise
residential chronology. Linked studies, international case sources and legislation were not separately reviewed, and the
article does not establish current legislative or lane status.
