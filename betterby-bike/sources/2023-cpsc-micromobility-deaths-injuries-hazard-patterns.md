# Micromobility Products-Related Deaths, Injuries, and Hazard Patterns: 2017–2022

James Tark, U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission, Directorate for Epidemiology, September 2023.
A staff report, not reviewed or approved by the Commission. [Original report, supplied PDF](../../raw-sources/research/2023-cpsc-micromobility-deaths-injuries-hazard-patterns.pdf).
The cover and content identify 2017–2022, despite the PDF metadata's older 2017–2021 title.

## Scope and methods

U.S. surveillance of standing electric scooters, hoverboards and low-speed e-bikes for 2017–2022, not all bicycles
or powered two-wheelers. Mopeds, mobility scooters, seated scooters and non-electric products are excluded.
NEISS provides weighted national ED-treated injury estimates from a stratified hospital probability sample;
product codes and narratives determine inclusion. Data were extracted March 6, 2023, with weights updated April 18.
Scooter coding changed in 2020; follow-up identified additional e-scooters among unspecified scooter cases.[^cpsc2023]

The 2022 telephone/online scooter survey produced 432 completed investigations, including 309 verified e-scooter
cases from 3,719 assignments. Weights were raked to demographic and hospital-stratum margins to reduce nonresponse
bias. Separately, CPSRMS supplies anecdotal fatality reports and 314 completed in-depth hazard investigations;
these are not nationally representative. Duplicate incident reports were consolidated. Death reporting can lag
by two years, especially affecting 2021–2022. Eight fatalities overlap the hazard-investigation set.[^cpsc2023]

## Results and interpretation

- Estimated ED visits rose from 34,000 in 2017 to 93,100 in 2022, totaling 360,800. E-scooters increased,
  but hoverboards had a declining overall trend despite a 2020 rebound. E-bike estimates were 24,400 in 2022;
  annual 2018–2021 e-bike estimates fail NEISS reliability criteria. These are burdens, not per-trip risks.
- CPSC knew of 233 deaths: 111 e-scooter, 104 e-bike and 18 hoverboard deaths. Motor-vehicle collisions
  dominated reported scooter/e-bike fatalities; pedestrian deaths and fire deaths also occurred.
- The 2022 scooter follow-up estimates 37% rental-related injuries and 13% helmet use among injury cases.
  There is no uninjured comparison group or riding-exposure denominator; conditions at injury do not prove causes.
- Fire hazards appeared in 212 of 314 selected investigations, alongside brake, electrical and structural problems.
  This distribution does not estimate the prevalence of hazards among products or rides.
- Among the 76% of estimated 2019–2022 injuries with known race/ethnicity, non-Hispanic Black consumers represented
  29% overall and 32% for scooters and e-bikes, versus 13% of the general population. Missing information and
  younger/possibly more urban users make that population comparison an unsuitable per-user risk estimate.[^cpsc2023]

## Access, overlap and reporting cautions

Read the full 42-page PDF through text extraction, including methods, all supporting tables, updated 2021 results
(Appendix C) and the questionnaire on pp. 38–42; visually checked pp. 20 and 34. No underlying records or earlier
annual report were independently reviewed. Publication-title, author, CPSC/NEISS and result searches found no
existing publication/study duplicate. This report extends the 2022 annual report's 2017–2021 surveillance and
updates 2021 survey results, not independent confirmation of those earlier data.

Internal inconsistencies remain unresolved: Table 2.5's road/sidewalk shares (63%/29%) differ from adjacent prose
(74%/23%), and its printed total is 41,700 rather than the stated 51,700 denominator. Table 2.6 and several
Appendix C tables have inconsistent sample totals. Fatality prose sometimes says 232/103 rather than the
consistent main-table totals of 233/104; rental-scooter hazard allocations also differ between prose and Table 3.4.
Use the overall fatality table totals, not disputed rental breakdowns. Appendix B also conflicts with blanket
prose about rental-estimate reportability: some annual rental cells meet its numerical criteria, though rental
identification remains incomplete. No attempt is made to silently repair these entries.
