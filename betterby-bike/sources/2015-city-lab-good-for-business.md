# The Complete Business Case for Converting Street Parking Into Bike Lanes

Eric Jaffe, CityLab, March 13, 2015.
[Original article](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2015-03-13/every-study-ever-conducted-on-the-impact-converting-street-parking-into-bike-lanes-has-on-businesses)
·
[Local PDF](../../raw-sources/news/2015-city-lab-good-for-business.pdf).

## Reporting and evidence

Prompted by San Francisco's Polk Street debate, Jaffe assembles an annotated roundup advertised as 12 studies.
He cites the *San Francisco Examiner* on about 2.5 years of debate and merchants' opposition to a proposed loss of
140 parking spaces. The roundup's claim that parking-to-bike-lane conversions have little or no business impact,
and sometimes benefits, is Jaffe's synthesis, not a pooled effect or systematic review. Its selection is described
as every major study the author knows of, with no reproducible search or inclusion procedure.[^citylab2015]

The entries mix customer-mode surveys, before/after sales comparisons, merchant perceptions and a parking-capacity
calculation. Several do not observe a parking conversion at all. Underlying links below were recovered from the PDF;
the linked studies were not reviewed.

## Results and underlying references

- **Portland:** repeats [existing customer-spending evidence](/findings/business/portland-bike-customer-spending.md):
  lower monthly bicycle spending at groceries, higher at bars, restaurants and convenience stores. It describes 78
  businesses while linking [earlier coverage](/sources/2012-citylab-pedestrians-bikes-spending.md) and a
  [Clifton manuscript](https://wiki.cecs.pdx.edu/pub/ItsWeb/TrbConferences/Clifton_TRB2013_ConsumerBehaviorAndTravelChoices_submitted.pdf).
  Sample correspondence with the earlier article's 89 businesses remains unresolved.
- **East Village, NYC:** a [survey of 420 people](http://transalt.org/sites/default/files/news/reports/2012/EVSS_Final.pdf)
  on First and Second avenues reportedly attributed 95% of aggregate retail spending to non-drivers; cyclist weekly
  spending averaged about $163 versus $143 for drivers. This is a survey in a neighborhood with protected lanes,
  not a measured lane-induced sales change.
- **New Zealand:** a [nine-shopping-area survey](http://www.nzta.govt.nz/resources/research/reports/530/docs/RR-530-Reallocation-of-road-space.pdf)
  reports higher per-trip spending by drivers; the suggestion of higher longer-term non-driver spending is an
  interpretation, not an observed monthly result. See [Finding](/findings/business/nz-shopping-mode-spending.md).
- **Dublin:** [two shopping streets](http://arrow.dit.ie/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1010&context=comlinkoth)
  show merchants overestimating car arrivals. Grafton Street cyclist monthly spending was €228 versus €237 for
  drivers, near parity rather than a cyclist advantage.
- **Los Angeles:** [York road-diet research](http://la.streetsblog.org/wp-content/pdf/york_blvd_final_report_compress.pdf)
  is summarized as having little business effect; revenue rose in both treated and untreated sections.
  See [Finding](/findings/business/york-road-diet-business-results.md).
- **Vancouver:** the [downtown separated-lane business study](http://former.vancouver.ca/ctyclerk/cclerk//20110728/documents/penv3-BusinessImpactStudyReportDowntownSeparatedBicycleLanes-StantecReport.pdf)
  reports a net sales decline, with survey-response concerns and smaller losses in limited sales records.
  See [Finding](/findings/business/vancouver-separated-lane-sales-reports.md).
- **Toronto:** a [Bloor Street survey](http://www.bikeleague.org/sites/default/files/bikeleague/bikeleague.org/programs/bicyclefriendlyamerica/bicyclefriendlybusiness/pdfs/toronto_study_bike_lanes_parking.pdf)
  finds only 10% of patrons drove; predicted benefits of conversion were not observed post-installation effects.
  See [Finding](/findings/business/toronto-bloor-customer-spending.md).
- **San Francisco:** a [survey of 1,187 shoppers](http://trb.metapress.com/content/l668640620x82m24/) in major retail
  centers, discussed in a congestion-pricing debate, reports 60% arriving by transit, walking or cycling and higher
  monthly non-driver spending through more frequent visits. It is not a bicycle-only or lane-conversion comparison.
- **Seattle:** repeats [65th Street](/findings/business/seattle-65th-street-retail-sales.md) and
  [Greenwood](/findings/business/seattle-greenwood-retail-sales.md) via
  [Jaffe's 2013 coverage](/sources/2013-citylab-bikes-biz.md), not independent sales evidence.
- **Davis:** [Popovich and Handy's study](http://trb.metapress.com/content/a1373577291368l2/) estimates higher monthly
  bicycle-customer spending on Target-type goods. See [Finding](/findings/business/davis-downtown-customer-spending.md).
- **Bristol and Graz:** Bristol's merchant-versus-customer mode discrepancy is quantified; Graz is described as
  similar without figures in the prose. The link is [earlier CityLab coverage](https://www.citylab.com/life/2012/11/4-reasons-retailers-dont-need-free-parking-thrive/3978/),
  not a primary report. See [Dublin/Bristol Finding](/findings/business/merchant-car-arrival-overestimates.md).
- **Melbourne:** [Alison Lee's thesis](http://colabradio.mit.edu/wp-content/uploads/2010/12/Final_Thesis_Alison_Lee.pdf)
  supplies a capacity-based argument already represented in the
  [parking-space Finding](/findings/business/melbourne-parking-retail-revenue.md), not a measured conversion return.[^citylab2015]

## Limits and synthesis

Customer spending, sales-tax receipts and merchant reports are different measures. Local associations and predicted
benefits do not prove that removing parking increases sales or cannot harm businesses. Vancouver's adverse account
also qualifies the article's broad opening conclusion. Portland, Seattle and Melbourne overlap earlier ingestions;
Toronto and New Zealand were also cited in the business advocacy report, not new independent replications.
[Evaluating street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) integrates these distinctions.

All ten PDF pages were read as extracted text, including the complete substantive prose. Embedded chart images were
not visually reviewed; figures here use prose only. Primary methods, chart-only values, uncertainty and currencies
where only dollar signs are supplied remain unresolved. The July 2024 print header is not the publication date.
