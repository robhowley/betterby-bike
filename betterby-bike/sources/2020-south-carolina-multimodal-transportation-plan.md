# South Carolina 2040 Multimodal Transportation Plan Update

## Publication and access

The contents identify an SCDOT long-range plan updated in **July 2020**, not Brookings' *Connecting the DOTs*.
The retrieval manifest records discovery of this SCDOT PDF from that Brookings landing page. The original
publication link above is from the manifest, not a newly accessed website. The plan retains a 2040 horizon
from its 2014 predecessor.

This is a **partial, cycling-focused reading** of the supplied 285-page capture: cover/contents, introduction
and development account (printed pp. 1–3 and opening of p. 4), bicycle/pedestrian inventory (pp. 44–45),
needs introduction (p. 60), bicycle/pedestrian needs (pp. 69–70), and revenue/funding-gap discussion and
Table 11-2 (pp. 72–74). Other modal chapters, public-comment appendices, performance appendices and
underlying datasets were not substantively reviewed. Figures were not visually inspected; extracted
prose and table text support the finding.

## Purpose and reporting basis

SCDOT combines stakeholder input, agency databases, design standards, cost estimates and federal analytical
tools to identify modal needs and compare them with projected revenues. Roadway, bridge, transit and
walking/cycling needs cover 22 years; partner-agency freight, port and aviation needs have different,
shorter horizons. This is a planning and program account, not an intervention evaluation.[^plan]

## Cycling-relevant account

The plan reports establishing a Pedestrian and Bicycle Safety Program in 2018 with **$5 million annually**,
and announcing development of a safety action plan in 2019. It also reports approximately **$7.4 million
annually** for eligible Transportation Alternatives projects and **$138 million annually** across 21
MPO/COG entities for broader system upgrades that can include walking and cycling. Those amounts do not
form a measured cycling-only spending total.[^plan]

Walking/cycling needs draw on accommodation deficiencies and adopted regional/local plans. Sidewalk needs
are included in primary/secondary highway modernization, and additional accommodation needs are included
in the MPO/COG program. A standardized needs-data and prioritization method was still proposed. Table
11-2 has no separate bicycle/pedestrian row, so it cannot establish a statewide cycling funding gap.[^plan]

The inventory also reports **165 pedestrian and 23 bicyclist fatalities in 2018**, citing NHTSA, alongside
plans to target high-crash locations. These are historical secondary counts, not exposure-based risk or
evidence that the new safety program reduced harm. No safety effect is extracted.

## Limits and unresolved inconsistencies

Allocations, eligible programs, assessed needs and actual expenditures are distinct. The reviewed sections
do not supply cycling-only disbursements, delivered network coverage, or evaluated safety/access outcomes.
They do not establish current program terms.

Page 72 reports average revenue of $1.89 billion annually, while its gap discussion and Table 11-2 use
$1.94 billion annualized funding. Page 74's bullet calls $8.91 billion funding, whereas Table 11-2 labels
it the total gap; the table distinguishes annualized figures from the 2019–2040 total. These discrepancies
are not reconciled here. The table also does not allocate broader program totals to cycling. The printed
fatality-rate comparisons on p. 44 omit denominators and are not reused quantitatively.

## Finding

- [South Carolina's 2020 plan does not isolate cycling expenditure or its funding gap](/findings/south-carolina-2020-active-travel-funding-limits.md).
