# Cities and Transit Agencies Are Finally Getting On Board With Bus Stop Data

Maylin Tu, Next City, March 30, 2026.
[Original article capture](../../raw-sources/news/cities-and-transit-agencies-are-finally-getting-on-board-with-bus-stop-data.pdf).
The supplied PDF contains the complete substantive article on
pp. 1–4, with funding acknowledgment and author information on p. 5 and subsequent site material.
Linked datasets, standards proposals and legislation were not reviewed.

## Reporting basis

Tu interviews NYC sanitation spokesperson Joshua Goodman, MBTA accessibility manager Laura Brelsford,
MBTA transit-technology deputy director Josh Fabian and Transit app policy lead Stephen Miller.
LA Metro supplies an emailed statement. The story was supported by the Solutions Journalism Network's
How Government Responds Innovation Fund. It reports implementation accounts, not a controlled evaluation.[^tu2026]

## Main points

- NYC sanitation created an app after January snowfall to geotag unsheltered bus stops and crosswalks
  and record whether they were certified clear. Goodman contrasts this with earlier work that was not
  systematically tracked. The article supplies no clearance-time or accessibility comparison.
- Brelsford describes an inventory of roughly 7,000 MBTA stops, begun almost a decade earlier, including
  adjacent crossings and curb ramps. It identified 280 completely inaccessible stops; most reportedly
  were remedied. She says 99% of stops fall outside MBTA jurisdiction, making municipal coordination
  necessary alongside agency capital spending, state grants and fair share tax revenue.
- MBTA has published facilities data since 2018, mainly for train-station accessibility. Fabian and
  others are working on standardized amenity fields in GTFS. The article describes this as early-stage
  work, not an adopted universal amenity standard; Miller describes producer/consumer demonstrations
  and community voting as steps toward adoption.
- Transit app's Rate-My-Ride reports reportedly informed Santa Monica stop cleaning and Vancouver
  lighting priorities. Nineteen of its 180 transit partners used the feature at publication; this is
  not the share of all transit agencies. No city partnerships had yet been formalized.
- LA Metro says municipal control limits its operational use of amenity information. Its analysis of
  all 12,000 system stops was reportedly not public. A California statewide stop-registry bill had been
  introduced, not enacted in this account.[^tu2026]

## Limits and relationship to existing evidence

There is no inventory dataset, audit protocol, exact MBTA remediation count or completion date in the
article. App-user feedback is not representative rider sampling. Reported maintenance responses and
standardization proposals do not establish improved journey completion, equitable amenities, mode shift
or cycling safety. Tu and interviewees argue for coordination; a shared data format alone cannot compel it.
The article's broad characterization of existing GTFS amenity gaps is reporting, not a technical review
of every accessibility field.

Existing [older-adult mobility coverage](2026-streetsblog-aging-transportation.md) discusses missing benches
and low reported transit use. This article adds distinct inventory and governance accounts, not another
measurement of older-adult participation or unmet need.
