# Deaths linked to London air pollution have fallen 40%, study estimates

Gwyn Topham, The Guardian, June 23, 2026. Read the complete three-page local capture,
including reporting and photo caption on pp. 1–2; p. 3 contains only “Most viewed.”

## Reporting and evidence

The article covers Imperial College London's Environmental Research Group analysis of London's
2019–2024 air pollution and estimated attributable premature deaths. It reports nitrogen dioxide
levels down 41% and fine particulate pollution down 28%, alongside an approximately 40% decline
in estimated pollution-linked deaths. Updated scientific evidence revised the 2019 mortality estimate
from about 4,000 to 6,400–8,000; the comparable 2024 estimate was 3,800–5,100.[^topham2026]

Researcher David Dajnak describes marked improvement alongside continuing health risks. The article
reports stronger associations with cardiovascular conditions, dementia and diabetes as well as respiratory
disease. Bexley, Havering and Sutton were among boroughs with the highest proportions of deaths
attributable to pollution in 2024, not necessarily the highest absolute counts.[^topham2026]

Mayor Sadiq Khan presents the results as evidence that Ulez saves lives, but the article and its photo
caption explicitly say the Imperial report does not single out Ulez or another policy. Ulez began in
central London in 2019, expanded in 2021 and became London-wide in 2023. The article reports roughly
97% vehicle compliance and cites a separate prior-year Greater London Authority study estimating
roadside NO2 27% below a no-Ulez scenario. That is a different comparison from the 41% temporal decline.
[March 2025 coverage](/sources/2025-guardian-ulez-expansion-air-quality.md) apparently reports the same
GLA result but calls it a decline since 2019; that comparison wording remains unresolved without the
primary report. These accounts are not independent evidence. It also describes school air filters and
growth in zero-emission buses. Royal College of Physicians
adviser Stephen Holgate praises sustained action; Mums for Lungs calls for more action.[^topham2026]

## Limits

This is secondary news coverage, not a reviewed primary mortality analysis. The capture does not supply
the study title, a direct report link, calculation methods, exposure definitions, population adjustments
or the meaning of the reported mortality ranges. Its Imperial link leads to a Guardian topic page.
Estimated attributable deaths are not individually observed pollution-caused death counts. Comparing the
old 4,000 estimate directly with 2024 would mix scientific assumptions. The reported trend cannot isolate
Ulez, cycling infrastructure or a particular mode shift; the separate GLA study was also not reviewed.
