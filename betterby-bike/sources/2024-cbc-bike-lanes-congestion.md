# Do bike lanes really cause more traffic congestion? Here's what the research says

Nicole Mortillaro, CBC News, October 22, 2024; updated November 5, 2024.
[Captured publication](../../raw-sources/news/do-bike-lanes-really-cause-more-traffic-congestion-here-s-what-the-research-says-cbc-news.pdf).

## Reporting basis

Against Ontario's proposed provincial approval requirement for bike lanes replacing vehicle lanes, CBC combines
selected research and municipal reporting with interviews with Eco-Counter's David Beitel and University of
Toronto's Shoshanna Saxe. This is not a systematic review or a new traffic evaluation. The November clarification
changes the deck from universal benefits to lanes that **can** ease congestion, reduce emissions and benefit
businesses.[^cbc2024]

## Results and competing explanations

- The distinct [Bloor West travel-time Finding](/findings/operations/toronto-bloor-west-reported-travel-delays.md)
  preserves reported increases of 2.4–4.4 minutes eastbound and 1.5–3.6 minutes westbound by time of day,
  comparing November 2022–March 2023 with the same period a year later. Saxe attributes rising times to pandemic
  recovery, but CBC provides no controlled comparison establishing either that explanation or a lane-only effect.
  CBC links the [city dashboard](http://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/8fb5-BWCEDashboardFinal-AODA.pdf).
- Through a [2016 CIVITAS congestion review](https://epub.wupperinst.org/frontdoor/deliver/index/docId/6597/file/6597_Reducing_Congestion.pdf),
  CBC repeats NYC's 35% Columbus Avenue travel-time reduction. Its rounded 4.5-to-3-minute account is not a new
  study; the existing [NYCDOT Finding](/findings/operations/nyc-pbl-traffic-performance.md) retains 4:38 to 3:00
  and sampling limits. Turn-lane explanations do not isolate bike lanes from the redesign package.
- Other case studies reportedly found no traffic effect or delays from seconds to just over a minute. Copenhagen's
  easier motor access is a quoted city interpretation, not a quantified causal effect in this article.
- Bike Share Toronto trips reportedly rose from 665,000 in 2015 to 5.7 million in 2023. These are shared-bike trips,
  not all cycling, unique riders or demonstrated substitution for driving. Beitel and Saxe's induced/latent-demand
  explanations and one-to-three-year timelines are interview claims, not universal response estimates.
- CBC reports Paris cycling growth of 54% in 2018–2019 and a 5% car-use decline in 2010–2018. Saxe's separate
  claim that 30% of Paris trips are by bike lacks a denominator, date and method. It cannot replace the
  [existing geographic survey shares](/findings/networks/paris-reported-travel-shares.md), including 11.2%
  cycling in central Paris; the discrepancy remains unresolved.
- A [2019 Bloor business report](https://www.tcat.ca/wp-content/uploads/2017/12/Bloor-Economic-Impact-Study-Full-Report-2019-09-03.pdf)
  reportedly found more customers on a different stretch. This is brief secondary coverage of the Bloor evidence
  family already discussed in [CBC's economic roundup](/sources/2019-cbc-good-for-business.md), not independent
  confirmation. Broad safety and emissions statements do not quantify a local project benefit.[^cbc2024]

## Access and limits

Read the complete six-page text capture, including captions and clarification; substantive content ends on p. 5.
Visually checked pp. 4–5 for the travel-time quotation and correction. Some photographs and the embedded video
are unavailable in the capture; there are no reproduced research tables or methods appendices. Linked primary
reports and live pages were not reviewed. Missing sampling, controls and uncertainty prevent a general no-delay
claim or attribution of observed increases solely to lanes.
