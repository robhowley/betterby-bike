# Statista: passenger transport carbon footprints (2024)

## Reporting and evidence

[Statista's 4 June 2024 article](https://www.statista.com/chart/32350/greenhouse-gas-emissions-by-mode-of-transport/)
credits Tristan Gaudiaut as writer and Anna Fleck as translator. It summarizes an analysis published by
Visual Capitalist; its infographic credits OWID, ICCT and the British government via Visual Capitalist.
Those underlying sources were not independently reviewed. The six-page PDF's substantive text is on
p. 3 and the chart spans pp. 3–4. A cookie overlay obscures the rendered pages; extracted text and the
embedded chart image recovered the substantive content.[^article]

## Main reporting

The chart reports grams of CO2 equivalent per passenger-kilometer: cruise ship 250; domestic short-haul
flight 246; diesel car 171; petrol car 170; medium-haul flight 151; long-haul flight (over 3,700 km) 148;
motorbike 114; bus 97; plug-in hybrid 68; electric car 47; domestic train 35; Eurostar 4. These are
reported comparison values, not new Statista measurements or estimates of all forms of pollution.[^article]

The text cautions that national electricity mixes, technology and transport networks change the
comparison. It describes electric cars as generally having lower lifetime carbon footprints than
combustion cars while emphasizing electricity generation. That prose does not establish a common
lifecycle boundary for every chart value.

## Limits and connections

The capture gives no underlying data year, occupancy assumptions, uncertainty intervals or consistent
geographic and emissions-accounting boundary for the chart. It includes neither walking nor cycling.
The figures therefore cannot establish bicycle-project savings, local fleet emissions or a universal
ranking independent of loading and energy supply.
