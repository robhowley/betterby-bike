# Oregon's proposed kei-truck legalization

Kea Wilson, **Streetsblog USA, January 22, 2026**. [Original article, captured PDF](../../raw-sources/news/aisle-be-damned-dems-and-gop-unite-in-oregon-in-bid-to-legalize-kei-trucks-streetsblog-usa.pdf).

## Issue and reporting basis

Wilson reports a bipartisan Oregon proposal to make small Japanese-style kei trucks registrable for road use.
The account centers on State Sen. Anthony Broadman's interview, including a brewery owner's difficulty using a
small truck for narrow-alley deliveries and rural interest in farm hauling. It names Republican co-sponsors
Virgle Osborne and Alex Skarlatos and describes this as Broadman's third attempt. These are reported motivations
and political support, not observations of a completed program.[^wilson2026]

The article attributes Oregon's registration barrier partly to missing vehicle-code definitions for size,
engine capacity and age. It reports that the proposal would prohibit operation on highways with speed limits
above 65 mph, while leaving federal restrictions on importing vehicles less than 25 years old unchanged.
Neither the bill text nor current state or federal law was independently reviewed; this is a dated proposal,
not legal guidance.[^wilson2026]

## Claims and qualifications

Wilson reports used mini-truck prices of **$6,000–$12,000** and asserts better fuel efficiency than larger trucks,
without a market sample or fuel-consumption comparison. Broadman argues that smaller, slower vehicles could
reduce costs and pedestrian danger, and that reuse could avoid new-vehicle production. No ownership-cost model,
crash comparison, cyclist outcome or lifecycle-emissions analysis is supplied. The article's general annual
car-cost claim of more than $12,000 is not a measured saving from choosing a kei truck.[^wilson2026]

The account acknowledges unresolved supply restrictions, vulnerability in a fleet of larger vehicles and the
limits of smaller cars as an affordability solution. Broadman supports broader non-driving options and denser,
amenity-rich neighborhoods and does not claim kei trucks suit every trip. His predicted safety and environmental
benefits remain advocacy, not evaluated effects.[^wilson2026]

## Coverage and related evidence

All substantive article text on PDF pp. 1–5 was reviewed; subsequent pages contain site furniture. Linked articles
and legislation were not reviewed. The article provides no study methods, outcome tables or independent evaluation.

No new Finding is warranted: the useful addition is the policy debate, not a reusable measured effect. Existing
[vehicle-geometry and compatibility evidence](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md#vehicle-geometry-visibility-and-compatibility-measure-different-hazards)
does not establish the benefit of legalizing this vehicle class; the
[Topic synthesis](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md#kei-truck-legalization-is-not-an-evaluated-safety-intervention)
retains that distinction. No earlier coverage of this publication or Oregon proposal was found.
