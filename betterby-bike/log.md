# Ingestion history

## 2026-09-18

Ingested [Tin Tin, Woodward and Ameratunga (2010)](sources/2010-nz-bike-injuries.md), _Injuries to pedal cyclists on New
Zealand roads, 1988-2007_, adding historical national injury evidence and
[Interpreting cycling injury risk](topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).

- [Injury rates](findings/safety/nz-cycling-injury-rates-1988-2007.md): preserved the post-1996–99 increase alongside
  the earlier decline and lower historical serious-injury rate.
- [Crash mechanisms](findings/safety/nz-cycling-crash-mechanisms-2003-2007.md): separated non-collision cases,
  motor-vehicle categories and severity-dependent case shares.
- [Children and severity](findings/safety/nz-child-cycling-injury-severity.md): retained the qualification that the
  overall age pattern did not hold for serious injuries.
- [Brain injuries and helmet law](findings/safety/nz-brain-injury-trends-helmet-law.md): distinguished an observed
  decline from an unestablished causal explanation.

This adds one observational study, not evidence of infrastructure or helmet-law effectiveness.

Ingested [Lusk et al. (2011)](sources/2011-montreal-cycle-track-injury-risk.md), _Risk of injury for bicycling on cycle
tracks versus in the street_, adding a Montreal infrastructure comparison and a separate reanalysis of published
sidewalk-bikeway data:

- [Relative injury risk](findings/safety/montreal-cycle-track-relative-injury-risk.md): lower pooled risk on six
  Montreal tracks, retaining mixed route-level results, traffic-danger differences and exposure timing limits.
- [Absolute injury and crash rates](findings/safety/montreal-cycle-track-absolute-rates.md): separated EMR injuries from
  police-recorded motor-vehicle crashes and documented distance assumptions.
- [Intersection-only comparison reanalysis](findings/safety/intersection-only-sidewalk-risk-reanalysis.md): attributed
  the Palo Alto reanalysis to Lusk et al. and retained its assumed allocation of non-intersection crashes.

Revised [Interpreting cycling injury risk](topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) to distinguish infrastructure associations
from causal effects and kilometre-based EMR rates from hour-based inpatient/death rates.

Ingested
[NYCDOT (2011), _Evaluation of Solid Green Bicycle Lanes to Increase Compliance and Bicycle Safety_](sources/2011-nyc-green-paint.md)
adding evidence on motorist compliance rather than a measured injury or ridership effect:

- [Driver behavior](findings/design/nyc-green-paint-driver-behavior.md): retained lower reported encroachment,
  comparable standing/parking and mixed Bleecker Street results.
- [Measurement limits](findings/design/nyc-green-paint-measurement-limits.md): documented inconsistent site totals and
  clear-lane percentages, and distinguished citywide trends from unisolated paint effects.

Revised [Interpreting cycling injury risk](topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) to distinguish behavioral measures from injury
outcomes. Source reporting discrepancies remain unresolved.

Ingested [Wolfson (2011), _Bike Lanes_ memorandum](sources/2011-nyc-wolfson-bike-lanes-memo.md), preserving the mayoral
office's policy claims rather than treating the memo as a research study:

- [Protected-lane crash claims](findings/safety/nyc-wolfson-protected-lane-crash-claims.md): retained the reported
  typical 40% and some-location >50% all-road-user injury-crash reductions, with absent methods and unsupported causal
  attribution explicit.
- [Pedestrian fatality counts](findings/safety/nyc-wolfson-pedestrian-fatality-counts.md): retained four deaths in
  2001–05 and three in 2006–10 without converting small citywide counts into a proven risk reduction.

Revised [Interpreting cycling injury risk](topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) to separate these claims from documented route
comparisons. The Source also preserves polling, consultation, lane-removal and street-space claims. Both PDF pages were
read; underlying poll and crash analyses remain unreviewed.

Ingested
[NYCDOT (2012), _Measuring the Street: New Metrics for 21st Century Streets_](sources/2012-measuring-the-street.md),
adding selected project-level outcomes without treating them as isolated bike-lane effects:

- [8th/9th Avenue injuries](findings/safety/nyc-eighth-ninth-injury-changes.md): retained 35% and 58% all-user injury
  reductions, distinct from injury crashes and cyclist risk.
- [9th Avenue retail sales](findings/business/nyc-ninth-avenue-retail-sales.md): retained up to 49% growth versus 3%

  growth without assuming net new cycling.
- [Hoyt Avenue travel times](findings/operations/nyc-hoyt-avenue-travel-times.md): preserved the reported 51% northbound
  “improvement” without inventing its calculation.

Added [Evaluating street redesign outcomes](topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) and revised
