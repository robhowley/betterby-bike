# Ingestion history

## 2026-09-18

Ingested [Tin Tin, Woodward and Ameratunga (2010)](sources/2010-nz-bike-injuries.md), _Injuries to pedal cyclists on New
Zealand roads, 1988-2007_, adding historical national injury evidence and
[Interpreting cycling injury risk](topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).

- [Injury rates](findings/safety/nz-cycling-injury-rates-1988-2007.md): preserved the post-1996–99 increase alongside
  the earlier decline and lower historical serious-injury rate.
- [Crash mechanisms](findings/safety/nz-cycling-crash-mechanisms-2003-2007.md): separated non-collision cases,
  motor-vehicle categories and severity-dependent case shares.
- [Children and severity](findings/safety/nz-child-cycling-injury-severity.md): retained the qualification that the
  overall age pattern did not hold for serious injuries.
- [Brain injuries and helmet law](findings/safety/nz-brain-injury-trends-helmet-law.md): distinguished an observed
  decline from an unestablished causal explanation.

This adds one observational study, not evidence of infrastructure or helmet-law effectiveness.

Ingested [Lusk et al. (2011)](sources/2011-montreal-cycle-track-injury-risk.md), _Risk of injury for bicycling on cycle
tracks versus in the street_, adding a Montreal infrastructure comparison and a separate reanalysis of published
sidewalk-bikeway data:

- [Relative injury risk](findings/safety/montreal-cycle-track-relative-injury-risk.md): lower pooled risk on six
  Montreal tracks, retaining mixed route-level results, traffic-danger differences and exposure timing limits.
- [Absolute injury and crash rates](findings/safety/montreal-cycle-track-absolute-rates.md): separated EMR injuries from
  police-recorded motor-vehicle crashes and documented distance assumptions.
- [Intersection-only comparison reanalysis](findings/safety/intersection-only-sidewalk-risk-reanalysis.md): attributed
  the Palo Alto reanalysis to Lusk et al. and retained its assumed allocation of non-intersection crashes.

Revised [Interpreting cycling injury risk](topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) to distinguish infrastructure associations
from causal effects and kilometre-based EMR rates from hour-based inpatient/death rates.
