# NYC full-BRT theoretical speed benchmark

## In brief

- A Spring 2025 report proposes 13.2 mph as an average theoretical full-BRT speed on analyzed NYC bus-priority
  corridors, not a measured post-upgrade result.
- The benchmark combines Google Maps travel times at 3 a.m. with 12 seconds per bus stop; passenger-dependent boarding
  delay and real implementation constraints limit its interpretation.
- Conflicting current-speed baselines in the report prevent treating the headline as one consistent forecast of
  additional gains.[^hook2025]

## Calculation and scope

Footnote 29 describes taking end-to-end mixed-traffic travel times when roads are generally free-flowing,
then adding 12 seconds fixed dwell per stop for slowing, doors and rejoining traffic. The authors treat
nighttime travel times as including signal delay and distinguish fixed dwell from passenger-dependent
boarding/alighting time. They report that 19 of 33 analyzed corridors could reach at least 10 mph.
This is a benchmark, not a calibrated peak-period bus operations simulation or a tested network design.[^hook2025]

Realized speeds depend on passenger loads, signal operation, intersection interference, accessibility,
stop configuration and service patterns. No uncertainty range or independently validated upgrade forecast
is supplied. International BRT examples are secondary comparisons with different street grids and operating
conditions, not matched controls for NYC.[^hook2025]

Table 2 and Figure 13 give current weighted speed as 7.5 mph; the executive-summary text uses 8.5 mph
and Figure 3 labels a bus-lanes/SBS average 9.2 mph. The conclusion refers to another roughly 4 mph on
SBS corridors. These populations/baselines are not reconciled, so they should not be combined into a
single percentage or time-saving estimate. No cycling, safety or all-person travel-time benefit is
measured by the benchmark.[^hook2025]
