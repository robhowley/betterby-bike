# Budapest bridge highlights report a five-year traffic elasticity of 0.5

## In brief

- Three study highlights report that the percentage change in induced road traffic over five years was about
  half the percentage capacity change on average across **eight major Budapest bridge capacity changes**
  (elasticity **0.5**); definitions, comparison design and uncertainty are not visible.
- They describe this relationship as relatively stable over a **55-year analysis period** and capacity reduction
  as having a similarly strong effect as expansion, but supply no separate estimates to assess that claim.[^highlights]

## What the reported measure means

Elasticity compares proportional traffic change with proportional capacity change. The reported 0.5 is not
50% of all traffic disappearing, an annual growth rate or a measured congestion reduction. The five-year response
window differs from the 55-year historical analysis span; neither supplies calendar dates in this crop.[^highlights]

## What cannot be assessed

Only the highlights were read. They do not identify the eight changes, the balance of expansions and reductions,
the traffic-count boundary, capacity units, counterfactual, other network changes or statistical uncertainty.
“Relatively stable” and “similarly strong” are the highlights' characterizations, not visible tests of temporal
stability or symmetry. Without methods, these claims cannot establish a causal response, distinguish diversion
from less travel, predict a particular lane removal or show improvements in speed, delay or cycling.

The [existing article excerpt](induced-traffic-excerpt-reported-ranges.md) mentions an unnamed Hungarian study.
Possible overlap is unresolved, so these are not independent confirmations. Its metropolitan lane-mile ranges
also differ in scope and timing from the Budapest bridge highlights and should not be pooled.
