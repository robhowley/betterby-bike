# CBC reported longer Bloor West driving times after a street redesign, without isolating the cause

## In brief

- CBC's October 2024 reporting quotes Toronto's Bloor West comparison as average driving-time increases
  of **2.4–4.4 minutes eastbound** and **1.5–3.6 minutes westbound**, depending on time of day, between
  Runnymede Road and Aberfoyle Crescent after bike lanes and other street changes.[^cbc2024]
- The comparison covers November 2022–March 2023 versus the same months one year later. It reports an adverse
  travel-time outcome, not proof that bike lanes alone caused it or that other corridors were unaffected.
- An interviewed researcher attributes increases to pandemic recovery. CBC supplies no controlled analysis
  demonstrating that explanation or quantifying the share attributable to the redesign.[^cbc2024]

## Measure and attribution limits

CBC quotes “average increases in motor vehicle times ... from 2.4 to 4.4 minutes eastbound and 1.5 to 3.6
minutes westbound,” followed by the time-of-day qualification. These are reported ranges of additional time,
not before/after total journey durations; do not subtract the endpoints to produce a single treatment effect.
The capture does not give the time-band table, sample sizes, baseline trip durations, uncertainty or untreated
comparison routes. The linked municipal dashboard was not independently read.[^cbc2024]

Shoshanna Saxe's objection is that the earlier period reflected lower pandemic-era travel and that travel times
rose elsewhere too. That is a competing explanation, not a reason to erase the observed increases or conclude
that the redesign had zero effect. Conversely, the project included measures beyond bike lanes, and before/after
association cannot assign all additional minutes to bicycle space.
