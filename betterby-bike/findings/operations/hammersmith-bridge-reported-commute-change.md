# Hammersmith Bridge survey reportedly found 21% of former car commuters stopped driving to work

## In brief

- Kruse reports that 21% of surveyed commuters who had driven over London's Hammersmith Bridge no longer drove
  to work in the weeks after it closed to vehicles other than buses and bicycles.
- This is a reported change among former bridge commuters, not 21% fewer car trips across London or a 21% shift
  to cycling. Sample size, follow-up losses and mode-specific shares are absent.[^kruse]

## Reported comparison

The paper describes the bridge as unable to carry its load of 30,000 vehicles a day and closed since “last February,”
without specifying the year. London's Transport department reportedly surveyed users a few days before closure,
then contacted the same people in the following weeks. Some work commuters switched to public transport and others
walked or cycled. Kruse reports that surrounding congestion had not markedly increased but supplies no measured
travel times, traffic-count series or congestion definition.[^kruse]

## Limits

The 30,000 vehicles/day is the bridge's reported prior load, not the survey sample or the denominator for the 21%.
Kruse does not give recruitment methods, response rates, attrition, uncertainty or a comparison group. The result
therefore cannot establish representative or lasting adaptation, count the trips eliminated rather than shifted,
or isolate which travel alternatives caused the change. This is secondary reporting; the original survey was
not independently reviewed.[^kruse]
