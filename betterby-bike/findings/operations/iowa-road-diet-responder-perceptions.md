# Iowa emergency responders reported mixed road-diet experiences

## In brief

- Around 14 Iowa 4-to-3 lane conversions in ten cities, 51% of 163 response-time answers recalled unchanged
  times, 33% slower and 16% faster.[^corcoran2024]
- Responders described both blocked center lanes and easier passage through added space. These voluntary,
  retrospective accounts are not timed trips or measured frequencies of obstruction.[^corcoran2024]

## Survey basis

Conversions occurred between 2015 and spring 2020. The emergency-responder survey analyzed **167 eligible responses
from 239 submissions**; the abstract's 170 conflicts with the main results.[^corcoran2024]

## Different questions measure different experiences

- **Driving safety (160 answers):** 38% perceived no change, 36% less safe and 26% safer.
- **Route preference (135 drivers):** 52% neutral, 34% preferred converted roads and 14% avoided them.
- **Keeping the conversion (161 answers):** 31% favored keeping it, 34% opposed and 27% were neutral. These reported
  categories do not exhaust the total; the questionnaire also offered an “Other” response.
- **Overall emergency-response impact:** Table 4 lists 41% negative/slightly negative, 22% no effect, 29%
  positive/slightly positive and 7% mixed after conversion. Section 2.2.2 frames these percentages around respondents
  aware of the project beforehand; the table does not state its denominator. Do not apply them to all 167 respondents
  or merge them with the response-time percentages.[^corcoran2024]

Open-text negative accounts described driver confusion, failure to yield and vehicles blocking the center turn lane,
forcing responders to maneuver around traffic. Positive accounts described wider lanes, space to pull over and use
of the center lane to pass. Two independent reviewers coded themes, with a third resolving differences. These
accounts suggest mechanisms worth investigating, not measured frequencies of obstruction or causal safety effects.
The authors recommend yielding education and pre-project communication, but did not test their effectiveness.[^corcoran2024]

## Local differences and limitations

Appendix B reports more favorable post-conversion opinions in Cedar Rapids than Des Moines. Its tables list **28%
negative/slightly negative in Cedar Rapids versus 56% in Des Moines**, and **36% versus 22% positive/slightly positive**.
The Des Moines narrative's 41% negative excludes the table's additional 15% slightly negative. Table headings give
city totals of 38 and 58, but do not resolve question-specific denominators or the prior-awareness restriction;
these percentages should not be converted into respondent counts.[^corcoran2024]

Participation was voluntary, retrospective and uneven across cities; about 58% of respondents came from Cedar Rapids
and Des Moines, and volunteer providers were poorly represented. Strong opinions could affect participation and recalled
expectations. The null [Cedar Rapids incident analysis](cedar-rapids-road-diet-response-rates.md) does not disprove
individual adverse experiences across ten cities, and the survey does not establish a measured delay.
