# Selected Manhattan traffic measures stayed level or improved alongside protected lanes

NYCDOT's 2014 report describes maintained or improved motor-vehicle performance, not a universal congestion effect:

- **Columbus Avenue, 96th–77th:** morning travel time (7–10 a.m.) fell from 4 minutes 38 seconds to 3 minutes, labelled
  −35%. Surveys were July 25, 2010 and July 19, 2011, two runs per hour. Separately, NYCDOT describes maintained vehicle
  volumes at 96th–95th, comparing June 4–10, 2010 with February 23–March 1, 2011.
- **Eighth Avenue, 23rd–34th:** daily average travel time (7 a.m.–8 p.m.) fell from 4:20 to 3:43, labelled −14%.
  AM peak fell 2:47 to 2:25 (−13%), midday 6:01 to 4:44 (−21%), and PM peak 3:38 to 3:10 (−13%). Before measurements
  were September 30–October 1 and October 6–8, 2009; after measurements November 16–18, 2010.
- **First Avenue:** NYCDOT describes level weekday taxi speeds through the project area, comparing November 2009
  with November 2010, 9 a.m.–4 p.m. Trips had at least 80% of their route on the stated segment; the chart covers
  segments from 14th to 59th Streets.
- **Manhattan central business district:** NYCDOT describes steady taxi speeds as the protected network expanded.
  The measure includes weekday yellow-taxi trips, 8 a.m.–6 p.m., Bowling Green to 59th Street, river to river,
  excluding listed major holidays. It is wider than treated corridors and excludes Columbus Avenue.[^nyc2014]

The Columbus chart labels times as minutes.seconds: 4.38 is not 4.38 decimal minutes. Its travel-time observations are
on two individual dates, and its separate volume comparison spans different seasons. Eighth Avenue also uses short,
seasonally different windows. Taxi samples are not all vehicles, and no uncertainty estimates or untreated travel-time
controls are supplied. These observations show that measured improvements can coexist with protected-lane redesigns;
they do not prove lanes alone caused faster trips or rule out delay at other times or places.
