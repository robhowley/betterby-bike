# NYC bus-priority package speeds

## In brief

- A Spring 2025 NYC report gives a weighted morning-peak route-speed increase from 7.0 to 7.5 mph (8.0%) across
  bus-priority categories, not a controlled causal effect.
- The sole busway/SBS combination, 14th Street, rose from 4.5 to 5.5 mph (22.3%); five busways without newly bundled SBS
  measures averaged 3.7%.
- Small, differently timed categories and mixed route outcomes prevent ranking individual lane or boarding treatments as
  independently proven effects.[^hook2025]

## Comparisons

Table 2 reports the following category summaries. Percentages and speed changes are retained as printed,
not recomputed from rounded category means.[^hook2025]

| Treatment category | Sample size | Reported change, mph | Reported increase |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Curbside lanes | 2 | 0.04 | 0.3% |
| Median lanes | 3 | 0.4 | 6.1% |
| Offset lanes | 7 | 0.5 | 6.9% |
| Boarding/stop-consolidation only | 2 | 0.6 | 7.6% |
| Offset lanes plus SBS measures | 8 | 0.6 | 10.2% |
| Service-lane median alignment plus SBS | 2 | 0.7 | 10.4% |
| Curbside lanes plus SBS measures | 3 | 1.5 | 18.9% |

The appendix retains adverse and null comparisons: Woodhaven's Q52/Q53 SBS average changes from 13.67
to 13.58 mph (reported −1%); Webster's Bx41 SBS changes from 10.15 to 8.39 mph (−17%); E.L. Grant's
Bx35 remains 5.08 mph. Kings Highway's B82 SBS rises from 6.83 to 8.29 mph (21%). These are route
comparisons, not proof that a particular lane design caused improvement or deterioration.[^hook2025]

## Interpretation limits

Speeds include dwell and signal time and generally cover whole routes, not just treated links. Older
projects use secondary NYC DOT evaluations; later ones use MTA data. The report does not supply a
controlled counterfactual, uncertainty intervals or a reproducible account of weighting. Categories can
reuse routes: Q44 SBS represents both Main Street and Archer Avenue busways. Their entries are not
independent replications. S79 lacks all-door boarding despite inclusion in a category bearing that label.
Table 2's 2023 labels conflict with appendix entries for 2024 installations. The p. 25 offset/SBS narrative
and some appendix arithmetic also differ from the table, so the table is reported rather than silently
harmonized.[^hook2025]

The report's Jay Street example contrasts a 1.5 mph PM-peak speed gain on the busway with a 1.5 mph loss
on its approach (October 2019 versus October 2023). Equal and opposite speed changes do not demonstrate
zero net journey-time change without segment lengths and comparable weighting. This is distinct from
the main morning-peak route comparison.[^hook2025]
