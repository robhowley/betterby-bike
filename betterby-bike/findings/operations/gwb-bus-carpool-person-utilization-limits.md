# GWB bus-carpool person utilization and its limits

## In brief

- On the New Jersey approaches to the George Washington Bridge, November 1986–October 1987 observations
  averaged 430 carpools and 70 buses using the reserved lane during 7–9 a.m.
- NJDOT estimated about 5,000 people in those vehicles, roughly 21.5% of main/lower-plaza people, using up to
  50 people per bus. This is estimated utilization, not a measured increase in person capacity or new riders.
- A further 2,500 estimated riders used existing bus-booth priority only. Counting them as beneficiaries of
  the new reserved approach would overstate its scope.[^powers1988]

## Counts, assumptions and denominators

Observers sampled auto occupancy and vehicle counts, with February and August omitted because of weather.
Table 1 assigns 1,500 people to 430 carpools (6.5% of people) and 3,500 to 70 lane-using buses (15%).
The report uses an approximate total of 23,000 people and 12,700 autos/buses; its summary identifies the
person-share denominator as the main and lower plazas. These are two-hour totals, not hourly lane capacity.
Truck volume and PIP approach traffic should not silently be included in this denominator.[^powers1988]

The further 50 booth-only buses account for an estimated 2,500 people (11%). Table 1 combines the groups
into 7,500 people (32.5%) in 550 vehicles receiving some priority, including both lane and booth-only users.
The table's percentages are rounded. Bus occupancy remains the substantive uncertainty: the prose says
“up to 50” per bus, while the table uses 50 throughout; about 57% of lane buses were commuter buses,
with the rest including charters and school trips. Actual passenger loads by service are not reported.[^powers1988]

## What this does not establish

The roughly mile-long facility extended earlier bus priority and used shoulder space where the express road
was narrowest. It is not evidence that converting a general traffic lane to a bicycle lane leaves all traffic
unaffected. The report describes uncongested priority travel, but its delay envelope uses four pre-opening
years, and quoted maximum savings vary between 7 and 8 minutes. No average causal journey-time effect or
net network person-capacity gain is identified. Utilization also does not establish mode shift: carpool
counts stayed roughly constant despite a small reported share increase.[^powers1988]
