# NYC loading-zone double-parking reduction is reported for some sites, not the whole program

## In brief

- Open Plans' June 2023 report says **some NYC neighborhood loading-zone pilot sites observed 73% less double
  parking**. It supplies no baseline counts, observation dates, site count or comparison-site results.
- This is secondary reporting of selected sites, not a 73% citywide reduction, a typical program effect or evidence
  of fewer cyclist injuries.[^openplans]

## Intervention and reporting basis

Neighborhood loading zones provide short-term curb space in residential areas for noncommercial loading and
passenger access, such as unloading groceries or picking up a family member. They differ from commercial loading
zones and from technology-based smart-loading-zone pilots. Open Plans cites NYC DOT's April 2023
[Loading Zone Expansion report](https://www.nyc.gov/html/dot/downloads/pdf/loading-zone-expansion-progress-report.pdf)
for the percentage; that primary report was not independently reviewed.[^openplans]

The percentage is described as a reduction, but Open Plans does not specify its denominator, observation duration,
or how the sites were selected. There is no table of outcomes across successful, unchanged and adverse sites,
no uncertainty estimate and no method for separating designation from signage, enforcement or other changes.

## Interpretation

Providing legal loading space is the proposed mechanism for reducing double parking. The same curb-use table warns
that without enforcement the zones may become ordinary illegal parking and lose their intended function. That is
an implementation warning, not a measured null subgroup. Open Plans recommends expanding loading zones, but the
reported favorable examples do not quantify an expansion's expected effect or prove reduced congestion or injuries.
