# Carmageddon reports toll-road forecast shortfalls without comparable study details

## In brief

- *Carmageddon* says the M6 Toll, opened near Birmingham in **2003**, was expected to carry **75,000 vehicles/day**
  but never rose much above **50,000/day**, falling to **30,000 cars/day during the 2008 financial crisis**.
  Forecast horizon, count sources and later observation dates are absent.
- The book also attributes average traffic overestimation of **around 20%** to Standard & Poor's and quotes a
  2014 *Transportation* article describing widespread shortfalls. Neither underlying study was reviewed;
  missing samples and denominators prevent treating 20% as a general correction factor.[^book]

## What the reporting supports

The M6 case compares a forecast with reported traffic, not equivalent tolled and untolled roads or measured
before/after pricing effects. The financial-crisis observation is a distinct economic period, not evidence that
tolls alone caused the drop. No toll price, control route, travel-time saving or mode-shift measurement appears.

The book quotes a 2002 Standard & Poor's statement that optimism is a consistent forecasting trend, followed by
the 2014 journal quotation that actual traffic in many countries rarely reproduced forecasts. No article title,
authors, country list, project sample, forecast horizon or uncertainty is provided. The 20% denominator is not
defined, so it should not be restated as actual traffic being 20% below forecast. Independence between the
quoted research accounts and the M6 example is unknown.[^book]

These are secondary reports supporting caution about forecasts, not a reviewed systematic estimate or proof
that all toll roads underperform. The [Source](/sources/carmageddon.md) consolidates the same book's later M6
reference rather than counting it again. [Street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) keeps
forecast performance separate from congestion relief and project value.

[^book]: Supplied tolls PDF p. 1, printed p. 117, M6 Toll paragraphs and paragraph beginning “Studies of toll roads”.
