# Green Line conclusion reports lower adjacent-road traffic, with a weakening effect

## In brief

- A Twin Cities Green Line LRT study conclusion reports **18% lower annual average daily traffic on adjacent
  road segments**, using an adjusted before/after comparison with control roads, not a citywide traffic estimate.
- The reported reduction weakened from **22% in the first two operating years to 16% in the next two**;
  calendar years are not supplied.
- Only a conclusion excerpt is available: supporting tables, uncertainty and detailed methods cannot be checked.
  Lower traffic volume does not establish shorter travel times or a bicycle-lane effect.[^greenline]

## Reported comparison

The excerpt describes two difference-in-differences models, one for before/after opening and one for changes
over three periods, with a “negative binomial link function.” Both adjusted for road classification, land use
and transit supply. The percentages are the authors' reported adjusted effects, not raw vehicle-count declines.
The excerpt supplies neither baseline AADT in vehicles per day nor segment counts, treatment/control boundaries,
model coefficients, confidence intervals or significance tests.[^greenline]

## Interpretation and limits

The 22% and 16% estimates are successive periods in the same study, not independent confirmations. The
conclusion attributes possible attenuation to induced development and travel switching from other routes or
modes; those mechanisms are interpretations, not separately demonstrated results here. Its forecast of further
attenuation is explicitly an untested hypothesis.[^greenline]

Without full methods, control comparability and before-treatment trends cannot be assessed. Local AADT does
not measure regional vehicle-distance traveled, congestion delay, transit mode shift, cycling or injuries.
The report's causal wording therefore remains attributed rather than independently verified. The publication's
authors, date and full title are also absent from the supplied page.[^greenline]
