# Connecticut simulations predict site-dependent costs of prohibiting right turns on red

## In brief

- CTDOT's Synchro comparison of existing operation versus NTOR at **18 intersections in five Connecticut
  locations** predicted widely differing operational impacts, not observed post-installation changes.[^ctdot2024]
- Modeled delay rose from **21.1 to 51.6 seconds (145%)** at Cheshire's I-691 westbound ramps but from
  **18 to 18.2 seconds (1%)** at Clinton's I-95 northbound on-ramp/N High Street. Selected peak periods and
  nonrepresentative sites limit transfer to other intersections or statewide policy.[^ctdot2024]

## Scenario results

Sites were chosen because recent Major Traffic Generator analyses supplied previously checked turning
counts and traffic conditions. Appendix tables cover Clinton evening peak, Cheshire morning peak,
Norwalk Saturday peak, and Vernon and Fairfield evening peaks.

At the Cheshire westbound ramps, modeled fuel consumption rises from 44 to 61 gallons/hour (39%) and
the sum of queue lengths from 1,246 to 1,529 feet (23%). At the Clinton northbound on-ramp, both fuel use
(6 gallons/hour) and summed queues (236 feet) remain unchanged at the reported precision. Clinton Crossing
Driveway's 60% delay increase is from 6.5 to 10.4 seconds, illustrating why relative changes need baselines.
The queue measure is a sum, not the maximum queue on one approach.[^ctdot2024]

## Limits and interpretation

No uncertainty intervals, full timing/calibration settings or representative sampling scheme are provided.
The report does not demonstrate how retiming, dynamic pedestrian-phase restrictions, route changes or
mode shifts would alter these estimates. Fuel predictions are not a measured emissions inventory.

CTDOT uses these results to support site-specific operational analysis and recommends against a blanket
ban. The simulations do not test that recommendation's net safety or social benefit. Claims that longer
queues induce risky behavior are possible mechanisms, not crashes measured by these scenarios.
Municipal accounts of dynamic-sign benefits similarly lack controlled before/after injury estimates.
