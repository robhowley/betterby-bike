# An unidentified article reports traffic growth after metropolitan lane-mile expansion

## In brief

- A cropped, unidentified article reports **3%–6% more traffic in the first few years** and **6%–10% in the
  “following five to ten years”** for every **10% increase in metropolitan lane miles**; study locations,
  dates and methods are unavailable.
- These are secondary reported ranges, not verified causal estimates, measured congestion changes or a forecast
  for a particular road. The excerpt does not define “traffic.”[^excerpt]

## What can be retained

The passage describes evidence spanning projects, regions, counties and states, but its opening is incomplete.
It does not give study counts, baseline volumes, untreated comparisons, statistical uncertainty or an explanation
of how the ranges were synthesized. The later range should not be added to the earlier one, annualized or assigned
more precise timing than the quoted wording supports. Lane miles measure road provision, not travel distance;
no basis is supplied for equating the unspecified traffic measure with vehicle miles traveled.[^excerpt]

The article's interpretation that roads fill before reconstruction goes beyond the numerical traffic-growth claim.
No speed, delay, reconstruction interval or capacity-utilization measure is visible. The assertion that a Hungarian
study finds a reverse response to capacity reduction has no visible magnitude or study identity and does not justify
applying the expansion ranges symmetrically to closures.[^excerpt]

## Evidence relationships

The [Source](/sources/induced-road-traffic-and-capacity-change-article-excerpt.md) records image-only, excerpt-only
access. Neither the full article nor the studies behind its visible link labels were read. Without study identities,
this cannot be counted as independent confirmation of [Kruse's reduction reporting](kruse-capacity-reduction-traffic.md)
or [Litman's congestion discussion](vtpi-congestion-measurement-limits.md).

The [street-redesign Topic](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) distinguishes changes in road supply and traffic
volume from congestion, travel delay and project-specific outcomes.
