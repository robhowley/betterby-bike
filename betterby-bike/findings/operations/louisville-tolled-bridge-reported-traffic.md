# Louisville's reported tolled-bridge decline is not regional traffic disappearance

## In brief

- Citing City Observatory, *Carmageddon* reports around **120,000 vehicles/day in 2010** on Louisville's John F.
  Kennedy Memorial Bridge and around **60,000 cars/day across two bridges** after expansion and tolling.
- The book reports **$2 per crossing**, with commuter discounts, but gives no follow-up count date or counting
  method. It suggests diversion to the nearby untolled Second Street Bridge, not measured disappearance of half
  the region's driving.[^book]

## Comparison and limits

The book describes a six-lane bridge connecting Louisville, Kentucky, and Jeffersonville, Indiana, on I-65,
followed by a $1 billion Abraham Lincoln bridge adding six lanes. It dates opening and toll introduction to
2017; that chronology is secondary reporting, not verified against primary records. Its claim that traffic
never returned to the previous level has no dated endpoint.[^book]

The before count concerns one bridge, the after count both bridges. The text shifts from “vehicles” to “cars”
without defining count categories. No control route, network traffic total, trip survey, measured delay, or
uncertainty interval is supplied. Capacity and pricing change together, so this account cannot isolate either
intervention's effect. The author acknowledges uncertainty about where drivers went, then proposes diversion
as the plausible explanation. That is not an observed mode shift or reduction in total vehicle travel.

The conclusion that the additional lanes were pointless is the author's interpretation, not a benefit-cost
analysis. The [Source](/sources/carmageddon.md) consolidates the book's later references to this same case;
they are not independent evidence. The original City Observatory article is not identified or reviewed.

[Street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) distinguishes local crossing counts from wider
traffic changes and travel-time effects.
