# McGuinness diversion and emergency-access claims preceded project evaluation

## In brief

- June 2023 Streetsblog reporting attributes to NYC DOT a forecast that removing a travel lane on McGuinness Boulevard
  could divert **30%–40% of traffic** to adjacent streets. No observed post-redesign traffic change or model methods are
  supplied.[^duggan2023]
- DOT said responding ambulances and police could use the proposed bicycle lanes. That describes a proposed access
  mechanism, not demonstrated passability or unchanged response times.[^duggan2023]

## What the article reports

The Greenpoint proposal would reduce four moving lanes to one per direction, adding protected bicycle lanes, pedestrian
islands, daylighting and block-level loading zones. The article attributes the diversion estimate to DOT's previous-year
presentation, naming Manhattan Avenue as a possible receiving street.[^duggan2023]

Officials separately estimated that 30%–50% of McGuinness drivers used it as a shortcut between the Brooklyn-Queens and
Long Island expressways. The article suggests those drivers might bypass the area. The shortcut share and diversion
forecast are different quantities; they cannot be subtracted to estimate neighborhood traffic reduction. The report does
not provide forecast periods, underlying counts, model assumptions or uncertainty.[^duggan2023]

Opponents claimed emergency services would be blocked. DOT's reply that ambulances and police could drive in bicycle
lanes supplies neither response-time records nor a test of usable lane width, entry points or obstructions. The article
does not establish either an emergency delay or a no-delay outcome, and its statement does not explicitly cover fire
apparatus.[^duggan2023]
