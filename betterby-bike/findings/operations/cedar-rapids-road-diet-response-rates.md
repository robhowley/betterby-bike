# Cedar Rapids road diets showed no significant adjusted emergency response-rate change

## In brief

- Cedar Rapids fire records from 2014–2020 showed no significant adjusted change after four 4-to-3 lane conversions
  in alarm-to-arrival minutes per inferred kilometre, not driving time alone.[^corcoran2024]
- The estimate put the before-conversion rate 2.4% above the after rate, but uncertainty included no change.
  This is not an established improvement.[^corcoran2024]
- Assumed routes, no untreated-road controls and [conflicting printed figures](cedar-rapids-response-measurement-limits.md)
  limit interpretation. The result does not prove no delay.[^corcoran2024]

## Measure and estimate

Lower minutes-per-kilometre rates mean faster responses, not call frequency. Table 7 reports a **before/after rate
ratio of 1.024 (Wald 95% CI 0.992–1.056)**, with after as reference.[^corcoran2024]

## Comparison and site variation

Table 6 reports **1,202 before and 2,665 after trips (3,867 total)** on 42nd Street, Johnson Avenue, Mt Vernon Road
and Wiley Boulevard. The Poisson model used log route distance as an offset and adjusted for site, season, weighted
lane count and speed limit, traffic signals, peak traffic and percentage of trip on a converted segment. Conversion-year
trips were excluded. A site-by-conversion interaction did not substantially improve model fit; the additive model
supplied the pooled estimate above.[^corcoran2024]

The interaction model's Figure 4 shows little change at three sites and an apparent faster post-conversion rate on
42nd Street. That site had only **14 before versus 493 after trips**, producing much greater pre-conversion uncertainty.
It should not be presented as a reliable site-specific time saving. Wiley Boulevard supplied about 61% of trips,
so the pooled evidence is not balanced across sites.[^corcoran2024]

## Interpretation and limits

This adds quantitative local evidence beyond favorable responder anecdotes, but not a controlled causal estimate or
an equivalence test. Non-converted roads were not comparison controls, routes were assumed rather than tracked, and
alarm-to-arrival time includes pre-departure activity. Effects on a short converted segment can be diluted in whole-trip
measures. The findings do not establish response performance for other cities or designs without a center turn lane.

The source's prose reports 3,872 trips and a 0.922 lower confidence bound, conflicting with Tables 6–7 used above.
These unresolved differences and other [measurement limits](cedar-rapids-response-measurement-limits.md) qualify
numerical reuse; both printed confidence intervals include one.[^corcoran2024]
