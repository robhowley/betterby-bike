# Congestion intensity does not measure total commuter burden

## In brief

- Litman's 2023 report illustrates that slower peak traffic need not mean more delay per commuter: its historical
  Table 1 gives New York **37 annual delay hours per commuter**, versus Houston's **48**, despite a slightly worse
  Travel Time Index (1.34 versus 1.33).
- The comparison weights auto-commuter delay by auto commute share. It is neither all residents' travel burden nor
  an estimate of cycling's effect, and combines sources labeled 2015 and 2009 rather than measuring 2023 conditions.[^litman]

## What the table measures

Table 1 cites Texas Transportation Institute 2015 and American Community Survey 2009 inputs. New York's 74 annual
hours per auto commuter multiplied by its 50% auto commute share gives 37 hours per commuter; Houston's 61 hours
multiplied by 79% gives about 48. New York ranks fifth by Travel Time Index but thirteenth by commuter delay;
Houston ranks sixth and fourth, respectively, among the table's 15 regions. The report's executive summary and
Table 2 use different historical values and should not be substituted into this calculation.[^litman]

The methodological point is that congestion intensity omits exposure: how far and how often people drive in
congested conditions. Weighting by auto share spreads estimated auto delay across commuters; it does not directly
measure pedestrians', cyclists' or transit riders' delays, nor include non-work travel. The report sometimes uses
“per capita” language, but this table's denominator is **commuters**, not the whole population.

## Implications and limits

Litman recommends assessing door-to-door access, person-delay across modes, trip distances and induced travel,
with explicit baseline speeds and time valuations. These are evaluation recommendations, not a controlled finding
that compact development or bicycle lanes caused New York's ranking. His separate illustrative bus-lane and cost
scenarios show how assumptions change an appraisal, not observed project effects. The underlying TTI and ACS
records were not independently reviewed; regional boundaries and mixed-year comparability cannot be verified here.[^litman]

For applications, distinguish this measurement issue from
