# Carmageddon's induced-demand percentages use different denominators

## In brief

- *Carmageddon* reports a tentative **2% increase in overall driving per 10% increase in road capacity** from an
  unnamed British Department for Transport-commissioned study drawing on U.S. and Dutch highway networks.
- Its separate urban estimates of **up to 40% after six months and 100% after four years** concern the share of
  **added capacity used**, not 40% or 100% growth in all driving. Study identities, dates and methods are absent.
- These are secondary reports from a partial book spread, not verified causal estimates, a local forecast or
  evidence that every widening returns to its former congestion level.[^book]

## What the numbers mean

The book describes the 2%/10% relationship as 20% of the capacity growth being used by more cars. It then contrasts
whole-network studies with urban estimates, explaining the four-year 100% as the entire extra capacity being used
again. The 20%, 40% and 100% figures should not be added, treated as percentage-point changes in mode share or
substituted for the existing article's metropolitan traffic-growth ranges.[^book]

The passage does not define how road capacity or the “amount of driving” was measured. It provides no baseline,
study sample, untreated comparator, statistical uncertainty, or time horizon for the 2% estimate. The urban
figures are presented as estimates “as high as,” not a documented average or guaranteed timetable. No speed or
delay table supports a numerical congestion effect.

## Interpretation and overlap limits

The author acknowledges that induced demand's scale and context are disputed, and quotes the commissioned study
as expecting higher responses on urban or highly congested routes. The explanation that alternative modes affect
travel choices is a proposed mechanism, not a measured cycling shift. The book also says induced demand is not
inherently bad, beginning an incomplete rural-access example; additional driving alone is not a welfare verdict.[^book]

The [Source](/sources/carmageddon.md) consolidates two excerpts of the same book. Only printed pages 114–115 support
this Finding; the full book and underlying studies were not reviewed. Missing study identities prevent establishing
independence from the [unidentified lane-mile article](induced-traffic-excerpt-reported-ranges.md) or
[congestion synthesis](vtpi-congestion-measurement-limits.md). The book's West Side Highway story repeats a
[previously represented closure case](kruse-capacity-reduction-traffic.md), not another capacity experiment.

[Street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) keeps capacity, traffic and delay measures distinct.
