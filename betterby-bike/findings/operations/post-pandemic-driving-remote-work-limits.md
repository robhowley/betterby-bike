# Post-pandemic driving growth does not establish a remote-work effect

## In brief

- Streetsblog reports StreetLight's estimate that
  **US per-capita vehicle miles traveled rose 12% from May 2019 to May 2024**, while
  **88 of the 100 most populous metros** had increases in total mileage.
- These aggregate trends occurred despite continuing remote work, but do
  **not establish that remote work caused increased driving** or quantify the effects of bike lanes, outdoor dining or
  congestion pricing.[^article]

## What was reported

The article also reports lower congestion in just two of the 100 metros, San Francisco and
Albuquerque, comparing spring 2024 with spring 2019. A scatterplot for the top 25 metros shows
a positive relationship between VMT change and congestion change, with San Francisco's VMT
roughly 14% lower. It does not estimate an intervention effect or imply that every mileage
reduction yields the same congestion response.[^article]

## Limits

This is secondary reporting of one StreetLight analysis, not independently reviewed data.
The article provides no remote-versus-office-worker comparison, causal identification,
sampling method, uncertainty intervals or detailed population denominator. National per-capita
mileage and metro total mileage are different measures. The congestion prose mixes percent
and percentage points; the plot labels percent change and places Los Angeles near zero despite
a caption claiming worsening in 24 of 25 metros. The captioned downtown plot is absent in the PDF.
These limitations prevent precise cross-metro congestion-effect comparisons from this capture.

The [Source](../../sources/2024-streetsblog-post-pandemic-driving.md) preserves the reporting and
interviews. The [street-redesign synthesis](../../topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#aggregate-driving-trends-do-not-identify-remote-work-effects)
explains why these trends are background context rather than project evaluation.

[^article]: Streetsblog, September 16, 2024, supplied PDF p. 2 (May-to-May national mileage and 100-metro comparisons), p. 3 (top-25 plot), pp. 4–5 (interpretations).
