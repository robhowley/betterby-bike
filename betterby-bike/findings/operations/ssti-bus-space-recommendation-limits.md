# SSTI's bus-space recommendation lacks transferable operating conditions

## In brief

- SSTI's February 2026 research coverage reports benefits from bus priority at high demand, describing **at least 40%**
  of road space and quoting **approximately half** as recommendations, not observed project effects.
- The article also reports benefits from more bicycle space at moderate traffic, but supplies no effect sizes, traffic
  thresholds, study setting or methods. These claims cannot establish a universal allocation rule or a measured cycling
  benefit.[^murphy2026]

## What is reported

The article attributes the bus-space argument to research linked as DOI
[10.1016/j.tra.2026.104884](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tra.2026.104884), not reviewed here.
It describes mutually reinforcing bus speed and ridership changes, and recommends limited, well-managed
bus access to general-purpose lanes during high demand. It does not quantify passenger attraction,
waiting-time reductions or the advantage over a specified baseline. The low-traffic comparison is
qualitative: more space for cars generally lets them move faster.[^murphy2026]

## What cannot be concluded

The two reported bus-space formulations are not a tested 40%–50% optimum interval. The article does
not define whether space means lane counts, width or network allocation, nor provide occupancy,
service-frequency, demand or mode-choice assumptions. It does not establish a measured causal feedback
between lane allocation and ridership. Its safety assertion points to separate earlier coverage and is
not a safety result from the newly linked study.[^murphy2026]

This differs from the [Baku model's accounting problems](baku-bus-lane-model-measurement-limits.md):
missing methods in secondary reporting cannot be filled with assumptions from a different study.
The [NYC route-speed comparisons](nyc-bus-priority-package-speeds.md) concern observed packages, not
confirmation of this allocation threshold. See [street-redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#bus-lane-simulations-are-screening-scenarios-not-observed-redesign-effects).

[^murphy2026]: SSTI, February 3, 2026, PDF pp. 1–2, demand-dependent road-space discussion and quoted study recommendations; primary study unreviewed.
