# Complete Streets policy scores do not rank street safety

## In brief

- Smart Growth America reports scoring 43 Complete Streets policies adopted in 2023–2024; San Antonio
  leads at 96/100, while New Jersey's Asbury Park and Red Bank rank ninth and tenth at 70 and 69.
- These are policy scores, not measured safety or cycling benefits. The captured resource page omits
  scoring rules, selection methods and implementation outcomes.[^sga]

## Reported comparison

| Rank | Jurisdiction or organization | Score / 100 |
| ---: | --- | ---: |
| 1 | San Antonio, TX | 96 |
| 2 | Nashville, TN | 89 |
| 3 | Clyde, OH | 85 |
| 4 | Bluffton, OH | 81 |
| 5 | Boca Raton, FL | 80 |
| 6 | East Baton Rouge Parish, LA | 78 |
| 7 | Appleton, WI | 74 |
| 8 | Rockford Metropolitan Planning Organization, IL | 72 |
| 9 | Asbury Park, NJ | 70 |
| 10 | Red Bank, NJ | 69 |

The page describes these as the highest-scoring policies within its review. It does not establish
that all adopted policies were assessed or supply enough detail to reproduce the scores. Cities,
a parish and an MPO appear together; this is not a comparison of equivalent road-user populations.[^sga]

## Interpretation and limits

The publisher anticipates safer travel and more mobility options, but the capture supplies no crash,
exposure, mode-share or completed-project data. A lower score therefore does not demonstrate greater
road danger, nor does a higher score demonstrate implementation or a causal benefit. The full linked
report was not reviewed, so these limits describe what can be concluded from the captured page.

The [Asbury Park speed pilot](design/asbury-park-right-turn-speeds.md) measures a different outcome
and period. [State active-travel funding provisions](access/us-2024-active-travel-funding-equity-policies.md)
are likewise a separate policy inventory, not replication of this ranking.
