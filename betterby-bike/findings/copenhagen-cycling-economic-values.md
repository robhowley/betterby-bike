# Copenhagen cycling economic values were reported as 4.80 or 10.09 kroner per kilometre

## In brief

- The Copenhagen Post's June 2018 account attributes to finance-ministry figures an economic gain of 4.80 Danish
  kroner per kilometre cycled in Copenhagen, or 10.09 kroner if it replaces an equivalent car journey.[^gadd2018]
- These are alternative values, not amounts to add or measured cash savings. WEF repeats the figures, not an
  independent valuation study.[^fleming2018][^gadd2018]
- The methods and baseline for the lower value are unspecified. Neither figure measures the return from a
  particular cycle path.[^gadd2018]

## Valuation scenario

WEF's October 2018 account repeats these values and multiplies them by 1.4 million daily bicycle kilometres to
report a potential **US$1.05m–2.17m daily benefit**. That is a valuation scenario, not observed savings; its upper
end applies the car-replacement value to all that distance without measuring displaced car travel. This repeats
the finance-ministry attribution, not an independent valuation study.[^fleming2018]

## Interpretation and limits

The article does not identify the calculation precisely or provide its methods, included costs and benefits,
price year, uncertainty or baseline for the 4.80-kroner value. The ministry material has not been reviewed.
Do not reinterpret these values as cyclists' income, government cash savings, business sales or measured returns
from a particular cycle path. Applying the larger figure requires a car journey to be displaced; the article
provides no estimate of how often that happens.

The [ULI report](/sources/2016-active-transportation-real-estate.md) separately presents a Copenhagen network-return
forecast. A project return and a per-kilometre valuation are not interchangeable or independent confirmations
of the same result.
