# Reduced-driving US energy and health scenarios

## In brief

- UCS's 2024 US scenarios assume 20%/40% less light-duty driving in 2050 than a net-zero pathway with
  EIA-projected demand; all scenarios meet the same 2050 climate target.
- Table 1 estimates $110/$201 billion less energy-system capital investment over 2025–2050 and
  $32–64/$63–128 billion in air-quality health savings (2024 dollars, discounted at 2%).
- These are conditional modeled benefits, not observed mode shift or returns to a bicycle-lane program;
  the narrative and table disagree on some monetary totals.[^freedom2024]

## Comparisons and results

The low/high reductions correspond to about 3%/27% less total light-duty VMT in 2050 than in 2023,
not 20%/40% less than today's driving. Economy-wide energy modeling builds on UCS's earlier clean-energy
analysis. The report associates reduced demand with transit, walking, cycling and land-use changes,
without estimating the effectiveness of a specified intervention package.[^freedom2024]

Table 1 reports 188/376 GW (4%/8%) less electricity-generating capacity needed in 2050 and
2,850/5,700 TWh less cumulative electricity demand over 2025–2050. Capacity (GW) and cumulative energy
(TWh) are different measures. Estimated cumulative EV lithium demand falls 250,000/530,000 metric tons
(7%/15%). The table's on-road air-quality health estimates include 2,200–4,500/4,500–9,000 avoided deaths
for low/high reductions over 2025–2050; these are not crash fatalities.[^freedom2024]

## Limits

The table's $110/$201 billion energy capital figures differ from p. 11's $108–197 billion. The table is
used here without claiming to resolve that difference. Its health ranges are reported model ranges, not
confidence intervals from an intervention trial; detailed uncertainty calculations and the referenced
Table A-1 were not available in the supplied main report. Underlying models were not independently read.

These are savings in selected accounts, not a complete cost-benefit calculation including the transit,
walking/cycling and land-use changes needed to deliver the assumed demand reductions. Nor do national
aggregates demonstrate who receives the savings, maintained access or a cycling-only emissions effect.
Do not automatically add energy, health and [household savings](access/us-freedom-to-move-household-savings.md)
into a net-benefit headline.[^freedom2024]
