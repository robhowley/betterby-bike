# Per-passenger carbon comparisons need occupancy and accounting boundaries

## In brief

- Statista's June 2024 secondary chart reports 170 grams CO2 equivalent per passenger-kilometer for
  petrol cars, 97 for buses, 47 for electric cars and 35 for domestic trains.[^article]
- Missing occupancy, data-year and accounting assumptions prevent treating these as universal local
  emissions factors. No walking or cycling figure is supplied, and no mode-shift outcome is measured.

## What the comparison measures

The denominator is one passenger traveling one kilometer, not one vehicle-kilometer. The chart also
reports 171 for diesel cars, 68 for plug-in hybrids and 4 for Eurostar. Its source credit is OWID,
ICCT and the British government via Visual Capitalist. These are secondary reported values, not
independent replications or a reviewed primary dataset.[^article]

The accompanying text explicitly cautions that electricity supply, technologies and transport networks
vary by country. It discusses electric cars' lifetime footprints, but the chart does not specify that
all modes share a lifecycle boundary. Occupancy and the trips actually replaced would be needed to
apply per-passenger values to a local intervention; uncertainty and common geographic scope are also
unspecified. Greenhouse gas emissions should not be read as a ranking of particulate exposure or
other pollution harms.
