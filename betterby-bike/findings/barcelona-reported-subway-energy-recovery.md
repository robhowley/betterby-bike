# Barcelona reports recovered braking energy supplying one-third of subway train energy

## In brief

- In 2024 Grist reporting, Barcelona's MetroCHARGE director said regenerative braking supplied 33% of train energy; this
  is an operator account without published measurement methods, not an independently evaluated project effect.
- A 41% share after further inverter installation, about 3,885 metric tons of annual CO2 savings and four-to-five-year
  payback were expectations, not demonstrated outcomes.[^donback2024]

## What was reported

Recovered braking electricity supplies trains, station equipment and neighborhood EV chargers.
Three inverters were reportedly installed and 13 in progress, with four chargers opened in July and
three more planned. The reported project cost was €7.8 million ($8.6 million); expected payback combines
energy savings with charging revenue, rather than energy savings alone. Barcelona already had
regenerative braking on all trains, reportedly since the 1980s, so the 33% share cannot be treated as
an incremental saving caused entirely by MetroCHARGE.[^donback2024]

## Limits

The article supplies no absolute electricity totals, pre-project baseline, monitoring period, emissions
factors or uncertainty. Its opening allocation claim that one-third of recovered energy goes to trains
uses a different denominator from the director's 33% of train demand. The expected end-of-September
rollout is not confirmed by the October 7 republication. A separately reported 1.8°F temperature decrease
has no documented measurement design. None establishes a general return for transit investment,
renewable generation, mode shift or cycling infrastructure.

This is distinct from [global electric two-/three-wheeler oil displacement](electric-two-wheelers-reported-oil-displacement.md):
recovering rail energy is not replacing car travel. See [street-redesign climate evidence](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#climate-targets-are-not-project-emissions-results).
