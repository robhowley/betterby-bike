# Melbourne bicycle parking reportedly supported more retail revenue per space and time

*Protected Bike Lanes Mean Business* reports **69 cents in retail revenue per hour per square foot of bicycle
parking**, compared with **19 cents for on-street car parking**, labeling the result Melbourne, Australia, 2008.
It cites Allison Lee's 2008 University of Melbourne master's thesis, *What is the Economic Contribution of Cyclists
Compared to Car Drivers in Inner Suburban Melbourne's Shopping Strips?* The accompanying prose describes a full
bicycle corral as about four times as lucrative per unit of space and time as full car parking.[^business]

This concerns retail spending associated with parking space, not parking fees, business profits or a measured sales
increase after replacing a car space. The report does not reproduce occupancy assumptions, turnover measurements,
sample size, currency denomination, uncertainty or conversion from the original study's units. Its prose explicitly
says 19 and 69 cents, while the graphic displays a cent sign with .19 and .69. Values here follow the prose; the
notation discrepancy is not resolved without the underlying thesis, which was not reviewed.

CityLab's 2015 roundup links Alison Lee's thesis and reports hourly spending of about **$27 for drivers versus
$16.20 for cyclists**. Assuming **six bicycles fit in one car space**, it multiplies cyclist spending to **$97.20 per
hour** and attributes the economic-gain argument to Lee. This is a capacity-based comparison, not an observed return
from converting a space. It does not resolve occupancy, turnover, currency or the earlier report's unit notation,
and repeats the same underlying research rather than adding an independent study.[^citylab2015]

The same page of the business report describes ten bicycle spaces replacing one car space
outside San Francisco's Green Apple Books.
That interview account is not the Melbourne measurement and does not establish a fourfold return for the bookstore.
