# Five NYC Open Streets had stronger restaurant sales recovery than low-outdoor-dining controls

## In brief

- By June–August 2021, restaurant/bar taxable sales on five selected NYC Open Streets averaged 19% above
  pre-pandemic baseline, versus 29% below on nearby controls with low outdoor-dining participation.[^recovery]
- Chinatown sales still fell 8%. Against separate high-outdoor-dining corridors, only three of five Open
  Streets had faster growth; two were similar.[^recovery]
- These are selected-corridor recovery comparisons, not an isolated street-closure or bicycle-lane effect.

## Evidence

Baseline sales are average quarterly taxable sales during March 1, 2017–February 29, 2020. The study follows
six pandemic quarters through August 31, 2021; its overview frames recovery at summer 2021. Matching considered
street size, restaurant/bar numbers, traffic lanes, transit proximity and earlier economic trends.[^recovery]

| Neighborhood and Open Street | Main control | Open Street sales change | Control sales change | Borough sales change |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Astoria: Ditmars Boulevard, 33rd–36th | Ditmars Avenue, 28th–33rd (report's name) | +44% | −7% | +6% |
| Park Slope: 5th Avenue, Union–4th | 7th Avenue, Union–4th | +26% | −17% | +6% |
| Prospect Heights: Vanderbilt, Atlantic–Park Place | Flatbush, Dean–8th Avenue | +20% | −40% | +6% |
| Koreatown: E 32nd, Broadway–Madison | W 36th, 6th Avenue–Madison (report's name) | +15% | −49% | −22% |
| Chinatown: Pell, Bowery–Mott; Doyers, Pell–Bowery | Bowery, Grand–Chatham Square | −8% | −31% | −22% |

The reported +19%/−29% average is a 48-percentage-point contrast, not a 48% causal increase or a citywide
sales-weighted total. The additional high-Open-Restaurants comparisons are described only as three faster and
two similar; neither corridor identities nor magnitudes or statistical tests are supplied.[^recovery]

## Limits

No sales denominators, uncertainty intervals or detailed estimation methods are given. “Significantly” is the
report's wording, not a documented statistical result. Nonrandom selection, additional dining capacity and
uneven pandemic recovery prevent isolating Open Streets from other influences. Inflation and seasonal adjustment
are not documented; the baseline averages multiple quarters rather than specifying a same-season comparison.
Local sales may reflect displaced spending, prices or business turnover, not net citywide gains, profits or
cyclist spending. Only full-closure, early-participating corridors meeting privacy thresholds were selected.
