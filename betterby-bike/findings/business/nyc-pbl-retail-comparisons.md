# Ninth and Columbus Avenue retail sales grew faster than named comparison corridors

## In brief

- NYCDOT's 2014 presentation reports sales growth from before construction to two years after: 47% on a Ninth Avenue
  segment, versus 43% on Tenth and 23% on Seventh.[^nyc2014]
- A Columbus Avenue segment grew 20%, versus 12% on Amsterdam and 9% on another Columbus segment; exact boundaries
  appear below.[^nyc2014]
- These repeat a 2013 analysis, not a new study; missing comparison methods and other local changes prevent isolating
  protected-lane effects.

## Evidence and limits

NYCDOT's September 2014 presentation attributes these combined retail-sales comparisons to its December 2013
*The Economic Benefits of Sustainable Streets* report:[^nyc2014]

| Project corridor | Project sales growth | Comparison site 1 | Comparison site 2 |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Ninth Avenue, 23rd–31st | 47% | Tenth Avenue, 16th–26th: 43% | Seventh Avenue, 16th–23rd: 23% |
| Columbus Avenue, 77th–96th | 20% | Amsterdam Avenue, 77th–96th: 12% | Columbus Avenue, 70th–77th: 9% |

Both projects outgrew their named comparisons, although Ninth Avenue's margin over Tenth Avenue was only four
percentage points. The slide calls these similar corridors but does not supply matching procedures, baseline sales,
business counts, uncertainty or calendar periods. These are aggregate sales changes, not profits, business survival or
spending by cyclists. Other street changes and local economic differences prevent attributing the difference to a
protected lane alone.

The 2013 economic report was not independently reviewed. This presentation is secondary reporting of that analysis,
not another independent study. Its Ninth Avenue 47% result uses different comparisons from the 2012 brochure's
“up to 49% versus 3% borough-wide.” Retain both with their own scope rather than treating one as a correction or
counting them as independent confirmation.[^nyc2014]
