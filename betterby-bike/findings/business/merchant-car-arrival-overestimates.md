# Dublin and Bristol merchant estimates overstated customers' car arrivals

CityLab's 2015 roundup reports two separate comparisons showing why merchant beliefs cannot substitute for customer
travel surveys:[^citylab2015]

| Setting | Merchant estimate of car arrivals | Reported customer car-arrival share | Scope supplied in prose |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Grafton Street, Dublin | 13% | 10% | One of two shopping avenues; sample size unstated |
| Henry Street, Dublin | 19% | 9% | The other Dublin avenue; sample size unstated |
| Bristol | 41% | 22% | 840 customers and 126 merchants surveyed |

Dublin merchants also underestimated bicycle patrons, without numerical shares in the prose. In Bristol, merchants
estimated 6% arrived by bicycle versus a reported 10%; they also thought customers lived farther away than they did.
The article describes similar misperceptions in Graz but supplies no prose figures, so Graz is not another quantified
comparison here.

These are distinct local studies, not a pooled estimate of merchant error. Survey dates, response rates, uncertainty
and detailed sampling are absent from the article's prose, and primary reports were not reviewed. Mode-share gaps
do not measure spending, prove that all merchants misjudge customers, or establish the sales effect of removing parking.
