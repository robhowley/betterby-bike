# Selected NYC Open Streets retained more pre-pandemic restaurant and bar tax filers

## In brief

- In summer 2021, pre-pandemic restaurant/bar filer retention on selected Open Streets versus nearby controls
  was 86% versus 67% in Manhattan and 92% versus 82% in Queens.[^recovery]
- Brooklyn Open Streets retained 88%; control retention is 80% in the report's prose but 81% in its table.
  Neither value can be resolved from the supplied report.[^recovery]
- Continued tax filing is a survival proxy, not proof that Open Streets prevented those closures or saved a
  specified number of jobs.

## Evidence and limits

NYC's Department of Finance tracked pre-pandemic restaurants and bars still filing in summer 2021. Manhattan
combines Chinatown and Koreatown Open Streets, Brooklyn combines Park Slope and Prospect Heights, and Queens
covers Astoria. These are studied corridors grouped by borough, not all businesses in each borough. Controls
were selected for similar street and business characteristics but had low Open Restaurants participation.
The report gives a March 2017–February 2020 baseline and March 2020–August 2021 study period.[^recovery]

Although the table is headed “Percent Change in Pre-Pandemic Filer Retention,” the accompanying explanation
identifies these as shares of pre-existing businesses retained, not percentage changes in retention rates.
No underlying cohort counts, uncertainty intervals or detailed rules for temporary non-filing are provided.
Nonrandom selection and heterogeneous pandemic impacts limit causal interpretation. Aggregated retention can
coexist with business losses, and stakeholder claims of otherwise inevitable closure are testimonials rather
than measured counterfactuals. The executive summary's broader comparison with borough-wide survival is not
quantified in this table and should not replace its actual corridor comparator.
