# Selected transit-connected Phoenix and Austin neighborhoods reportedly outpaced home-price benchmarks

## In brief

- Realtor.com reports **194% and 208% home-price increases in 2019–2025** in two Phoenix neighborhoods along light rail,
  versus **159% citywide**. These are the article's stated increases, not independently verified estimates.[^conte2026]
- Near UT Austin, it reports **26% growth in 2022–2025**, versus a **10% fall in Austin's median home price**. Selected
  locations, unclear price definitions and absent controls prevent attributing these differences to transit or
  walkability.

## Reporting and limits

Conte attributes the Phoenix figures to Realtor.com data and describes the locations as transit-connected ZIP codes,
but supplies no ZIP identifiers, transaction counts, price series or adjustment methods. The article also gives
**168% growth near UT Austin versus 135% across the metro**, without explicitly restating the period for that pair.
Its benchmarks vary between city and metropolitan geography. These figures should not be silently reinterpreted
as price-index levels or pooled into a common access premium.[^conte2026]

Economist Jake Krimmel interprets walkable transit access as scarce and desirable, with a post-pandemic return of
interest in selected downtowns. That is an explanation offered by an interviewee, not a controlled estimate.
Home mix, wider market changes and other amenities could contribute. No rents, displacement, resident savings,
cycling infrastructure effect or causal impact of transit opening is measured.
