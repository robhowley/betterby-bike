# Green Lane business-visit responses were mostly unchanged and varied by city

## In brief

- In a 2013 five-city survey around new protected routes, most riders and nearby residents reported no change in
  business visits; 19% of 1,005 intercepted bicyclists stopped more often and 1% less often.[^monsere2014]
- Of 1,771 residents answering the general business question, 12% were more likely to visit and 9% less likely;
  Chicago's Dearborn and Milwaukee corridors had unfavorable balances.[^monsere2014]
- These were self-reported visits or intentions, not sales, with no before survey or matched control group.

## Evidence and limits

| Respondent group and measure | Increased/more likely | Decreased/less likely | Reported n |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Intercepted bicyclists: frequency of stopping at shops | 19% | 1% | 1,005 |
| Residents who bicycled on the facility: stopping frequency | 20% | 6% | 718 |
| Residents generally: likelihood of visiting a corridor business | 12% | 9% | 1,771 |

The first two questions asked about frequency attributed to the protected lane. The third asked about likelihood since
construction, not actual purchases. These groups are not independent replications and their measures should not be
pooled as a spending effect.

## Mixed local results

Among residents generally, Dearborn in Chicago had 8% more likely versus 11% less likely to visit businesses (n=193);
Milwaukee had 10% versus 21% (n=294). Other surveyed cities had favorable balances, including Barton Springs in Austin
at 27% versus 2% (n=86). These site differences should accompany any overall favorable claim.[^monsere2014]

## Limits

The wider survey received 2,283 resident responses (23% of deliverable mailings) and 1,111 intercept responses
(33% of distributed postcards); business questions had the smaller denominators shown above. These are selected-route,
self-reported responses without matched controls or a before survey, vulnerable to nonresponse, recall and selection.

The study did not establish revenue, profits, employment or business survival effects. A planned establishment-level
sales-tax analysis was not completed in this report because suitable data/facilities were limited; follow-up economic
work was proposed. No later analysis was reviewed here.
