# Selected NYC Open Streets had lower reported storefront vacancy than surrounding neighborhoods

## In brief

- A DCP graphic reproduced by Streetsblog in November 2024 shows vacancy of 8.1%, 6.7% and 3.3% on
  Vanderbilt, Orchard and Columbus Open Streets, versus 13.3%, 16.0% and 8.0% in their neighborhoods.
- Separately, a program volunteer reported 7.1% vacancy on Berry Street versus 15.1% across Williamsburg.
- These selected occupancy comparisons do not establish that Open Streets reduced vacancies or increased sales;
  baseline trends, comparable survey methods and causal controls are absent from the article.[^lebowitz2024]

## Reported comparisons

| Corridor | Corridor storefronts | Vacancy | Neighborhood comparison | Neighborhood vacancy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Vanderbilt Avenue | 86 | 8.1% | Prospect Heights | 13.3% |
| Orchard Street | 149 | 6.7% | Lower East Side | 16.0% |
| Columbus Avenue | 121 | 3.3% | UWS–Lincoln Square | 8.0% |

These figures were read from the reproduced DCP graphic, not the underlying report. Neighborhood storefront
counts, measurement dates and uncertainty are not provided. The article's “around half” caption generalizes
across differing contrasts and should not be read as a program-wide estimate.[^lebowitz2024]

North Brooklyn Open Streets volunteer Brent Bovenzi supplied the separate Berry Street comparison after
walking the corridor. He said three vacant shops had signs announcing new occupants; those remained vacant
in his account. Neither his storefront denominator nor the comparability of his count with Williamsburg's
rate is documented. This is not a fourth DCP measurement.[^lebowitz2024]

## Interpretation and limits

Occupancy does not measure sales, profits, jobs or net citywide business creation. Nonrandom corridor
selection, preexisting commercial strength and remote-work patterns could affect the comparisons. The
article supplies no before/after control design to separate these influences from Open Streets. Interviewees'
claims that pedestrian priority benefits businesses and deserves more funding are interpretations, not
additional independent estimates.

The [2022 restaurant-sales comparison](nyc-open-streets-sales-recovery.md) uses a different period,
population and outcome. These figures broaden the measures considered, rather than replicate that estimate.
See [street-redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) for the synthesis.
