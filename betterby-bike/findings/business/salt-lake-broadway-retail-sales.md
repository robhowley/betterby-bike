# Salt Lake City's Broadway sales grew after a redesign that removed some parking

ULI attributes to Salt Lake City's transportation department a retail comparison following a **nine-block Broadway
Avenue upgrade**. It removed **30% of on-street parking**, added protected bike lanes and improved sidewalks and
crosswalks. Sales in the **first six months of the following year rose 8.8%** against the same six months a year
earlier, compared with **7% citywide growth**. ULI also reports **59% of business owners supported** the improvements
and **18% opposed** them.[^uli2016]

The sales comparison is a **1.8-percentage-point growth difference**, not an 8.8% causal effect of removing parking.
ULI does not give calendar years, business counts, baseline sales, survey denominator, uncertainty or matching methods.
Citywide trends supply context but cannot remove all local economic differences. The combined intervention does not
isolate the bike lanes or parking removal; sales do not measure profits, survival or spending by cyclists.

The underlying municipal study was not independently reviewed. Business-owner support is a separate survey measure,
and the opposition should be retained alongside the favorable sales comparison.[^uli2016]
