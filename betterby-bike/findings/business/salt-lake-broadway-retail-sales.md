# Salt Lake City's Broadway sales grew after a redesign that removed some parking

## In brief

- ULI and Streetsblog report 8.8% retail-sales growth on Salt Lake City's nine-block Broadway redesign versus 7%
  citywide, after walking improvements and protected lanes replaced 30% of on-street
  parking.[^uli2016][^streetsblog2015]
- The 1.8-percentage-point growth difference does not isolate bike lanes or parking removal. Both accounts repeat the
  same municipal evidence, not independent studies.
- The comparison period is unresolved: Streetsblog says the first halves of 2013 and 2015; ULI describes a
  year-over-year comparison.[^streetsblog2015][^uli2016]

## Evidence and limits

ULI attributes the comparison to Salt Lake City's transportation department. Broadway's upgrade improved sidewalks
and crosswalks. ULI describes sales in the **first six months of the following year** against the same six months
a year earlier. It also reports **59% of business owners supported** the improvements and **18% opposed** them.[^uli2016]

Streetsblog's October 2015 account identifies sales-tax data. Its two-year comparison differs from ULI's wording;
the discrepancy remains unresolved without the underlying analysis. Streetsblog adds that part of the road went
from five travel lanes to three, alongside planters, public art, colored pavement and crossing improvements.[^streetsblog2015]

The sales comparison is a **1.8-percentage-point growth difference**, not an 8.8% causal effect of removing parking.
Neither account supplies baseline sales, a business panel, uncertainty or matching methods. Citywide trends supply
context but cannot remove all local economic differences. The combined intervention does not isolate bike lanes or
parking removal; sales do not measure profits, survival or spending by cyclists.[^uli2016][^streetsblog2015]

The underlying municipal study was not independently reviewed. The
[merchant survey](salt-lake-broadway-merchant-responses.md) is a separate measure, including opposition and reported
sales declines alongside the favorable aggregate sales comparison.
