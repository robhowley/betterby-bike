# Salt Lake City's Broadway sales grew after a redesign that removed some parking

ULI attributes to Salt Lake City's transportation department a retail comparison following a **nine-block Broadway
Avenue upgrade**. It removed **30% of on-street parking**, added protected bike lanes and improved sidewalks and
crosswalks. Sales in the **first six months of the following year rose 8.8%** against the same six months a year
earlier, compared with **7% citywide growth**. ULI also reports **59% of business owners supported** the improvements
and **18% opposed** them.[^uli2016]

Streetsblog's October 2015 account identifies **sales-tax data for the first half of 2013 versus the first half of
2015**, reporting the same 8.8% and 7% values. That is a two-year comparison, unlike ULI's year-over-year wording.
Both accounts describe the same municipal evidence, not independent confirmations; the period discrepancy remains
unresolved without the underlying analysis. Streetsblog adds that part of the road went from five travel lanes to
three, alongside planters, public art, colored pavement and crossing improvements.[^streetsblog2015]

The sales comparison is a **1.8-percentage-point growth difference**, not an 8.8% causal effect of removing parking.
Neither account supplies baseline sales, a business panel, uncertainty or matching methods. Citywide trends supply
context but cannot remove all local economic differences. The combined intervention does not isolate bike lanes or
parking removal; sales do not measure profits, survival or spending by cyclists.[^uli2016][^streetsblog2015]

The underlying municipal study was not independently reviewed. The
[merchant survey](salt-lake-broadway-merchant-responses.md) is a separate measure, including opposition and reported
sales declines alongside the favorable aggregate sales comparison.
