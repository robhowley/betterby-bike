# South Carolina's 2020 plan does not isolate cycling expenditure or its funding gap

## In brief

- SCDOT's July 2020 plan reports **$5 million annually for pedestrian/bicycle safety**, approximately
  **$7.4 million annually for eligible Transportation Alternatives projects**, and **$138 million annually
  for broader MPO/COG system upgrades** that can include walking/cycling. These are different program
  scopes, not amounts that can all be counted as cycling expenditure.
- Walking/cycling needs are embedded in broader highway-modernization and regional-program categories.
  The 2019–2040 funding-gap table supplies **no separate bicycle/pedestrian row**, so it cannot establish
  a cycling-only shortfall or whether cycling needs were fully funded.[^scdot]

## What the plan reports

The Pedestrian and Bicycle Safety Program was established in 2018. Page 69 describes the $5 million
annual allocation as supporting high-crash assessments under engineering, enforcement, education and
emergency response. It is not solely a bicycle-infrastructure construction budget. Transportation
Alternatives funds support eligible cultural, historic and environmental transportation projects and
are described as typically used for bicycle/pedestrian facilities, without a mode-specific allocation.
The $138 million MPO/COG program is divided among 21 entities for rural and urban system upgrades.[^scdot]

Needs estimates include planning, design, right-of-way and construction costs, drawing on deficiencies
and adopted MPO/COG/local plans. Sidewalks fall under primary/secondary highway modernization;
additional accommodation needs fall under the MPO/COG assessment. SCDOT describes future work to
standardize data collection and project prioritization, not an already complete comparable statewide
cycling-needs inventory.[^scdot]

## Interpretation limits

Table 11-2 reports the broader MPO/COG program at $138 million annualized funding against $238 million
annualized need, a $100 million yearly gap ($2.2 billion over 2019–2040). That is **not a bicycle/pedestrian
gap**. Nor does the lack of a separate mode row mean that no active-travel funding exists.

These are historical planning statements, not audited expenditures, verified current commitments,
completed facilities or measured outcomes. The reviewed text does not support adding the program
amounts into one cycling total or comparing their cost-effectiveness. Broader revenue/summary wording
inconsistencies are retained in the Source rather than silently reconciled.[^scdot]
