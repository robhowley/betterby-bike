# Green Lane riders rated vertical buffers more comfortable than paint alone

In a 2013 intercept survey along protected lanes in Austin, Chicago, Portland, San Francisco and Washington, D.C.,
respondents rated generic diagrams with buffers more comfortably than a standard striped lane. On a six-point scale,
more than 80% selected 4–6 (the comfortable half) for each buffer design. Planters, flexposts and raised curbs received
the highest shares in the top two categories; vertical separation rated above paint-only buffers.[^monsere2014]

Ratings of actual facilities likewise favored separation. On a different four-point agreement scale for whether the
buffer “makes me feel safe,” Table 8-5 reports 3.60 for Dearborn's parked-car buffer, 3.58 for Oak/Fell flexposts,
and 3.04–3.06 for paint-only sections in Portland and Milwaukee. These are perceptions, not crash-resistance measures:
flexposts scored highly despite providing little physical protection from vehicle intrusion.[^monsere2014]

## Limits and interpretation

The generic-diagram task is a stated preference, not a trial of riding every design. Actual-site comparisons mix buffer
width, bicycle space, traffic conditions and rider populations; they cannot isolate buffer material. Respondents already
rode these facilities, so people deterred from using them may be missing. The overall intercept survey had 1,111
responses (33% of distributed postcards); the displayed buffer figures/table do not supply item-level sample sizes.

Comfort can matter for willingness to cycle without establishing an injury reduction. The authors' recommendation to
provide more protection to increase comfort should not be converted into an estimated safety effect.
