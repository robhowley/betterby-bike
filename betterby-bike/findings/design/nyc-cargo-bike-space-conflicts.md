# NYC cargo-bike drivers reported conflicts in both bike lanes and general traffic

## In brief

- In Curbed's September 2025 Brooklyn reporting, Amazon cargo-bike drivers described cyclists telling them to leave bike
  lanes and motorists objecting to their slower speeds in general traffic. One said the bike lane felt much
  safer.[^chang2025]
- These are selected interviews, not a conflict-rate or crash-risk comparison. The article explicitly lacks
  cargo-bike-specific traffic and safety data; wider lanes are a proposed response, not an evaluated remedy.[^chang2025]

## What the account establishes

Reporter Clio Chang observed delivery vehicles in Brooklyn and Manhattan and interviewed drivers, alongside accounts of
public-rulemaking opposition and Transportation Alternatives' support for replacing larger trucks. Reported vehicle
dimensions and speed restrictions help explain the space-and-speed dispute but do not establish which facility is
safest. One driver's enthusiasm for wider lanes documents a preference, not an observed change in comfort or safety
after construction.[^chang2025]

The account has no systematic sampling, number of encounters, travel exposure, before/after comparison or injury series
for cargo bikes. DOT's cited overall micromobility injury decline cannot establish cargo-bike effects. The article's
secondary European delivery-speed comparison concerns a different setting and outcome, and does not resolve the NYC
safety question.[^chang2025]

## Relationship to other evidence

Unlike the [Green Lane comfort surveys](green-lanes-buffer-comfort.md), this account does not quantify preferences. Both
belong in the
[street-redesign synthesis](../../topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#comfort-support-and-correct-use-are-different-outcomes)
without treating comfort, operational conflict and injury risk as interchangeable.
