# Boston queue-box abstract reports noncompliance without usage rates or safety effects

## In brief

- Ahammed et al.'s abstract reports noncompliance in two-stage turn queue-box use at **three Boston intersections
  containing eight boxes**, based on video trajectory analysis of left-turning cyclists; observation dates are
  unstated.[^ahammed]
- No rider counts, usage percentages, definition of noncompliance or comparison estimates are supplied. The
  abstract does not establish how common this behavior was, whether it violated traffic law, or whether boxes
  improved safety or comfort.[^ahammed]

## What was examined

The authors first inventoried design elements of **81 boxes**, then collected video at the three intersections.
The inventory count is not a cyclist denominator or 81 independent behavioral sites. The trajectory analysis
examined positioning and use of the eight boxes for left turns. Although the abstract explains that boxes can
also support right turns from left-side bicycle facilities, it does not report a right-turn analysis.[^ahammed]

## What cannot be inferred

The reported noncompliance is a qualification to assuming installed treatments will be used as intended, not a
measured failure rate. The abstract says the result contrasts with earlier studies but neither identifies them
nor supplies comparable measures. It does not report a before/after or untreated-site effect estimate, site-level
rates, sampling duration or uncertainty. No specific design feature can be identified as the cause from the
available material.[^ahammed]

Safety and comfort are the authors' stated motivations, not reported outcomes. Their suggestion that the work
could inform standards and education is not evidence that either intervention changes behavior or injuries.
The complete supplied screenshot was read, but the full study, supporting tables and detailed limitations were
not available in it.[^ahammed]
