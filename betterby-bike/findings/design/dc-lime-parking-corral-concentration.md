# DC Lime parking became more concentrated near new corrals

## In brief

- DC Lime trip-end concentration near parking corrals increased **60% after installation**; **93% of locations
  improved**. Riders had to lock to infrastructure but were not required to use these corrals.
- This GPS before/after measure is a relative change in local parking concentration, not 60 percentage points, a
  physical parking-compliance audit or a measured reduction in pedestrian injuries.[^lime2024]

## Measure and results

Researchers compared trip ends within **20 meters** of each corral location against those within **50 meters**,
before and after installation. The smaller radius accommodates GPS drift; the larger approximates a distance
from which a rider could see a corral. Figure 14 shows roughly 28% before and 45% after, consistent with the
reported 60% relative increase. These approximate bar heights are not exact tabulated values.[^lime2024]

The report identifies on-street placement, intersection daylighting and high-volume bike routes as common
features of high-use corrals. Those observations and design recommendations are not separately estimated
causal effects. The 93% improving share also means improvement was not universal; outcomes for remaining
locations are not broken out.[^lime2024]

## Limits

GPS proximity cannot show that a scooter was physically inside a corral, correctly locked or clear of pedestrian
space. The report supplies no untreated-site counterfactual, uncertainty estimate or detailed observation dates
for this parking comparison. Local demand and simultaneous program changes can affect concentration. Suggested
operating-mileage and pedestrian-safety benefits were not measured.[^lime2024]
