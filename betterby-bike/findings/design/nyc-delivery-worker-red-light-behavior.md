# NYC delivery workers reportedly waited for green more often than other riders

## In brief

- Streetsblog reports that 55% of observed NYC delivery workers waited for green, while 58% of
  recreational/commuter riders disobeyed a red light in a late-2025 Hunter College study.[^article]
- Among workers, 27% stopped then continued and 18% continued at speed. These observations do not
  establish that workers had the lowest crash or injury risk.[^article]

## Scope and interpretation

The reported study observed more than 1,700 micromobility riders at 155 intersections over three weeks,
using 56 researchers; delivery workers represented approximately 40% of observed vehicles. The sample
included bicycles, e-bikes, mopeds and scooters, not only pedal cyclists. The red-light subgroup counts,
classification rules and uncertainty are not supplied by the article.[^article]

The 58% comparison combines noncompliance behaviors; it should not be compared directly with workers'
18% proceeding at speed. The article distinguishes stopping before proceeding from running a light at
speed and quotes the study describing stop-then-go behavior as illegal but not necessarily unsafe.
No injury outcomes or exposure denominators establish the headline's “safest” ranking. Livelihood and
experience explanations come from the researcher interview, not a tested mechanism.[^article]
