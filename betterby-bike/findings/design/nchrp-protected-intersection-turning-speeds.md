# Two protected-intersection comparisons showed movement-specific speed differences

## In brief

- In NCHRP's 2018 Salt Lake City observations, southbound right turns averaged **12 mph at a protected
  intersection versus 13 mph at a nearby comparison**, based on only 15 and 42 vehicles.[^report]
- Left turns were slower at the protected Salt Lake City site but faster at Berkeley's; Berkeley had no usable
  right-turn comparison. These site associations do not establish a general speed or injury reduction.[^report]

## Measurements

Radar speeds were adjusted for the angle to vehicle movement, excluding angles above 30 degrees. The
report used one-tailed unequal-variance t-tests; the displayed means are rounded mph.[^report]

| Movement and city | Protected mean (n) | Comparison mean (n) | Reported p-value |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Southbound right turn, Salt Lake City | 12 (15) | 13 (42) | 0.0260 |
| Left turn, Salt Lake City | 14 (43) | 15 (84) | 0.0034 |
| Left turn, Berkeley | 17 (85) | 15 (59) | 0.0004 |

Protected sites were 300 South/200 West in Salt Lake City and The Alameda/Hopkins in Berkeley; comparisons
were 300 South/200 East and The Alameda/Marin, respectively. This was a between-site comparison, not a
before/after speed evaluation.[^report]

Berkeley's treated right turns averaged 14 mph (n=179), but comparison measurements exceeded the angle
threshold. Salt Lake City's northbound right turns were also excluded. The authors' expectation that other
right-turn directions would behave similarly is not an observed result.[^report]

Bicycle speed samples after exclusions were only 5 versus 5 in Berkeley and 2 versus 11 in Salt Lake City.
Neither comparison was significant. Some Berkeley readings were zero, and the radar was not recommended
below 10 mph, so these nulls do not establish equal bicycle speeds. Site geometry, grades and selective
movement coverage prevent isolating protected-intersection design or inferring corridor travel delay.[^report]
