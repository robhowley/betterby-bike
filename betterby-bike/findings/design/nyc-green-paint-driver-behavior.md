# NYC green lanes had less reported motorist encroachment, but not less standing

NYCDOT's September 2010 observations, conducted from 4–6 pm on Manhattan and Brooklyn streets, reported lower motorist
encroachment on solid-green curbside bicycle lanes than on unpainted lanes. Neither group had physical protection; two
of three controls were not curbside.[^nyc2011]

| Reported aggregate measure                          | Green | Unpainted |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ----- | --------- |
| Motorists driving on the bicycle-lane boundary line | 7%    | 16%       |
| Motorists driving inside the bicycle lane           | 4%    | 7%        |
| Motorists standing in the bicycle lane              | 1%    | 1%        |
| Lane clear when cyclists were present               | 96%   | 93%       |

These are the report's percentages, not independently reconstructed estimates. Chart 2 lists aggregate counts of 623
cyclists and 3,027 cars for green lanes and 626 cyclists and 2,437 cars for controls, but the green totals exclude the
fifth listed site. The exact denominators for clear-lane observations are not supplied, and some clear/obstructed
percentages conflict. See [measurement limits](/findings/design/nyc-green-paint-measurement-limits.md) before using the
aggregate figures.[^nyc2011]

## Site comparisons were mixed

Brooklyn's Clinton Street curbside comparison recorded 881 cars and 117 cyclists at the green site versus 1,112 cars and
112 cyclists at the unpainted site one block north. Driving in the lane was 1% versus 11%, driving on its line 7% versus
16%, and clear-lane observations 98% versus 83%.[^nyc2011]

Bleecker Street recorded 985 cars and 177 cyclists at the green site versus 910 cars and 315 cyclists at the unpainted
site about two blocks east. Line encroachment was lower (5% versus 15%), but driving inside the lane was slightly higher
(2% versus 1%), and the lane was less often clear when cyclists were present (97% versus 100%). Standing was 0% at both
sites.[^nyc2011]

## Interpretation

The overall pattern suggests better lane-boundary compliance, not a universal improvement in every behavior or location.
NYCDOT reported no apparent change in in-lane driving associated with cyclists' presence. Cyclists' anecdotal belief
that paint deterred parking should not replace the observed null standing/parking comparison.[^nyc2011]

This cross-sectional study did not isolate paint from street layout, traffic or route differences, and reported no
confidence intervals or significance tests. It did not measure crashes or injuries attributable to the treatment, so
lower encroachment is not a quantified safety benefit.
[Interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) explains that distinction.
