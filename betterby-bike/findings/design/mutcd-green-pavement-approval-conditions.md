# Green pavement supplemented required markings under IA-14

FHWA's 15 April 2011 interim approval permitted optional green pavement in marked bicycle lanes and specified lane
extensions to make them more conspicuous. It did not mandate green pavement or allow it to replace required
longitudinal lines, words, symbols or arrows. Within lanes, green could cover all or part of the length, or form a
rectangular background behind markings.[^memo]

For extensions across intersections, driveways, ramps and the beginning of turn bays, green could supplement a pair of
dotted boundary lines, not replace them. It could cover all or part of the extension or follow the dotted pattern,
filling only between opposing dotted segments.[^memo]

## Conditions and material guidance

Jurisdictions had to request and obtain FHWA approval, agree to the technical conditions, and maintain an inventory of
locations. A state could request approval for all its jurisdictions. The required agreement included restoring sites
to MUTCD compliance within three months after a final rule, and terminating use if the jurisdiction determined that
significant safety concerns were attributable to the device; FHWA also reserved the right to terminate approval for
indications of safety concerns.[^memo]

The memo specified daytime and nighttime chromaticity coordinates and a daytime luminance factor Y of 7–35.
Retroreflectivity was permitted but neither required nor recommended. It advised selecting applied marking materials
to minimize loss of traction for cyclists.[^memo]

These are historical approval conditions, not verified current design requirements. They define permitted use, not
proof that green pavement reduces collisions. See the
[approval's evidence limits](mutcd-green-pavement-evidence-limits.md) and
[Interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).
