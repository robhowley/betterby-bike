# Asbury Park speed associations differed by traffic direction

## In brief

- In Asbury Park's March–April 2022 pilot, temporary delineated lanes were associated with roughly 4–5% lower average
  speeds for Cookman through traffic than no lane. Perpendicular Asbury traffic showed no significant reductions.[^younes2024]
- Paint alone was associated with about 5.1% lower top speeds for Kingsley-to-Cookman through traffic, an exception to
  the abstract's right-turn-only summary. “Top” means each vehicle's 95th-percentile speed in camera view.[^younes2024]
- This nonrandomized intersection comparison cannot isolate lane effects or measure crashes or whole-trip delay.[^younes2024]

## Movement comparisons

The pilot modeled six movements separately; the new lane ran on both sides of Cookman Avenue. The
[right-turn association](asbury-park-right-turn-speeds.md) was strongest.[^younes2024]

| Movement | Model observations | Delineated: log average-speed coefficient (SE) | Delineated: log top-speed coefficient (SE) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Kingsley to Cookman, straight | 1,799 | −0.045 (0.011), p < 0.01 | −0.088 (0.010), p < 0.01 |
| Cookman to Kingsley, straight | 2,928 | −0.055 (0.021), p < 0.01 | −0.037 (0.011), p < 0.01 |
| Asbury westbound, straight | 787 | 0.041 (0.040), not significant at 0.05 | −0.026 (0.017), not significant at 0.05 |
| Asbury eastbound, straight | 1,406 | −0.008 (0.034), not significant at 0.05 | −0.007 (0.017), not significant at 0.05 |
| Asbury to Cookman, left turn | 1,029 | 0.015 (0.033), not significant at 0.05 | −0.021 (0.012), p < 0.10 only |

The Cookman through-movement estimates correspond to roughly 4–5% lower average speeds and about 8.4% and 3.7%
lower top speeds, respectively, as summarized by the authors. Perpendicular Asbury traffic did not show significant
reductions, and the left-turn top-speed estimate met only the paper's weaker p < 0.10 threshold. Null results are
not proof of exactly zero effect.[^younes2024]

Paint-only average-speed reductions were significant only for right turns. However, **Table 6 also reports a
paint-only Kingsley-to-Cookman top-speed coefficient of −0.052 (SE 0.013, p < 0.01)**, approximately 5.1% lower
(calculated as 100 × [exp(−0.052) − 1]). This qualifies the abstract's statement that paint-only speed reductions
occurred solely for right turns. Other non-right-turn paint-only top-speed estimates were not significant at
p < 0.05.[^younes2024]

All models adjusted for directional hourly flow, stopped/free-flowing behavior, weekend and signal functionality.
“Top” is each vehicle's within-trajectory 95th-percentile speed. Directional contrasts support the authors' local
traffic-calming interpretation, but traffic movements at one intersection are not independent study replications
or randomized controls. Short sampling, differing geometry and temporary materials limit generalization; the
models do not estimate citywide traffic delay or crashes.
