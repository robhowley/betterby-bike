# NJDOT bicycle-space and merge instructions

## In brief

- The supplied NJDOT manual says curb extensions should not intrude on bicycle operating space and transverse rumble
  markings should stay outside cyclists' operating space.
- It also directs on-street bicycle lanes to end before roundabouts and at the start of mid-block median-island tapers,
  with cyclists merging into traffic. These historical instructions are not evidence that merging improves safety or
  comfort.[^manual]

## Different treatments, different bicycle arrangements

Section 15.3.3 says bicycle lanes should not be marked within a roundabout's circulatory roadway. Off-street
facilities should go around the intersection entirely or may end before it so cyclists mix with traffic. At a
mid-block median island, an approaching bicycle lane must end at the beginning of the taper. The Choker text
repeats a merge instruction explicitly worded for a mid-block island; it should not be generalized to every
possible choker layout.[^manual]

Section 15.3.4 says curb extensions may only be used with full-time upstream on-street parking and specifies a
1.5-foot offset from through traffic, alongside the instruction not to intrude on bicycle operating space.
Transverse rumble markings must avoid bike lanes, shoulders, shared parking lanes or wide curb lanes where
cyclists normally ride; where there is no bike lane or shoulder, it specifies a 3-foot minimum and 4-foot maximum
clear distance between rumble stripe and pavement edge.[^manual]

## Limits

These statements come from the BDC15MR-01 traffic-calming chapter of a 2015-titled manual containing later
revisions. They are not verified current requirements. Figure dimensions and other chapters were not reviewed,
so this is not a complete design specification. No comparison of cyclist crashes, conflicts, accessibility or
comfort across these arrangements is reported in the reviewed chapter.

A project described as accommodating cycling therefore need not maintain a continuous separated route.
That distinction matters when interpreting [street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md),
but the manual does not measure the consequences of a gap or merge.
