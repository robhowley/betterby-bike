# NYC observed sidewalk riding was lower beside protected lanes, but other behaviors were mixed

## In brief

- A late-2025 NYC observational study reported sidewalk riding of 2.8% beside protected lanes,
  7.4% beside painted lanes and 12.9% where no lane existed.[^article]
- Other behavior differences were mixed. These are location comparisons, not before/after changes,
  evidence that lanes caused compliance, or measured pedestrian-injury reductions.[^article]

## Reproduced table

Streetsblog reproduces Hunter College's Table 2 for micromobility observations. The article describes
155 intersections and bikes, e-bikes, mopeds and scooters, not a bicycle-only sample.[^article]

| Measure | No bike lane | Painted lane | Protected lane |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Column sample shown | 824 | 500 | 338 |
| Riding on sidewalk | 12.9% | 7.4% | 2.8% |
| Continued through red at speed | 26.1% | 35.5% | 21.1% |
| Paused/slowed then continued | 28.5% | 18.4% | 31.8% |
| Stopped and waited for green | 45.5% | 46.1% | 47.1% |
| Yielded to pedestrians at congested crossings | 68.8% | 58.1% | 69.7% |
| Speed above 15 mph | 23.4% | 27.8% | 26.5% |

Protected-lane sites had less at-speed red-light running than either comparison, but full waiting was
similar across categories. Yielding was similar to no-lane sites and higher than painted-lane sites.
The above-15-mph share was higher than at no-lane sites, so the table does not support uniformly lower
speeds with protection. The article's broad claim of decreased recklessness needs these qualifications.[^article]

## Limits

The column samples sum to 1,662, while the article reports more than 1,700 riders overall. Missing-data
or inclusion rules and behavior-specific eligible counts are not explained; the heading counts should
not be assumed to be red-light or congested-crossing denominators. No uncertainty, adjusted comparisons,
site-selection details or speed-measurement method are supplied. Rider mix, traffic and location may
confound comparisons. Feeling safer is the researcher's interpretation, not a reported survey outcome.
Only the article and its reproduced table were reviewed, not the full underlying study.[^article]
