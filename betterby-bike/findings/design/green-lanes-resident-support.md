# Most surveyed Green Lane neighbors supported more protected lanes

Among nearby residents responding to a 2013 survey in Austin, Chicago, Portland, San Francisco and Washington, D.C.,
75% supported building more protected lanes elsewhere and 21% disagreed. Support included 69% of primary car/truck
commuters. Overall, 91% supported separating bikes from cars, a broader proposition than a specific lane design.
Table 9-2 reports a total n=2,277, including 920 car/truck commuters.[^monsere2014]

Support varied: 64% of Chicago respondents supported more protected lanes, versus 76–80% in the other cities.
Neighborhood desirability reportedly increased for 43% and decreased for 14%; remaining responses were no change
or no opinion, not necessarily endorsement.

## Stated willingness is not subsequent behavior

Overall, 62% agreed they would be more likely to ride if separated from motor vehicles by a barrier. Agreement was
85% among residents classified as “Interested but Concerned,” based on cycling interest, ability and comfort questions.
This identifies an interested audience; it does not mean 85% became new cyclists or predicts an 85% ridership increase.
The typology is a survey classification, not a measured demographic population.[^monsere2014]

## Limits

The resident survey received 2,283 responses, 23% of deliverable mailings, from selected neighborhoods near new lanes.
Respondents were older, more often homeowners and more educated than nearby Census populations. The results are not
citywide opinion estimates; nonresponse and differing site samples may affect the pooled percentages. Support can
coexist with specific objections about driving, parking and crossing safety.
