# Asbury Park delineated lanes were associated with lower right-turn speeds

## In brief

- For 2,655 right-turning vehicles in Asbury Park's 2022 pilot, delineated lanes were associated with about 21%
  lower average speeds and 27.6% lower top speeds than no lane.[^younes2024]
- Paint-only reductions were smaller: about 11% and 14.3%, respectively. Both comparisons are against no lane,
  not a direct test of delineators against paint.[^younes2024]
- “Top” means each vehicle's 95th-percentile speed in camera view. The nonrandomized pilot also changed lane
  width and turning geometry, so it cannot isolate delineators or establish injury reductions.[^younes2024]

## Sampling and adjusted results

Vehicles turned right from Cookman onto Asbury Avenue in Asbury Park, New Jersey, across ten sampled dates in
March–April 2022. The temporary delineated treatment was a striped lane with cones and plastic delineators.[^younes2024]

| Condition versus no lane | Log average-speed coefficient (SE) | Log top-speed coefficient (SE) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Delineated lane | −0.234 (0.020) | −0.323 (0.012) |
| Paint-only lane | −0.118 (0.026) | −0.154 (0.015) |

All four coefficients have p < 0.01 in Tables 5–6. These are separate log-linear OLS outcomes adjusted for hourly
traffic flow in that direction, stopped/free-flowing behavior, weekend and signal functionality. Percentages reflect
exponentiated coefficients, not percentage-point changes. Both conditions use no lane as reference; the tables do
not provide a direct significance test of delineators versus paint.[^younes2024]

Descriptively, free-flowing right turns averaged **12.4 mph without a lane, 10.9 mph with paint and 10.4 mph with
delineators**. For vehicles classified as stopped at red, the corresponding averages were **5.5, 5.6 and 4.5 mph**:
paint alone did not lower that subgroup's unadjusted average. Mean per-vehicle top speeds for free-flowing turns were
25.3, 20.2 and 15.9 mph, respectively. These are not the adjusted percentage comparisons.[^younes2024]

“Top speed” means the 95th percentile of each vehicle's frame-by-frame speeds within camera view, not the fastest
vehicle or the 95th percentile across drivers. Average speeds include observed stopping. Neither outcome measures
whole-trip delay, impact speed or injury risk.

The pilot also narrowed traffic lanes, removed parking and changed turning geometry. A single nonrandomized
intersection comparison cannot isolate delineators or establish a durable effect of permanent lanes. Vehicle type,
presence of cyclists and temporary-material behavior were not fully accounted for. Safer conditions are the authors'
interpretation, not a measured crash reduction.[^younes2024]
