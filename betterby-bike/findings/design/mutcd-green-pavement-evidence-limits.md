# FHWA's favorable assessment did not quantify injury effects

In its 15 April 2011 IA-14 memorandum, FHWA said its review of available experimental green-pavement data found the
tested bicycle applications satisfactorily successful. It reported more accurate cyclist positioning across
intersections and conflict areas and no notable negative operational effects observed in the experiments. Cyclists
reported feeling safer, while motorists reported greater awareness of cyclists and their likely position in the
roadway. FHWA judged the treatment to have low risk of safety or operational concerns.[^memo]

This is FHWA's qualitative synthesis of experiments, not new independently measured results. The memo names U.S.
experimental jurisdictions but supplies no study-level comparison groups, dates, sample sizes, effect magnitudes,
uncertainty estimates or crash/injury rates. Feeling safer and positioning more accurately cannot be converted into a
percentage reduction in injuries; absence of reported notable negative operational effects does not establish absence
of harm.[^memo]

New York City appears among the authorized experimental jurisdictions. The memo does not identify the
[NYCDOT green-lane report](/sources/2011-nyc-green-paint.md) by title or map individual studies to conclusions, so it
should not be counted as independent confirmation of that report's
[driver-behavior results](nyc-green-paint-driver-behavior.md). The favorable federal assessment and NYC's mixed local
results address different levels of reporting, not necessarily contradictory evidence.

The [historical approval conditions](mutcd-green-pavement-approval-conditions.md) also required responses to safety
concerns and advised minimizing material-related traction loss. Approval was conditional permission, not a safety
guarantee. See [Interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).

[^memo]: FHWA (15 April 2011), Research on Green Colored Pavement for Bike Lanes and FHWA Evaluation of Results
    p. 2; safety-termination agreement p. 3; material guidance p. 4.
