# NYC bike-lane violation estimates are extrapolations, not measured enforcement benefits

## In brief

- Open Plans reports **5,763 blocked-bike-lane violations from 13 Manhattan traffic-camera feeds**, citing a
  February 2023 Vade analysis. This is sampled obstruction reporting, not a citywide census.
- The reported **6.8 million annual NYC violations and $780 million citation value** are extrapolations, not
  observed revenue or a measured benefit of automated enforcement. Sampling, camera exposure and scaling details
  are insufficient in Open Plans' account to reproduce them.[^openplans]

## What the report says

Open Plans describes a “657 day span,” a median of roughly 2.7 violations per camera per day, and extrapolated
annual totals of 2.5 million violations in Manhattan and 6.8 million citywide. It assigns potential citation values
of $285 million and $780 million respectively, and reports that NYC captures only 1% of bike-lane violations.
All belong to the same underlying Vade analysis, not independent corroborating observations.[^openplans]

The cited source is Vade's February 28, 2023
[New York City Captures 1% of Over 6 Million Bike Lane Violations Worth $780M Per Year](https://www.vade.ai/post/new-york-city-captures-1-of-over-6m-bike-lane-violations-worth-780m-per-year).
Only Open Plans' report was reviewed. Vade is also listed among its interviewed technology companies.

## Limits on reuse

Open Plans does not say whether 657 days describes elapsed time or summed camera observation days, nor provide
camera operating hours, site selection, detection accuracy, duplicate-event handling or the extrapolation formula.
The stated median is not an average that can be multiplied by all camera-days to reconstruct the total. Preserve
the printed wording rather than silently changing its denominator or deriving a corrected rate.

Manhattan camera locations cannot be assumed representative of the rest of NYC. Potential citations are not
collectible net revenue: detection, legal eligibility, collection, costs and behavior change remain unmeasured
here. The reported 1% capture figure depends on the extrapolated denominator, not a complete observation of
violations. No before/after enforcement comparison, reduction in obstruction duration, cyclist exposure measure
or injury outcome is supplied. Open Plans' conclusion that the estimates support automated enforcement is a
policy interpretation, not an estimated safety effect.[^openplans]
