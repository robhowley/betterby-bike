# Green Lane intersection designs differed in observed positioning and compliance

Monsere et al.'s 2013 video and survey evaluation distinguished **mixing zones**, where bicycles and turning vehicles
share space, from **turning zones**, where vehicles cross into a turn lane beside a marked through bicycle lane, and
intersections with separate bicycle/turn signal phases. Observed behavior varied substantially by design and site.[^monsere2014]

## Positioning did not always match stated understanding

- At L Street's post-restricted entry/through-bike-lane intersections in Washington, D.C., Table 7-14 reports correct
  lane use by 87% of turning motorists and 91% of through bicyclists.
- Portland's NE Multnomah/9th mixing zone with yield-entry markings had 93% intended turning-motorist lane use, but
  only 63% intended bicyclist use when a car was present; others passed in buffer space.
- Wrong-lane turning ranged from 1% to 18% across the compared designs, highest at Fell/Baker's green-skip mixing
  zone in San Francisco. That treatment had been replaced by the time of the report. The authors interpret this as a
  warning that extensive green marking may deter motorists from entering space they are meant to share.[^monsere2014]

These are site/design-specific percentages with different eligible movements, not percentages of the entire video
sample. Over 90% of surveyed bicyclists generally said they understood where to ride, but this did not guarantee
correct positioning on diagram questions or video.

## Signal separation still depends on compliance

At three Dearborn intersections in Chicago, bicycle-signal compliance was 77–93% and driver left-turn-signal
compliance 84–92%. Between 2% and 6% of motorists started on red but then waited in the intersection or crosswalk.
The denominators differ: bicyclists arriving on green were excluded, while driver percentages cover all turning
vehicles and only the first queued vehicle normally has the opportunity to violate. These percentages therefore do
not provide a fair direct ranking of driver versus cyclist compliance.[^monsere2014]

## Limits

The observational comparison did not randomize designs or control volumes, widths, signal timing and network context.
The report's recommendations for marked entry points and through bicycle space are interpretations of observed
behavior, not experimentally isolated design effects. Correct positioning is not itself an injury outcome.
