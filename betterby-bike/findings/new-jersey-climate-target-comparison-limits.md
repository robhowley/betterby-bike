# New Jersey's climate-target comparison and its limits

## In brief

- Rutgers' April 2025 report converts New Jersey's goals to **40% below 2015 net emissions by 2030
  and 76% by 2050**, versus its cited U.S. goals of **44–47% and 100%**. These are historical targets,
  not achieved cuts or verified current commitments.[^report]
- The authors judge the state’s 2050 goal closer to a 2°C pathway than to 1.5°C. This assumes New Jersey
  follows U.S. percentage reductions, without a state-specific model or any estimate of cycling benefits.[^report]

## Comparison basis

New Jersey's targets were 50% below 2006 by 2030 and 80% below 2006 by 2050. Using net emissions of
121.7 million metric tons CO2-equivalent in 2006 and 100.6 in 2015, the report gives target annual levels
of 60.9 and 24.3 million metric tons. It uses net accounting consistent with the state's progress reporting,
although the cited target instruments do not expressly specify net reductions.[^report]

Table 2 reports these U.S. modeled percentage reductions below 2015 for 2050:

| Scenario | Median | 10th–90th percentile model range |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 1.5°C, no/limited overshoot | 90% | 82–101% |
| 1.5°C, high overshoot | 80% | 66–96% |
| 2°C, >67% likelihood | 72% | 56–87% |
| 2°C, >50% likelihood | 53% | 36–82% |

The overlapping model ranges do not assign New Jersey a deterministic warming outcome. Values above 100%
mean net negative annual emissions. High overshoot allows greater early warming and requires stronger later
cuts/removals to return to 1.5°C. The authors recommend net-zero by 2050; that policy recommendation is
distinct from the modeled medians and from implementation feasibility.[^report]

## What cannot be inferred

Land-use sequestration accounting may differ across models and inventories. The report assumes comparability
and uses simplified harmonization rather than AR6's full procedure. It does not evaluate international
climate finance, feasibility, or sector/project contributions. State targets alone cannot establish how
much driving must shift to cycling or what a bike lane would save.

The main-table U.S. comparison is retained as reported: the appendix uses inconsistent 2015 U.S. inventory
values and gives 45–47% in one summary rather than 44–47%. No independent recalculation or current-policy
check was performed. The [Source](/sources/2025-new-jersey-greenhouse-gas-target-comparison.md) records
additional reporting inconsistencies.
