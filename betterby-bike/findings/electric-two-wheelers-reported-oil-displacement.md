# Reported global electric two- and three-wheeler oil displacement

## In brief

- A November 2023 commentary attributes about **one million barrels/day** of avoided global oil demand
  to electric two- and three-wheelers, roughly **1%** of global oil demand and **four times** electric cars'
  displacement, citing secondary estimates rather than presenting its own analysis.
- This concerns a reported fleet of over **280 million mopeds, scooters, motorcycles and three-wheelers**,
  not 280 million pedal e-bikes or a fourfold per-vehicle advantage. The underlying methods and precise
  estimate year remain unconfirmed.[^azhar2023]

## What the comparison means

Azhar and Uzair credit widespread uptake in China and other moped-using countries for the aggregate
advantage. Their body compares this fleet with over 20 million electric cars and 1.3 million commercial
EVs described as on the road “last year.” The fourfold wording refers to electric cars in the article's
opening, despite the headline/deck's broader “electric vehicles” wording.[^azhar2023]

The article attributes the million-barrel estimate to Bloomberg New Energy Finance and links its
2022 Electric Vehicle Outlook. “Last year” in this 2023 article suggests 2022, but the linked report's
date leaves the underlying estimate year unresolved. Neither that report nor its counterfactual oil-use
assumptions were reviewed. No uncertainty interval, bicycle-only contribution or mode-replacement
breakdown is supplied.[^azhar2023]

## Interpretation limits

Avoided oil demand relative to a counterfactual does not necessarily mean total observed oil demand
fell. It is also distinct from lifecycle greenhouse-gas savings, which depend on manufacturing,
electricity and the trips replaced. These global stock-based figures do not estimate the effects of
adding a bicycle lane or adopting an e-bike in a particular city.

[Evaluating street redesign outcomes](/topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md) distinguishes fleet context
from project-level travel and emissions evidence.
