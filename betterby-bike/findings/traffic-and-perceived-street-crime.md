# Traffic and perceived street crime in a British longitudinal survey

## In brief

- Leeds reports that residents perceived more street crime when heavy traffic increased, and less when
  traffic decreased, in a longitudinal survey of households in England, Scotland and Wales.
- The outcome is perceived crime, not counted burglaries, violence or vandalism. The news article gives
  no effect size or uncertainty interval, and does not establish a particular street treatment's benefit.

## Evidence and interpretation

The June 29, 2026 university article describes Understanding Society data from tens of thousands of
households, following residents over time and separately recording interviewers' assessments of heavy
traffic on the nearest road. Exact sample size, observation years, model controls and attrition are not
reported. Researchers interpret the relationship as causal and propose weakened community ties and
visible disorder as mechanisms. The coverage supplies neither enough methods to assess that causal
interpretation nor measured mediation effects.[^leeds2026]

Perceptions can capture experiences absent from police statistics, but cannot be converted into crime
incident counts or rates. The article's roughly 10% recorded-crime decline belongs to earlier London
low-traffic-neighbourhood research, not the nationwide survey. Neither that earlier research nor the
new paper was independently reviewed for this Finding.[^leeds2026]
