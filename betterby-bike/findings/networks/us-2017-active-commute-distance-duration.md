# US 2017 bicycle work trips averaged shorter distances than car work trips

## In brief

- In US 2017 NHTS estimates reproduced by ORNL, to/from-work trips averaged **2.72 miles and 21.79
  minutes by bicycle**, versus **12.21 miles and 25.47 minutes by car**.
- Walking work trips averaged **1.19 miles and 15.26 minutes**. These compare different observed
  journeys and travelers, not the time saved by changing mode on the same commute.[^tedb40]

## Measures and interpretation

Table 9.17 reports average work-trip length and duration by 21 modes, generated from the NHTS website.
A trip is a movement in public space between two identifiable points; these are not daily round-trip
commute totals. Car is a distinct row from SUV, van and pickup, so its mean should not be described as
an average for all private motor vehicles.[^tedb40]

The table does not provide route-matched comparisons, distance distributions, uncertainty intervals or
mode-specific sample sizes. A shorter observed cycling mean does not show how many car commutes are
feasible by bicycle, what their cycling duration would be, or whether safe routes exist. Differences in
who travels and where they go are not controlled.

## Distance measurement limits

The report's NHTS methods note says 2017 added online response and mapping, with derived trip distances
appearing about **10% shorter than self-reported trips**. It does not give a mode-specific correction
for this table. This warning prevents interpreting distance differences across survey editions as pure
behavioral change; it does not justify adjusting these means by 10%.[^tedb40]

These historical descriptive estimates do not establish cycling energy savings or an infrastructure
effect. See [trip-purpose distributions](us-2017-active-travel-trip-purposes.md) for non-work travel and
[commuting and access](../../topics/active-commuting-and-access.md) for the distinction between observed
journeys and usable alternatives.

[^tedb40]: Transportation Energy Data Book, Edition 40 (2022, updated June), p. 9–22, Table 9.17 and
    trip definition; p. 9–6, NHTS sampling, survey changes and online distance mapping.
