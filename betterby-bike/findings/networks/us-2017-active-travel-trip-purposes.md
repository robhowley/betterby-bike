# Most US walking and cycling trips in the 2017 NHTS were not work trips

## In brief

- In national 2017 household-survey estimates reproduced by ORNL, **20% of bicycle person-trips and
  6% of walking person-trips** were to/from work. These are purposes within each mode, not commute mode shares.
- **Other Social/Recreational** accounted for 31% of cycling and 30% of walking trips; shopping for
  12% and 14%. Work-only indicators therefore omit most recorded active-travel trips, but the survey
  does not measure unmet demand or the effect of street improvements.[^tedb40]

## What was counted

Figure 9.9 labels its totals as **3,575 million bicycle person-trips** and **38,947 million walking
person-trips**. These are expanded national estimates from sampled US households, not respondent counts
or counts of distinct travelers. Its Home To Work category is described as to/from work in the caption.
Work-Related Business is a separate category, at 1% for each mode.[^tedb40]

The figure also separates Visit Friends/Relatives (9% cycling, 7% walking) from Other Social/Recreational.
Consequently, the 31% cycling figure is not the share of all broadly defined social travel. Percentages
may not sum to 100 because of rounding.[^tedb40]

## Interpretation limits

These are 2017 trip-purpose distributions, not present-day patterns, per-person participation rates,
travel distances or shares of all US trips. No sample counts or uncertainty intervals accompany the
figure. ORNL reproduces NHTS tabulations, not independent confirmation of that survey. Neither the
purpose mix nor the trip totals establish infrastructure benefits, equitable access or car replacement.

See [ACS commuting measurement limits](acs-active-commuting-measurement-limits.md) for the distinct
worker denominator, and [commuting and access](../../topics/active-commuting-and-access.md) for synthesis.

[^tedb40]: Transportation Energy Data Book, Edition 40 (2022, updated June), p. 9–24, Figure 9.9,
