# Shared micromobility surveys report replacing walking as well as driving

## In brief

- NABSA's 2021 industry report summarizes user surveys as 37% car-trip replacement and 37% walking replacement;
  10% replaced transit and 4% were trips that otherwise would not occur.[^nabsa2021]
- These averages combine surveys from 2018–2021, with a broader sample for car replacement. They are not a
  representative 2021 trip census or directly measured vehicle-mile reduction.[^nabsa2021]

## What was compared

The printed p. 5 chart separates taxi/rideshare (17%) from auto driver/passenger (20%), alongside walking (37%),
transit (10%), new trips (4%), personal bikes (5%) and other modes (7%). “Other” includes other shared
micromobility, personal scooters and unspecified alternatives. Car replacement includes taxi and rideshare,
not just privately driven vehicles.[^nabsa2021]

Methods specify averages of published surveys from 19 systems/cities between 2018 and 2021, including a national
Bird dataset. Auto replacement additionally incorporates Atlanta, Kelowna and Santa Monica, which reported only
that statistic. Thus the rounded chart cannot be treated as a single common-sample probability distribution.
The methods do not supply respondent counts, weighting by North American trip volumes or uncertainty intervals.
Reported hypothetical alternatives do not directly measure displaced traffic or vehicle miles.[^nabsa2021]

The report cites Meroux et al. (2022) as suggesting higher auto replacement and longer displaced auto trips when
using end-of-trip push notifications. That is a secondary methodological comparison, not an independently reviewed
result or justification for replacing NABSA's displayed percentages.[^nabsa2021]
