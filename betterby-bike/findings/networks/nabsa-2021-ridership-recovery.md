# North American shared micromobility recovered partly in 2021

## In brief

- NABSA estimates 128 million North American shared-micromobility trips in 2021, about 53% above 2020 but 18%
  below 2019. More vehicles and cities did not mean annual ridership had fully recovered.[^nabsa2021]
- About 232,000 vehicles were deployed on an average day, 20% above 2019. These are compiled industry estimates,
  with incomplete smaller-system data and some fleet imputation, not complete observed counts.[^nabsa2021]

## Supporting measures

The report gives 83 million trips in 2020 and 157 million in 2019. Its 2021 total includes 62.2 million scooter
trips and 65.8 million bike trips (47.0 million pedal-bike and 18.8 million e-bike). Country totals are 107.6 million
for the U.S., 12.9 million for Canada and 7.5 million for Mexico. The average fleet grew from 194,000 vehicles in
2019 and 169,000 in 2020. At least 298 cities had service in 2021, versus 292 and 224 respectively.[^nabsa2021]

City coverage is not a count of separate modal systems: 97 cities had both bikeshare and scooters, and the report
lists 204 bikeshare and 191 scooter systems. The p. 2 chart labels its city-count series “systems,” but p. 1 and
the database definitions distinguish these units. The monthly recovery comparison uses a named subset of systems,
not necessarily the same universe as annual North American estimates.[^nabsa2021]

## Measurement limits

Trip estimates combine agency/operator surveys and online data; some smaller-system trip data were unavailable.
Missing vehicle counts were estimated from system trips, utilization and service days for the technology type.
The reported 2.0 trips per vehicle per service day is an aggregate rate, not a typical-city guarantee.
Average distance (1.4 miles) and duration (15 minutes) use trip-weighted operator responses; point-to-point
bikeshare data without GPS can understate distance. No response rate or uncertainty interval establishes the
precision of these totals, and pandemic recovery does not identify an infrastructure effect.[^nabsa2021]
