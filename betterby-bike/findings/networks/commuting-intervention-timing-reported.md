# Commuting support reportedly worked differently around life changes

## In brief

- Slate's December 2024 review reports that workplace tools including carpool matching and personalized
  transit plans failed to shift commuting, while a Danish free-transit trial increased use only among
  people who had recently moved home or changed jobs.
- Information before UCLA students arrived reportedly reduced driving and increased transit use.
  Effect sizes and persistence are not given; these reports do not establish a cycling benefit or a
  universal advantage of targeting life changes.[^zipper2024]

## Evidence and interpretation

Zipper cites Kristal and Whillans's 2019 workplace-experiment account, a Danish timing study and a
2016 UCLA report. These are different populations and interventions, not head-to-head comparisons
of a single program delivered at different times. Their primary publications remain unreviewed.
The Danish result is described as increased public-transport use, without a corresponding quantified
reduction in driving. UCLA's reported direction of change supplies no magnitude or follow-up duration.

The article also reports that only one in ten UK car commuters changed mode annually in a 2016
study. That is a share of car commuters changing to any other mode, not an annual cycling-adoption rate.
Status quo, present and sunk-cost biases are proposed explanations for persistence, not separately
estimated causes in this news account.[^zipper2024]

Zipper proposes welcome packages of transit and bikeshare passes for movers or new hires. Clark
emphasizes that behavioral support must accompany good alternatives. Neither recommendation establishes
that information or incentives can compensate for unsafe routes, poor service or distant destinations.

## Limits

The secondary account lacks sample sizes, allocation details, confidence intervals and sustained
follow-up results. A London strike example describes transit route changes, not mode substitution;
Brussels' pandemic bike-lane account does not isolate infrastructure from simultaneous disruption.
These examples cannot supply an expected percentage change for a local cycling program.
