# Bloomington's 7-Line had larger Lime volume growth than two comparison corridors

## In brief

- June–August Lime trip volumes on Bloomington's **7th Street increased 100% from 2020 to 2024**, spanning completion of
  its bidirectional protected lane in 2021.
- Growth was **19% on East 3rd Street without a lane** and **28% on North Walnut Street with an existing painted lane**.
  This three-corridor comparison is not a controlled estimate of protected-lane superiority or net new trips.[^lime2024]

## Evidence and limits

The 7-Line connects downtown with Indiana University. East 3rd Street is another east–west downtown connector;
North Walnut is a north–south commercial arterial. Incomplete OpenStreetMap road attributes prevented the
matched difference-in-differences analysis used in DC, so Bloomington's analysis focused on these downtown
streets. Counts came from GPS trajectories passing within 10 meters of street-segment midpoints.[^lime2024]

The report's “five times” language compares percentage growth (100% versus 19%), not final trip volumes or
five times as many newly generated journeys. Nearly 60% of Bloomington segment trip volumes used lanes,
compared with about one quarter of segments having infrastructure. That distribution is consistent with
concentrated use, but destination locations, network structure and route diversion can also shape it.
The report provides no uncertainty estimate for the corridor growth comparison; it cannot isolate lane type,
citywide mode shift, bicycle-only trips or the number of new users.[^lime2024]
