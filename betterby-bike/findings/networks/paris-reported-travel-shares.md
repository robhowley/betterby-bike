# Reported cycling share exceeded driving in central Paris, but suburban travel differed

## In brief

- Euronews's April 2024 coverage of an Institut Paris Region survey reports bicycles for 11.2% of
  central-Paris trips versus cars for 4.3%; walking (53.3%) and transit (30%) remained larger shares.[^frost2024]
- Geography matters: cars accounted for 61% of trips within more distant suburbs, while transit
  accounted for 66% of inner-suburb/Paris and 77% of outer-suburb/Paris journeys.[^frost2024]
- These are reported trip shares, not shares of people or proven infrastructure effects. The underlying
  study was not reviewed, and the article omits fieldwork dates and precise boundaries.

## Survey and comparison limits

The survey covered 3,337 Île-de-France residents aged 16–80, using GPS tracking and travel journals.
That is the regional participant sample, not the denominator of each geographic trip share.
Recruitment, weighting, trip classification and uncertainty are not supplied. The article also reports
almost half of trips between the city's two ring roads by car.[^frost2024]

Cycling is contrasted with 3% of Paris trips in 2010, but the article does not establish matching methods
or boundaries. Its use of both “Paris” and “city centre” prevents a precise geographic comparison.
Neither the historical contrast nor the alongside-reported cycling investment and car restrictions
isolates a policy effect. The percentages describe journeys, not the proportion of residents who ever
cycle, and suburban patterns prevent extending the headline across Île-de-France.
