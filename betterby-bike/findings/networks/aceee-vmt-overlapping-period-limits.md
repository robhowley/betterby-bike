# ACEEE's averaged VMT changes can hide growth in the latest comparison

## In brief

- ACEEE's 2025 Scorecard reports a **−2.7% average change in New Jersey motor-vehicle miles per capita**,
  combining **−9.0% for 2012–2021, −0.6% for 2013–2022 and +1.5% for 2014–2023**. A negative average
  therefore does not establish decline in the latest comparison or continuous annual decline.
- The three overlapping periods are not independent observations of a policy effect. They do not measure
  cycling substitution, total VMT reduction or improved access.[^aceee2025]

## Method and policy contrast

ACEEE describes calculating three “10-year” percentage changes and averaging them. Table 23 attributes
the calculations to FHWA 2024 and Caltrans 2025 data. These are long-period changes, not annual growth
rates or three changes between 2021 and 2023. Reported results are retained without recomputing them
from underlying data.[^aceee2025]

The rubric awards two outcome points for an average decline of at least 5%, one for a decline of at
least 1% but less than 5%. New Jersey receives one outcome point and zero target-policy points. In
contrast, Colorado and Massachusetts each receive two target-policy points but zero outcome points,
with positive reported average changes of 2.3% and 3.2%, respectively. This distinguishes credited
targets from achieved VMT reductions; it is not proof that targets failed or caused growth.[^aceee2025]

## Limits

- Pandemic-era endpoint years and differing start years complicate interpretation; the report does not
  isolate pandemic, population, economic or transport-policy contributions to these state changes.
- A per-person rate can fall while total vehicle miles rise. It does not identify which journeys were
  replaced, suppressed or shortened, or whether walking and cycling increased.
- No causal comparison, uncertainty interval or cycling-project effect is provided for these figures.
- The report's national target counts and some scoring descriptions conflict. This Finding uses the
  explicitly labeled Table 23 state entries, not a reconciled national count.
