# London cycling recovery depends on the indicator and measurement period

## In brief

- TfL's spring 2022 counts estimate 18% more weekday cycle-kilometres across London than in 2019;
  the seven-day trip estimate rose 14% and journey stages 13%. These are different measures, not competing
  estimates of one quantity or isolated infrastructure effects.[^report]
- A methods revision changed absolute distance estimates without genuine growth. The 2020–2021 area counts
  also cannot reliably represent whole-year cycling, particularly the unusually low 2021 count period.[^report]

## What was measured

Area-based counts, operating since 2015, cover central London (the Congestion Charge zone), inner and outer
London. Weekday distance growth from 2019 to spring 2022 was up to 27% in central, 18% in inner and 16% in
outer London. Weekends are not counted in this programme; seven-day trip/stage estimates use LTDS adjustments.
A trip is a complete one-way movement assigned to its usual longest-distance mode; a stage is a single-mode
part of a trip.[^report]

The distinct consolidated annual series reports 2021 cycle trips 5.4% below 2020 but 23.3% above 2019.
Tables 2.1–2.2 round totals to millions, so their displayed values should not be used to recalculate precise
percentage changes. These annual estimates should not be replaced by the single 2021 area-count observation,
which TfL says conflicts with other datasets and was collected during unusually low demand.[^report]

## Why the qualifications matter

The revised distance methodology was applied retrospectively and largely preserved relative changes. Its step
change in absolute levels is methodological: do not splice older publications' levels onto the revised series.
The reviewed section describes simplification/revision but does not provide the full calculation specification.
Pandemic restrictions affected both data collection and trip purpose, location and timing.[^report]

A small continuous-counter sample, mostly in central and inner London, showed October 2022 weekday demand
about 20–25% above the pre-pandemic level and weekends about 90% above. Fine weather and wider transport
network conditions qualify these short-period observations. They are not London-wide annual averages and
are not an independent confirmation of the area-count percentage.[^report]

These observations support cycling recovery but cannot identify which new infrastructure, changed commuting,
leisure travel or other influences caused it. Neither distance growth nor trip growth measures displaced driving.
