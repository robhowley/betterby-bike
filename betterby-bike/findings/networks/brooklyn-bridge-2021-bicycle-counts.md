# Brooklyn Bridge bicycle counts rose after the September 2021 lane conversion

## In brief

- NYC DOT's automated counters recorded 4,206 average daily Brooklyn Bridge bicycle crossings in
  October 2021, up 88% from 2,239 in October 2020, after a protected two-way lane opened September 14.
- The increase measures use of this crossing, not unique cyclists, net new citywide cycling or a
  causal lane effect; pandemic changes, weather and route redistribution were not controlled.[^dot2021bridge]

## Reported counts

DOT identifies automated, 24-hour counters as the measurement basis. Its monthly average daily
ridership figures are:[^dot2021bridge]

| Month | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | Reported 2020–2021 change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| September | 2,654 | 2,336 | 3,635 | +56% |
| October | 2,492 | 2,239 | 4,206 | +88% |

September 2021 includes days before and after opening, so it is not a full post-opening month.
The project repurposed a motor-vehicle lane, separated cycling from the pedestrian promenade and
added connecting-path, signal, barrier and traffic changes. It cannot isolate physical protection
from these concurrent changes.[^dot2021bridge]

## Interpretation limits

The release says Manhattan Bridge and the other East River bridges remained largely consistent
with historical trends, but supplies no comparable counts or controlled estimate. That statement
does not rule out route switching. No counter-error assessment, missing-day handling, uncertainty
or weather adjustment is provided. Higher counts than both 2019 and 2020 strengthen the descriptive
comparison, not the claim that all additional crossings were induced by the project.

Official and advocacy claims of safety, emissions and congestion benefits are not measured outcomes
in this release. Its separate citywide 2019 and Midtown 2015–2020 figures predate opening and cannot
be attributed to the conversion.
