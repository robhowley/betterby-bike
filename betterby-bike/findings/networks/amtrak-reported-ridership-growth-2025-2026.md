# Reported Amtrak growth does not establish substitution away from driving or flying

## In brief

- Business Insider reports 34.5 million Amtrak customer trips in FY2025; Frontier Group reports 5.1%
  growth from FY2024 and further 6% growth using May FY2026 figures. These are secondary accounts
  of operator statistics, not independent datasets or counts of unique travelers.[^vlamis][^frontier]
- State-supported trains reportedly grew 9% year to date through May FY2026 versus the corresponding
  FY2025 period. Neither count establishes how many car or air trips were displaced or any cycling gain.[^frontier]

## Counts and comparisons

Business Insider's January 2, 2026 article reports 34.5 million customer trips for the fiscal year ending
September 2025 and $2.7 billion in adjusted ticket revenue. Its 10% year-over-year increase follows the
revenue figure and should not be interpreted as ridership growth. The article's interviews attribute
appeal to investment, convenience and competing travel costs, without measuring their contributions.
Its FY2025 account and Frontier Group's later coverage are repeated reporting of the same operator's
annual results, not independent confirmations of an investment effect.[^vlamis]

The August 26, 2026 article calls FY2025 a record year. Its opening describes 23.9 million “people” riding
in FY2026, but links that figure to the May 2026 monthly performance report. It should not be presented as
a completed annual total or a count of distinct individuals. The article does not reproduce the operator's
counting definitions; its wording alone cannot distinguish repeat journeys from unique travelers.[^frontier]

For individual corridors, Mardi Gras between New Orleans and Mobile reportedly reached 100,000 riders
in under eight months against a 60,000 first-year forecast. This compares accumulated use with a forecast,
not treatment and control routes. Borealis between St. Paul and Chicago reportedly grew 140% from 2024
to 2025 after adding a second daily connection. The article does not establish equal operating periods
for this new service, so that percentage cannot be treated as mature-service demand growth.[^frontier]

## Interpretation and limits

The author attributes momentum to improved service, new equipment and higher competing travel costs,
but supplies no design separating these influences from recovery after the pandemic or service availability.
There is no prior-mode survey, passenger-distance comparison or emissions accounting. Reported local
renewal around Mardi Gras is also not a quantified economic evaluation. The results support a bounded
account of reported rail use, not causal claims about investment, gas prices or reduced driving.[^frontier]
