# Green Lane route counts rose, but growth included route substitution

Monsere et al.'s 2014 evaluation compared city before counts with 2013 city/video after counts on recently installed
protected lanes in five U.S. cities. Table 6-1 reports the following facility-level increases, combining city and video
estimates where both existed. These are changes in bicycles counted on routes, not citywide cycling or unique riders.[^monsere2014]

| Route | Reported count increase |
| --- | ---: |
| Barton Springs, Austin | 58% |
| Bluebonnet, Austin | 46% |
| Rio Grande, Austin | 126% |
| Dearborn, Chicago | 171% |
| Milwaukee, Chicago | 21% |
| NE Multnomah, Portland | 68% |
| Fell, San Francisco | 46% |
| L Street, Washington, D.C. | 65% |

Table 6-1 lists Oak as unavailable, although Table 6-3 reports two Oak morning comparisons of +26% and +35%. The
21–171% headline describes the summary estimates, not every individual count comparison. For example, Milwaukee's
city estimate was +4%, versus +38% from video comparisons; L Street's video morning comparison was −4%, versus +131%
in the afternoon. Dearborn and Rio Grande also added two-way bicycle travel to one-way streets.[^monsere2014]

## Route shifts and other-mode trips both contributed

Asked how they would have made the intercepted trip before the facility existed, respondents answered: same bicycle
route 65%, another bicycle route 24%, another mode 10%, and no trip 1%. Table 6-4 totals **1,316 route responses**, not
1,316 unique riders: Oak/Fell respondents appear twice. Dearborn differed sharply, with 60% reporting another bicycle
route and 21% another mode (n=123). These are recalled/hypothetical alternatives from current users, not longitudinally
observed mode switches.[^monsere2014]

## Limits

Count periods, locations and methods varied, usually covering only short periods within a year after installation.
Weather was not explicitly adjusted; school-term differences affected Bluebonnet. No comparable parallel-route counts
were available to measure displacement. Citywide 2010–12 ACS commuting growth used a different measure and period,
and its margins of error were ignored. Neither the route counts nor the survey establish the causal net increase in
cycling from construction or a typical percentage gain for another city.
