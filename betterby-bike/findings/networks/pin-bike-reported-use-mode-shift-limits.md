# Pin Bike respondents reported frequent use, not a measured mode shift

## In brief

- SIAMUS's August 2025 report describes 517 Pin Bike respondents across nine Italian cities. Chart 10
  reports 29% using the service daily and 45% four or more days weekly, near the prose's approximately
  75% frequent-cycling figure. This is reported service use, not a cycling increase.[^siamus2025]
- About 43% reported cycling before Pin Bike and 25% prior car use. Without paired trip changes or
  a comparison group, these shares do not measure car replacement or an incentive effect.[^siamus2025]

## What the survey adds

Approximately 7,000 users were invited through the app in Bari, Bergamo, Bologna, Lecce, Pescara,
Sassuolo, Florence, Turin and Trento. Responses came mainly from Bari, Bologna and Florence. Seven
cities' initiatives had ended, while Florence and Bologna remained active. The report also mentions
historical questionnaires without clearly separating their contribution.[^siamus2025]

Respondents' reported modal-choice motivations shifted toward environmental sustainability (17% before,
31% with/after the program) and economic benefit (16% to 27%); 33% cited incentives/rewards as a
participation driver. These are stated reasons, not shares of trips switched. Commute-time distributions
were described as almost identical before and during participation, a null descriptive comparison
rather than evidence of faster journeys.[^siamus2025]

## Interpretation limits

Chart 10 asks about Pin Bike service use; its two frequent-use categories sum to 74% after rounding.
It also shows 1% reporting no use. The report's claim that all users began cycling is therefore not
established by this chart, nor can service frequency establish how much total cycling changed.

The approximate response fraction is 7.4% (517 divided by about 7,000). Meeting a sample-size target
does not resolve nonresponse or participant selection. Recalled baseline modes, pooled projects,
unspecified fieldwork dates and item denominators, and absent uncertainty/control comparisons prevent
causal or city-population inference. The report's stated/revealed preference terminology does not turn
these responses into independently observed trip substitution.[^siamus2025]
