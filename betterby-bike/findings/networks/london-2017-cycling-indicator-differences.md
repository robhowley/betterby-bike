# London cycling indicators moved in different directions in 2017

## In brief

- TfL estimated London cycle trips fell 0.3% from 2016 to 2017, while daily distance cycled rose 4.3%,
  from 3.768 million to 3.931 million kilometres. These are different measures, not contradictory estimates
  of the same quantity.[^report]
- Area counts rose but strategic cordon crossings fell. Neither a selected route's growth nor its decline
  establishes the change in total London cycling or an infrastructure effect.[^report]

## Supporting measures

Table 5.1 reports daily cycle-kilometres as follows. Values below convert its thousands of kilometres to millions.[^report]

| Area | 2016 | 2017 | Reported change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Central London | 0.525 | 0.530 | +0.8% |
| Inner London | 1.736 | 1.789 | +3.0% |
| Outer London | 1.507 | 1.612 | +7.0% |
| Greater London total | 3.768 | 3.931 | +4.3% |

The same table's area-count indicator rose 3.9% across Greater London. It is a normalized count measure, not
240 individual cyclists in all London. Central London has the highest count density, but only 2% of Greater
London's land area; inner and outer London account for more total cycling distance.[^report]

Central-cordon cycle crossings fell 12% in the latest comparison; boundary-cordon crossings fell 16% against
2015. Inner-cordon counts were not collected in 2017. The report's introductory cordon sentence refers generally
to 2016, but its boundary-specific comparison explicitly uses 2015; do not relabel both changes as 2016–2017.[^report]

## Interpretation limits

Central area counts are quarterly, unlike annual inner/outer area counts. Winter troughs are about 20% below
summer peaks, so season matters when interpreting a short count. In 2016, cycles were 24% of daytime vehicles
counted in central London and 33% in peak periods. Those are vehicle shares, not shares of all person trips,
London-wide travel or residents who cycle.[^report]

Table 2.1 assigns trips to the mode normally used for the longest distance; counts and kilometres do not use
that denominator. TfL reports growth exceeding 50% on many Superhighway/Quietway routes, but the reviewed sections
do not provide a route-by-route controlled estimate. These observations cannot isolate new trips, rerouting,
longer travel or particular infrastructure effects.[^report]
