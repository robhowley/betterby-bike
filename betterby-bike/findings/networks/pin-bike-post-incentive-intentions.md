# Pin Bike users expressed willingness to continue without incentives, not observed retention

## In brief

- In SIAMUS's nine-city Italian Pin Bike survey, reported in August 2025, likelihood of continuing
  cycling without financial incentives averaged 4.2 on a 1–5 scale. This measures stated intention,
  not observed riding after payments stop.[^siamus2025]
- About 98% said they would join another initiative and overall project ratings averaged about
  4.7/5. Favorable responses among participants do not establish lasting change or effects among
  nonparticipants.[^siamus2025]

## What was asked

The questionnaire asks whether users would keep cycling without financial incentives. The report
specifies 1 as unlikely and 5 as extremely likely. A 4.2 mean is neither a retention percentage nor
an estimate of additional cycling. Rejoining a rewarded program is also a different outcome from
continuing without rewards.[^siamus2025]

There were 517 responses from approximately 7,000 invited users. The projects covered Bari, Bergamo,
Bologna, Lecce, Pescara, Sassuolo, Florence, Turin and Trento; seven cities' initiatives had ended,
while Florence and Bologna remained active. The report pools these experiences rather than presenting
separate observed retention rates at defined intervals after incentives ended.[^siamus2025]

## Why the distinction matters

The authors label these results a lasting behavioral impact. No longitudinal post-incentive trip
comparison or untreated control is reported to establish that interpretation. Existing cyclists,
voluntary respondents and satisfied users may be overrepresented; item-specific sample sizes,
response distributions and uncertainty are not supplied for these means. Neither app certification
nor the survey's mixed-preference label validates the hypothetical future outcome.

The results support favorable intentions among respondents, not proven persistence, car substitution,
net environmental benefits or an isolated gamification effect.
