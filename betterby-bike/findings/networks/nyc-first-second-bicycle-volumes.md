# NYC reported up to 177% higher bicycle volumes on First and Second Avenues

NYCDOT's 2012 report gives an **up to 177% increase in bicycle volumes** for First and Second Avenues in Manhattan. The corridor project combined protected green bicycle lanes, offset bus lanes, pedestrian refuges, separated left-turn lanes and dedicated signal phases.[^nyc2012]

The same panel reports 18% higher bus speeds, 12% higher bus ridership, 37% fewer injury crashes and 47% fewer commercial vacancies (versus 2% more borough-wide). These are accompanying project outcomes, not independent confirmations of the bicycle-volume result.[^nyc2012]

The report does not identify the bicycle count sites, baseline counts, observation periods, seasonal adjustments, uncertainty or calculation underlying “up to.” The value is not established as an average across both avenues. Corridor counts cannot distinguish newly generated cycling from cyclists changing routes, establish citywide mode-share growth, or isolate the bicycle lanes from the other changes.
