# London reported 26% more daily cycle stages in 2024 than in 2019

## In brief

- IanVisits reports TfL's estimated **1.33 million daily cycle journeys in London in 2024, 26% above 2019**.
  The accompanying chart defines the quantity as cycle stages averaged across a seven-day week, not unique
  cyclists, complete trips or a population-normalized cycling rate.[^mansfield2024]
- The increase accompanied strategic-network expansion from **90 km in 2016 to over 400 km in September 2024**.
  Different comparison periods and no causal analysis prevent attributing the cycling increase to that expansion.[^mansfield2024]

## Measure and comparison

The chart labels 2024 stages as 0.18 million in central London, 0.65 million in inner London and 0.49 million
in outer London. The respective 2019 labels are 0.13, 0.52 and 0.40 million. These rounded 2024 components
sum to 1.32 million, not the prose's 1.33 million. Preserve the reported total and percentage rather than
reconstructing a precise estimate from rounded labels. The graphic explicitly omits comparable 2020–2021
values because of pandemic impacts; missing bars are not zero cycling.[^mansfield2024]

The article reports just over 27% of Londoners within 400 metres of the strategic cycle network, with a
40% target for 2030. This describes proximity and an ambition, not evidence of connected, comfortable or
equitably usable routes. Neither stage totals nor proximity establish displaced driving or improved safety.[^mansfield2024]

## Interpretation limits

The commissioner attributes use to good infrastructure, but the article provides no controls, uncertainty
estimates or accounting for other travel changes. Only the news article and reproduced chart were reviewed,
not the underlying 2024 TfL methods. The [2022 TfL measurement account](london-2022-cycling-recovery-measurement.md)
distinguishes stages from complete trips and distance, and warns about revised series and pandemic sampling.
This later reporting concerns related monitoring, not an independent study; do not splice absolute levels
across publications without establishing comparability.
