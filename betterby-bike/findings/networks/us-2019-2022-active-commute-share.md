# U.S. walking/bicycling commute share partly rebounded in 2022

## In brief

- Among U.S. workers aged 16 and older, excluding Puerto Rico, usual walking/bicycling commuting was **2.9% in
  2022**, up from **2.6% in 2021** but below **3.2% in 2019**, according to ACS 1-year estimates.[^census2024]
- This combines two modes and includes home-based workers in the denominator. It does not show bicycle-only
  trends, shares of all trips or the effect of cycling infrastructure.[^census2024]

## Comparison and context

| Usual means of transportation to work | 2019 | 2021 | 2022 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bicycle or walked | 3.2% | 2.6% | 2.9% |
| Public transportation | 5.0% | 2.5% | 3.1% |
| Drove alone | 75.9% | 67.8% | 68.7% |
| Worked from home | 5.7% | 17.9% | 15.2% |

These selected categories from Figure 2 describe shares of workers, not trip totals or a complete mode table.
Active commuting, transit and solo driving all rose as shares between 2021 and 2022, while home-based work
fell. That pattern does not establish that workers switched directly from one particular mode to another.[^census2024]

## Uncertainty and scope

The report describes the combined active-commute share as smaller than in 2019 and larger than in 2021.
Its accuracy notes say comparative statements are statistically significant at 90% confidence unless noted
otherwise, but Figure 2 does not display margins of error. Estimates are subject to sampling and nonsampling
error. The report omits 2020 because pandemic nonresponse compromised standard ACS estimates; included years
used consistent weighting methods.[^census2024]

Respondents report their usual commute mode in the previous week, choosing the method used most days when
it varies by day. These annual survey estimates do not measure non-work travel, all commuting days, separate
walking and cycling trends, or causally identified effects of pandemic policies or street projects. Rising or
falling shares need not imply corresponding changes in total bicycle trips.[^census2024]
