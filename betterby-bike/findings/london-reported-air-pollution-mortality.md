# London's reported pollution-attributable mortality decline, 2019–2024

## In brief

- Guardian coverage of Imperial research reports roughly 40% fewer estimated pollution-attributable
  premature deaths in London in 2024 than 2019: 3,800–5,100 versus a revised 6,400–8,000.
- These are attributed mortality estimates, not counted deaths known to have been caused by pollution.
  The report does not isolate Ulez or any other policy's contribution.[^topham2026]

## Comparison and limits

The same coverage reports nitrogen dioxide levels down 41% and fine particulate pollution down 28%
over 2019–2024. New scientific evidence increased the earlier 2019 mortality estimate of about 4,000.
That older figure is therefore not a consistent baseline for the 2024 estimate. The article does not
explain the mortality ranges, so they should not be labeled confidence intervals.[^topham2026]

The underlying analysis was not reviewed; exposure measures, population adjustments and mortality
calculation details are unavailable in the capture. Mayor Khan's claim that Ulez saved lives is an
interpretation, not a policy-specific result of this analysis. A separate GLA study's reported 27%
roadside NO2 reduction relative to a no-Ulez scenario has a different denominator and research basis
from this temporal pollution decline. [Earlier coverage](/sources/2025-guardian-ulez-expansion-air-quality.md)
words that 27% as a decline since 2019 instead; the comparison wording remains unresolved and the
accounts are not independent evidence. Neither figure quantifies cycling-project health benefits.
