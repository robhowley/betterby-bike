# British casualty severity was more consistently associated with vehicle weight and height than length

## In brief

- In selected British police-recorded pedestrian/cyclist casualties, 2004–2023, heavier and taller striking cars
  generally had stronger injury-severity associations than longer cars. Separate models do not establish
  independent weight or height effects, or effects of changing vehicle dimensions.
- For adults, the heaviest and tallest categories each had 38% higher fatality odds than their respective lowest
  categories. Adult pedestrian killed/seriously injured (KSI) results were largely null; not every subgroup
  showed a clear dose-response pattern.

## Comparisons

The study linked car make/model/year to manufacturer dimension data and fitted separately adjusted models for
kerb weight, height and length. The reference categories were <1,550 kg, <1,575 mm and <4,600 mm, respectively.
The highest categories were ≥2,050 kg, ≥1,750 mm and ≥4,900 mm. These are overall vehicle height measurements,
not bonnet-height or direct-vision measurements.[^goodman2025]

| Outcome/group | Highest weight OR (95% CI) | Highest height OR (95% CI) | Highest length OR (95% CI) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Fatal/nonfatal, adults 19+ | 1.38 (1.11–1.71) | 1.38 (1.13–1.68) | 1.12 (0.88–1.43) |
| Fatal/nonfatal, children 0–9 | 3.75 (1.49–9.45) | 3.20 (1.37–7.44) | 1.85 (0.56–6.08) |
| KSI/slight, adult pedestrians | 1.02 (0.92–1.12) | 0.99 (0.90–1.08) | 1.01 (0.92–1.12) |
| KSI/slight, adult cyclists | 1.11 (1.00–1.23) | 1.27 (1.15–1.39) | 1.00 (0.90–1.11) |

Sample sizes differ by available dimension. Adult fatality models included 228,360 casualties for weight,
215,095 for height and 215,332 for length; corresponding ages 0–9 samples were 37,584, 34,300 and 34,363.
Adult pedestrian KSI samples were 116,741, 108,942 and 109,073; adult cyclist samples were 111,619,
106,153 and 106,259. These are model totals, not counts in the highest categories.[^goodman2025]

Children aged 0–18 had increasing KSI odds across weight and height categories, but fatality results were less
consistent: weight ORs were 1.64 (1.25–2.16) at 1,550–2,049 kg and 1.34 (0.69–2.61) at ≥2,050 kg.
Length was mostly weak or inconclusive, not universally null: ages 0–9 KSI OR was 1.11 (1.03–1.21) for
4,600–4,899 mm but 1.01 (0.80–1.28) for ≥4,900 mm. Thus the authors' broad dose-response conclusion should
not be applied to every outcome or group.[^goodman2025]

## Limits and relationships

Dimensions were not mutually adjusted and were not included alongside SUV status. Correlated characteristics
cannot be separated into causal contributions. Make/model/year averages among February 2025 UK registrations
may not exactly represent individual vehicles in historical crashes. Width was excluded for inconsistent
mirror inclusion, and supplementary electric/plug-in-hybrid weight-threshold analyses were not reviewed.
Police reporting, complete-case selection and unmeasured impact speed further limit inference. Odds ratios
compare severity among recorded casualties, not crash incidence or per-trip harm.[^goodman2025]
