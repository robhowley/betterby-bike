# CPSC investigations reveal charging and product hazards, not their prevalence

## In brief

- Among 314 completed U.S. micromobility incident investigations for 2017–2022, CPSC identified 212 fire
  hazards: 45 scooter, 139 hoverboard and 28 e-bike incidents. Many occurred during or just after charging.
- Brake failures, unexpected power loss and structural problems also appeared. The selected completed
  investigations reveal possible mechanisms, not population hazard frequencies or comparisons of product safety.[^cpsc2023]

## Reported mechanisms

CPSC reviewed 89 scooter, 166 hoverboard and 59 e-bike investigations originating in CPSRMS reports.
Of 45 scooter fire-hazard incidents, 29 occurred while charging and seven after charging. Of 139 hoverboard
fire-hazard incidents, 86 occurred while charging or just after; most e-bike fire reports described charging.
The category includes smoke, sparks, overheating or melting as well as fires, so it is not 212 fatal fires.[^cpsc2023]

Other leading reported problems included 19 scooter brake incidents, 20 hoverboard electrical incidents and
24 e-bike miscellaneous product incidents. E-bike reports included pedals separating, tires failing, wheels
detaching and frame/steering problems. Table 4.1 separately records four e-bike brake incidents. Some incidents
involved multiple product issues. These accounts broaden prevention beyond road collisions without quantifying
the effects of equipment standards, charging practices or infrastructure.[^cpsc2023]

## Selection and overlap

Reports came from complaints and other nonprobability sources, not representative surveillance of every ride
or product. Many initiated investigations could not be completed because products were unavailable or consumers
did not cooperate. CPSC explicitly warns the completed set does not necessarily reflect even the prevalence of
incidents in CPSRMS. Eight fatalities overlap the [reported-death analysis](/findings/safety/cpsc-micromobility-reported-fatalities.md);
this is not an additional independent mortality sample. Do not generalize the large fire share to all
micromobility injuries or add it to [national ED estimates](/findings/safety/cpsc-micromobility-ed-injury-trends.md).

See [interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).
