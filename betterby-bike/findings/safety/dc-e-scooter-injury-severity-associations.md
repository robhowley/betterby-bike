# Road and weekly riding were associated with greater severity among injured DC scooter riders

## In brief

- Among adults treated at a Washington, DC emergency department in March–November 2019, moderate-or-worse injuries
  occurred in 58% of road cases versus 32% elsewhere; the adjusted probability was about twice as high on roads.
- At least weekly riders also had greater adjusted injury severity than less frequent riders. Both associations concern
  already-injured patients, not the chance of being injured on a ride or a causal effect of facility
  choice.[^cicchino2021]

## Results and methods

The outcome was at least one Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) score ≥2 injury. Of 105 enrolled riders, 103 had clinical
injury data and 39 met that threshold. Road cases had 14/24 such injuries, versus 25/78 in other known locations
(unadjusted p =0.02). “Other” pooled sidewalks, bike lanes, trails/off-road locations and alleys, not sidewalks alone.
Intersection cases were classified by the facility used immediately beforehand.[^cicchino2021]

Stepwise logistic regression retained age, sex, location, motor-vehicle collision and riding frequency at p <0.15.
The authors converted odds ratios to relative risks:

| Comparison | Adjusted RR (95% CI) |
| --- | --- |
| Road versus other location | 1.96 (1.23–2.36) |
| At least weekly use in past 30 days versus less often | 1.86 (1.11–2.32) |
| Age, each additional year | 1.02 (1.00–1.05) |
| Female versus male | 1.72 (0.98–2.24) |
| Struck by motor vehicle versus other circumstances | 0.19 (0.02–1.10) |

Only road location and riding frequency reached p <0.05. Age and sex each had p =0.06, despite the rounded age
interval ending at 1.00; vehicle collision had p =0.07. Weekly use was not significant unadjusted (21/47, 45%, versus
18/55, 33%; p =0.22). The low vehicle-collision estimate rests on one AIS ≥2 case among ten collisions and is not
proof of protection. The separate unadjusted speed-cap comparison was inconclusive (29/84 capped at 10 mph versus
8/16 uncapped, p =0.24), not evidence that caps have no effect.[^cicchino2021]

## Limits

Actual speeds were not measured. Higher speeds and risk-taking are proposed explanations, not demonstrated mechanisms.
The small single-hospital sample, missing overnight cases and exclusion of patients too injured to communicate limit
inference, particularly for serious trauma: only two assessed riders had AIS ≥3 injuries. No riding exposure was
measured, so neither the road nor frequency association establishes injury incidence or an infrastructure effect.[^cicchino2021]
