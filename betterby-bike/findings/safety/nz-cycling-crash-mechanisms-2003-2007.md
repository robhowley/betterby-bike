# Non-collision crashes accounted for two-fifths of New Zealand cycling death and inpatient cases in 2003–07

Among cyclists aged five or older who died or had a first public-hospital injury admission lasting at least one day
after a crash on New Zealand public roads in 2003–07, non-collision crashes accounted for 40.35% of cases. Cars, pick-up
trucks and vans accounted for 25.51% of all cases, but 38.49% of serious cases.[^nz2010]

| Mechanism                                          | Share of all cases | Share of serious cases |
| -------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ | ---------------------- |
| Non-collision crash                                | 40.35%             | 33.57%                 |
| Collision with car, pick-up truck or van           | 25.51%             | 38.49%                 |
| Collision with heavy transport vehicle or bus      | 1.79%              | 5.35%                  |
| Collision with two- or three-wheeled motor vehicle | 0.35%              | 0.58%                  |
| Unspecified                                        | 21.23%             | 8.10%                  |

Rows are selected categories, not a complete partition. Serious injuries were estimated as consistent with Abbreviated
Injury Scale (AIS) scores ≥3. The 38.49% figure is for cars, pick-ups and vans, not all motor vehicles.[^nz2010]

Injury profiles also differed: Table 4 reports internal traumatic brain injuries in 29.05% of motor-vehicle collision
cases versus 14.90% of other crash cases; upper-extremity fractures occurred in 25.91% versus 40.33%, respectively.
These are diagnosis-specific shares based on up to ten diagnoses per case, so they are not mutually exclusive. “Other
crashes” includes more than non-collision crashes.[^nz2010]

## Interpretation and limits

Motor-vehicle collisions alone do not describe the full inpatient cycling injury burden. However, these case shares are
not probabilities of injury per crash or rates per hour spent in each type of traffic environment. They cannot establish
the effectiveness of any street treatment.

The large unspecified category limits attribution. Coding and estimated severity may be inaccurate, and
emergency-department-only, primary-care and self-treated injuries were excluded. Recommendations to address both
collision hazards and cyclist-only crashes are the authors' interpretation, not tested interventions.[^nz2010]
