# NYC hydrant-zone intersections had higher normalized injury counts, not proven parking-removal harm

## In brief

- In NYC's 2017–2023 cross-sectional analysis, intersections with approaching-side hydrants or bus stops
  had **1.3 times the normalized pedestrian and total injury measures** of intersections without them.
- These are ratios of nearby-count-normalized injuries, not risks per pedestrian, cyclist or vehicle.
  There are no installation dates or before/after observations establishing that parking removal caused harm.
- Missing exposure, unknown illegal parking and restricted sample eligibility limit application to a
  universal daylighting policy or purpose-built hardened treatments.[^dot2025]

## What was compared

For each intersection, DOT divided its injuries by average injuries at nearby intersections (up to three
along each street within one mile). It then divided the mean of these normalized outcomes at zone
intersections by the mean at non-zone intersections. The total-injury sample included 20,543 intersections,
7,558 with zones; the pedestrian sample included 7,113, with 2,747 zone intersections. Intersections with
no nearby injuries could not support normalization and were excluded. Hydrant and bus-stop counts overlap,
so they cannot be added as separate sites.[^dot2025]

The summary table reports ratios of **1.3 for both outcomes**, **2.0 for both at intersections with more
than one daylit approach**, and **1.4 for total injuries on truck routes**. Displayed results are reported
as significant at p<0.05, but exact p-values and confidence intervals are absent. Subgroup comparisons
are not a demonstrated jointly adjusted causal model.[^dot2025]

## Why the association is not a policy effect

The features were not installed expressly for daylighting and existed for decades or longer. Eligibility
excluded protected bike lanes, hardened daylighting, No Standing Anytime signs, Open Streets and street
improvement projects, as well as certain complex sites, parks and airports. Findings therefore apply to a
selected subset, not every NYC intersection. Nearby injury normalization substitutes for unavailable
pedestrian volume and precise traffic exposure, and aggregated seven-year counts can conceal different
trends. Some eligibility data predate the study endpoint.[^dot2025]

DOT proposes risk compensation and faster/wider turns as explanations and recommends against universal
sign-only daylighting. These are interpretation and policy advice, not directly measured mechanisms or
an evaluated universal-policy effect. Unknown parking noncompliance also means a mapped parking restriction
is not proof that the sightline was actually clear.[^dot2025]
