# Aggregate e-bike injury totals do not identify e-moto involvement

## In brief

- Velo reports California e-bike incidents rose **1,800% from 2018 to 2023**, but gives neither baseline
  counts nor a breakdown separating compliant e-bikes from electric motorcycles.
- Its claim that e-motos overwhelmingly cause the injuries is not established by the supplied statistics;
  distinguishing vehicle classes is necessary before assigning their share of the burden.
- Injury counts and outcomes among selected trauma patients are not injury risk per trip.[^velo2025]

## What the article does and does not show

The December 4, 2025 commentary describes high-powered machines sold with defeatable speed limiters.
Those examples support examining classification, but do not identify which machines were involved in
California's reported incidents or the article's national head-trauma trend.

The reported Marin County comparison concerns e-bike trauma patients brought to emergency care by
ambulance: nearly one in eight died, with a reported death rate 37 times the pedal-bicycle comparison.
Without case counts, selection details, exposure or vehicle specifications, this cannot establish either
all-rider fatality risk or the fraction attributable to e-motos. The primary study was not reviewed.
Battery-fire totals likewise cannot be allocated to vehicle classes from the supplied information.[^velo2025]

The author's proposals for import enforcement, truthful marketing and consistent definitions remain
recommendations. The article measures neither their effects nor those of bans or licensing.
