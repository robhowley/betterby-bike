# New Jersey's report-card fatalities per bike commuter are not travel risk

## In brief

- The League's **2024 New Jersey report card** reports **14.1 fatalities per 10,000 bike commuters**, ranked **28/50
  states**, alongside **0.3% of commuters biking to work** (29/50). It does not identify the data years or fatality
  numerator's precise coverage.[^njcard2024]
- This commuter-normalized indicator is **not risk per trip or mile**, and cannot be read as 14.1 deaths among every
  10,000 bicycle commuters. The separate **Safety category score of 48% of available points (27/50)** is a rubric score,
  not a fatality percentage.[^njcard2024]

## What the card supports

The federal-data table supplies the values and rankings, not underlying counts, uncertainty or calculation
methods. Commuting share excludes other cycling purposes. The card does not establish that the deaths counted
occurred during commuting, or that commuters capture total cycling exposure comparably across states.
Publication in 2024 does not make these 2024-only observations.[^njcard2024]

The report's overall **26/50** rank and category scores belong to an assessment based on a survey of more than
100 data points completed by state transportation departments and bicycling advocates. They are not equivalent
to the federal table's individual rankings or evidence of causal safety effects. A lower overall rank than the
previous card does not by itself establish rising cyclist risk.[^njcard2024]

## Access and interpretation limits

The complete one-page card was reviewed, but the separate use guide, survey responses and underlying federal
datasets were not. The page provides no observation periods, numerator definition, exposure matching or
uncertainty intervals. Its combined walking/biking share of more than 30% of traffic deaths is a different,
all-traffic-fatality denominator, not cyclists' individual probability of death.[^njcard2024]

See [Interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) for why counts, commuter-normalized
indicators and travel-exposure rates answer different questions. The [national pedalcyclist fatality series](us-pedalcyclist-fatalities-2010-2020.md)
has explicit years and coverage qualifications; it does not supply the missing methods for this state card.

[^njcard2024]: Report card p. 1, “Federal Data on Biking,” “Categories,” national-rank heading, “Feedback,” and bottom
    survey-methods note.
