# Front-end profiles and pedestrian fatality

## In brief

- Road to Zero reports an IIHS study of nearly 18,000 single-vehicle, single-pedestrian crashes in seven U.S.
  states: fronts over 40 inches high were associated with roughly 44–45% higher fatality risk than fronts at most
  30 inches high with a sloping profile.[^rtz2024]
- Medium-height blunt fronts had 26% higher reported risk, but low/blunt and medium/sloped fronts were labeled
  similar risk. These secondary crash-severity results are not deaths per mile or measured effects of redesign.[^rtz2024]

## Comparison and reporting basis

The report identifies Hu, Monfort and Cicchino (2024),
[*The association between passenger-vehicle front-end profiles and pedestrian injury severity in motor vehicle crashes*](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jsr.2024.06.007).
The primary paper was not independently reviewed. The report's reproduced IIHS graphic uses low/sloped fronts
(≤30 inches) as the reference:

| Profile | Reported difference from low/sloped reference |
| --- | --- |
| Low/blunt, ≤30 inches | Similar risk |
| Medium/sloped, 30–40 inches | Similar risk |
| Medium/blunt, 30–40 inches | +26% |
| Tall/sloped, >40 inches | +45% |
| Tall/blunt, >40 inches | +44% |

The prose rounds the tall-front result to 45% regardless of shape. These are relative differences, not percentage-point
changes in absolute death probability. The graphic's category labels overlap at 30 inches; no more precise boundary
rule is inferred here.[^rtz2024]

## Limits

The synthesis does not give crash years, state identities, model controls, confidence intervals or absolute baseline
fatality risk for this result. “Similar risk” is not proof of equivalence. The result concerns pedestrians already
in recorded crashes, not the probability of being struck. It neither isolates weight from all other vehicle features
nor supplies a cyclist-specific estimate. The report's separate proposed 1.25-metre height cap is a policy recommendation,
not a tested threshold derived from this comparison.[^rtz2024]
