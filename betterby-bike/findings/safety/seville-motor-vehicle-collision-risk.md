# Seville's motor-vehicle collision rate fell while collision counts rose

Across Seville, police-recorded bicycle–motor-vehicle collisions per million estimated bicycle trips fell from
**15.71 in 2006** to **7.40 in 2007**, when a connected cycle-track network was built, and **6.12 in 2013**.
The 2006–2013 decline is about **61%**, calculated from the reported rates. Actual recorded motor-vehicle collision
counts rose from **48 to 100** over those endpoints, while estimated trips rose from 3.055 to 16.333 million.[^seville]

Rates did not decline every year: they rose to **8.37 in 2009** and from **4.24 in 2012 to 6.12 in 2013**.
All recorded bicycle crashes rose from **56 in 2006 to 126 in 2013**. Collisions with pedestrians rose from 2 to 7,
collisions with other bicycles from 1 to 5, and bicycle-only crashes from 5 to 14. These are counts, not evidence that
per-trip risk for those crash types increased.[^seville]

The main rate covers **motor-vehicle collisions anywhere in the city**, not injuries on cycle tracks or all cycling
injuries. The authors restrict it because police reporting of off-carriageway crashes might have changed after network
construction. Even for motor-vehicle collisions, stable reporting is an assumption; the actual reporting fraction is
unknown. Exposure is estimated using counts and, later, bike-share rentals and observed bicycle-type shares.[^seville]

This uncontrolled before/after comparison supports lower reported collisions per estimated trip alongside greater
cycling, not an isolated infrastructure effect. It supplies neither uncertainty intervals for the endpoint rate change
nor a track-versus-roadway comparison. Rates for 2000–2005 additionally assume constant exposure at the 2006 level.
