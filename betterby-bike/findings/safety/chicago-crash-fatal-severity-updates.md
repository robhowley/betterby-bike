# Chicago's published crash severity could miss later fatal updates

## In brief

- In August 2022, Chicago Crash Browser editor Steven Vance described fatal-severity upgrades that did
  not reach city data-portal records; he manually marked those crashes fatal in the browser.[^vance2022]
- A fatal-crash filter can therefore depend on whether later severity updates are incorporated, not
  just initial reporting. The article gives no affected count or evidence that corrections were complete.

## What the account establishes

Vance calls this the most common data change he was making at the time. He says each manual change to
“most severe injury” would be noted and would make the crash appear when users filtered for fatal
crashes. This is the editor's description of his process, not an independent audit of either dataset.
The article separately warns that weekly counts can change as police upload reports.[^vance2022]

No total, denominator, fatality follow-up interval or measured difference between city and browser
counts is supplied. The account does not quantify pedestrian or cyclist undercount, establish current
conditions or show that a severity-field correction also updates person-level records. News coverage
was intended for fatal pedestrian and bicycle crashes, but completeness was not demonstrated.

## How to use it

When comparing fatal-crash extracts, distinguish initial records, subsequent severity updates and
editorial corrections. Unlike [New Jersey's public-access and geocoding limits](new-jersey-2024-crash-data-access-limits.md),
this concerns changes to records already published. Neither establishes risk per trip or a safety
benefit from opening data; see [injury-risk interpretation](../../topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).
