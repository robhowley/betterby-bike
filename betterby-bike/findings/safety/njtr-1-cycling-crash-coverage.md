# NJTR-1 does not capture all cycling crashes

## In brief

- New Jersey's January 2023 NJTR-1 manual excludes pedalcyclist crashes unless a motor vehicle in transport is involved.
  Bicycle-only falls and bicycle–bicycle crashes without such involvement are outside this form's scope.
- Reportable crashes involve injury, death or property damage exceeding $500 to any one person; non-reportable reports
  are not submitted to NJDOT for processing.
- These are coverage rules, not a measured undercount or evidence of reporting compliance. NJTR-1 counts cannot describe
  all cycling injuries or risk per ride.[^manual]

## Reporting boundary

The manual covers qualifying crashes on highways, private roads, driveways and other public or private
property. Its exclusion is therefore not simply a public-road restriction. Pedestrians and pedalcyclists
are recorded when involved with a motor vehicle in transport; personal conveyances have the same
motor-vehicle involvement condition. Mopeds, snowmobiles, ATVs and dirt bikes have separately stated
statutory reporting provisions.[^manual]

The manual also requires a change report when a crash-related death occurs after the initial report,
within 30 days of the crash. Initial and updated records need not contain identical fatality information.
This is a reporting requirement, not evidence about actual update delays or compliance.[^manual]

## Interpretation

Do not treat absence from NJTR-1 as absence of injury. Hospital-based surveillance may capture cases
excluded by design here, even before considering unreported eligible crashes. No reviewed records quantify
the share omitted. These historical instructions do not establish current practice or a facility-safety effect.

[Device coding](/findings/safety/njtr-1-low-speed-ebike-classification.md) creates a separate classification
issue within eligible reports. See [cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) for comparisons with
national traffic and hospital statistics.

[^manual]: Manual, pp. 8–11 (reportability, locations and excluded devices), pp. 16–17
    (submission threshold and fatal change reports), and p. 127 (personal conveyances).
