# U.S. e-bike and powered scooter injury rates per resident rose in 2019–2022

## In brief

- Burford et al. report that U.S. ED injury rates per resident increased 293.0% for e-bikes and 88.0% for
  powered scooters between 2019 and 2022, while hoverboard rates fell 26.2%.
- These are rates per general population, not per ride or mile. More riding could increase the measured burden
  without increasing individual trip risk; the study cannot separate those changes.[^burford2024]

## Burden and trend qualifications

The weighted four-year estimate is 1,933,296 micromobility ED injuries (95% CI 1,717,064–2,149,529):

| Device | Estimated ED injuries, 2019–2022 | 95% CI |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Bicycle | 1,623,143 | 1,436,665–1,809,621 |
| Powered scooter | 164,712 | 132,797–196,628 |
| E-bike | 56,963 | 41,206–72,721 |
| Hoverboard | 88,478 | 73,882–103,073 |

These are national weighted estimates, not raw patient counts. Hoverboard table headers instead show 88,477.
Powered scooter growth began to plateau by 2022; Figure 2 and the text suggest a late-period decrease among
18–44-year-olds. Bicycle and hoverboard population rates were highest before 2021 and subsequently declined.
Thus not every device or age group followed sustained growth.[^burford2024]

## Limits and related evidence

Rates use Census populations per 100,000, not measured device use. The paper's sales/ridership explanations
are interpretations, not estimates of how much increased use explains increased injuries. These ED estimates
do not capture all injuries or isolate infrastructure effects. Trends are reported in the main text; supporting
online Appendix Tables B–C and Figure A were not accessed, so annual endpoints and their precision are not
independently checked here.

[CPSC's earlier report](/findings/safety/cpsc-micromobility-ed-injury-trends.md) uses overlapping NEISS years,
but different scope and classification procedures. Its e-bike annual reliability warnings should not be erased
by treating this paper as independent replication or joining the estimates into one series.
See [injury profiles](/findings/safety/us-micromobility-ed-injury-profiles.md) and
[interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).

[^burford2024]: Burford et al. (2024), methods pp. 1366–1367; results p. 1367; “Trends” p. 1369;
    Figure 2 p. 1371; limitations p. 1373. Online trend appendix not reviewed.
