# Chicago speed cameras reportedly reduced expected severe crashes by 15%

## In brief

- Streetsblog's January 2022 coverage attributes to a University of Illinois Chicago study a **15% reduction in expected
  fatal and serious-injury crashes citywide during 2015–2017** from Chicago's camera program, but not benefits at every
  camera site.
- This is a secondary estimate without the underlying model, comparison construction or uncertainty intervals. It is not
  a cyclist-specific risk estimate or a comparison against road redesign.

## Evidence and limits

Wilson presents the UIC estimate while discussing criticism that coverage of ticketing inequities understated
safety benefits. The article does not provide baseline crash counts, exposure, site-specific results or enough
methods to assess attribution. The primary study was not reviewed. Its international crash-reduction range
and selected NYC speeding-violation figures concern different settings and measures and should not be pooled
with the Chicago estimate.[^wilson]

Safety improvement can coexist with [unequal ticket burdens](../access/chicago-speed-camera-ticket-geography.md).
Neither result establishes the net effect of abolishing cameras or changing fines.
