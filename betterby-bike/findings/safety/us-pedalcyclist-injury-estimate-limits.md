# U.S. pedalcyclist injury estimates and reporting breaks

## In brief

- BTS's 2021 edition reports an estimated **49,000 injured U.S. pedalcyclists in 2019** in the highway
  series. This is a police-report-based motor-vehicle crash estimate, not all cycling injuries or serious injuries alone.
- The apparent jump from 45,000 in 2015 to 64,000 in 2016 cannot establish a comparable injury increase:
  the sampling system changed in 2016. The table has no 2020 estimate, not zero injuries.[^nts2021]

## Supporting table and methods

Table 2-2 reports 45,000 injured pedalcyclists in 2015, 64,000 in 2016, 50,000 in 2017,
47,000 in 2018 and 49,000 in 2019. The 2020 cell is “U,” defined as data not available.
These estimates concern persons injured, not numbers of crashes.[^nts2021]

The table's footnote s explains that the Crash Report Sampling System (CRSS) replaced the National
Automotive Sampling System General Estimates System (NASS GES) in 2016, with a different sample design.
It explicitly states that 2016 and later estimates are not comparable with 2015 and earlier estimates.
Starting with 2018 reporting, NHTSA also changed from solely sampled injury estimates to combining
FARS injuries in fatal crashes with CRSS/GES estimates of injuries in nonfatal crashes.[^nts2021]

## What is missing

Appendix E describes the police-reported motor-vehicle crash sampling basis and the exclusion of unreported
crashes. This is not surveillance of bicycle-only falls or all medically treated cycling injuries.
The glossary's highway injury categories include incapacitating, nonincapacitating evident and possible
injuries. The totals therefore cannot substitute for hospitalized or serious-injury counts.[^nts2021]

Table 2-2 supplies no pedalcyclist confidence intervals or travel-exposure denominator. The differences
between 2017, 2018 and 2019 point estimates do not by themselves establish statistically significant
changes, risk per trip, or effects of street treatments. Nor should the 2016 break be applied indiscriminately
to the separate [fatality series](us-pedalcyclist-fatalities-2010-2020.md), which has its own early-estimate caveat.

See [Interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) for why case coverage, severity and
exposure must match before comparing studies.
