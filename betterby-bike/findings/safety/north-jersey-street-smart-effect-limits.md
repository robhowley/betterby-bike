# North Jersey Street Smart accounts do not isolate program safety effects

## In brief

- In August 2021 reporting, police described better law adherence in Newton and a slight pedestrian-crash decline
  in Morristown alongside Street Smart activities, but supplied no numerical before/after comparisons.[^morel2021]
- Fair Lawn police said fewer crashes after its **January 2020** campaign launch were likely due to less driving
  and walking during COVID-19. These accounts do not establish a campaign effect or an advantage over street redesign.[^morel2021]

## Activities and reported changes

The three towns were among 13 North Jersey communities participating in TransOptions' Street Smart program.
Activities included traffic-safety signs, outreach, pedestrian decoys and police education or enforcement. Newton's
Lt. Michael Monaco described increased adherence to traffic laws; Morristown's Sgt. Brian LaBarre reported a small
crash decline and fewer darting-out incidents causing serious injuries. These are interviewed officers' assessments,
not independently measured compliance or severity changes.[^morel2021]

Fair Lawn Capt. Joseph Dawicki's pandemic explanation preserves an important alternative: fewer crashes can
accompany fewer opportunities for crashes, without a reduction in risk per crossing. The article supplies no exposure
series to test that explanation, either. TransOptions' claim of overall success and plans for future infrastructure
are not additional effectiveness estimates.[^morel2021]

## Limits

No matched control towns, comparable observation periods, numerical program effects or uncertainty are given.
The reporting neither proves benefit nor demonstrates ineffectiveness. Education, enforcement and engineering are
presented together, without estimating individual contributions. Statewide pedestrian fatality shares and local
historical totals in the article are context, not program outcomes. Underlying campaign evaluations were not reviewed.
