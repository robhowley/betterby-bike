# Manhattan toll-zone early crash counts fell, without an isolated pricing effect

## In brief

- Streetsblog reports **90 crashes and 37 people injured** in Manhattan below 60th Street during
  January 6–17, 2025, versus **199 crashes and 76 injuries** in its stated comparable 2024 window:
  approximately **55% fewer crashes and 51% fewer injuries**.[^kuntzman2025]
- These short-window counts do not isolate congestion pricing's effect or measure cyclist risk per trip;
  weather, exposure, comparator dates and reporting completeness are not adequately accounted for.

## Comparison and evidence

The January 23 article credits city statistics and Crashmapper. Its early-2023 comparison reports
173 crashes and 51 injuries, giving reported 2025 declines of 48% and 27%. These are all reported
crashes and people injured, not bicycle-specific outcomes or injury rates. The underlying records
were not independently reviewed.[^kuntzman2025]

The article describes the 2025 window as 10 business days plus one weekend and the 2024 window as the
same 12-day period, but calls the 2023 comparator its first 12 days. The chart caption says first
10 working days of each calendar year. The plot is absent from the PDF, so its dates and claimed
2021–2022 comparisons cannot be recovered. These descriptions do not establish matched calendar
or weekday exposure across years.

## Interpretation limits

NYC DOT cautions that it is too soon to draw conclusions; weather differences are explicitly unadjusted.
There is no comparison outside the zone, uncertainty interval, severity breakdown or assessment of delayed
crash reporting. The separately quoted 7.5% decrease in entering vehicles is not a matched exposure
denominator for these crash and injury counts. Neither sustained benefits nor a protected-bike-lane
safety effect can be inferred from officials' and advocates' explanations.[^kuntzman2025]

Like [NYC's shutdown-era count comparison](nyc-2020-shutdown-speeding-and-crash-counts.md), this distinguishes
lower reported crash burden from exposure-based risk. See the
[cycling injury synthesis](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md#early-toll-zone-counts-do-not-isolate-a-pricing-effect).
