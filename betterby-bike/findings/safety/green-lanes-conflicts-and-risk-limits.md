# No collisions in the Green Lane video sample does not establish injury risk

Monsere et al. reported **no collisions or near collisions in 144 hours of intersection safety video covering nearly
12,900 bicycles** in 2013. The observations covered protected-lane intersections in Chicago, Portland, San Francisco
and Washington, D.C., not the Austin facilities included elsewhere in the five-city study. No major or substantial
conflicts involving emergency maneuvers were identified.[^monsere2014]

## Low-level conflicts still occurred

The study treated conflicts as surrogate safety measures because recent installations lacked enough crash history.
“Minor” conflicts involved precautionary braking or a direction change; “precautionary” conflicts were lower-risk
adjustments. Section 8.3.3 reports 379 precautionary conflicts with motor vehicles, 216 with pedestrians and 70 with
other bicycles. The six reported minor bicycle/motor-vehicle conflicts are not a count of all minor conflicts:
Tables 8-8–8-9 show five at mixing/turning zones, one with a motor vehicle at Dearborn/Randolph, and an additional
pedestrian conflict at Dearborn/Congress, seven across all categories.[^monsere2014]

The report generally found higher exposure-normalized conflict rates at mixing zones than turning zones, but the
conflicts were predominantly low severity, designs had different traffic exposures, and the authors caution that
this does not establish a safety problem or a causal design ranking.

## Rider reports answer a different question

The intercept survey reports 18 riders with a collision (less than 2%) and roughly a third reporting a near collision.
These were respondent-defined events, not events from the video window. Table 8-7 totals 1,247 route responses with
Oak/Fell respondents counted twice; some riders denied a collision initially but later selected a collision type.
Its aggregate/category totals should not be treated as a clean exposure-based rate. No injuries appeared in submitted
narratives, but injuries were not explicitly requested, so this is not proof that none occurred.[^monsere2014]

Similarly, 96% of intercepted riders said cycling safety had increased (Table 8-3, n=1,045). That is a perception among
users, not a measured change in injury probability; riders avoiding the facilities may be absent.

## What can be concluded

The video did not reveal serious conflicts during the sampled hours. It cannot demonstrate zero underlying risk,
measure a before/after injury reduction, or substitute for long-term matched injury and exposure data. Neither the
surveys nor video establish effects on fatal or severe injuries.
