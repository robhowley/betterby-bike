# CPSC scooter follow-up describes injury conditions, not their causal effects

## In brief

- CPSC's 2022 U.S. e-scooter follow-up estimates rental scooters accounted for 37% of ED injury cases and
  helmet use for 13%. These are injury-case characteristics, not rental-versus-owned risks or helmet effects.
- Only 309 of 3,719 assigned investigations were completed and verified as in-scope e-scooters (8%);
  weighting addresses observed differences, not necessarily all nonresponse bias.
- Surface shares conflict between the report's prose and table, and no riding-exposure denominator exists.
  The study cannot rank roads and sidewalks by injury risk.[^cpsc2023]

## Sampling and weighting

Telephone interviews and online surveys produced 432 completed scooter investigations; 309 were verified
e-scooters, including 24 originally coded unspecified. The 3,410 remaining assignments were incomplete or
out of scope, so the 8% is an in-scope completion fraction, not a pure survey response rate.
Raking matched known assigned-case margins for sex, age, race, hospital stratum and e-scooter response rate.
Age, race and stratum categories were collapsed; missing contacts, unreachable patients and refusals remained
important selection concerns. The report describes scenario results as representing the participating portion.[^cpsc2023]

Tables 2.3–2.9 use a stated weighted total of 51,700 injuries and retain unspecified responses. Rental injuries
were estimated at 19,100 (37%); non-rental and unspecified were combined at 32,500 (63%), not all privately
owned. Helmet use was 6,600 (13%), lights/headlamp 21,900 (42%), and none/unspecified 26,400 (51%).
Equipment selections could overlap. These are not protective-effect estimates.[^cpsc2023]

## Conditions do not establish causes

The weighted estimates include 23% reporting darkness/difficulty seeing, 11% reporting a distraction and 32%
carrying something. Questionnaire A5 asks whether it was dark or difficult to see, not whether darkness caused
the crash. There is no uninjured rider group to identify causal effects of visibility, distraction or baggage.
The executive summary's causal wording is stronger than these questions support.[^cpsc2023]

Table 2.5 reports paved-road and sidewalk shares of 63% and 29%, whereas adjacent prose says 74% and 23%.
Its total is printed as 41,700, inconsistent with the section's 51,700 denominator and the listed estimates.
Multiple surfaces are permitted, but this does not reconcile the conflicting total or prose. These values
are retained as a reporting conflict, not resolved into a facility comparison. Several other sample totals,
including Table 2.6 and updated 2021 Appendix C tables, also fail to reconcile with their rows.

Appendix B's narrative-identified rental estimate for 2022 is 7,900, unlike the 19,100 follow-up estimate.
Incomplete rental identification and different estimation bases prevent treating these as interchangeable.
Likewise, neither should be divided by shared-fleet trips without checking population and geographic coverage.
