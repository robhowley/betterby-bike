# Vehicle-generation changes in forward visibility

## In brief

- In 17 measured vehicles across six popular U.S. nameplates, each latest sampled model had less visible ground
  within the forward 10 m semicircle than its earliest sampled model. CR-V and Suburban losses were about 58%
  and 51%; sedan losses were 7–8% and may be measurement error.[^epstein2025]
- This was not a uniform decline at every redesign or distance: Grand Cherokee improved between 2020 and 2023,
  and results in the 10–20 m band were mixed. These are static geometry measurements, not injury-risk changes.[^epstein2025]

## Measured comparisons

Table 1 reports visible ground area using a fixed 50th-percentile male eyepoint, with the seat at mid-height
and mid-track. The assessment covers 180° ahead of the driver; radii start at the eyepoint, not the bumper.
The following preserves the earliest and latest measured rows for each nameplate, not a common year interval.[^epstein2025]

| Nameplate | Measured years | Visible area within 10 m, m² | Share of 10 m semicircle visible | Visible area in 10–20 m band, m² |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chevrolet Suburban | 2006 → 2023 | 88.5 → 43.3 | 56.4% → 27.6% | 416.6 → 399.2 |
| Ford F-150 | 2001 → 2015 | 68.2 → 56.3 | 43.4% → 35.9% | 410.6 → 410.4 |
| Honda CR-V | 1997 → 2022 | 106.3 → 44.2 | 67.7% → 28.1% | 420.4 → 385.3 |
| Honda Accord | 2007 → 2021 | 102.1 → 93.6 | 65.0% → 59.6% | 381.3 → 411.0 |
| Jeep Grand Cherokee | 2006 → 2023 | 97.9 → 79.0 | 62.3% → 50.3% | 385.4 → 406.3 |
| Toyota Camry | 2008 → 2023 | 96.0 → 89.2 | 61.1% → 56.8% | 386.8 → 408.1 |

The 58% CR-V loss is a relative reduction in visible area, not a 58-percentage-point loss or a 58% increase
in crash risk. Its visible share fell 39.6 percentage points. Grand Cherokee's intermediate 2020 measurement
was 70.8 m² within 10 m, below both 2006 and 2023. F-150's outer-band endpoints were nearly unchanged;
Accord, Grand Cherokee and Camry gained outer-band visible area.[^epstein2025]

## Limits and related evidence

The sample contains selected accessible vehicles and generations, not all cars, all redesigns or a fleet-weighted
trend. Table 1 has no uncertainty intervals; the authors explicitly caution that the small sedan losses may be
measurement error. Figures 4–6 show differing hood, mirror and pillar patterns rather than one uniform mechanism.
The [measurement-limit Finding](ground-plane-visibility-inference-limits.md) explains why these changes cannot
supply cyclist injury probabilities or a visibility-attributable death toll.
