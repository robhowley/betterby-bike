# U.S. pedalcyclist traffic fatalities, 2010–2020

## In brief

- BTS's 2021 edition reports 623 U.S. pedalcyclist traffic fatalities in 2010 and 846 in 2019,
  but not an uninterrupted rise: the count fell from 871 in 2018 to 846 in 2019.
- Its 2020 figure of 891 is an **early estimate**. These motor-vehicle traffic deaths are neither all
  cycling deaths nor a measure of risk per trip, mile or hour.[^nts2021]

## Supporting series

Table 2-1 reports the following annual pedalcyclist fatalities; Table 2-19 repeats the 2010–2019 values,
not an independent confirmation.[^nts2021]

| Year | Fatalities reported in the 2021 edition |
| --- | ---: |
| 2010 | 623 |
| 2011 | 682 |
| 2012 | 734 |
| 2013 | 749 |
| 2014 | 729 |
| 2015 | 829 |
| 2016 | 853 |
| 2017 | 806 |
| 2018 | 871 |
| 2019 | 846 |
| 2020 | 891 (early estimate) |

## Coverage and interpretation

Appendix F identifies NHTSA's January 12, 2021 personal communication as the 1980–2019 highway source,
and *Early Estimates of Motor Vehicle Traffic Fatalities and Fatality Rate by Sub-Categories in 2020*,
Table 2, as the 2020 source. The reviewed PDF does not supply an uncertainty interval for the 891 estimate;
it should not be relabeled as a final or currently revised count.[^nts2021]

The highway fatal-crash definition requires a motor vehicle on a trafficway and a death within 30 days.
FARS excludes nonpublic roads. Bicycle-only falls are therefore outside this traffic-fatality measure.
The series has no cycling exposure denominator and cannot establish changes in individual riding risk,
the causes of the annual changes, or the effects of bicycle infrastructure.[^nts2021]

See [injury-estimate limitations](us-pedalcyclist-injury-estimate-limits.md) for a different outcome with a
sampling-system break, and [cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) for comparisons with
exposure-based and broader crash-coverage studies.
