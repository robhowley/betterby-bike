# Lime's reported incident rates fell without identifying a bike-lane safety effect

## In brief

- DC's **reported incidents per Lime trip fell 39% between 2021 and 2024**, alongside a **38% rise in bike-lane use**.
  Coincidence does not establish that lanes caused the decline.
- Bloomington's rate was relatively stable during **2021–2023**, then reportedly fell **71% in 2024 to date**. This
  September 2024 report is not a full-year comparison, and unreported incidents remain unknown.[^lime2024]

## Reporting basis

Lime accepts reports via its app, phone and website, including third-party reports, and exchanges information
with police. The analysis divides reported incidents by trips and describes annual trends. Although the methods
also propose examining incident location, the reported safety results do not supply facility-specific incident
and exposure comparisons sufficient to isolate lane safety effects.[^lime2024]

The report calls 99.99% of DC trips over its program history and 99.98% of Bloomington trips since 2021
“incident free.” These should be understood as trips without a recorded report, not exhaustive injury
surveillance. More than 90% of Bloomington's reported incidents reportedly required no medical attention.
That denominator is reported incidents, not all crashes or all injured riders.[^lime2024]

## Limits and interpretation

No reporting-completeness estimate, uncertainty interval or exact 2024 safety cutoff is supplied. Changes in
reporting, user mix, exposure per trip, vehicles or other road conditions can affect trends. Comparing two
similar percentage changes does not estimate an infrastructure effect; the ridership regression elsewhere in
the report is not a safety regression. Trip-based rates also differ from per-mile injury rates and from
severity among hospital patients. The authors' positive infrastructure explanation is an interpretation.[^lime2024]
