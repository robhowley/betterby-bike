# NYC residential battery-fire reports describe blocked exits, not risk per device

## In brief

- THE CITY's review of a dozen NYC fire-marshal reports since January 2023 describes fatal battery fires
  involving indoor charging, obstructed exits and repair/storage concentrations, including deaths of residents
  who were not riding the devices. These selected cases do not estimate risk per battery or trip.
- Reported FDNY totals fell from 18 deaths in 2023 to five in 2024, but fires rose from 268 in all of 2023
  to 270 through December 27, 2024. Lower fatalities alone do not establish fewer fires or effective prevention.[^article]

## Circumstances reported

An August 2023 Ozone Park fire began with an e-bike battery bought on eBay, charging in the first-floor
stairwell beside two older batteries. Smoke blocked the only exit from upstairs; a 93-year-old resident died.
An April 2023 Astoria fire killed two siblings after a scooter was charged near the only stairwell exit
with an incompatible Amazon-purchased charger and an extension cord. These are attributed marshal accounts,
not comparative tests of retailers, chargers or charging locations.[^article]

A Bushwick fatal fire involved an apartment repair business with 50 batteries and unattended charging.
After the June 2023 Chinatown repair-shop fire killed four residents, investigators recovered 1,260
batteries. Batteries removed into steel drums subsequently reignited on multiple days. The cases describe
hazards during storage and disposal as well as charging, without estimating their frequency.[^article]

## What cannot be concluded

The article attributes dangerous conditions to uncertified batteries and incompatible equipment, but supplies
no representative sample or certified-equipment comparison. It cannot quantify the reduction achievable with
certification, safe charging facilities or enforcement. The annual totals have unequal observation windows
and no exposure denominator; no explanation for the different fire and death trends was tested.

For the Harlem fire, residents reported earlier management complaints and a 311 report. DOT said the cited
311 case concerned bicycle racks, not charging, and did not release the text. A confirmed ignored city
battery-hazard report therefore cannot be inferred from the article.[^article]

## Related evidence

[CPSC's selected national investigations](/findings/safety/cpsc-micromobility-investigated-hazards.md)
also describe charging hazards but cover 2017–2022 and use different ascertainment. Do not pool these
accounts with that series or [ED injury profiles](/findings/safety/us-micromobility-ed-injury-profiles.md).
The [injury-risk Topic](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) distinguishes resident fire harm from exposure-based
