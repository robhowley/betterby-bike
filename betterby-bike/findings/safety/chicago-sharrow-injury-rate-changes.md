# Chicago sharrow groups had larger weighted injury-rate increases

## In brief

- Chicago's weighted injury rate rose 103.8% in sharrow-only groups, versus 10.2% with new lanes and 12.1% with no
  new treatment, comparing 2011–2012 with 2013–2014.[^ferenchak2019]
- The sharrow increase differed from no new treatment; the lane comparison was at the authors' 5% significance
  threshold. The sharrow increase alone did not meet that threshold.[^ferenchak2019]
- Rates measure injuries per year per 100 resident bicycle commuters, not a doubling of injury counts or proof of
  sharrow harm. Separate models estimated falling total injury counts in sharrow groups.[^ferenchak2019]

## Measurement

Installations occurred in 2012–2013. Injuries include police-recorded dooring and non-dooring cases.[^ferenchak2019]

| Treatment added | Block groups | Before | After | Change | Change % | Within-group p |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| None | 1,948 | 8.70 | 9.75 | +1.05 | +12.1% | 0.260 |
| Sharrows only | 42 | 8.97 | 18.28 | +9.31 | +103.8% | 0.085 |
| Bicycle lanes only | 149 | 16.63 | 18.32 | +1.69 | +10.2% | 0.986 |

Rates and absolute changes are **weighted injuries per year per 100 bicycle commuters**, as published in Table 4.
They are not injuries per 100 trips or percentages of riders injured. The sharrow within-group increase did not reach
p < 0.05. Between-group tests of rate changes gave p = 0.004 for sharrows versus none and p = 0.050 for sharrows
versus lanes (the authors classify both as significant at 95% confidence). Lanes versus none was inconclusive,
p = 0.779. These are different tests, not conflicting significance statements.[^ferenchak2019]

## Counts, weighting and causal limits

The authors separately report significant decreases in modeled total injury counts for sharrow and no-new-treatment
groups, and an increase for lane groups. They explain the different weighted-rate pattern by changes being
concentrated in groups with different commuter populations: sharrow rate increases occurred in groups with more
commuters, while lane-group increases were concentrated in groups with fewer commuters. The 103.8% result is not a
doubling of injury counts.[^ferenchak2019]

Treatment was not randomly assigned. “None” means no installation in 2012–2013, not necessarily an infrastructure-free
street. Lane groups pool standard, buffered and protected lanes. Resident commuters are an imperfect exposure proxy,
and the block-group analysis cannot establish the risk of riding directly on a sharrow, an individual installation's
effect, or differences in severe injuries. Baseline totals and component rates in Table 4 also do not add up; the
published total-rate comparison is retained without reconstructing it.[^ferenchak2019]
