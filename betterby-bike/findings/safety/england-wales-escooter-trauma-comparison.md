# Reported e-scooter trauma profiles do not establish injury risk per trip

## In brief

- Guardian coverage of 2020–2022 trauma records from 18 English and Welsh cities reports more than
  threefold traumatic brain injury and blood-vessel damage comparisons for e-scooter versus motorbike cases.
- Internal-organ injuries were reportedly 1.5 times as likely, but fractures were less likely than for
  motorbike or bicycle cases. The injury profile is mixed, not uniformly worse.
- These are secondary reports of registry injury comparisons, not injury rates per trip or kilometre;
  the article provides neither riding exposure nor full statistical methods.[^guardian2026]

## What the comparison measures

The article describes David Bodansky's national major trauma registry analysis, published in Scientific
Reports. It says cyclists also fared better for brain, blood-vessel and internal-organ injuries, without
numerical bicycle comparisons. The capture does not identify the effect-measure scale, sample sizes,
confidence intervals, adjustment variables or detailed inclusion criteria. Its wording should not be
converted into a precise adjusted risk ratio.[^guardian2026]

The reported female excess in collisions and severe injuries likewise lacks exposure denominators and
model details. Fewer than 6% of e-scooter riders reportedly wore helmets, but that does not isolate a helmet
effect. Suggested wheel-size and handlebar-height mechanisms and calls for mandatory training remain
interpretations or proposals, not measured causes or demonstrated remedies.[^guardian2026]

## Relationship to other evidence

Unlike the [DC single-ED mileage comparison](dc-scooter-bicycle-ed-mileage-rates.md), this coverage supplies
no distance denominator. Different settings and case selection also prevent pooling its injury comparisons
with the [DC injury profiles](dc-scooter-bicycle-injury-profiles.md). The
[injury-risk Topic](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) separates injury composition from incidence.
