# New Jersey's 2025 traffic-death snapshot is not its full-year forecast

## In brief

- A news excerpt attributes **350 New Jersey traffic deaths through August 18, 2025** to State Police data; this is an
  all-road-user count, not a cyclist risk rate.
- Its **648-death full-year projection** is not an observed annual total. The chart labels **298 deaths as projected**,
  and the excerpt supplies no forecasting method or uncertainty.[^njmidyear]

## What the figures measure

The opening says New Jersey is “on pace” for 648 deaths. The accompanying 2008–2025 chart distinguishes cyclists,
drivers, pedestrians, passengers and a projected segment. The latest bar therefore mixes a reported partial-year
count with a forecast; it must not be compared with earlier full-year bars as if 2025 were complete. The displayed
298 projected deaths plus the reported 350 give 648, but this arithmetic does not establish how the forecast was
produced or whether it accounts for seasonality.[^njmidyear]

## Limits and relationship to other evidence

Only the excerpt was reviewed, not underlying police records or chart calculations. It gives no exposure denominator,
reporting-completeness definition or uncertainty interval. The selected child and cyclist fatalities illustrate losses,
not a representative sample for estimating cyclist risk, age-specific rates or effects of street design or enforcement.

The [earlier state/national death comparison](new-jersey-national-traffic-death-trends.md) concerns percentage changes
with an unspecified year and baseline. This explicitly dated snapshot cannot fill those missing details or establish
a comparable trend.
