# StreetVision behavior changes do not validate crash prediction or prevention

## In brief

- Streetsblog reports Flagstaff hard braking down 24.5% and phone distraction down 14% after a
  signal-to-roundabout conversion, and Kenmore school-zone speeding down 30% within two weeks of
  24/7 camera enforcement. These are behavior measures, not crash or injury reductions.
- The April 2026 article centers on StreetVision's developer and supplies neither prediction-accuracy
  results nor the baselines and comparison groups needed to attribute these changes to the interventions.

## What the measures establish

StreetVision combines insurance-linked volunteer cellphone data with official crash records. The Flagstaff
changes were reportedly detected within weeks; the Kenmore figure concerns a different intervention and city.
Neither account identifies sample sizes, traffic exposure, baseline dates, uncertainty or whether its
percentage refers to event counts, rates or another behavior metric. Pooling the figures into one safety
effect or treating them as independent validations of the platform would be unwarranted.[^article]

The developer describes a dataset of 55 million drivers, but scale alone does not show representative
coverage of uninsured drivers, nonparticipants or particular locations. The article provides no held-out
test, false-positive rate or calibration result to assess the advertised ability to predict crashes.
No reported pedestrian or cyclist injury outcome establishes that using the tool prevented harm.[^article]

The article's separate distraction comparisons also need care: phone-in-hand presence within a minute
before about one-third of crashes, as asserted by the developer, is not the same measure as cellphone
involvement in 8% of crash deaths attributed to NHTSA. Different denominators and definitions prevent
using the contrast to calculate an undercount or the share of crashes caused by phones.[^article]

## Related evidence

[Chicago's reported camera crash reduction](chicago-reported-speed-camera-crash-reduction.md) concerns
a modeled severe-crash outcome, not the Kenmore speeding measure. [NYC shutdown reporting](nyc-2020-shutdown-speeding-and-crash-counts.md)
likewise distinguishes detected behavior from crash counts and exposure. The
[injury-risk synthesis](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md#behavior-is-not-an-injury-outcome) keeps these
behavior indicators separate from demonstrated injury prevention.
