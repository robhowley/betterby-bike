# Reported New Jersey enforcement decline and highway crash increase

## In brief

- A cropped Times passage reports New Jersey state-trooper traffic citations fell **81% in August 2023 versus the year
  before**, following a slowdown starting in July. A second commentary instead describes 81% over an eight-month period
  beginning in July 2023; the discrepancy remains unresolved, not independent confirmation.[^nj2024][^njexcerpt2]
- The surrounding news item reports **27% more crashes on the state's main highways**, but gives no baseline or exact
  comparison period. These coincident changes do not establish that reduced enforcement caused the increase or quantify
  cyclist risk.[^nj2024]

## What was reported

The Times passage covers citations for speeding, drunken driving, cellphone use and other violations statewide,
with troopers described as patrolling busy highways and remote rural roads. It says the slowdown lasted more than
eight months and coincided with an almost immediate motor-vehicle crash increase, based on records obtained by
the newspaper. The surrounding item says crashes continued rising in six of the next seven months, without
identifying those months or the comparison used. The citation and crash percentages have different stated scopes
and cannot be combined into an enforcement-to-crash effect estimate.[^nj2024]

A second commentary attributes the 81% decline to an eight-month period beginning in July 2023 and repeats an
“almost immediate uptick” in crashes without quantifying it. It does not clarify the citation comparator. Its
investigation account concerns whether the slowdown was intentional, not an established motive or completed
causal analysis. Do not substitute this period description for the more specific year-on-year statement above.[^njexcerpt2]

## Limits

Two single-page image excerpts were reviewed, not the full Times article or underlying records. The 27% is supplied
by the surrounding unidentified news item, not the visible Times passage. Neither provides crash totals, exposure,
severity, untreated comparators or adjustment for other changes in the material available here. Counts are not
per-trip or per-mile risk, and no cyclist-specific result is shown. Komanoff's accompanying suggestion that dozens
of deaths could have been prevented is opinion, not an estimated attributable death toll. The embedded quotation
and surrounding retelling, along with excerpt 2's commentary, are one reporting chain, not independent studies.[^nj2024][^njexcerpt2]
