# Motor vehicles dominated reported scooter and e-bike fatalities, but harm extended beyond riders

## In brief

- CPSC's 2023 report identified 233 U.S. deaths associated with micromobility in 2017–2022: 111 e-scooter,
  104 e-bike and 18 hoverboard deaths. Reporting was incomplete, especially for 2021–2022.
- Motor-vehicle collisions accounted for 75 scooter and 58 e-bike deaths. Ten pedestrians died after being
  struck by scooters or e-bikes; 19 deaths across all products involved fires. These reported counts do not
  measure nationwide mortality rates or comparative risk per trip.[^cpsc2023]

## What the reported cases show

Table 3.4 assigns the 233 deaths to 135 motor-vehicle accidents, 34 control issues, 19 fire hazards,
12 pedestrian accidents, one pavement-related event, four other events and 28 unspecified falls.
The 12 pedestrian-accident deaths include four pedestrians struck by scooters, six pedestrians struck by
e-bikes and two e-bike riders who died after striking pedestrians. They are not 12 pedestrian deaths.
Fire deaths comprised four scooter, 13 hoverboard and two e-bike deaths. Control issues included striking
fixed objects or curbs; many falls lacked enough information to identify a mechanism.[^cpsc2023]

## Ascertainment and comparison limits

CPSRMS combines complaints, news reports, authorities, death certificates and other reports, supplemented here
by NEISS fatalities. It is not a nationally representative sample or complete census. Death-certificate reports
can lag two years. Table 3.1 records 76 deaths in both 2021 and 2022, not a measured year-on-year increase.
Counts and year assignments can change as further reports arrive.[^cpsc2023]

Main-table totals consistently give 233 overall and 104 e-bike deaths, although nearby prose sometimes says
232 and 103. Rental-scooter motor-vehicle/control counts disagree between narrative and Table 3.4; no such
subdivision is used here. These unresolved inconsistencies further discourage fine-grained comparisons.

The pattern does not contradict the [mostly non-collision DC scooter injuries](/findings/safety/dc-e-scooter-incident-mechanisms.md):
fatal reports and one hospital's interviewed injury patients have different severity and selection.
[Selected product investigations](/findings/safety/cpsc-micromobility-investigated-hazards.md) also overlap this
fatality set and must not be added as independent deaths. See [interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).
