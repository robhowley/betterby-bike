# NYC protected-lane corridors reported fewer injuries overall, with little cyclist-count change

## In brief

- NYCDOT's 2014 comparison of 12 Manhattan protected-lane projects reported 20% fewer injuries overall and 17%
  fewer injury crashes after redesign.[^nyc2014]
- Cyclist injuries changed little, from 100 to 98 (2% fewer); most improvement was in pedestrian and motor-vehicle
  occupant injuries.[^nyc2014]
- Counts are not clearly annual averages or period totals. They neither measure cyclist risk per trip nor isolate
  protected lanes from other redesign elements and trends.[^nyc2014]

## Projects and reported values

NYCDOT's September 2014 report pools treated portions of 12 Manhattan projects on six avenues (seven miles), installed
in 2007–2011 and eligible because at least three years of after data existed. Its NYPD AIS/TAMS before/after chart gives:

| Measure | Before | After | Reported change |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Crashes with injuries | 514 | 426 | −17% |
| Motor-vehicle occupant injuries | 221 | 166 | −25% |
| Pedestrian injuries | 280 | 220 | −22% |
| Cyclist injuries | 100 | 98 | −2% |
| Total injuries | 601 | 484 | −20% |

These are the chart's displayed values. The panel does not clearly say whether they are annual averages or period
totals; do not label them three-year totals. The report's separate corridor-risk method specifies three-year before
and after periods. Its rounded percentage labels are retained rather than recalculated.[^nyc2014]

The overall improvement is mostly in non-cyclist injuries. Near-constant cyclist injuries alongside rising bicycle
counts can coexist with a falling exposure-normalized measure, but the aggregate chart alone is not cyclist risk per
trip. Injury crashes are events, whereas injuries count casualties.

Projects also changed signals, pedestrian refuges, vehicle lanes and sometimes the street network. Without an untreated
safety comparison, exposure for all modes or uncertainty estimates, the changes do not identify the isolated effect of
protected lanes. Eligibility excludes newer projects and does not establish results for all NYC lanes.[^nyc2014]
