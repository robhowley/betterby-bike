# North Jersey pedestrian death counts have incomplete geographic coverage

## In brief

- NorthJersey.com's collected police records report **296 pedestrian deaths in 2010–2020** across five North Jersey
  counties, but omit Essex municipalities relying on county records, including Newark. This is not a complete regional total.[^gomez2021]
- Counts fell from **33 in 2019 to 32 in 2020** in the collected records. Without walking or traffic exposure,
  this does not establish safer walking or an effect of a safety program.[^gomez2021]

## Reporting basis and boundaries

The newspaper requested fatal pedestrian motor-vehicle crash reports from 171 police departments. It reports fatal
incidents in 67 towns and county totals of Bergen 145, Passaic 61, Morris 49, Essex 30 and Sussex 11. The assertion
that 171 towns submitted data coexists with explicit unfulfilled Essex requests, so participation should not be
read as complete case coverage. Geographic rankings describe collected deaths, not risk per resident or trip.[^gomez2021]

The article reports a peak of 34 deaths in 2018, followed by 33 and 32. Its national rising trend and 2021 statewide
all-motor-vehicle fatality figures use different populations and periods and do not contradict this local comparison.
Pandemic changes in travel are a plausible alternative to a safety improvement, not measured exposure adjustments.[^gomez2021]

The article separately reports 294 crashes, about 15% hit-and-runs and citations in roughly 25% of crashes. Deaths
and crashes are different units; citation frequency is not a culpability estimate. The headline's “ten years” does
not resolve whether the stated 2010–2020 endpoints represent eleven full calendar years. No annual average is inferred.
Underlying records were not reviewed, and the repeated article within the PDF is not another observation sample.
