# DC scooter riders had higher estimated single-ED visits per citywide mile than cyclists

## In brief

- At one DC hospital, estimated visits per million citywide miles were 20.7 for scooters in 2019 versus 5.5 for
  bicycles in 2015–2017: rate ratio 3.76 (95% CI 3.08–4.59), using March–November observation periods.
- This is not complete citywide injury surveillance: visits cover one ED's staffed recruitment hours, while
  mileage is modeled citywide. The scooter rate remained higher under tested assumptions, not every possible bias.[^paper]

## Numerators and exposure

The rate analysis used 121 screened scooter riders and 483 cyclists, including refusals and some patients too
injured to communicate, unlike the injury-profile analysis of 99 and 337 interviewees. After-hours presentations
and patients missed before screening remained excluded. Figure A3 estimates one otherwise eligible non-interviewed
scooter case was a non-rider and removes it from the rate numerator.[^paper]

Scooter exposure starts with 4,737,871 operator-reported shared trips, divided by the sample's rounded 85% shared
use to estimate 5,573,966 total trips. Multiplication by interviewees' mean 1.05-mile pre-injury trip gives
5,852,664 miles. Trip length was missing for 12 of 99 scooter interviewees.[^paper]

Bicycle exposure assumes 16,663 average ACS bicycle commuters each made 339 annual commute trips (a national
all-worker estimate), then divides by the injured sample's 52% commute-trip share. Prorating to 720 study days
and multiplying by mean pre-injury trip length of 4.10 miles yields 21,428,372 trips and 87,856,326 miles.
These are extrapolations, not observed citywide cycling totals.[^paper]

## Sensitivity and limits

Table A5 reports scooter rates of 19.5 or 22.0 when estimated cases are restricted to trips entirely within DC,
using sample or operator trip lengths (1.05 or 0.93 miles). Corresponding cycling rates are 7.1 with the 52%
commute assumption and 2.7 with a 20% commute assumption; all-trip cycling rates are 5.5 and 2.1 respectively.
The alternative assumptions preserve the direction, but confidence intervals do not resolve structural exposure
error or whether either mode is disproportionately used near this hospital.[^paper]

Only one of five adult-serving DC EDs contributed cases. Recruitment hours differed by an hour, years differed,
and sample-derived trip lengths and commute/shared-use proportions may not represent all travel. The estimates
cannot rank facilities, establish an intrinsic vehicle effect or predict current risk as fleets and users change.
