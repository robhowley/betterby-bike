# NYC shutdown camera tickets rose while reported crash counts fell

## In brief

- Streetsblog reported NYC camera-issued speeding summonses 83% higher across 22 shutdown weekdays beginning
  March 23, 2020 than in a comparable January–February period, not an 83% rise in the share of drivers speeding.
- The same article reported citywide collisions down 77% and injuries down 75% between late March and late April,
  alongside an estimated 80–90% fall in driving distance. These differently specified comparisons do not establish
  safer cycling or a precise change in crash risk per mile.[^article]

## What the counts measure

The camera comparison draws on city open data as reported by Streetsblog. The p. 2 chart compares 22 ordinal
weekdays in March–April with January–February. The article contests NYPD's claim that speeding was declining:
a slight decline from the shutdown peak can coexist with counts far above the earlier baseline. The article
attributes collision and injury reductions to NYPD and the driving-distance estimate to StreetLight Data.[^article]

These are detected violations, overall crash/injury counts and estimated vehicle miles traveled, respectively.
No cyclist-specific injury trend, mode-specific exposure, uncertainty intervals or controlled intervention
comparison is supplied. The pre-shutdown comparison dates and matched baselines for crashes and driving are
not fully specified. Camera locations, operating hours and traffic passing cameras are not controlled for.
Underlying datasets were not reviewed, so the figures remain attributed news reporting rather than a reproduced
analysis.[^article]

## Interpretation

Lower total crashes can coexist with more detected speeding during a large travel disruption. Neither total
alone establishes risk to an individual road user. The author proposes reduced congestion as the explanation;
this article does not isolate that mechanism or estimate the effects of cameras or bicycle infrastructure.
The [Chicago camera safety estimate](chicago-reported-speed-camera-crash-reduction.md) concerns a different
place, design and outcome, not independent confirmation of the NYC shutdown comparison.

See [Interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md#shutdown-ticket-and-crash-counts-measure-different-things)
for the distinction between all-user counts, cyclist outcomes and travel exposure.

[^article]: Streetsblog, May 4, 2020, PDF p. 2 (camera comparison and chart), pp. 3–4 (NYPD collision/injury figures and StreetLight travel estimate).
