# A 2018 review reports mixed ride-hailing and alcohol-related crash results

## In brief

- A 2018 Alcohol Justice report describes a California study as finding about six fewer DUI fatalities per
  100, with gains absent during surge pricing, but a separate 100-county comparison found no difference in
  alcohol-related or overall traffic fatalities.
- Its four-city study summary reports fewer nonfatal alcohol-related crashes in some cities, not others,
  and no difference in overall injury crashes. These are distinct studies and outcomes, not a pooled estimate.
- The evidence as reported supports uncertainty about setting-specific benefits, not proof that ride-hailing
  eliminates impaired driving or never helps. None of these summaries evaluates an offset to a later-last-call policy.[^latenight2018]

## Reporting basis

Appendix I identifies Greenwood and Wattal's California analysis (*MIS Quarterly*, 2017), Brazil and Kirk's
comparison of the 100 most populous U.S. metropolitan counties (*American Journal of Epidemiology*, 2016), and
Morrison et al.'s four-city interrupted time-series analysis. The prose calls the latter a 2018 study while the
reference lists 2017 and volume 187(2), pp. 224–232. This ingestion does not resolve the bibliographic discrepancy.
The report does not provide study sample periods, crash counts, confidence intervals or sufficient model details
to assess comparability or reproduce the estimates.[^latenight2018]

## Limits and interpretation

The underlying papers were not independently reviewed. These are historical findings selected for an advocacy
report opposing later bar hours, not a comprehensive or current ride-hailing assessment. Fatalities, nonfatal
alcohol-related crashes and all injury crashes answer different questions. A null association is not proof of no
effect, and a positive result in one setting does not establish general protection. The report's discussion of
cost, surge pricing and limited late-night transit is a proposed explanation and policy argument, not an isolated
test of those mechanisms or of cyclist safety.[^latenight2018]
