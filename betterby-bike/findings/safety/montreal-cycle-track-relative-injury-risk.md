# Six Montreal cycle tracks had lower pooled injury risk than reference streets

## In brief

- Across six Montreal two-way cycle tracks, pooled emergency-response-recorded injury risk was 28% lower than on
  nearby streets without bicycle facilities.[^lusk2011]
- Only three tracks showed statistically clear reductions; the other three comparisons allowed both lower and
  higher risk.[^lusk2011]
- This is a route association, not an installation effect: 1999–July 2008 injuries were compared using short 2009
  cyclist counts, and underlying traffic danger differed between routes.[^lusk2011]

## Route estimates

Pooled emergency-medical-response injury relative risk (RR) was 0.72, 95% CI 0.60–0.85. Injuries covered the
April–November cycling seasons from 1 April 1999 through 31 July 2008; exposure came from simultaneous two-hour
commuting counts in mild weather in 2009.[^lusk2011]

| Cycle track       | Injury RR (95% CI), versus its reference street(s) |
| ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Brébeuf           | 0.42 (0.26–0.68)                                   |
| Rachel            | 1.18 (0.87–1.60)                                   |
| Berri             | 0.48 (0.31–0.75)                                   |
| Maisonneuve       | 0.32 (0.17–0.62)                                   |
| Christophe Colomb | 1.01 (0.61–1.68)                                   |
| René Levesque     | 1.01 (0.60–1.68)                                   |

Three tracks had significantly lower risk. The remaining three did not differ significantly; their intervals allow both
benefit and harm. Thus, no statistically significant increase is not proof of equal or lower risk on every
route.[^lusk2011]

Table 2 reports 340 track injuries and 191 reference-street injuries, with 4,009 versus 1,612 counted cyclists. The
approximately 2.5-fold track use is a route comparison, not evidence that installing tracks caused a 2.5-fold ridership
increase. RR used injury counts divided by simultaneous cyclist counts, not separately estimated historical kilometres
for the reference streets.[^lusk2011]

## Limits on interpretation

The routes were selected to offer similar journeys and approximately similar intersection exposure, but this was not a
randomized or before-and-after intervention study. The motor-vehicle-occupant injury ratio, a proxy for underlying
traffic danger, was 1.05 overall (95% CI 0.95–1.16), but only 0.09 at Brébeuf and 0.22 at Maisonneuve. Lower background
traffic danger could contribute to their favourable comparisons; the pooled proxy does not remove route-level
differences.[^lusk2011]

EMR records could not identify whether intersection casualties had been riding along the studied route or a cross
street; the authors adjusted injury counts using police-recorded crash proportions. Short 2009 counts may not represent
relative exposure throughout the earlier injury period. Injury severity was unavailable, and six two-way tracks in one
city do not establish effects for all protected-lane designs.[^lusk2011]
