# NYC hardened daylighting had a pedestrian injury benefit, but not a significant all-mode benefit

## In brief

- Across 189 NYC intersections hardened in 2019–2021, pedestrian injuries fell by **0.14 per intersection-year
  more than at nearby untreated intersections** (p=0.01), using two years before and after installation.
- The 567 sign-only sites showed no significant pedestrian or all-mode injury difference; hardened sites
  also showed no significant all-mode difference. Null results do not establish zero effect.
- Hardening included several different designs, mostly Citi Bike stations. The result does not isolate
  visibility, prove a cyclist benefit or establish that all hardening treatments work equally well.[^dot2025]

## Results and methods

DOT used 2017–2023 crash records, excluding other interventions during the comparison period. Controls
were up to three non-daylit intersections along each street within one mile, intended to adjust for local
COVID-era changes. The outcome is the average difference in changes in annual injuries, not a percentage
change or exposure-based injury rate.[^dot2025]

| Treatment | Intersections | Pedestrian injuries/year difference | p-value | All-mode injuries/year difference | p-value |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Sign-only | 567 | 0.00 | 0.96 | -0.06 | 0.27 |
| Hardened, pooled | 189 | -0.14 | 0.01 | -0.10 | 0.42 |
| Neckdowns, included in hardened | 14 | -0.50 | 0.02 | -0.58 | 0.34 |

Other hardened subgroups comprised 134 Citi Bike sites, 33 turn-calming sites, three bike corrals and five
enhanced crosswalks. None of those subgroup pedestrian or all-mode results reached p<0.05. Citi Bike's
pedestrian estimate was -0.11 (p=0.08), not a separately established benefit. Total injuries are nonfatal
injuries across all travel modes; cyclist outcomes were not separately estimated here.[^dot2025]

## Limits

Small subgroups, nonrandom siting and unknown parking compliance constrain interpretation. Intersection-level
crash assignment cannot locate harm on the treated approach. Nearby trends are a proxy for local conditions,
not a demonstration of parallel trends or equal exposure. Confidence intervals and multiplicity adjustment
are not supplied. Neckdowns shorten crossings as well as improve visibility, and the pooled result cannot
identify the contribution of either mechanism. A larger neckdown estimate does not establish superiority
in a direct head-to-head comparison.[^dot2025]
