# British SUV injury severity associations differ by age and travel mode

## In brief

- Among selected police-recorded pedestrian/cyclist casualties in Great Britain, 2004–2023, SUVs were associated
  with 14% higher fatality odds for adults, 77% higher for ages 0–18 and about three times the odds for ages 0–9,
  compared with passenger cars after adjustment. These are severity comparisons, not risk per trip.
- Adult cyclists had 11% higher odds of being killed or seriously injured rather than slightly injured;
  adult pedestrian odds were not clearly different. Child severity associations were larger.

## Results

The study analyzed 359,003 casualties in crashes involving one passenger car or SUV and no other motor vehicles.
All estimates below are adjusted odds ratios (OR), not percentage-point changes or risk ratios.[^goodman2025]

| Outcome and group | Casualties analyzed | SUV versus passenger car OR (95% CI) |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Fatal/nonfatal, adults 19+ | 231,692 | 1.14 (1.02–1.28) |
| Fatal/nonfatal, children 0–18 | 127,311 | 1.77 (1.33–2.35) |
| Fatal/nonfatal, children 0–9 | 38,324 | 3.09 (1.92–4.97) |
| KSI/slight, adult pedestrians | 118,978 | 0.97 (0.93–1.02) |
| KSI/slight, adult cyclists | 112,714 | 1.11 (1.06–1.16) |
| KSI/slight, children 0–18 | 127,311 | 1.18 (1.12–1.24) |
| KSI/slight, children 0–9 | 38,324 | 1.35 (1.23–1.47) |

The child strata overlap. Fatality estimates combine pedestrians and cyclists; they are not cyclist-only effects.
Age interactions were strong for both outcomes (p<0.001). The KSI mode interaction was evident in adults
(p<0.001), not children (p=0.94); no interaction with urban/rural status or year was detected (p>0.05).
Adjustment generally increased SUV estimates, primarily through vehicle and driver age: SUVs were newer and
more often driven by middle-aged drivers. The minimally adjusted adult pedestrian KSI OR of 0.91 became
0.97 after full adjustment, so the apparent protective association did not persist.[^goodman2025]

## What these estimates cannot establish

Models adjust for casualty, driver, vehicle-age, location and crash-condition covariates, including speed limit,
not actual impact speed. They exclude unidentified vehicles, other body styles and missing covariates. Reporting,
selection and residual confounding prevent interpreting the associations as isolated vehicle-design effects.
Neither SUV crash frequency per vehicle nor pedestrian/cyclist risk per trip was measured.

The authors calculate attributable fractions using the 26% SUV share of striking cars in 2023 and adjusted ORs:
4%, 17% and 35% for adult, child and young-child fatalities; 3%, 4% and 8% for adult cyclist, child and young-child
KSI. These are hypothetical all-SUV replacement calculations, not observed intervention benefits. They require
causal assumptions and use odds ratios in a risk-based formula, particularly consequential for common KSI.
No fraction was calculated for the nonsignificant adult pedestrian KSI association.[^goodman2025]
