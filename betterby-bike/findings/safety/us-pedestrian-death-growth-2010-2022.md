# US pedestrian deaths reportedly rose 75% from 2010 to 2022

## In brief

- Transportation for America's May 2024 coverage reports **7,522 US pedestrian deaths**, **75% above 2010**,
  in its discussion of 2022 federal data, averaging more than 20 people per day.[^araya2024]
- This is growth in deaths, not a 75% increase in risk per walk. The article supplies no walking-exposure
  denominator or comparison identifying a street-design effect.[^araya2024]

## Evidence and interpretation

The article announces Smart Growth America's Dangerous by Design 2024 report, whose metro rankings use
2018–2022 fatalities. It says almost every metro was deadlier in 2022 than 2021 but does not supply the
ranking formula or rate tables. The national endpoint is described as the latest federal reporting rather
than separately labeled with a year; 2022 is the surrounding comparison year.[^araya2024]

The report itself was not reviewed. This is secondary reporting by a program of the report-producing
organization, not an independent analysis. No uncertainty, exposure series, causal design evaluation or
cyclist-specific result is available in the article. Its demographic risk statements lack numerical comparisons.

[Madison's municipal page](/sources/madison-20-is-plenty.md) also reports 7,522 national pedestrian deaths
in 2022, but compares with 2009. The shared endpoint is overlapping background evidence, not replication.
