# U.S. micromobility ED injury estimates rose, but not for every product

## In brief

- CPSC estimates U.S. ED-treated injuries involving e-scooters, hoverboards and low-speed e-bikes rose from
  34,000 in 2017 to 93,100 in 2022, with 360,800 over six years. These are counts, not injury risks per ride.
- E-scooter injuries increased overall, whereas hoverboard injuries declined overall despite a 2020 rebound.
  E-bike injuries were higher in 2022 than 2017, but annual 2018–2021 estimates fail reliability criteria.[^cpsc2023]

## Estimates and uncertainty

NEISS weights hospital cases to national ED estimates; it does not count all injuries or provide trip exposure.
Appendix B reports:

| Product | 2017 visits | 2022 visits | 2017–2022 total | 2022 sample / coefficient of variation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| All three products | 34,000 | 93,100 | 360,800 | 2,440 / 0.15 |
| E-scooters | 7,700 | 51,700 | 169,300 | 1,413 / 0.15 |
| Hoverboards | 22,800 | 17,100 | 138,400 | 490 / 0.20 |
| E-bikes | 3,500 | 24,400 | 53,200 | 537 / 0.27 |

Rounded product totals need not sum to the combined estimate. CPSC reports increasing 2017–2022 linear trends
for all micromobility and scooters (p<0.01), a decreasing hoverboard trend (p=0.04), and a significant e-bike
2017/2022 difference (p<0.01). Hoverboard visits rose from 22,100 in 2019 to 27,100 in 2020 before declining.
The combined 2022 estimate was 21% above 2021 (p<0.01).[^cpsc2023]

NEISS reportability requires at least 1,200 estimated injuries, at least 20 sampled cases and coefficient of
variation below 0.33. E-bike annual CVs in 2018–2021 were 0.48, 0.36, 0.41 and 0.35. Their inclusion in a
combined total does not make each annual point reliable. The Figure 1.1 intermediate e-bike line uses the
2018–2021 average, not valid annual observations.[^cpsc2023]

## Limits on comparison

Scooter product codes changed in 2020. Special studies in 2020–2022 recovered 162 cases previously coded as
unspecified scooters, with recalibrated weights. Rental identification in hospital narratives is incomplete;
[follow-up survey estimates](/findings/safety/cpsc-scooter-follow-up-limits.md) use a different basis.
Neither changing counts nor differences between products establish changing risk per mile, a safer product,
or an infrastructure effect. These measures also differ from
[traffic-only pedalcyclist fatalities](/findings/safety/us-pedalcyclist-fatalities-2010-2020.md).

See [interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).
