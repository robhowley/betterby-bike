# Reported global automobility mortality is broader than crash deaths but incomplete

## In brief

- Streetsblog's March 2024 coverage of a nearly 500-study review reports at least 1.67 million annual deaths worldwide
  from crashes, car-related air pollution and lead exposure, approximately one in 34 deaths.[^wilson2024]
- This is an incomplete, secondary burden estimate, not cyclist risk per trip or deaths preventable by a particular
  cycling or street-design intervention. The underlying review was not read.[^wilson2024]

## What the estimate includes

The article's infographic reports 1.3 million crash deaths annually and roughly 370,000 pollution
deaths. Prose gives 246,000 pollution-related deaths and 120,000 lead-exposure deaths annually;
the infographic rounds the total to 1.7 million. The article also reports a cumulative 60–80 million
deaths since the automobile's advent, without a detailed historical calculation.[^wilson2024]

## What cannot be inferred

The reported global headline excludes a comprehensive account of noise, sedentary travel and other
health pathways. A separate cited estimate of 10,100 noise-related premature deaths annually covers
32 European countries, not the world, and should not simply be added to the global headline.
The article does not establish common reference years, uncertainty intervals, or how overlaps
between causes were handled. Its claim of undercounting does not identify the magnitude of missing
harm or a realizable reduction from mode shift.[^wilson2024]

The interview recommends safer walking, cycling, wheeling and transit, but explicitly acknowledges
that this review did not quantify the benefits of a replacement transport system. A harm inventory
is neither a net-benefit comparison nor an intervention evaluation.[^wilson2024]
