# Connecticut right-turn crash counts do not estimate the effect of a red-turn ban

## In brief

- Connecticut recorded **14 serious/fatal pedestrian right-turn crashes at signals** in January 2015–August
  2023: four on red, five on green and five with unknown phase. These are pedestrian cases, not cyclist risk.[^ctdot2024]
- Two of the four confirmed red-phase cases occurred with existing turn prohibitions. Unknown phases,
  existing restrictions and absent travel exposure prevent estimating how many a blanket ban would avert.[^ctdot2024]

## Counts and scope

Table 2 reports eight of the 14 crashes on approaches with existing NTOR restrictions: two green-phase,
two red-phase and four unknown-phase crashes. Four of the 14 were fatal, all on green or unknown phases;
the other ten were serious injury. Absence of a confirmed red-phase fatality is not evidence of zero risk.

For September 1, 2020–August 31, 2023, Table 1 reports 3,662 pedestrian crashes statewide, 381 at signals,
72 involving right turns at signals and five serious/fatal right-turn cases. Only one serious/fatal case was
confirmed on red, a serious injury involving violation of an existing NTOR restriction. The 72 cannot be
relabeled as red-turn crashes.[^ctdot2024]

## Measurement limits

The report describes 227 pedestrian right-turn crashes at signals since 2015, with UConn estimating roughly
one third on red, one third on green/yellow and one third unknown. The reported 30% at signals with a
restriction on at least one approach does not establish that the involved approach prohibited the turn.
Page 8's 5% serious/fatal statement is inconsistent with Table 2's 14/227 (about 6.2%).

There is no pedestrian-crossing or vehicle-turn denominator, comparison policy group or causal estimate.
Existing restrictions are deliberately placed where conflicts are expected, so counts at restricted sites do
not measure ineffectiveness. These limitations extend the [historical RTOR classification warning](right-turn-on-red-classification-limits.md)
with a separate, more recent Connecticut dataset, not an independent cyclist-injury study.
