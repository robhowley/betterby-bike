# Mixed evidence on vehicle compatibility

## In brief

- Road to Zero reports that car-driver fatality rates in crashes with 1–4-year-old SUVs were 28% above car–car
  crashes in 2013–2016, versus 132% above in 1989–1992. For pickups, the excess remained about 159% versus
  158%. These are historical secondary comparisons, not current per-mile risks.[^rtz2024]
- Its EV discussion is not uniformly adverse: a cited fixed-barrier analysis found no evidence of greater
  battery-electric vehicle aggressivity than internal-combustion vehicles. Injury outcomes in actual mixed-vehicle
  collisions remain unresolved in this report.[^rtz2024]

## Historical comparison

The report cites Monfort and Nolan (2019),
[*Trends in aggressivity and driver risk for cars, SUVs, and pickups: Vehicle incompatibility from 1989 to 2016*](https://doi.org/10.1080/15389588.2019.1632442).
It relates improved SUV outcomes to better alignment of energy-absorbing structures, while suggesting weight helps
explain persistent pickup incompatibility. The primary paper was not independently reviewed. The report does not
provide full rate denominators, adjustment details or uncertainty intervals; the endpoint percentages above come
from prose, not estimates from graph coordinates.[^rtz2024]

The p. 31 graphic's values track the prose's **percentage excess** over car–car outcomes, not absolute death
probabilities. A 28% excess is 1.28 times the comparison rate, not 28% of that rate. It does not show that all SUVs
became harmless to car occupants or that the voluntary 2003 compatibility agreement alone caused the change.
The synthesis also reports Greenwell (2012) finding a fatality reduction for compatible SUV–car crashes but not
pickup–car crashes.[^rtz2024]

## Electric-vehicle uncertainty

Road to Zero warns that battery weight and stiffness could worsen compatibility, but also cites Mueller et al. (2024),
[*Comparison of frontal crash compatibility metrics between battery-electric and internal-combustion-engine passenger vehicles*](https://doi.org/10.1080/15389588.2024.2337126),
as finding no evidence of greater EV aggressivity in NCAP stationary-object tests. That study was not independently
read. Barrier metrics are not observed injury rates in collisions between vehicles of different sizes; the report
identifies that translation and battery-protection tradeoffs as research gaps. Neither blanket EV harm nor proven
EV/combustion equivalence follows from this synthesis.[^rtz2024]
