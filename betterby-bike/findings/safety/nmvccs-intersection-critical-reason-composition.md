# Inadequate surveillance led assigned reasons in selected US intersection crashes

## In brief

- Among selected US 2005–2007 crashes with driver-attributed critical reasons, **44.1%** of intersection
  crashes were assigned inadequate surveillance, versus **7.3%** of non-intersection crashes.
- Turning with an obstructed view accounted for **7.8%** of the intersection subset; its reported **335-fold**
  intersection/non-intersection share ratio compares already-crashed groups, not risk per obstructed turn.
- These are investigator-assigned reasons in a restricted tow-away crash sample, not cyclist-specific
  results, causal responsibility or evidence that daylighting prevents a particular percentage of crashes.[^choi2010]

## Population and denominator

NMVCCS sampled crashes between 6 a.m. and midnight with a damage-towed light passenger vehicle among the
first three vehicles. Intersection-related means the vehicle assigned a critical reason was turning left,
crossing over or turning right at an intersection. The 2,183 intersection cases represent 787,236 weighted
crashes; the driver-attributed subset is 2,102 cases representing 756,570 crashes. Its comparator represents
1,289,283 non-intersection crashes with driver-attributed reasons.[^choi2010]

Table A7 reports 860 observed inadequate-surveillance cases (weighted 333,666) and 155 obstructed-view
cases (weighted 58,702) in the intersection driver subset. Other leading assigned reasons were false
assumptions about another's action (8.4%), illegal maneuver (6.8%), internal distraction (5.7%) and gap/speed
misjudgment (5.5%). In contrast, too-fast/aggressive driving led the non-intersection subset at 22.8%, versus
1.0% at intersections. This contrast is not evidence that speed is irrelevant to intersection injury severity.[^choi2010]

## Interpretation limits

Appendix B defines the relative ratio as one reason's share among intersection crashes divided by its share
among non-intersection crashes. It gives 6.1 for inadequate surveillance. The much larger 335 ratio for
obstructed-view turns is not a crash-incidence ratio, and no uncertainty interval accompanies it. The report
says obstructed sightlines typically involved an intervening vehicle in transport, not specifically parked cars.
Neither the classification nor these ratios estimate a parking-removal treatment effect.[^choi2010]
