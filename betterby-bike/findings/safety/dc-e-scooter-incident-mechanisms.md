# Falls dominated the sampled DC scooter injuries, with separate harm to non-riders

## In brief

- In a Washington, DC hospital sample from March–November 2019, 71/105 injured scooter riders fell because of reported
  surface features, infrastructure or other reasons without another vehicle or road user involved; ten collided with
  moving motor vehicles.
- Six additional injured non-riders included four encounters with parked scooters. These are case descriptions, not
  injury rates or proof that vehicle collisions and sidewalk conflicts are generally minor.[^cicchino2021]

## Rider incidents

Table 1 separates 26 surface-feature falls (25%), 17 infrastructure-related falls (16%) and 28 other falls (27%).
Examples include potholes, uneven pavement and driveway lips, based on rider reports rather than an engineering
assessment of cause. Surface features were reported in 8/24 road incidents (33%), 2/8 bike-lane incidents (25%) and
15/60 sidewalk incidents (25%). These proportions do not measure the probability of injury on each facility.[^cicchino2021]

Ten riders collided with moving vehicles; four others fell while avoiding one. One rider collided with a pedestrian
and eleven were injured avoiding pedestrians. Those avoidance cases should not be counted as pedestrian injuries.
Seven of the ten moving-vehicle collisions occurred at intersections. Only one collision rider had AIS ≥2 injury,
compared with 38/93 in other circumstances (p =0.09); the adjusted difference was also inconclusive. The authors
contrast this mild collision sample with fatality evidence cited from elsewhere, not independently reviewed here.[^cicchino2021]

## Non-rider incidents

Four non-riders were injured around parked scooters: two pedestrians tripped, one cyclist struck a scooter and
one cyclist fell avoiding one. Three incidents were on sidewalks and one on a multi-use trail; three scooters were
upright and one was lying down. Two further cases involved moving scooters at intersections: a struck pedestrian
and a cyclist falling while avoiding a turning scooter. Four of the six non-riders had extremity injuries, three
of them AIS =2; two non-riders were admitted and underwent orthopedic procedures.[^cicchino2021]

## Limits and related evidence

Small numbers, interview eligibility and incomplete overnight recruitment prevent population estimates, fatal-injury
inference or evaluation of a parking, maintenance or intersection intervention. The study documents several mechanisms
worth considering, not the effectiveness of proposed countermeasures.[^cicchino2021]
