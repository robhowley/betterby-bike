# Montreal cycle-track injury and crash rates relied on estimated cycling distance

## In brief

- Six Montreal two-way cycle tracks had 8.5 emergency-response-recorded injuries and 10.5 police-recorded
  bicycle–motor-vehicle crashes per million estimated bicycle-kilometres.[^lusk2011]
- Injuries cover 1999–July 2008 cycling seasons; crashes cover 2002–06. These overlapping measures must not be added
  or used to estimate how often crashes cause injuries.[^lusk2011]
- Rates depend on estimated cycling distance, omit unrecorded injuries and provide no injury-severity comparison.[^lusk2011]

## Rates and distance estimates

Emergency medical response injury records covered cycling seasons from April 1999 to July 2008; police crash records
covered the 2002–06 seasons.[^lusk2011]

| Cycle track       | Injuries per million bicycle-km | Motor-vehicle crashes per million bicycle-km |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------- |
| Brébeuf           | 4.1                             | 1.9                                          |
| Rachel            | 11.6                            | 15.7                                         |
| Berri             | 12.5                            | 16.4                                         |
| Maisonneuve       | 2.3                             | 3.2                                          |
| Christophe Colomb | 14.1                            | 19.3                                         |
| René Levesque     | 12.3                            | 13.9                                         |
| All six           | 8.5                             | 10.5                                         |

The aggregate denominator was an estimated 4.18 million bicycle-kilometres per cycling season. The authors interpolated
daily counts between 2000 and 2008 samples, multiplied by 200 effective cycling days within the April–November season,
then by segment length and an expert-estimated fraction of the segment ridden (0.6–0.9). These assumptions matter to the
absolute rates.[^lusk2011]

## What the rates do not establish

These are historical, record-based rates, not all injuries, fatality rates or present-day estimates. Injury severity was
unavailable. The sample was too small to identify which design features made some tracks safer, and the two tracks with
the lowest rates also had much lower traffic-danger proxies than their comparison streets. The paper's comparison with
published crash rates elsewhere does not control for differences in populations or ascertainment.[^lusk2011]
