# Local streets had lower injury odds, while several other route comparisons were inconclusive

## In brief

- Among 690 adults injured cycling in Toronto and Vancouver in 2008–09, local streets without bicycle infrastructure
  or designated as bike routes without the traffic-calming classification had about half the adjusted injury odds
  of major streets with parked cars and no bicycle infrastructure.[^teschke2012]
- Major streets without parking or bicycle infrastructure had 37% lower odds. Painted lanes, shared lanes,
  traffic-calmed local routes and the listed paths had inconclusive comparisons.[^teschke2012]
- These within-trip associations do not test street changes. Inconclusive comparisons do not establish that
  treatments have no benefit.[^teschke2012]

## Route comparisons

The case-crossover study compared emergency-department injury sites with randomly selected sites from the same trips.
The common reference was **major streets with parked cars and no bicycle infrastructure**. Estimates below adjust for
grade, streetcar/train tracks and construction, while matching rider and stable trip characteristics.[^teschke2012]

| Route type | Adjusted injury OR (95% CI) |
| --- | --- |
| Major street, parking, shared lane | 0.71 (0.21–2.45) |
| Major street, parking, bike lane | 0.69 (0.32–1.48) |
| Major street, no parking, no bike infrastructure | 0.63 (0.41–0.96) |
| Major street, no parking, shared lane | 0.60 (0.21–1.72) |
| Major street, no parking, bike lane | 0.54 (0.29–1.01) |
| Local street, no bike infrastructure | 0.51 (0.31–0.84) |
| Local street, designated bike route | 0.49 (0.26–0.90) |
| Local street, designated bike route with traffic calming | 0.66 (0.35–1.26) |
| Sidewalk or other pedestrian path | 0.87 (0.47–1.58) |
| Paved multiuse path | 0.79 (0.43–1.48) |
| Unpaved multiuse path | 0.73 (0.23–2.28) |
| Bike-only path away from streets | 0.59 (0.20–1.76) |

Local streets without bike infrastructure, designated local bike routes without the traffic-calming classification,
and major streets without parking or bike infrastructure had significantly lower adjusted odds than the common
reference. All other rows had confidence intervals spanning one. Lower point estimates are not proof of benefit;
intervals also allow increased odds. The [cycle-track result](/findings/safety/toronto-vancouver-cycle-track-injury-odds.md)
was the remaining, and lowest, route estimate in this same model.[^teschke2012]

## What these comparisons do not establish

These are not direct tests of adding traffic calming, removing parking or painting lanes on otherwise identical streets.
For example, 0.66 for traffic-calmed routes versus 0.49 for designated routes does not establish that traffic calming
increases risk. Local streets had lower observed traffic speeds and volumes, and shared lanes grouped sharrows with
shared bus/bike or high-occupancy-vehicle lanes. The study cannot separate every design feature's contribution.[^teschke2012]

Injury sites and controls were matched within trips, but infrastructure was observed afterward. Sparse route categories,
urban-core hospital recruitment and exclusion of children, fatal injuries and riders unable to recall their trip limit
precision and generalization. These odds ratios are neither absolute rates nor installation effects.[^teschke2012]
