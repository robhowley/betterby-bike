# Hoboken reportedly had four years without traffic deaths by July 2022

## In brief

- Streetsblog reported **four consecutive years without traffic deaths** in Hoboken, New Jersey, by July 14,
  2022, in a city of about **60,000 residents**. This is a historical news account, not an audited annual series.
- Officials described daylighting, curb extensions and other street changes, but the article supplies no
  comparison design to isolate their effects. Zero deaths does not mean zero nonfatal injuries.

## Evidence and limits

The article reports the zero-death interval while interviewing the mayor and transportation director about
lowering speed limits from 25 to 20 mph. That speed-limit change was being pursued at publication and cannot
explain the preceding four-year record. No subsequent speed or injury evaluation is supplied.[^article]

The report does not provide annual counts, baseline fatality frequency, exposure or statistical uncertainty.
Its mention of deaths in nearby Union City is not an adjusted comparison. Sharp explicitly warns that the
city could regress and that its existing urban fabric limits transferability. The evidence therefore describes
a reported local achievement, not a durable guarantee or an isolated daylighting effect.[^article]

[Open Plans' 2023 account](../../sources/2023-curb-the-chaos-solutions-for-cities-at-the-curb.md) also describes
Hoboken's zero-death years; repeated coverage is not another independent treatment evaluation.
The [reported 2023–2024 injury increase](hoboken-2023-2024-reported-injury-increase.md) concerns later nonfatal
injuries and does not contradict this earlier fatality count.
