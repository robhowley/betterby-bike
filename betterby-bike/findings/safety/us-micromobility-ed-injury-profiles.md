# U.S. micromobility ED cases had different injury profiles, not one severity ranking

## In brief

- Among U.S. 2019–2022 ED injury cases, internal injuries were more prevalent for e-bikes than bicycles
  (17.4% versus 14.0%; modeled prevalence ratio 1.2, 95% CI 1.01–1.5).
- E-bike hospitalization prevalence was 12.7%, but significantly higher only than hoverboards (3.2%),
  not bicycles or powered scooters. Hoverboards instead had more fractures and concussions than other modes.
- These comparisons describe already-injured patients, not injury risk per ride or an inherent device effect;
  the groups differ markedly in age and other characteristics.[^burford2024]

## Supporting comparisons

| Outcome, % of estimated ED cases | Bicycle | E-bike | Hoverboard | Powered scooter |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Hospitalized | 10.5 | 12.7 | 3.2 | 10.2 |
| Internal injury | 14.0 | 17.4 | 10.5 | 13.9 |
| Fracture | 29.2 | 29.7 | 41.6 | 31.8 |
| Concussion | 3.1 | 1.5 | 4.1 | 2.8 |
| Head injury | 18.8 | 21.4 | 15.9 | 19.8 |

Table 2's hospitalization 95% CIs are 9.6–12.1% for bicycles, 9.4–17.0% for e-bikes,
2.5–4.0% for hoverboards and 9.0–11.6% for powered scooters. Reported e-bike versus hoverboard
prevalence ratios are 4.0 (2.8–5.8) for admission, 1.7 (1.2–2.2) for internal injury,
1.4 (1.3–1.5) for soft-tissue injury and 1.3 (1.1–1.7) for head injury.
Hoverboard versus e-bike ratios are 1.4 (1.2–1.6) for fractures and 2.8 (1.6–5.0) for concussion.
Concussion is not interchangeable with the broader head-injury category.[^burford2024]

Burns were uncommon: 0.3% of e-bike versus 0.06% of bicycle cases, ratio 5.0 (1.8–14.3).
The authors warn that sparse burn and death estimates may be unstable; this does not measure battery-fire
prevalence or replace [CPSC's separate hazard investigations](/findings/safety/cpsc-micromobility-investigated-hazards.md).

## Interpretation limits

The methods describe survey-weighted, year-adjusted estimates and log-binomial comparisons, not a design
isolating device effects from age, exposure, setting or behavior. Children under 18 accounted for 76.2% of
hoverboard cases versus 14.5% of e-bike and 16.1% of powered scooter cases. The higher e-bike admission
point estimate is not evidence of a statistically established admission disadvantage against every device.
Suggestions that speed or weight explain severity are author interpretations, not measured mechanisms.

[Population-based trends](/findings/safety/us-micromobility-population-injury-trends.md) answer a separate
burden question. See [interpreting cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md).
