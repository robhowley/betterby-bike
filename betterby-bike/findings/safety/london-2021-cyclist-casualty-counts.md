# London's cyclist deaths and serious injuries increased in 2021

## In brief

- London recorded 999 cyclists killed or seriously injured in 2021, up 15% from 868 in 2020 and 33% from
  the 2017–2019 average of 748. Cyclist deaths rose from six to ten, despite a fall in all-user fatalities.[^report]
- These police-reported casualty counts describe harm, not risk per trip or kilometre. Pandemic changes in
  cycling volume and travel patterns prevent attributing the increase to infrastructure or comparing it directly
  with spring 2022 cycling growth.[^report]

## Supporting comparisons

Table 4.1 reports the following London cyclist killed-or-seriously-injured (KSI) counts and baselines:

| Period | Cyclist KSI | Reported change to 2021 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 2005–2009 average, back-estimated | 737 | +36% |
| 2017–2019 average | 748 | +33% |
| 2020 | 868 | +15% |
| 2021 | 999 | Reference year |

The +15% and +36% changes carry the table's 95% significance marker, using a Poisson probability distribution;
no such marker is displayed for the +33% comparison. This statistical treatment of counts does not adjust for
exposure or establish a cause.[^report]

All-user KSI was 3,580 in 2021, 17% above 2020 but 9% below the 2017–2019 average, while all-user deaths
fell 22% to 75. Thus the all-user fatality improvement does not describe the cyclist outcome. Child cyclist KSI,
a subset rather than an additional count, was 24 versus 22 in 2020 and a pre-pandemic average of 20; it was
62% below the back-estimated 2005–2009 average of 63.[^report]

## Exposure and coverage limits

These are TfL's STATS19-based police-reported road casualties, not all cycling injuries or a hospital cohort.
The report contextualizes the 36% long-term cyclist KSI rise with a 63% rise in cycle journeys through **2020**.
That denominator ends a year before the **2021** casualty numerator and is not a matched risk estimate.
The [revised area-count series](/findings/networks/london-2022-cycling-recovery-measurement.md) also warns that
2021 counts poorly represent the whole year. No new per-trip or per-distance risk is calculated here.

Pandemic restrictions reduced overall activity but altered mode mix and road use; 2020 is an unusually low
all-user baseline. Neither comparison establishes that new cycle lanes caused harm or that more cycling alone
explains the increase. The adverse cyclist counts must be retained alongside evidence of cycling recovery.
