# Streetsblog's reported 2024 pedestrian injury counts by vehicle category

## In brief

- A Streetsblog chart attributed to 2024 NYPD data reports **9,610 car/truck pedestrian injuries** out of **9,647
  total**, in an apparent NYC setting whose coverage is not defined.[^chart]
- Its two-category legend implies **37 e-bike injuries** by subtraction, not a visible numeric label. These counts show
  a much larger reported car/truck burden, not risk per trip or encounter.[^chart]

## What is displayed and what is derived

The chart labels its total “All pedestrian injuries,” gives the car/truck count and identifies the other category
as e-bike. The implied remainder is **9,647 − 9,610 = 37**. This calculation assumes the two displayed categories
exhaust the chart's total; the underlying data were not accessed. The small e-bike slice is not zero.[^chart]

## Limits on interpretation

Only the one-page chart was reviewed, not a full article or primary NYPD records. It supplies no injury-severity
threshold, coding rules, extraction method, missing-record assessment or treatment of other vehicle categories.
“All” is the chart's label, not verified comprehensive coverage. NYC is inferred from the NYPD attribution and
publisher domain rather than an explicit geographic definition.[^chart]

There are no vehicle-trip, distance or pedestrian-encounter denominators. Thus the chart cannot quantify relative
risk at equal exposure, establish fault, or measure a bike-lane effect. Its caption's weight/speed explanation is
an interpretation, not a tested causal result. Counts refer to injuries, not a separately identified fatality measure.
