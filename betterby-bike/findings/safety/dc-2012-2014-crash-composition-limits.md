# DC intersection crash share and leading crash types do not measure exposure-based risk

## In brief

- An abstract describing Washington, DC pedestrian and bicycle crashes in **2012–2014** reports **68% at
  intersections**; it supplies neither the crash count nor separate pedestrian and bicycle shares.
- It identifies vehicle left turns (parallel paths) as the leading pedestrian crash type and “open door to traffic” as
  the leading bicycle type, without counts or percentages for either.
- These are reported crash-composition results from an abstract, not risk per trip or evidence that a particular street
  treatment reduces injuries.[^dcabstract]

## Evidence and interpretation

The abstract describes digitization and classification using NHTSA PBCAT and LMCM typologies. The 68% wording
appears to concern the combined crash set; it must not be relabeled as 68% of bicycle crashes. A leading type
need not comprise a majority. The statement that drivers were at fault or in violation gives no percentage,
mode split or fault definition, so it does not establish a quantified driver-responsibility share.[^dcabstract]

The study also used CHAID trees to investigate fatal and disabling-injury crashes. Bicycle-only and
construction-zone crashes are identified as associated with greater bicycle severity, without effect sizes,
case counts or model details. This describes severity among analyzed crashes, not the likelihood of crashing
at construction zones. PEDSAFE/BIKESAFE countermeasures were listed, not evaluated.[^dcabstract]

## Limits and related evidence

Only the single-page abstract image was available and reviewed. Counts, exposure denominators, case inclusion,
classification reliability and supporting tables are missing. The abstract mentions sparse crash groups and
police-form shortcomings but does not provide their detailed implications. No facility comparison or causal
design estimate can be recovered from these statements.

The [2019 DC hospital comparison](/sources/2021-washington-dc-e-scooter-bicycle-injuries.md) uses a different
population and period, not another report of this crash sample. See [cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md)
for the distinction between crash composition, injury severity and risk at equal exposure.
