# Reported New Jersey and national traffic-death trends

## In brief

- A cropped roundup quoting Times reporting says New Jersey traffic deaths rose **23% in “the first six months of the
  year”**, while fatalities fell **3% nationally**. The visible text does not specify the year or comparison
  baseline.[^njexcerpt3]
- This contrast accompanies reporting of fewer State Police tickets, but does not establish that reduced enforcement
  caused more deaths or quantify cyclist risk.[^njexcerpt3]

## What the excerpt supports

The item credits Tracey Tully at the New York Times. New Jersey is the context for the quoted increase;
“across the country” describes the decline. No underlying counts, road coverage, road-user breakdown, exposure
or uncertainty estimates appear. The filename's 2024 cannot by itself date “the year” in the quotation, and
a year-on-year baseline should not be supplied from inference.[^njexcerpt3]

These are reported changes in deaths, not the **27% main-highway crash increase** in the
[related enforcement/crash Finding](new-jersey-enforcement-crash-trends.md). Different outcome definitions and
unspecified periods prevent combining them into a crash-fatality rate or attributing a number of deaths to
the slowdown. The national change is not a matched comparison controlling for other New Jersey changes.

## Evidence limits

Only the supplied one-page crop was reviewed, not the Times article or death records. This repeats the same
Times enforcement story covered by the other excerpts, rather than providing an independent study. It neither
establishes a causal enforcement effect nor rules one out. There is no cycling-specific result.[^njexcerpt3]
