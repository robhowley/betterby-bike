# NJTR-1 low-speed e-bike classification

## In brief

- New Jersey's 2023 NJTR-1 manual defines pedalcyclists as riders of nonmotorized vehicles powered solely by pedals, not
  all bicycle-shaped devices.
- In eligible motor-vehicle crashes, low-speed e-bike and e-scooter operators are recorded as pedestrians on personal
  conveyances, using vehicle-type code 17 and a narrative device description.
- A pedalcyclist-only count can therefore omit low-speed e-bike users, while pedestrian counts need not describe walkers
  alone. These are coding instructions, not evidence of consistent implementation.[^manual]

## Device and field definitions

Box 83 distinguishes B1/B2 pedalcyclists from P1/P2 pedestrians. Its pedestrian definition includes
wheelchair users, skaters, non-powered scooter users and people on personal conveyances. Vehicle-type
boxes 108/109 separately distinguish pedalcycles (13), mopeds (11) and personal conveyances (17).
The appendix explicitly tells officers to record personal-conveyance operators as pedestrians and put
the device description in Box 145.[^manual]

The appendix defines low-speed e-bikes as two- or three-wheeled devices with operable pedals and a motor
under 750 watts: class 1 assistance ends at 20 mph; class 2 may propel without pedaling but cannot assist
at 20 mph. Its low-speed e-scooter definition specifies a standing floorboard, handlebars and maximum
motor-powered speed below 19 mph. Do not extend this classification to every electric two-wheeler:
the manual separately defines mopeds, including certain electric bicycles above 20 mph and up to 28 mph,
and provides distinct moped reporting rules.[^manual]

## Interpretation limits

Code 17 groups several device types, including motorized wheelchairs and hoverboards; it is not an e-bike-only
variable. Device-specific analysis needs more than this code, and the narrative is not guaranteed complete.
No record-level audit, later edition or current law was reviewed. The manual supplies no comparative injury
rate, exposure denominator or evidence that a coding change caused an observed trend.

[Coverage exclusions](/findings/safety/njtr-1-cycling-crash-coverage.md) apply before these classifications:
standalone personal-conveyance crashes without a motor vehicle in transport are excluded. See
[cycling injury risk](/topics/cycling-injury-risk.md) for why traffic and hospital mode categories need matching.
