# NYC left-turn casualty shares do not measure risk per turn

## In brief

- In NYC's 2010–2014 reported crash records, left-turning vehicles accounted for 19% of pedestrian/bicyclist
  deaths and severe injuries (KSI), versus 6% for right turns. This is not a threefold risk per turn.
- Pedestrians made up 86% of left-turn KSI, versus 75% of other KSI; these combined-mode counts mainly
  describe pedestrian harm, not a cyclist-specific risk comparison.[^report]

## Population and interpretation

NYSDOT supplied records of crashes reported to NYPD. Left-turn pedestrian/bicyclist fatalities had a
median age of 67, versus 50 for right turns and other movements; 53% were aged 65 or older, versus
36% and 30%, respectively. These describe the fatality distribution, without age-specific travel exposure.
The study reports 108 left-turn pedestrian/bicyclist deaths out of 859 total in 2010–2014.[^report]

The separate manual review of 1,105 reports from 2009–2013 selected locations with at least ten
left-turn pedestrian/bicyclist injuries. Within that selected group, 69% of crashes involved receiving
streets at least 60 feet wide, 51% minor-street approaches versus 23% major approaches (26% no discernible
distinction), and 61% two-way receiving streets. These are crash compositions, not controlled design-risk
estimates or a representative sample of all intersections.[^report]

No turning-volume, pedestrian-crossing or bicycle-trip denominators establish relative risk. DOT's
higher-speed and visibility explanations are plausible interpretations and cited external research,
not independently tested mechanisms in these crash shares. Its citywide intersection-concentration
slide also has inconsistent counts/percentages and should not support an exact citywide denominator.
