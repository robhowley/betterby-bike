# NYC turn treatments had lower left-turn injury counts but mixed broader outcomes

## In brief

- NYC DOT reported fewer combined pedestrian/bicyclist left-turn injuries after restrictions, turn bays,
  left-turn-only signals and leading pedestrian intervals (LPIs), with declines of 14–41% across different
  samples. These uncontrolled counts cannot rank causal effectiveness.
- Turn bays had nearly flat total pedestrian/bicyclist injuries (332 to 339, +2%) and increased cyclist
  injuries (48 to 80, +67%). DOT says left-turn cyclist numbers were too small for statistical significance.
- At 104 LPI intersections, combined left-turn deaths/severe injuries fell from 41 to 18 (56%);
  that is not a cyclist-only or exposure-adjusted effect.[^report]

## Comparisons

All entries compare three years before with three years after treatment; injuries include fatalities.
Each sample has different installation dates, context and available NYSDOT crash years.[^report]

| Treatment | Intersections; installation years | Left-turn pedestrian/bicyclist injuries | All-movement pedestrian/bicyclist injuries |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Left-turn restrictions | 51; 2008–2010 | 113 → 67 (−41%) | 331 → 262 (−21%) |
| Left-turn bays, without left-turn-only signals | 140; 2009–2011 | 117 → 100 (−15%) | 332 → 339 (+2%) |
| Left-turn-only signals | 34; 1999–2011 | 55 → 37 (−33%) | 158 → 119 (−25%) |
| LPIs | 104; 2003–2011 | 282 → 243 (−14%) | 839 → 749 (−11%) |

The restrictions sample includes 21 restrictions and preceding/succeeding intersections where left turns
remain possible, addressing nearby displacement but not all network effects. The table, not p. 48's
misworded second bullet, identifies the 21% decline as total injuries across movements.

Turn bays were on two-way approaches as part of street improvement projects. Left-turn cyclist injuries
rose from 10 to 19 (+90%) while left-turn pedestrian injuries fell from 107 to 81 (−24%). Across all
movements, pedestrian injuries fell 284 to 259 (−9%), unlike the cyclist increase. Combined totals
therefore conceal differing mode-specific directions. Motor-vehicle occupant injuries fell 25% overall.
No claim of statistical significance for the total cyclist increase is established here.[^report]

At LPI sites, total combined KSI fell 115 to 73 (−37%), while through-movement KSI stayed at 40.
The report excludes KSI percentage changes for restrictions, bays and turn-only signals because samples
were too small for robust analysis. Multiple vehicle movements can appear in one crash, so movement
columns need not sum to totals.[^report]

## Limits and unevaluated treatments

No untreated controls, exposure denominators or uncertainty intervals are supplied. Selected sites,
regression to the mean, background trends and accompanying designs limit attribution and comparison
across treatments. Declines do not prove the treatments caused the benefits; increases do not prove harm.

The report did not evaluate split LPIs (including bicycle signals), pedestrian/bicycle split phases,
standalone pedestrian islands or one-way conversions because of data availability. Its turn-calming
and enhanced-daylighting pilot was under implementation/evaluation in 2016, not an established result.
