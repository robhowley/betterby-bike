# Philadelphia youth crashes and high-injury-network coverage

## In brief

- PBIC's 2024 brief reports **61% of Philadelphia youth pedestrian injury crashes outside the city's high-injury
  network**, from an analysis of five years of pedestrian crashes involving people under 18. Youth injury crashes
  were less represented on that network than adult injury crashes.[^brief]
- This is a secondary crash-location share, not evidence that off-network streets were riskier per trip or that a
  network intervention failed. Exact crash years, case counts, exposure and network-construction details are not
  supplied in the brief; the underlying report was not independently reviewed.[^brief]

## Evidence and denominator

PBIC describes high-injury networks as corridors with the highest levels of fatal and serious crashes across
pedestrians, bicyclists and motorists. The reported 61% instead concerns **youth pedestrian injury crashes**,
not only fatal/serious cases, not the percentage of children injured and not a share of all walking trips.
It therefore does not measure how well the network captured the severity category used to construct it.[^brief]

The same project reportedly identified three crash types comprising 89% of youth pedestrian crashes and roadway
variables associated with each. The brief does not name the types, variables or effect sizes, or give the adult
network percentage. Its reference 30 identifies Pullen-Seufert and Lambert's 2022
[Vision Zero for Youth demonstration summary](https://www.pedbikeinfo.org/cms/downloads/PBIC_InfoBrief_VZYPhila.pdf).
The project's 2019–2021 title is not a stated five-year crash-observation window.[^brief]

## What this supports

An all-user high-injury network need not locate most youth pedestrian injury crashes. PBIC consequently recommends
looking at youth separately and supplementing police reports with ED or trauma records. The brief cites greater
police underreporting for children and for less severe injuries, plus missing crash locations, but supplies no
Philadelphia-specific correction factor. Separate youth screening is a planning recommendation, not a demonstrated
injury-reduction intervention.[^brief]

This local result cannot establish national network coverage, exposure-adjusted youth/adult risk or a causal effect
of street redesign. It complements [youth-specific design guidance](/findings/design/pbic-youth-design-guidance-limits.md)
by showing why the target population and crash definition matter when selecting locations.

[active travel and access](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md).
