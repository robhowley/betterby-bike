# Safety findings

- NYC left turns: [casualty shares](nyc-left-turn-casualty-composition.md),
  [protected-lane movement counts](nyc-protected-lane-left-turn-injuries.md) and
  [mixed turn-treatment counts](nyc-left-turn-treatment-injuries.md), with combined-mode and uncontrolled-design limits.

- [Connecticut right-turn crash classification](connecticut-right-turn-crash-classification.md): pedestrian
  counts with unknown phases and existing restrictions, not cyclist risk or ban effectiveness.

- NJTR-1 reporting: [coverage exclusions](njtr-1-cycling-crash-coverage.md) and
  [low-speed e-bike classification](njtr-1-low-speed-ebike-classification.md), separating eligible crashes
  from device categories without claiming measured undercounts.

- [NCHRP protected-intersection safety limits](nchrp-protected-intersection-safety-limits.md): mixed yielding
  and compliance indicators, with two treated sites insufficient for a crash-effect estimate.

- NHTSA intersection crashes: [reason composition](nmvccs-intersection-critical-reason-composition.md)
  and [critical reason versus cause](nmvccs-critical-reason-not-cause.md), not risk per turn or driver fault shares.

- [Lime's DC and Bloomington reported incidents](lime-dc-bloomington-reported-incidents.md): falling
  reports per trip with partial-year 2024 data, not complete surveillance or an identified lane effect.

## Late-night travel

- [Bar-hour traffic comparisons](bar-hours-traffic-evidence-review.md): adverse Australian and contrary UK crash
  accounts, with DUI enforcement measures kept separate.
- [Mixed ride-hailing evidence](ride-hailing-alcohol-crash-review.md): setting- and outcome-specific benefits and nulls,
  not a measured offset to later closing hours.

## British vehicle severity

- [SUV severity by age and mode](great-britain-suv-severity-age-mode.md): stronger child fatality associations
  and null adult pedestrian KSI, conditional on recorded injury.
- [Weight, height and length](great-britain-vehicle-dimensions-severity.md): separate models with mixed subgroup
  results, not independent dimension effects or measured design-policy benefits.

## NYC daylighting

- [Before/after injury comparisons](nyc-daylighting-before-after-injuries.md): hardened pedestrian benefit,
  null sign-only results and no significant all-mode benefit.
- [Hydrant-zone injury associations](nyc-hydrant-zone-injury-associations.md): adverse cross-sectional
  associations do not establish a parking-removal policy effect.

## DC crash composition

- [DC 2012–2014 crash composition limits](dc-2012-2014-crash-composition-limits.md): abstract-only intersection
  share, left-turn and door-opening patterns, without mode-specific counts or exposure-based risk.

## Right-turn-on-red classification

- [Historical right-turn crash classification limits](right-turn-on-red-classification-limits.md): permission to
  turn on red does not establish the signal state; crash shares do not measure risk per turn or ban effects.

## Vehicle visibility measurements

- [Forward visibility across vehicle generations](vehicle-generation-forward-visibility.md): six nameplates'
  earliest-to-latest near-field losses, potentially uncertain sedan changes and mixed longer-distance results.
- [Ground-plane visibility inference limits](ground-plane-visibility-inference-limits.md): static ground
  occlusion at one eyepoint does not establish whole-person invisibility or injury risk.

## Light-truck design synthesis

- [Front-end profiles and pedestrian fatality](rtz-front-end-profile-pedestrian-fatality.md): reported tall-front
  disadvantage with similar-risk low/blunt and medium/sloped categories, not exposure-based risk.
- [Turning-crash denominator](rtz-turning-crash-denominator.md): turning-versus-straight odds among fatal crashes
  do not measure deaths per turn or establish a blind-zone mechanism.
- [Mixed vehicle compatibility evidence](rtz-vehicle-compatibility-mixed-evidence.md): historical SUV improvement,
  persistent pickup disadvantage and unresolved real-world EV compatibility despite a non-adverse barrier-test result.

## State report-card measures

- [New Jersey fatalities per bike commuter](new-jersey-report-card-safety-denominator.md): 14.1 per 10,000
  bike commuters with unspecified data years and numerator coverage, not per-trip risk or a Safety rubric score.

## Youth pedestrian crash targeting

- [Philadelphia high-injury-network coverage](philadelphia-youth-crash-network-coverage.md): PBIC reports 61%
  of youth pedestrian injury crashes outside the network, without exposure-based risk or exact observation years.

## U.S. micromobility ED comparisons, 2019–2022

- [Population-based injury trends](us-micromobility-population-injury-trends.md): rising e-bike/scooter burden
  and declining hoverboard rates, not injury risk per ride; overlapping CPSC surveillance.
- [Mixed injury profiles](us-micromobility-ed-injury-profiles.md): internal injuries, admissions, fractures and
  concussions give different comparisons among already-injured patients.
- [Clinical-note measurement limits](us-micromobility-narrative-measurement-limits.md): missing helmet status
  and positive-only alcohol/vehicle indicators cannot establish causal risk or protective effects.

## CPSC national micromobility surveillance

- [ED injury trends](cpsc-micromobility-ed-injury-trends.md): rising combined burden with declining hoverboard
  trend, e-bike reliability limits and no per-trip risk denominator.
- [Reported fatalities](cpsc-micromobility-reported-fatalities.md): motor-vehicle, pedestrian and fire deaths,
  with incomplete ascertainment rather than nationally representative mortality rates.
- [Scooter follow-up limits](cpsc-scooter-follow-up-limits.md): rental/equipment shares and injury conditions,
  not causal effects; substantial nonresponse and conflicting surface tabulation.
- [Investigated hazards](cpsc-micromobility-investigated-hazards.md): charging, brake and structural problems
  in selected reports, not hazard prevalence or an independent fatality sample.

## London pandemic casualties

- [Cyclist casualty counts in 2021](london-2021-cyclist-casualty-counts.md): 999 killed or seriously injured,
  33% above the pre-pandemic average despite lower all-user KSI; no matched per-trip risk estimate.

## U.S. national pedalcyclist statistics

- [Traffic fatalities, 2010–2020](us-pedalcyclist-fatalities-2010-2020.md): higher 2019 than 2010 counts with
  annual reversals; 2020 is an early estimate, and none of these counts measures risk per ride.
- [Injury estimates and reporting breaks](us-pedalcyclist-injury-estimate-limits.md): 49,000 estimated injured
  pedalcyclists in 2019; the 2016 sampling redesign prevents direct comparison with 2015 and earlier.

## Washington, DC e-scooter injuries

These Findings use overlapping 2019 scooter recruitment. The comparison publication adds 2015–2017 cyclists
and modeled mileage, not an independent scooter sample.

- [Scooter–bicycle ED mileage rates](dc-scooter-bicycle-ed-mileage-rates.md): higher estimated scooter rates,
  with single-hospital/citywide exposure mismatch and sensitivity analyses.
- [Scooter–bicycle incident comparison](dc-scooter-bicycle-incident-comparison.md): different case mechanisms,
  not facility-specific risks or measured maintenance benefits.
- [Scooter–bicycle injury profiles](dc-scooter-bicycle-injury-profiles.md): differing injury regions and concussion
  counts, but no significant overall severity difference.

- [Injury severity associations](dc-e-scooter-injury-severity-associations.md): road and weekly riders had higher
  adjusted severity, with inconclusive age, sex and vehicle-collision associations.
- [Incident mechanisms](dc-e-scooter-incident-mechanisms.md): falls dominated rider incidents; separate non-rider
  injuries included encounters with parked scooters.
- [Exposure and selection limits](dc-e-scooter-exposure-and-selection-limits.md): facility case counts, first-ride
  shares and injury definitions cannot supply per-trip risk or a safest-facility ranking.

## Reported 2024 NYC pedestrian injury counts

- [Streetsblog vehicle-category comparison](nyc-2024-streetsblog-pedestrian-injury-counts.md): 9,610 car/truck
  injuries out of 9,647 imply 37 e-bike injuries in the chart, without exposure-based risk or verified coverage.

## New Zealand road cycling injuries

These findings come from one study, not independent replications.

- [Injury rates, 1988–2007](nz-cycling-injury-rates-1988-2007.md): cycling rates rose after 1996–99 while other
  travel-mode rates fell.
- [Crash mechanisms, 2003–07](nz-cycling-crash-mechanisms-2003-2007.md): non-collision crashes accounted for two-fifths
  of cases, with different profiles by severity and motor-vehicle involvement.
- [Children and injury severity](nz-child-cycling-injury-severity.md): the elevated overall injury rate at ages 5–14 did
  not extend to the same serious-injury pattern.
- [Brain injuries and helmet-law inference](nz-brain-injury-trends-helmet-law.md): the observed decline does not
  identify a causal policy effect.

## 2012 helmet-law evaluation

These findings come from Clarke's secondary review. Its injury table repeats Tin Tin et al.'s national study rather
than supplying independent evidence.

- [Cyclist-to-pedestrian fatality ratio](nz-cyclist-pedestrian-fatality-ratio-1989-2009.md): an unadjusted activity-hour
  calculation with an unresolved 2006–09 count discrepancy.
- [53-premature-death estimate](nz-helmet-law-premature-death-estimate.md): an assumption-based counterfactual, not a
  measured mortality effect.

## Cycle tracks and route comparisons

The first two findings describe one Montreal study; the third is that publication's reanalysis of earlier Palo Alto
data, not an independent replication.

- [Montreal relative injury risk](montreal-cycle-track-relative-injury-risk.md): lower pooled risk, with three
  significantly lower and three inconclusive route comparisons.
- [Montreal absolute rates](montreal-cycle-track-absolute-rates.md): injury and motor-vehicle crash rates per estimated
  bicycle-kilometre, with different case definitions and distance assumptions.
- [Intersection-only sidewalk risk reanalysis](intersection-only-sidewalk-risk-reanalysis.md): adding assumed
  non-intersection roadway crashes changed the apparent risk comparison.

## Toronto–Vancouver within-trip infrastructure comparisons

These findings come from one study of 690 adult injury trips in 2008–09, independent of the Montreal study.

- [Cycle-track injury odds](toronto-vancouver-cycle-track-injury-odds.md): lowest adjusted odds, with few observations
  and a wide confidence interval.
- [Other route types](toronto-vancouver-route-type-comparisons.md): lower local-street odds alongside inconclusive
  adjusted estimates for painted/shared lanes and most paths.
- [Route hazards](toronto-vancouver-route-hazards.md): higher odds with rail tracks, downhill grades and construction,
  without measured intervention effects.

## NYC mayoral memo claims

These findings preserve reported figures from one policy statement, not independently reviewed crash analyses.

- [Protected-lane crash claims](nyc-wolfson-protected-lane-crash-claims.md): typical 40% all-road-user injury-crash
  reductions were asserted without supporting methods.
- [Pedestrian fatality counts](nyc-wolfson-pedestrian-fatality-counts.md): four deaths in bike collisions in 2001–05
  versus three in 2006–10, without exposure or nonfatal injury data.

## NYC street-redesign cases

- [8th/9th Avenue injury changes](nyc-eighth-ninth-injury-changes.md): reported 35% and 58% reductions in all-user
  injuries, without exposure or isolated treatment effects.

## NYC 2014 protected-lane analysis

These results share one agency presentation and overlap earlier NYC project locations.

- [Aggregate injury changes](nyc-pbl-injury-changes.md): 20% fewer all-user injuries, with only 2% fewer cyclist injuries.
- [Corridor cyclist-risk proxy](nyc-pbl-corridor-risk.md): seven decreases and one increase, using short bicycle counts.
- [Citywide cycling-risk indicator](nyc-cycling-risk-indicator-2001-2013.md): preliminary 75% decline in 2001–2013,
  not an absolute trip risk or protected-lane effect.

## NYC 2023 agency safety claims

- [All-user and senior-pedestrian serious casualties](nyc-dot-protected-lane-serious-casualty-claims.md): reported
  18.1% and 39% reductions in deaths and serious injuries, without analysis details; not East New York project outcomes.

## U.S. protected-lane conflict observations

- [Green Lane conflicts and risk limits](green-lanes-conflicts-and-risk-limits.md): no collisions in a short 2013
  video sample, but low-level conflicts and separately self-reported collisions; not an injury-risk estimate.

## Seville citywide network time series

These findings share one citywide study and its estimated bicycle-trip denominator.

- [Motor-vehicle collision risk](seville-motor-vehicle-collision-risk.md): lower rates per million trips alongside
  higher collision counts and some annual rate increases.
- [Network and safety-in-numbers models](seville-network-risk-models.md): improved fit from a before/after indicator,
  without identifying connectivity's causal effect or the direction of the numbers–safety relationship.

## Chicago shared-lane-marking comparison

These findings share one block-group study comparing 2011–2012 with 2013–2014, not three independent studies.

- [Total injury-rate changes](chicago-sharrow-injury-rate-changes.md): larger commuter-weighted increases in sharrow
  groups than lane or no-new-treatment groups, without establishing causal harm.
- [Dooring rates](chicago-sharrow-dooring-rates.md): sharrow increase and comparator decreases, all inconclusive at
  the 5% level within groups.
- [Exposure limits](chicago-sharrow-exposure-limits.md): resident commuters, spatial assignment and weighting do not
  measure corridor risk; baseline total/component reporting discrepancies remain unresolved.

## Reported U.S. city-level casualty comparison

- [Protected-lane casualty association](us-cities-protected-lane-casualty-association.md): Streetsblog reports 44%
  fewer deaths and 50% fewer serious injuries than an undefined average-city comparator; all-user outcomes,
  a painted-lane null report and equity caveats, not a reviewed primary analysis or causal installation estimate.

## North Jersey pedestrian reporting

Two articles in one 2021 series, not controlled evaluations or independent confirmations of program effectiveness.

- [Death counts and coverage limits](north-jersey-pedestrian-death-counts.md): 296 reported deaths in 2010–2020,
  with missing Essex records and no exposure-based risk ranking.
- [Street Smart effect limits](north-jersey-street-smart-effect-limits.md): favorable police accounts without
  numerical program effects; Fair Lawn's decline was attributed likely to pandemic travel reductions.

## New Jersey enforcement reporting

- [Enforcement decline and highway crash increase](new-jersey-enforcement-crash-trends.md): reported 81% year-on-year
  citation decline and 27% crash increase, without a crash baseline or causal estimate; a second commentary repeats
  the same reporting but describes the 81% decline over eight months, an unresolved period discrepancy.

- [New Jersey and national traffic-death trends](new-jersey-national-traffic-death-trends.md): excerpt 3 reports
  a 23% increase versus a 3% national decline, without an explicit year/baseline or causal estimate; mortality,
  not the related main-highway crash measure, from the same Times reporting chain.

## New Jersey partial-year death reporting

- [2025 death snapshot and projection](new-jersey-2025-death-snapshot-projection.md): 350 reported deaths through
  August 18 versus a 648-death annual forecast including 298 projected deaths; no forecast method or exposure denominator.

## U.S. vehicle weight and crash severity

These Findings share The Economist's original 14-state crash analysis, not independent studies or cyclist-risk estimates.

- [Own-vehicle and partner-vehicle fatalities](us-vehicle-weight-crash-fatalities.md): occupant protection alongside
  greater harm to the other vehicle, with an adjusted crash-conditional association and no per-mile risk estimate.
- [Fleet-downsizing estimate](us-heavy-vehicle-downsizing-estimate.md): estimated 12% fewer national multi-car deaths
  from downsizing the heaviest decile, not an observed policy effect or a claim that lighter is always safer.
