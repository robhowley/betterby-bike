# Safety findings

## New Zealand road cycling injuries

These findings come from one study, not independent replications.

- [Injury rates, 1988–2007](nz-cycling-injury-rates-1988-2007.md): cycling rates rose after 1996–99 while other travel-mode rates fell.
- [Crash mechanisms, 2003–07](nz-cycling-crash-mechanisms-2003-2007.md): non-collision crashes accounted for two-fifths of cases, with different profiles by severity and motor-vehicle involvement.
- [Children and injury severity](nz-child-cycling-injury-severity.md): the elevated overall injury rate at ages 5–14 did not extend to the same serious-injury pattern.
- [Brain injuries and helmet-law inference](nz-brain-injury-trends-helmet-law.md): the observed decline does not identify a causal policy effect.

## Cycle tracks and route comparisons

The first two findings describe one Montreal study; the third is that publication's reanalysis of earlier Palo Alto data, not an independent replication.

- [Montreal relative injury risk](montreal-cycle-track-relative-injury-risk.md): lower pooled risk, with three significantly lower and three inconclusive route comparisons.
- [Montreal absolute rates](montreal-cycle-track-absolute-rates.md): injury and motor-vehicle crash rates per estimated bicycle-kilometre, with different case definitions and distance assumptions.
- [Intersection-only sidewalk risk reanalysis](intersection-only-sidewalk-risk-reanalysis.md): adding assumed non-intersection roadway crashes changed the apparent risk comparison.

## NYC mayoral memo claims

These findings preserve reported figures from one policy statement, not independently reviewed crash analyses.

- [Protected-lane crash claims](nyc-wolfson-protected-lane-crash-claims.md): typical 40% all-road-user injury-crash reductions were asserted without supporting methods.
- [Pedestrian fatality counts](nyc-wolfson-pedestrian-fatality-counts.md): four deaths in bike collisions in 2001–05 versus three in 2006–10, without exposure or nonfatal injury data.

## NYC street-redesign cases

- [8th/9th Avenue injury changes](nyc-eighth-ninth-injury-changes.md): reported 35% and 58% reductions in all-user injuries, without exposure or isolated treatment effects.
