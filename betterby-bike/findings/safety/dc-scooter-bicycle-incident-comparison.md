# DC scooter and bicycle patients differed in incident circumstances

## In brief

- Among 99 scooter patients (2019) and 337 cyclists (2015–2017) interviewed at one DC ED, moving vehicles were
  involved in 13.1% versus 37.7% of incidents. Surface features accounted for 24.2% versus 14.2%, and
  infrastructure such as curbs for 16.2% versus 5.3%.
- These are shares of selected injury cases, not injury probabilities by facility or evidence of how much
  maintenance or separation from vehicles would prevent.[^paper]

## What the categories mean

Moving-vehicle incidents included collisions and falls to avoid vehicles: actual collisions were 10/99 (10.1%)
for scooters and 111/337 (32.9%) for cyclists. Surface and infrastructure categories also include collisions and
avoidance falls. Other falls comprised 26/99 (26.3%) and 59/337 (17.5%). Table 3 gives 127/337 =37.7% for bicycle
moving-vehicle involvement; the results prose's 39.5% is inconsistent with that table.[^paper]

Among cases with known location, scooters were more often on sidewalks before injury (55/98, 56.1%, versus
51/337, 15.1%); bicycles more often on roads (171/337, 50.7%, versus 24/98, 24.5%). Intersection cases were
assigned to the facility used before the intersection, not necessarily the location of impact.[^paper]

## Interpretation and limitations

Trip purposes differed: 35.4% of scooter cases were social trips versus 10.7% of cyclist cases; 52.1% of cyclists
were commuting versus 25.3% of scooters. First-time use was reported by 35/98 scooter riders, but cyclists were
not offered that response and were asked about their peak riding season rather than the past month. These
questions cannot establish a comparable novice rate or prove that inexperience caused the mode difference.[^paper]

The authors suggest well-maintained surfaces and caution against assuming bicycle countermeasures transfer
unchanged to scooters. This is an interpretation, not a maintenance or lane-installation evaluation. Different
years, recall, selection and absent facility exposure prevent ranking sidewalks, roads and lanes by safety.
