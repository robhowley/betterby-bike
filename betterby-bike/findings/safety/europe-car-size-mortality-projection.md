# European car-growth scenario projects 400 extra annual deaths by 2040

## In brief

- Guardian coverage of T&E's European vehicle-size analysis reports **400 additional vulnerable-road-user
  deaths per year by 2040, including 79 children**, under continued car growth versus returning average
  sizes to 2015 levels. This is a conditional projection, not an observed increase.
- The article corrects an earlier 2,600-per-year claim: **2,600 is cumulative**, not annual. It does not
  provide a cyclist-only estimate or establish benefits of a particular policy.[^niranjan2026]

## Scenario and research inputs

The continued-growth scenario extrapolates reported annual increases since 2000 of 1.2 cm in length
and 0.5 cm each in height and width for new European cars. The alternative returns average sizes to
2015 levels. The coverage cites Belgian research reporting 27% higher vulnerable-road-user death risk
per 10 cm higher bonnet and US research reporting an 81% increase for children. Those inputs are
secondary reporting, not independently reviewed causal effects.[^niranjan2026]

## Limits on interpretation

The article provides no uncertainty intervals, baseline death counts, exposure assumptions, detailed
mortality-model geography or fleet-turnover methods. Its aggregate category is vulnerable road users
(described as non-drivers), not cyclists alone. It excludes additional air-pollution and climate-related
deaths; a quoted researcher also identifies omitted vehicle weight. Missing harms do not by themselves
validate the model or quantify its bias. The correction does not specify the cumulative period's start.

The p. 2 chart's plotted lines are missing from the PDF, although labels and axes remain. Only the
reported text values are retained. The underlying T&E report and Belgian/US studies remain unreviewed.
This projection is not independent confirmation of their risk estimates and does not measure the
safety effect of taxes, size limits or street redesign.
