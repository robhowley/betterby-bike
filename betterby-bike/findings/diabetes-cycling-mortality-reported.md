# Cycling and mortality among people with diabetes

## In brief

- NPR's May 2024 article reports that a study of people with diabetes associated **one hour of cycling per week with
  about 22% lower premature mortality**.[^aubrey2024]
- The article does not specify the precise comparator, follow-up or absolute risk. This is secondary reporting of an
  association, not proof that cycling extends life or that the percentage applies to people without diabetes.

## Reporting and limits

Grace Lo cites this separate study while discussing cycling and longevity. NPR links the
[primary paper](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34279548/), which was not independently reviewed.
The article does not report its sample size, setting, age distribution, dose-category boundaries,
adjustment methods or uncertainty interval. Its rounded one-hour wording cannot establish a minimum
effective dose or a linear benefit for additional hours.[^aubrey2024]

Lo suggests benefits might be greater for people without diabetes; the article gives no comparison
supporting that suggestion. Nor does it establish how much of the association reflects cycling rather
than differences in health or behavior. The estimate is not an absolute percentage-point reduction,
and cannot quantify deaths avoided by building a bike lane or distinguish outdoor transport cycling
from other cycling.
