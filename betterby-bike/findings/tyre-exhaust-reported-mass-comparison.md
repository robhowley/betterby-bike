# Reported tyre wear greatly exceeds modern petrol exhaust particle mass, not necessarily airborne harm

## In brief

- The Guardian reports 2022 tests finding about **36 mg/km of used-tyre wear**, versus
  **0.02 mg/km of exhaust particles** from four modern petrol SUVs, described as a 1,850-fold contrast.
- This compares tyre wear on a Mercedes C-Class with exhausts on different vehicles. Only **11% of
  tyre-particle mass** was reported as airborne-sized; the headline ratio is not an airborne-exposure
  or health-risk multiplier.
- These tests do not measure a cycling intervention or establish whether electric cars produce more
  total pollution than combustion cars.[^article]

## What was measured

As reported, Emissions Analytics tested 14 tyre brands under normal road driving, some over their
full lifetime. Scales measured tyre mass loss and sampling behind the tyres measured particle
mass, number and size down to 6 nm. Exhaust measurements used four petrol SUVs from model years
2019–2020. No matched-vehicle comparison or representative fleet estimate is presented.

The p. 2 graphic gives used tyres as 36.5 mg/km, new tyres as 73.0 mg/km, exhausts as 0.02 mg/km
and the exhaust legal limit as 4.5 mg/km. The text rounds used tyres to 36 mg/km and quotes
1,850-fold; the displayed numbers do not exactly reproduce that ratio. Aggressive but legal
driving reportedly produced 5,760 mg/km of tyre emissions, a separate driving condition.

## What the ratio cannot establish

The article reports more than one trillion particles smaller than 23 nm per kilometre. Particle
number and mass are different measures: most particles by count were small enough to become
airborne but represented only 11% by weight. Total tyre wear should not be relabelled PM10 or
PM2.5, inhaled dose, toxicity or total vehicle emissions. Confidence intervals and sufficient
protocol details for a like-for-like airborne comparison are absent; underlying research was
not independently reviewed.[^article]

The [2014 JRC review](non-exhaust-pm10-review-share-limits.md) addresses traffic-related PM10
source shares, not this test ratio. The [London model](london-non-exhaust-mode-shift-scenarios.md)
estimates conditional future intervention effects, not an observed consequence of these tests.
The [street-redesign synthesis](../topics/street-redesign-outcomes.md#pollution-source-shares-are-not-measured-redesign-benefits)
keeps these distinct evidence types separate.

[^article]: Guardian, 3 June 2022, supplied PDF p. 2 graphic and p. 3 methods, results and airborne-fraction discussion; secondary reporting only.
