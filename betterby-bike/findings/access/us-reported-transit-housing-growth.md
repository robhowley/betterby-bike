# Realtor.com reports much more new housing away from transit than near it

## In brief

- Realtor.com's January 2026 coverage attributes **2 million new units near transit stations versus 17.6 million away**
  in **2000–2019** to Urban Institute analysis, nearly nine times as many away.[^conte2026]
- The article also reports housing growth near stations opened in **2000–2009** outpacing similar neighborhoods without
  transit by **about 8% by 2019**. Neither comparison establishes a causal transit effect or the number of homes needed.

## What the comparison measures

The [linked Urban Institute publication](https://www.urban.org/urban-wire/spurring-housing-development-near-transit-through-federally-funded-land-acquisition)
was not independently reviewed. Conte first describes the comparison as near versus far from stations, then calls
it urban areas “with” versus “without” stations. The article does not define the proximity boundary, urban-area
sample, land area, starting housing stock, comparison matching or uncertainty. Retain the reported near/far framing
without treating these counts as construction rates for comparable amounts of land.

The historical pattern was mixed: neighborhoods around stations opened in the 1960s–1980s reportedly grew more
slowly than other parts of their urban areas during the following 10 or 20 years. The newer-station comparison
reverses that direction, but the article does not clarify whether its 8% means a relative difference or percentage
points. No household demand, displacement or delivered affordability outcome is estimated.[^conte2026]
