# Shared micromobility user surveys show uneven demographic representation

## In brief

- In NABSA's pooled 2019–2021 city surveys, users in households earning over $100,000 were overrepresented by an
  average 20 percentage points relative to local demographics; those earning $15,000–$49,999 were underrepresented
  by 18 points. These are city-average composition gaps, not within-income-group participation rates.[^nabsa2021]
- Women were underrepresented by 13 points and White users overrepresented by 21 points. Selected cities, differing
  category coverage and unavailable data for minors and nonbinary genders limit generalization.[^nabsa2021]

## Comparison and coverage

NABSA compared user profiles from 24 named U.S. cities with equivalent city demographics from the 2020 American
Community Survey, then averaged each city's percentage-point difference. Not every city reported every category.
The surveys span 2019–2021, rather than describing a uniform 2021 population.[^nabsa2021]

The lowest income category (below $15,000) was slightly overrepresented (+2 points), unlike the next income band.
Users aged 25–44 were overrepresented (+20), while those aged 45–64 (−11) and 65+ (−14) were underrepresented.
Black users were underrepresented by 8 points and Latino users by 1. The report describes improved representation
of very-low-income users and People of Color versus 2020, without demonstrating a fixed-sample change.[^nabsa2021]

## What this does not establish

These gaps do not identify whether affordability, route safety, service geography, preference or survey selection
caused the differences. People under 18 and nonbinary/other genders absent from the census comparison were excluded
because data were unavailable. No response-rate or uncertainty analysis is supplied for the pooled profiles.
The separately reported expansion of equity programs does not establish that discounts or outreach caused a
change in representation, or that underserved residents gained usable access.[^nabsa2021]
