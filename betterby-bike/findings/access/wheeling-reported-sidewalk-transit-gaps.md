# Wheeling accounts describe destinations beyond bus routes without sidewalk connections

## In brief

- In September 2026 commentary about Wheeling, West Virginia, Angie Schmitt reports that older women
  credited transit with preserving independence, while outlying retail and services lacked usable bus and sidewalk connections.
- She describes Ohio-side social-service agencies outside current bus routes, without connecting sidewalks,
  having to arrange expensive transportation themselves. These are local accounts, not measured costs,
  representative unmet need or evidence that a sidewalk investment improved access.[^schmitt2026]

## Reporting basis and limits

Schmitt visited Wheeling for work concerning its transit system. She describes conversations with older
women and difficulties social-service agencies face getting sick and elderly people to stores, jobs and
medical appointments. Retail growth near highway exits is her reported context for worsening destination
access. The article provides no interview count, recruitment method, named agencies, route inventory,
trip records, cost data or comparison period.[^schmitt2026]

The account supports a bounded distinction: a bus can sustain some journeys while destinations beyond its
routes remain hard to reach. It cannot identify the relative contributions of service coverage, sidewalk
continuity, disability accommodations or destination location. Her call for large-scale sidewalk funding
does not establish that sidewalks alone would solve these journeys or reduce provider costs.

## Related evidence

The [older-adult transit-use estimate](us-older-adults-reported-transit-use.md) concerns national participation,
not Wheeling's destination gaps. Together they inform [commuting and usable access](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#transit-coverage-and-usable-service-differ)
without turning non-use into proof of unmet need.
