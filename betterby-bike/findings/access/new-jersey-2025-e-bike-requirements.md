# Reported June 2025 New Jersey e-bike access requirements

## In brief

- NorthJersey.com's **June 2025** account described New Jersey Class 1 pedal-assist and Class 2 throttle e-bikes with a
  **20 mph maximum and power of 750 watts or less** as requiring no license, registration or insurance. Bicycle laws
  still applied, with helmets required for riders **under 17**.[^wallace2025]
- Its Class 3 motorized-bicycle category included electric pedal bicycles reaching **20–28 mph** and required a license,
  registration, insurance and helmets. Municipal location or age restrictions could further limit riding. This is
  historical secondary reporting, not verified current law or proof of improved access.[^wallace2025]

## What the distinction supports

The article attributes the low-speed category to a 2019 change to Title 39. The New Jersey Bicycle and Pedestrian
Resource Center describes the earlier mismatch: e-bikes were treated as mopeds requiring MVC registration, but the
registration system was written for gas-powered vehicles and would not register them. The reported low-speed
exemption distinguishes administrative requirements between vehicle categories; it does not quantify savings,
compliance, adoption or the number of people gaining usable transportation.[^wallace2025]

The article specifically advises checking rules in one's own town and towns visited or ridden through. Its state
classification summary cannot establish permission to use a particular path or street, or eligibility for every age
group. Local rules are not listed.[^wallace2025]

## Qualifications

The three-page article, updated June 29, 2025, was read in full; its cited guide, statutes, MVC rules and municipal
ordinances were not separately reviewed. The article's simplified speed bands overlap at 20 mph and do not explain
motor-assistance cutoffs versus attainable speed. Do not infer exact vehicle eligibility or rules for faster devices
from these bands. Later legal changes and present-day requirements remain unexamined. Police reminders establish
neither an increase in risk nor an effect of the regulations on injuries.
