# New Jersey respondents reported unfair bicycle stops and place avoidance

## In brief

- In a 2015 New Jersey intercept survey, Figure 40 reports ever experiencing an unfair bicycle police
  stop among 23% of Black past-year riders, 14% of Hispanic riders and 28% of Mixed-Race riders.
  These are self-reported experiences in a selected sample, not exposure-adjusted or adjudicated stop rates.[^report]
- Black focus-group participants described avoiding Highland Park over profiling concerns; some Hispanic
  participants described avoiding Milltown. These accounts show perceived constraints on destinations,
  not a measured population-wide reduction in riding or an evaluated policing intervention.[^report]

## Question and supporting accounts

Both survey versions ask, “Have you ever been unfairly stopped by a police officer while riding a bicycle?”
The recall period is lifetime/ever, whereas rider classification uses only the past twelve months.
Figure 40 reports 20% among all past-year riders and 7% among non-riders; “non-rider” therefore does not
mean never exposed to bicycle policing. Figure 40 and the discussion give 14% overall, but p. 36 says 15%.
The discrepancy is unresolved and is not an uncertainty interval.[^report]

The 16-person Black focus group described stops attributed to curfew enforcement, suspicion that a
valuable bicycle was stolen and racial profiling. Authors interpret profiling as influencing where
participants rode more clearly than whether they rode. The Hispanic group reported no bicycle police
stops, while still expressing place-specific concerns; its reported size conflicts (10 versus 11).
Neither group's experience represents all members of its racial or ethnic category.[^report]

## Limits and interpretation

Surveys were collected at selected high-footfall sites in a reported 34 municipalities, not through
statewide random sampling. Group-level item counts, riding exposure, uncertainty and adjusted comparisons
are unavailable here; overall sample totals also conflict. Differences can reflect age, accumulated
riding, location, reporting or other influences, and cannot isolate discriminatory treatment statistically.
The accounts remain evidence of perceived unfairness and avoidance, rather than proof of no barrier
because an administrative finding is absent.[^report]

Authors propose stronger police-community relationships to address crime, theft and profiling concerns.
The study does not test that proposal or show that additional enforcement improves access.
