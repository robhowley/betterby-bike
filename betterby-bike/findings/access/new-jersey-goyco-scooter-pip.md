# Goyco's scooter rider was denied pedestrian PIP benefits

## In brief

- On May 14, 2024, New Jersey's Supreme Court held that David Goyco, injured on an electric scooter in
  Elizabeth in November 2021, was not entitled to pedestrian PIP medical benefits under his automobile policy.
- Unlike a muscular-powered bicycle, his scooter was motor-powered and designed for public-road use, placing
  him outside the No-Fault Act's pedestrian definition. Bicycle-like traffic rules did not establish coverage.
- This concerns the statute and policy decided in that case, not every e-bike, all possible compensation,
  verified current law or measured effects on medical care.[^goyco2024]

## Why traffic permission did not establish insurance eligibility

Goyco's Segway Ninebot KickScooter Max used a rechargeable electric motor and was not designed for muscular
propulsion. It could travel 15.5 mph and had a headlight, brake light and speedometer. The Court interpreted
“highways” broadly as public roads, not only high-speed routes. Those characteristics made it a vehicle
propelled by other than muscular power and designed primarily for highway use under N.J.S.A. 39:6A-2(h).
His policy followed the Act; no coverage beyond statutory requirements was argued.[^goyco2024]

The Court rejected importing the separate traffic-law definition that excluded low-speed scooters from
“vehicle.” It also rejected treating the 2019 bicycle-rule provision as granting pedestrian insurance status.
The opinion contrasts ordinary bicycles and discusses prior moped cases, but does not adjudicate every
pedal-assist configuration. Its discussion of insurance-cost containment supplies a legislative rationale,
not an estimate of premium effects. Actual benefits paid, treatment delays and alternative recovery were
not evaluated; later legal changes were not reviewed.[^goyco2024]
