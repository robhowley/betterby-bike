# ACEEE reports historical e-bike purchase assistance, not evaluated access gains

## In brief

- ACEEE's March 2025 report describes Colorado's **$450 point-of-sale e-bike tax credit**, introduced in
  April 2024, and Rhode Island's rebates capped at **$350 standard or $750 income-qualified**.
- Rhode Island's percentage caps also differ: the lesser of **$350 or 30%** of purchase price versus
  **$750 or 75%** for income-qualified buyers. These are reported program terms, not measured savings
  across households, increased cycling or verified current offers.[^aceee2025]

## Reported designs

Table 20 says Colorado's e-bike credit is available only at point of sale. It dates the Rhode Island
program's launch to October 2022, covers e-bikes and e-cargo bikes, and states a limit of two rebates per
household for each tier. It does not specify the income threshold in this entry or establish that tiers
can be combined. Do not sum the caps as a single entitlement.[^aceee2025]

## What this does not establish

This is ACEEE's policy compilation, drawing on agency information and public sources, not a controlled
incentive evaluation. The entries provide no participant counts, baseline purchase rates, counterfactual
purchases, trip replacement, route availability or measured distribution of benefits. A larger
income-qualified subsidy does not itself demonstrate that lower-income people gained equitable access.
The report's claim that assistance improves affordability is its rationale, not an estimated outcome.

Original program rules, remaining funds and later changes were not reviewed. Amounts and eligibility
should be attributed to the March 2025 report, not used as current purchasing advice.
