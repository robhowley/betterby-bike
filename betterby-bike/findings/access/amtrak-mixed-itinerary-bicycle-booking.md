# An Amtrak mixed-service round trip hid the bicycle booking option

## In brief

- In a July 23, 2026 League test, a Washington–Boston round trip combining a bike-eligible Northeast
  Regional outbound and an ineligible Acela return showed no bicycle add-on or space count at checkout.
- The page did not explain that the return leg caused the omission. This is one documented interface
  test, not evidence of how often riders abandoned trips or of the current booking system's behavior.[^amtrak2026]

## What the report observed

Appendix L distinguishes a train's “Services” icon indicating bike service from a live remaining-space
count. The latter appeared only on the later “Additional Items” page, which combined the round trip into
one step and offered the bicycle add-on only when every leg supported bike service. The test's mixed
itinerary removed that option entirely without identifying the incompatible leg.[^amtrak2026]

Having many booking channels therefore does not establish usable itinerary information. The report awards
all bicycle-service lines full booking-channel points but no website-functionality points under its adapted
criteria. These criteria reward early bike-compatible search, early capacity information and leg-specific
explanations, rather than merely having online ticket sales.[^amtrak2026]

## Limits and relationship

The League's account was read directly; the test was not repeated and no error-frequency or traveler-outcome
sample is supplied. Do not infer net ridership losses or that every channel failed. The PDF retains a draft
label. This is a distinct access barrier within the same report, not independent confirmation of its
[line-level benchmark](amtrak-bicycle-access-benchmark.md).

The [commuting and access Topic](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md) separates service availability from
whether a traveler can complete a usable journey.

[^amtrak2026]: League report, pp. 11 and 15; Appendix L (p. 60), dated test description; Appendix U (p. 69).
