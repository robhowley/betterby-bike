# Chicago modeled cancellation burdens differ by gender and household income

## In brief

- In Chicago's complete-transit-removal simulation, women account for 53.0% of all activity cancellations
  and 53.7% of non-work cancellations, compared with a cited 51% city population share.
- The lowest 40% of households by income account for 48.0% of all cancellations and 50.2% of non-work
  cancellations. These are shares of cancelled activities, not each group's cancellation rate.[^verbas2024]

## Denominators and interpretation

The lowest household-income quintile alone accounts for 26.3% of all cancellations and 28.5% of non-work
cancellations. Income quintiles contain equal numbers of households, not necessarily equal numbers of people
or baseline activities. Likewise, women's population share is not their share of activities before removal.
The paper interprets these distributions as disproportionate burdens; they do not by themselves identify
individual cancellation probabilities or income-adjusted gender effects.[^verbas2024]

These results concern the **City of Chicago**, unlike the metropolitan activity totals and economic valuation.
They arise from a modeled 2025 comparison removing all transit while assuming zero-car households buy one car
and one-car households buy another. The paper separately extrapolates a 12% care-trip loss using CMAP survey
counts and selected model categories; that is not independent confirmation of the gender/income outputs.[^verbas2024]

## Limits

No subgroup uncertainty intervals, baseline activity denominators or sensitivity to alternative vehicle-purchase
assumptions accompany these shares. They do not measure observed service-cut harm, cycling access or the equity
benefits of an incremental investment. Transport need, population composition and frequency of baseline activities
must remain distinct when comparing burdens.
