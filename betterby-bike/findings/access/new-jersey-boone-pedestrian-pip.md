# Reported pedestrian PIP coverage in New Jersey's Boone case

## In brief

- Michael J. Epstein's August 12, 2026 opinion column reports that New Jersey's Appellate Division required pedestrian
  Personal Injury Protection (PIP) medical benefits in *Boone*, involving an uninsured pedestrian struck by a Lyft
  driver actively carrying a passenger. This is secondary legal commentary, not an independently verified
  holding.[^epstein2026]
- The described dispute concerns medical-benefit coverage, not general rideshare fault or all damages. It does not
  establish eligibility for cyclists, delivery crashes or other driver activity states, or demonstrate faster treatment
  or fewer injuries.[^epstein2026]

## Reported dispute and interpretation

According to Epstein, the insurer argued that a policy issued under the Transportation Network Company Act
excluded its obligation to pay pedestrian PIP benefits. He reports that the court read New Jersey's insurance
laws together and rejected that argument; the article later names Liberty Mutual. He describes the decision as
occurring in late July, without providing an exact decision date in the prose.[^epstein2026]

Epstein's argument that injured people should receive prompt care while reimbursement disputes are resolved is
an endorsement of no-fault policy, not evidence that this claimant received payment or treatment sooner. His
comparison of identical crashes with different insurance arrangements is hypothetical, not a study.[^epstein2026]

## Limits and related evidence

Only the commentary was reviewed. The linked court opinion, statutory language, policy terms, later proceedings
and benefit limits remain unchecked. Do not use this account as current legal advice or extend its reported
passenger-carrying Lyft facts to every crash involving a transport platform.

[New Jersey's June 2025 e-bike requirements](new-jersey-2025-e-bike-requirements.md) concern a separate legal
question, permission and requirements to ride, and do not corroborate this PIP account. The
[access Topic](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md) distinguishes those requirements and post-crash benefits
from observed commuting, usable routes and measured affordability.
