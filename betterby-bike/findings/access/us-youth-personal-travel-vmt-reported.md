# Reported US youth personal travel fell without a matching vehicle-mile decline

## In brief

- SSTI reports US young adults' average daily trips fell nearly 50% and personal mileage fell 41%
  between 2017 and 2022, citing UCLA's NHTS analysis; the accompanying chart covers ages 15–29.
- The article also describes youth vehicle miles as roughly unchanged because remaining car trips
  were longer. It does not reconcile the measures' denominators, and neither claim establishes
  substitution toward cycling or improved access.[^ssti2025]

## Measures and interpretation

The chart labels daily personal miles traveled (PMT) per capita, comparing ages 15–29 with ages
30–59 across 2001, 2009, 2017 and 2022. PMT and vehicle miles traveled (VMT) are different measures:
this account cannot support treating the 41% reduction as a reduction in vehicle mileage. The prose
calls VMT roughly unchanged since 2017 but does not state whether this is per driver, per person
or an aggregate. No numerical reconciliation is possible from the capture alone.

Researchers suggest online shopping and digital recreation may replace trips. Fewer outings can
mean less participation rather than better transport alternatives. SSTI's suggestion that slower
growth permits rethinking road investment is a recommendation, not a measured street-redesign effect.
The underlying report was not read; sample sizes, survey-method changes and uncertainty definitions
are not supplied in this news account.[^ssti2025]
