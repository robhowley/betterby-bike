# Modeled household savings from less driving

## In brief

- Applying RMI's Smarter MODES calculator to its high-reduction US scenario, UCS reports more than
  $5.9 trillion in household vehicle-cost savings cumulatively through 2050, not annual savings.
- The scenario assumes roughly 40% less light-duty driving in 2050 than its net-zero comparison
  (27% below 2023). Savings include fuel, maintenance and depreciation, not just money freed for spending.
- The estimate does not measure savings caused by cycling or deduct a specified alternative-transport
  package's costs; it cannot show that every household benefits equally.[^freedom2024]

## Accounting and interpretation

The report describes an average yearly household ownership-cost reduction of $1,600 and presents
Figure 3 in discounted 2024 dollars. It attributes much of the later-year savings to avoided depreciation
because lower odometer mileage preserves vehicle value, interpreting this as akin to avoided purchase
cost. That valuation is distinct from immediate disposable income. The text does not identify $1,600 as
a guaranteed annual saving for each household or a cycling participant's measured saving.[^freedom2024]

Fuel is already part of this ownership estimate. The report's separate energy analysis also values
household fuel savings, so adding its fuel total to $5.9 trillion would overlap categories. The two tools
and their baselines were not independently examined. The main report supplies no household distribution,
income-specific savings or complete net accounting of the investments/services needed to replace driving.
Its proposal to redirect savings into transit and active travel is an interpretation, not a measured
budget transfer or evaluated equity improvement.[^freedom2024]
