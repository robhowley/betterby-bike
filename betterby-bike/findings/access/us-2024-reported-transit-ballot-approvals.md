# U.S. transit ballot measures reportedly passed at nearly 87% in 2024

## In brief

- Streetsblog's November 11, 2024 APTA-based coverage reports **46 of 53 initiatives passed that year**, nearly **87%**;
  it separately reports **$24.9 billion across 18 measures** in the November election.
- This describes measures placed on ballots, not the proportion of Americans supporting transit. Local defeats and a
  nullified San Francisco approval qualify the positive national account.
- Funding approval is not spending or evidence that transit, walking or cycling access improved.[^wilson2024]

## Scope of the result

The annual passage count and November funding total have different time scopes and denominators.
The article supplies neither the full inventory nor vote margins or rules for classifying measures.
Its interview with APTA's Jessica Grennan attributes success to bipartisan campaigns and destination
access, but does not test those explanations or compare voter preferences by party.[^wilson2024]

Several winning packages included active travel alongside other purposes: Wheat Ridge's reported
$75 million covers sidewalks, crossings, bike lanes and drainage; Seattle's $1.55 billion levy covers
sidewalks, paving, bridges and transit connections. These are not bicycle-specific funding amounts,
and their passage does not identify who benefits.[^wilson2024]

## Contrary outcomes and uncertainty

The article reports defeats in San Diego and Atlanta's Cobb and Gwinnett counties, among others.
Charleston County rejected a mixed package including an expressway extension, complicating any
interpretation of that vote as opposition to transit. San Francisco's approved Muni measure was
reportedly nullified by another measure; the article does not explain whether its headline totals
were adjusted accordingly. Do not sum its local examples or treat all listed approvals as available funds.
Underlying returns and APTA accounting remain unreviewed.[^wilson2024]
