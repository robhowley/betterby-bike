# Conditional ownership savings from a US transit expansion scenario

## In brief

- Streetsblog's January 2026 coverage of Transportation for America reports over $5.4 trillion in
  avoided US car-ownership costs during 2026–2045 under a roughly $4.6-trillion transit expansion scenario.
- Savings depend on an assumed reduction in the car-ownership rate of one-quarter of a percent each
  year, against projected ownership spending above $77 trillion, not an observed response to transit investment.
- The article does not establish net investment payback, household-specific benefits or cycling savings;
  it also gives a conflicting $4.7-trillion investment figure once.[^wilson]

## What the comparison does and does not show

The analysis aims to expand US transit toward selected international city benchmarks over 20 years.
Lead author Corrigan Salerno calls the assumed ownership reduction conservative, but that description
is not an empirical uncertainty bound. The article does not clarify whether the annual change means a
relative percentage or percentage-point reduction, nor provide calculations, discounting or price-year
details sufficient to reproduce the monetary totals. The primary analysis was not reviewed.[^wilson]

Wilson interprets avoided ownership costs exceeding investment as a scenario that would more than pay
for itself. Household avoided costs are not government revenue, however, and the coverage does not
establish complete net accounting or who would receive the savings. It identifies land-use and
financing questions beyond the report's scope, so usable alternatives cannot be inferred from fleet
expansion alone. These are projected gross avoided ownership costs, not measured cash savings for
current transit users.[^wilson]
