# New Jersey bicycle-barrier rankings have unresolved denominator and reporting conflicts

## In brief

- In Rutgers' June–August 2015 intercept surveys targeting Black and Hispanic communities across New
  Jersey, collision fear leads both prose and Table 5. The table reports 32% overall, but the underlying
  question permits multiple answers: this is not established as 32% of people or a single top choice.[^report]
- The narrative names collision fear, robbery/assault and pavement as the three biggest barriers.
  Table 5 instead ranks collision fear, police profiling and robbery/assault first through third,
  with pavement eighth. Neither a definitive top-three list nor statewide prevalence is warranted.[^report]

## What the table and instrument measure

Table 5 reports collision fear at 32%, police profiling at 15%, robbery/assault at 14%, and pavement
at 5% for “All Respondents”; the total row says 100%. Appendix A question 25 asks past-year riders
what prevents more cycling, while Appendix B question 20 asks non-riders what would concern them if
they rode. Both say “Select all that apply”, not “rank” or “choose the biggest”. The report does not
explain normalization or give item counts sufficient to recover respondent prevalence. Actual barriers
and hypothetical concerns are also different questions.[^report]

The contradiction is present in the printed table, not just text extraction. The executive summary,
results prose and conclusion repeat the pavement top-three claim; they are not independent evidence.
Other multiple-response results, including reasons for not riding and comfortable locations, likewise
should not be read automatically as percentages of people.[^report]

## Sampling and population limits

Surveys were offered at selected busy intersections, commercial corridors, transit locations and some
parks, mostly weekday afternoons, in a reported 34 municipalities. Race was not a screening condition.
The selection strategy aimed at coverage, not a probability sample of New Jersey residents. “Bicyclist”
means any cycling in twelve months, not commuting or frequent riding.[^report]

The report gives 2,061, 2,062 and 2,661 as survey totals in different sections. Table 2 uses varying
item bases and includes 116 under-18 respondents despite an adult target and prose claiming exclusion.
Without raw data and a reconciled analysis sample, do not derive population counts, confidence intervals
or causal racial differences from these percentages. The evidence identifies concerns to investigate,
not measured crash risk or the benefit of a proposed remedy.[^report]
