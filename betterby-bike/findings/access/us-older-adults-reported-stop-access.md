# Realtor.com reports missing nearby transit stops for many older Americans and higher use among nondrivers

## In brief

- Realtor.com's January 2026 article reports **nearly 58% of older Americans living in neighborhoods without public
  transit stops**, citing a Journal of Transport & Health study. The article omits age and neighborhood
  definitions.[^conte2026]
- Among **older nondrivers**, it reports **more than one in four using transit** and **nearly 17% using it frequently**.
  These have a different denominator from all older adults; the article does not define frequency or the observation
  period.

## Evidence and limitations

The [linked study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2214140525000027) was not reviewed.
Conte says higher walkability and denser stop coverage were associated with more transit use, particularly among
people transitioning away from driving. No sample size, adjusted estimate, confidence interval or design details
are supplied. This supports a reported association, not a demonstrated effect of adding stops or moving homes.[^conte2026]

A neighborhood stop does not establish accessible boarding, useful schedules or access to needed destinations;
absence of a stop does not itself quantify isolation or unmet travel demand. The article's argument for
transit-oriented housing is a recommendation, not an evaluated housing intervention.
