# U.S. workers without an available vehicle walked and biked to work at higher rates

Streetsblog's account of U.S. Census ACS results reports that workers with **no available vehicle** walked to work
at **four times** and bicycled at **three-and-a-half times** the rates of workers with **one available vehicle**.
The reproduced Census Figure 13 identifies **2008–2012** data. Reported commute rates declined as vehicle
availability increased across the chart's zero, one, two and three-or-more categories.[^streetsblog2014]

These ratios compare the percentages of workers using each commute mode, not their trip frequency, distance or
number of trips. Vehicle availability is the chart's measure; it should not be relabeled individual car ownership.
Exact absolute rates are not inferred from its unlabeled bars.[^streetsblog2014]

This is an unadjusted association, not evidence that removing a vehicle causes active commuting or that workers
without vehicles have adequate transport choices. Income, location and other differences are not separated here.
The article provides no uncertainty intervals or sample sizes, measures usual commuting rather than all travel,
and is secondary coverage of a Census report not separately reviewed.[^streetsblog2014]
