# Streetsblog reports low monthly transit use among urban older US adults

## In brief

- Streetsblog's January 2026 article reports that a **2022 study found about one in ten urban-dwelling older
  US adults used public transportation in the preceding month**. This is secondary reporting, not a reviewed
  primary-study estimate.[^wilson2026]
- The article omits sample details, age threshold, observation year and uncertainty. The result does not mean
  the other nine in ten lacked transit access, nor does it measure cycling or the effects of service improvements.

## What was reported

The article links [the 2022 study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9771957/) but does not reproduce its
methods. Its denominator is older urban adults, not all US adults, transit trips or commuters; its time window
is the preceding month, not usual commute mode. The primary study was not reviewed for this ingestion.

Wilson discusses unavailable or infrequent transit and missing amenities such as bus-stop benches as barriers.
Sidara executive Balsam Nehme argues that reliable, affordable, accessible transport helps older adults reach
healthcare, employment and social connections. Neither the reported percentage nor those statements isolates
how much non-use reflects service barriers, preferences, health, car availability or other factors.[^wilson2026]

## Interpretation and related evidence

Low reported use is a reason to investigate usable alternatives to driving, not an estimate of isolation or
unmet demand. No intervention, comparator or before/after change is supplied. The article cannot establish
that its proposed transit, housing or bicycle projects increased older-adult access or reduced emissions.

- [Commuting and access](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#older-adult-mobility-needs-extend-beyond-commuting)
  places this measure alongside worker and rider-composition measures without pooling them.
- [Realtor.com's stop-access and nondriver-use reporting](us-older-adults-reported-stop-access.md) cites a different
  study and different denominators; it is not a trend comparison or independent confirmation of this monthly-use figure.
- [Shared-micromobility representation](nabsa-user-representation-gaps.md) concerns city user profiles,
  not older adults' monthly transit participation; it is not an independent estimate of the same outcome.

[^wilson2026]: Streetsblog article, supplied PDF p. 4, paragraph beginning “In transit-poor communities”
    and Nehme's following remarks; p. 5, accessible-transit and neighborhood recommendations.
