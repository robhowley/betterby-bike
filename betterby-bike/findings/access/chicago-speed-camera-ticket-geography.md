# Chicago speed-camera ticket geography does not identify individual racial ticketing rates

## In brief

- Streetsblog's January 2022 account of ProPublica's Chicago investigation reports **38% of speed-camera tickets** sent
  to residents of majority-Black ZIP codes, compared with **27% of city population** living in those ZIP codes.
- That is a neighborhood-level burden comparison, not the race of individual ticketed drivers or a rate per trip. A
  separate reported UIC result concerns where ticket-issuing cameras were located, not where drivers lived.

## Evidence and limits

Wilson reports that cameras were not disproportionately concentrated in Black neighborhoods, yet cameras
in those neighborhoods issued a disproportionate share of tickets. The article links this pattern to wide,
high-speed roads and reports that the highest-revenue cameras were all on four-lane roads. These are
secondary accounts and interpretations, not an independently reviewed causal test of road design.[^wilson]

The article does not specify the observation window for the 38% share, ticket totals, driving exposure or
individual race. Population share is not exposure to camera-monitored driving; ecological classification
cannot establish individual racial disparities. Its broader account of penalties and financial distress must
not be treated as a speed-camera-only estimate. Proposals for ability-to-pay fines, safety-directed revenue
and redesign were not evaluated here.[^wilson]

This burden evidence can coexist with the [reported severe-crash reduction](../safety/chicago-reported-speed-camera-crash-reduction.md).
It does not show that either removing cameras or retaining the existing fine structure is the best response.
