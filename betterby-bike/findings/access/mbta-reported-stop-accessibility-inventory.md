# MBTA reports identifying and remedying inaccessible stops through a system inventory

## In brief

- Next City's March 2026 reporting describes an MBTA inventory of **roughly 7,000 bus stops** that
  identified **280 completely inaccessible stops**, with most reportedly remedied. The audit included
  adjacent crossings and curb ramps, not just the stop itself.[^tu2026]
- The account supplies no exact remediation total, completion date or rider-outcome comparison.
  It demonstrates reported barrier identification and action, not a measured gain in usable transit trips.

## What was reported

MBTA accessibility manager Laura Brelsford says the inventory began almost a decade before publication.
She reports that 99% of stops fall outside agency jurisdiction. The agency used the data to direct its
own investment and seek municipal improvements, with agency capital funds, state grants and fair share
tax revenue supporting upgrades. The inventory's findings therefore concern both transit facilities and
connecting municipal pedestrian infrastructure.[^tu2026]

## Interpretation and limitations

The primary inventory and audit criteria were not supplied or reviewed. The article does not explain
how “completely inaccessible” was assessed, how many additional stops had partial barriers, or whether
remediation was independently checked. The 280 is not a count of inaccessible stops remaining in 2026.
There is no comparison group, quantified change in completed trips or measured accessibility benefit
attributable to collecting data alone.

[Commuting and access](/topics/active-commuting-and-access.md#stop-inventories-and-maintenance-affect-usable-access)
places this account alongside service and maintenance constraints. It complements
[reported older-adult stop proximity](us-older-adults-reported-stop-access.md), but neither proximity nor
an inventory entry establishes that a person can reach and use a stop in practice.

[^tu2026]: Next City, supplied PDF p. 2, “For the MBTA, bus stop data started with accessibility,”
    and p. 3 opening paragraphs on funding and prioritizing improvements.
