# New Jersey respondents favored bicycle lanes, not a tested protected-lane intervention

## In brief

- Figure 43 of Rutgers' 2015 New Jersey survey reports 77% wanting a bicycle lane on their street,
  including 83% of past-year riders and 73% of non-riders. This is stated preference in a selected
  intercept sample, not statewide support or measured additional cycling.[^report]
- The question says “bicycle lane”, not “protected bicycle lane”. The recommendation's claim that
  more than 80% wanted protected lanes is not established by that item; treatment wording and
  response bases must stay attached to the result.[^report]

## What was asked

Appendix A question 21 and Appendix B question 23 offer “Yes”, “No”, and “I already have one on my
street”. Figure 43 displays only yes/no, without explaining the treatment of existing-lane responses
or supplying item counts. Its 77% therefore cannot be assumed to include every surveyed person.
The report's inconsistent overall survey totals add further uncertainty.[^report]

Within that chart, reported support is 75% among Black respondents and 85% among Hispanic respondents.
Among non-riders it remains 72% and 80%, respectively. Non-riders had not ridden in twelve months;
the result does not describe only people unable to ride or never interested in cycling.[^report]

## Infrastructure interest and practical constraints coexist

Separate questions ask whether lanes, off-street paths or secure destination parking would make people
ride more. The report describes these as prominent encouragements, with differing rankings across
groups. These are hypothetical responses, not observed uptake. In the focus groups, participants
welcomed physical separation after viewing pictures, but some rejected lanes on dead-end streets,
questioned whether lanes alone would induce riding or described distance, crime and family obligations
that made bicycle commutes impractical.[^report]

The surveys were conducted at selected busy sites in a reported 34 municipalities during summer 2015;
focus groups were small and concentrated in New Brunswick. Neither compares outcomes after lane
installation against a control. Support, comfort, intention, actual cycling and safety remain distinct.
Authors' protected-lane and secure-parking recommendations are plausible responses to reported needs,
not demonstrated benefit estimates.[^report]
