# London's weekly cyclist profiles differ from annual participation rates

## In brief

- Women were 34% of London's past-week cyclists in TfL's 2021/22 profile, 16 percentage points below equal
  representation. Lower-income people also remained underrepresented. These are shares of cyclists, not the
  percentage of each demographic group that cycles.[^report]
- A separate 2021 survey found no significant ethnicity differences in past-year cycling. This uses another
  sample, period and denominator; it does not establish equal weekly participation or equal access. Pandemic
  telephone LTDS data are not wholly comparable with the earlier face-to-face series.[^report]

## Weekly profile and survey changes

Figure 4.6 compares people cycling in the last week in 2010/11, 2019/20, 2020/21 and 2021/22 with the
2021/22 population profile. TfL describes improvement in women's representation, persistent overrepresentation
of White people, working people, higher-income households and ages 25–44, and underrepresentation of older
people and lower-income households. Inner London residents were overrepresented relative to outer London;
comparable historical area data were unavailable. No exact subgroup values are inferred from unlabeled bars.

The usual LTDS uses full-household face-to-face interviews and a detailed one-day diary. A scaled-down
telephone version operated in 2020/21 and 2021/22; the previous method resumed in April 2022 with questionnaire
adaptations. Figure 4.6's age categories start at 17. The reviewed pages do not give profile subgroup sample
sizes, confidence intervals or enough detail to separate genuine demographic change from survey effects.[^report]

## The different annual-participation result

TfL separately summarizes *Cycling potential in London's diverse communities* (2021): past-year cycling was
24% among Black people, 25% among Asian people, 31% in Mixed/Multiple ethnic groups and 28% among White
people, against a London average of 27%. TfL reports no statistically significant differences, not proof of
identical participation. It reports greater openness to future cycling among Black, Asian and Mixed/Multiple
ethnic groups than among White people, but intentions are not observed uptake.[^report]

That survey still found men, people under 35 and medium-/high-income groups more likely to have cycled in the
past year. The original survey and its sampling, weighting and statistical tests were not independently reviewed;
these are secondary results within TfL's report, not another confirmation of LTDS profiles.

Higher cycling volumes therefore do not establish equitable growth, nor do these surveys measure who benefited
from new lanes. TfL's recommendation to diversify cycling and extend it beyond leisure is a policy interpretation.
