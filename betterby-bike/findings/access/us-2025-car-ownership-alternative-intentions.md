# Reported U.S. interest in car-ownership alternatives did not measure mode shift

## In brief

- Newsweek's May 2025 coverage of a Harris survey commissioned by Turo reports 48% of respondents open to alternatives
  to buying or leasing a car.
- Among surveyed car owners, 42% believed giving up ownership would significantly ease financial strain; this is an
  expectation, not measured savings.
- The article provides no survey methods or observed travel changes. Interest in car access without ownership does not
  establish a shift to cycling or transit.[^newsweek2025]

## What the measures mean

The same coverage reports that 60% of surveyed owners experienced increased car costs over four years.
Turo's data officer says 57% of respondents were interested in car access without ownership's long-term
commitment. Car sharing and rentals can retain car travel, so these figures cannot be treated as
willingness to stop driving. They describe different questions and denominators, not a sequence of
people progressing from cost pressure to ownership abandonment.

The article separately reports a 13.4% year-on-year decline in the share of consumers intending to buy
a car within six months, attributed to WalletHub. It omits the underlying shares, so the decline cannot
be relabeled percentage points. Delayed purchases or purchases brought forward before tariffs are not
necessarily reduced ownership. This is a separate survey, not confirmation of the Harris result.

## Limits

Only the news article was reviewed. Sample sizes, field dates, sampling and weighting, question wording
and statistical uncertainty are unavailable. Turo commissioned the survey and sells car-sharing services;
its executive's interpretation of a generational shift is not an independently evaluated trend. Neither
survey measures access to destinations, household savings after giving up a car or infrastructure effects.
