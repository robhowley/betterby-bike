# More states prioritized high-need communities in active-travel funding by 2024

## In brief

- Safe Routes Partnership reports **38 states** defined and prioritized high-need communities in Transportation
  Alternatives Program (TAP) funding in **2024**, compared with **24 in 2022**.
- **17 states** provided matching funds for projects benefiting high-need communities in 2024. These are
  reported policy provisions, not evidence that more communities obtained funding or experienced safer travel.[^srp]

## What was reported

The publisher's resource page describes maps accompanying its *Making Strides 2024* policy report cards.
Its Figure 2 description supplies the 2022–2024 prioritization comparison; Figure 3 supplies the matching-fund
count. They describe different provisions and should not be added together as distinct participating states.
The publisher interprets the growth as recognition of funding-access barriers and an attempt to reduce them.[^srp]

## Limits

Only the captured resource page was reviewed, not the underlying report, state cards or scoring appendices.
It does not define high need or establish consistency of definitions and scoring across years. It supplies
no applicant counts, award rates, distribution of dollars, construction outcomes or travel/injury measures.
Jurisdiction coverage is not explicit, so no percentage of all states is inferred. Policy prioritization
is not a measured equity benefit, nor evidence of present-day eligibility.
