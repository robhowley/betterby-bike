# NABSA's 2021 emissions estimate is not a lifecycle climate balance

## In brief

- NABSA estimates North American shared micromobility offset approximately 54 million pounds of CO2 in 2021 by
  replacing auto trips. This is a calculation, not measured atmospheric emissions.[^nabsa2021]
- Operations, externalities and lifecycle effects are excluded for both shared micromobility and driving.
  The estimate therefore cannot establish net lifecycle savings or a universal benefit for each trip.[^nabsa2021]

## Calculation and boundary

The report combines taxi/rideshare and auto driver/passenger replacement rates, estimated total trips, average
trip distance from operator/agency responses and emissions factors. E-bike and scooter factors use published
energy estimates and U.S. grid factors; the auto factor comes from an EPA typical-passenger-vehicle memorandum.
The displayed reductions relative to replaced auto trips are 100% for pedal bikes, 97% for e-bikes and 98% for
scooters, within that restricted accounting boundary.[^nabsa2021]

These percentages do not include vehicle production or the operations needed to rebalance, collect or recharge
shared fleets. Nor do they establish a benefit for trips replacing walking or other low-emission travel. The
[mode-replacement inputs](/findings/networks/nabsa-mode-replacement-survey-limits.md) pool earlier user surveys;
the [trip and distance estimates](/findings/networks/nabsa-2021-ridership-recovery.md) have their own coverage and
measurement limits. The report does not propagate those uncertainties into an interval for avoided emissions.
The underlying energy and lifecycle studies were cited by NABSA, not independently reviewed here.[^nabsa2021]
