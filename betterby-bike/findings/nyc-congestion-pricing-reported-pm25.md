# NYC congestion pricing reportedly reduced daily-maximum PM2.5 in its first six months

## In brief

- Cornell reports **3.05 µg/m³ lower average daily maximum PM2.5** in Manhattan's Congestion Relief Zone
  during January–June 2025, **22% below a modeled no-pricing average of 13.8 µg/m³**, not a simple before/after change.
- Reported declines extended across the five boroughs (1.07 µg/m³) and the broader area (0.70 µg/m³).
  The coverage omits full modeling methods and uncertainty intervals; regional averages do not establish
  improvement at every location or measured health benefits.[^nutt2025]

## Evidence and limits

The [Cornell article](/sources/2025-cornell-congestion-pricing-air-quality.md) describes 42 metropolitan-area
monitors across 518 days and 17,758 observations, with traffic, demographic and weather data used in statistical
modeling. These totals do not imply a complete monitor-day panel. The article does not detail identification,
missing-data treatment, geographic weighting or uncertainty. The linked *npj Clean Air* study was not reviewed.
Vehicle entries reportedly declined about 11% in the first six months, but the article does not decompose the
pollution change into reduced driving, transit substitution or delivery rescheduling.[^nutt2025]

The article introduces the outer-area estimates as average declines without separately specifying their precise
outcome aggregation or counterfactual. The three geographic estimates are overlapping areas, not independent
replications. Researcher claims against suburban displacement cannot exclude localized increases. Suggested
mode shifts and public-health benefits are interpretations, not measured outcomes reported here.

Annual-average exposure standards cannot be directly compared with a six-month average of daily maxima.
This concentration result also differs from [London's modeled cumulative non-exhaust emission savings](/findings/london-non-exhaust-mode-shift-scenarios.md):
concentrations in µg/m³ are not emitted mass in tonnes, and neither result estimates a particular bicycle-lane benefit.
