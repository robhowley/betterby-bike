# London's modeled mode shift reduces non-exhaust PM, but does not establish a cycling-project return

## In brief

- A London scenario model reports cumulative non-exhaust savings through 2050 of **5,548 tonnes PM10, including
  2,610 tonnes PM2.5**, under assumed lower car use and higher bus use, relative to its projected baseline.
- This is not a measured bike-lane effect: travel changes are imposed, costs of achieving them are omitted,
  and the report inconsistently labels the reference year for its car-kilometre target.
- Faster electrification also lowers modeled non-exhaust emissions. The comparison excludes its exhaust and
  greenhouse-gas benefits, so it does not rank the full environmental value of mode shift versus electrification.[^eit2025]

## Model and comparison

The May 2025 EIT Urban Mobility/e:misia report extends LAEI 2019 fleet/activity data to 2050 using UK SIBYL trends.
The baseline already reaches 86% light-duty and 53% heavy-duty electrification by 2050. Scenario 4 accelerates this
to 100% of the whole fleet by 2050 and TfL buses by 2030, retaining baseline vehicle kilometres.
Scenario 5 assumes 33% fewer car kilometres and 25% more bus kilometres by 2041, promoting public transport,
cycling and walking. Interventions begin gradually in 2026. The prose on p. 62 compares the car reduction with
2015; Table 4 on p. 63 labels the changes relative to baseline. That discrepancy is unresolved.[^eit2025]

The Scenario 5 total on p. 68 is cumulative avoided non-exhaust particle mass, not an annual reduction,
ambient concentration or measured exposure change. PM2.5 is contained within PM10 and must not be added to it.
The combined mode-shift/electrification scenario has slightly less than 99% of the sum of the individual savings,
indicating overlapping effects rather than simple addition.[^eit2025]

## Accounting and headline limits

The report monetizes avoided PM damage with London damage-cost values and discounts future values; accelerated
electrification yields €194 million and the combined scenario €951 million over 2026–2050. These are gross modeled
benefits, not net returns: no implementation costs are included for these policy scenarios. The authors separately
cost technology scenarios, which cannot be ranked against gross policy benefits as if the measures were equivalent.[^eit2025]

The executive summary and p. 70 describe mode shift as offering “up to five times” the non-exhaust PM reduction of
faster electrification. Page 72 instead discusses roughly fivefold benefits for the **combined** scenario relative
to faster electrification. Do not transfer this headline to cycling alone, all emissions, or a measured causal effect.
Use the explicitly reported Scenario 5 mass totals with their assumptions instead.[^eit2025]

## Uncertainty and relevance

Literature-derived wear factors have substantial methodological variability, and heavy-duty estimates rely on
assumptions/extrapolation. No scenario uncertainty interval is given with the extracted totals. The model does not
identify the cycling share of replacement travel or the causal effect of a lane, fare or land-use policy. A local
application needs achievable travel changes, implementation costs and exposure modelling, not just source shares.

This adds a conditional intervention comparison to the [2014 JRC review's source-share evidence](non-exhaust-pm10-review-share-limits.md),
not an independent replication of its underlying studies. It also differs from [NABSA's auto-replacement emissions
accounting](nabsa-2021-emissions-accounting-limits.md).
